# Behavioral "Firsts" — Earliest Accepted Evidence for 10 Hominin Behavioral Milestones

Research compiled 2026-08-07. Every value below is sourced to a page fetched during this research session; the link next to each value is the page that states it. Values that could not be confirmed from a fetched source are marked **n.a.** and listed in the "Unconfirmed values" section at the end.

**How to read the dates.** Every entry in this file is a **one-sided (minimum) bound** unless noted: it is the earliest *known* instance, and new evidence can only push it older. Ma = millions of years ago; ka = thousands of years ago; cal BP = calibrated years before present (AD 1950).

## 1. Lomekwian / earliest stone knapping

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~3.3 Ma — the Lomekwi 3 (LOM3) artefacts were deposited **after** the Toroto Tuff, dated **3.31 ± 0.02 Ma**, and lie within the Mammoth reverse subchron (C2An.2r, 3.33–3.21 Ma) ([Harmand et al. 2015, *Nature* 521:310–315](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464); [full text PDF](https://eps.rutgers.edu/images/3.3-million-year-old_stone_tools_from_Lomekwi_3_West_Turkana_Kenya.pdf)). This is a **minimum bound** for intentional knapping — it is the oldest known, not the oldest possible.

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** The LOM3 section as a whole sits between the α-Tulu Bor Tuff at its base, **3.44 ± 0.02 Ma**, and the Lokalalei Tuff at its top, **2.53 ± 0.02 Ma**; the artefact horizon is constrained more tightly by the Toroto Tuff (3.31 ± 0.02 Ma) plus the Mammoth subchron interval 3.33–3.21 Ma ([Harmand et al. 2015 PDF](https://eps.rutgers.edu/images/3.3-million-year-old_stone_tools_from_Lomekwi_3_West_Turkana_Kenya.pdf)). Stated errors are ±0.02 Ma on the tuffs. Practical credible window: **~3.33–3.21 Ma**, with the critics' own reading of the published section implying ~3.27 Ma ([Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá 2016, *PaleoAnthropology*](https://paleoanthro.org/media/journal/content/PA20160046.pdf)).

**C. Dating method(s).** ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar on volcanic tuffs, combined with **magnetostratigraphy** and extrapolation from sediment-accumulation rates ([Harmand et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464); method combination confirmed in [Lewis & Harmand 2016, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920290/)). **No radiocarbon is involved** — the age is far beyond the ¹⁴C range, so the calibrated/uncalibrated question does not arise.

**D. Type site / key site.** Lomekwi 3 (LOM3), west of Lake Turkana, **Kenya** ([Harmand et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464)).

**E. Dispute status: contested-evidence.** The age model itself is not seriously challenged; what is challenged is whether the artefacts are *in situ* at the dated horizon. Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá argue the site lacks detailed microstratigraphy, that the assemblage may be an ex-situ palimpsest from a slope deposit with vertical migration of clasts, and that the Toroto-correlated tuffaceous lens is not actually documented within the LOM3 section ([Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá 2016, *PaleoAnthropology* 2016:46–53](https://paleoanthro.org/media/journal/content/PA20160046.pdf)). Notably they do **not** claim the date is wrong, only that it is unproven.

**F. Strongest published counter-claim (structured).**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Counter-claim | LOM3 assemblage is not demonstrably in primary context; stratigraphic association with the dated tuff not established |
| Source | [Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá 2016, *PaleoAnthropology* 2016:46–53](https://paleoanthro.org/media/journal/content/PA20160046.pdf); follow-up Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá 2019, *J. African Archaeology* 17(2):173–176 (cited in [Harmand-group review](https://hal.science/hal-05406726v1/document)) |
| Alternative date proposed | No alternative date offered; critics' reading of the published figure implies ~3.27 Ma, i.e. essentially the same age ([Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá 2016](https://paleoanthro.org/media/journal/content/PA20160046.pdf)) |
| Standing | **Minority.** The excavators replied (Harmand et al. 2019, *J. African Archaeology* 17(2):177–181), and a later review states the in-situ controversy "has been addressed (Harmand et al. 2019)," with 250+ additional artefacts recovered 2014–2024 ([review PDF](https://hal.science/hal-05406726v1/document)) |
| Dating method of the counter-claim | None — the critique is taphonomic/stratigraphic, not geochronological |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** No older knapping claim was confirmed from a fetched source (**n.a.**). Note that the Dikika cut marks (entry 2) at ~3.39 Ma would *imply* older tool use, but no tools were found there, and the marks themselves are contested.

**H. Plain-language summary.** About 3.3 million years ago, someone in northern Kenya was already smashing rocks together on purpose to make sharp-edged flakes — roughly 700,000 years before the first stone tools anyone had previously found.

**Maker.** No hominin fossils or cut-marked bones were recovered at LOM3, so the toolmaker is unidentified; *Australopithecus* and *Kenyanthropus* have both been suggested ([Harmand et al. 2015 PDF](https://eps.rutgers.edu/images/3.3-million-year-old_stone_tools_from_Lomekwi_3_West_Turkana_Kenya.pdf)).

## 2. Dikika cut marks (earliest claimed butchery)

**A. Earliest accepted date.** Older than **3.39 Ma** as claimed — but this is a **claim, not an accepted first**. The bones (DIK-55-2, DIK-55-3) come from the Sidi Hakoma Member, bracketed by tuffs to **3.42–3.24 Ma** ([McPherron et al. 2010, *Nature* 466:857–860](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09248)).

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** Older limit ~3.42 Ma, younger limit ~3.24 Ma from the bracketing tuffs ([McPherron et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09248)). Critics additionally note the specimens were **surface finds without secure provenience**, so their stratigraphic position within that bracket is not fixed ([Domínguez-Rodrigo, Pickering & Bunn 2010, *PNAS* 107:20929–20934](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107)).

**C. Dating method(s).** ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar on tuffs bracketing the Sidi Hakoma Member ([McPherron et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09248)). No radiocarbon.

**D. Type site / key site.** Dikika, Lower Awash Valley, **Ethiopia** ([McPherron et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09248)).

**E. Dispute status: contested-evidence.** The dating is fine; the *marks* are the problem. Domínguez-Rodrigo, Pickering & Bunn applied a configurational approach and concluded that marks F, G, H2 and I on DIK-55-3 are high-probability **trampling** damage produced by sediment abrasion, not butchery — the enclosing deposit is a coarse sandstone with particles from −3φ to 8 mm, ideal for trampling striations ([Domínguez-Rodrigo, Pickering & Bunn 2010, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107)).

**F. Strongest published counter-claim (structured).**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Counter-claim | The marks are trampling damage; earliest secure butchery remains 2.6–2.5 Ma at Gona and Bouri, Ethiopia |
| Source | [Domínguez-Rodrigo, Pickering & Bunn 2010, *PNAS* 107:20929–20934](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107) |
| Key quantitative support | 96% of experimental trampling grooves are broad/open vs 4% of stone-flake cut marks; ~70% of trampling marks are curved/sinuous vs 10% of cut marks ([same source](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107)) |
| Alternative date | ~2.6–2.5 Ma for earliest accepted butchery ([same source](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107)) |
| Standing | **Majority skeptical** — the trampling interpretation is widely cited; a follow-up experiment appeared as Domínguez-Rodrigo et al. 2012, *J. Archaeol. Sci.* 39(2):205–214 (cited in the reply exchange, [McPherron et al. 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1101298108)) |
| Dating method | Not a dating dispute — taphonomic |
| Excavators' reply | The bone-bearing sand layer is 1.5 m thick and the dated section is older than 3.24 Ma; blind tests were used; ESEM/EDX revealed a **stone chip embedded in one mark**; critics offered no trampling analogue for the deep V-shaped marks DIK-55-2-A1/A2 ([McPherron et al. 2011, *PNAS* 108(21):E116](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1101298108)) |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** Dikika itself **is** the still-older, not-generally-accepted claim relative to entry 1's tool record. It is rejected by many because of the trampling-damage argument and the lack of secure provenience for surface-collected specimens ([Domínguez-Rodrigo, Pickering & Bunn 2010](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1013711107)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** Two animal bones from Ethiopia have scratches that one team says were made by stone tools 3.4 million years ago, but most other researchers think the scratches came from the bones being trodden into gritty sand.

**Attributed hominin.** *Australopithecus afarensis*, the only hominin known from those deposits ([McPherron et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09248)).

## 3. Controlled use of fire

This is the most method-conflicted entry in the set. Three competing claims are laid out below, then a verdict.

### 3a. Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~**1.0 Ma** for Stratum 10, described by the authors as "the earliest secure evidence for burning in an archaeological context" ([Berna et al. 2012, *PNAS* 109(20):E1215–E1220](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)). **A 2026 paper pushes this to 1.79–1.07 Ma (probably ~1.8 Ma) for Stratum 11** ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026, *PLOS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)). Both are minimum bounds.

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** Stratum 10 has normal magnetic polarity and is bracketed by cosmogenic burial ages of **1.27 ± 0.19 Ma** and **0.98 ± 0.19 Ma**, and is therefore assigned to the Jaramillo subchron, **1.07–0.99 Ma** ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)). Stratum 11 falls in reversed interval R2, between the Olduvai and Jaramillo subchrons, i.e. **1.79–1.07 Ma**; Stratum 12 is normal and assigned to Olduvai, **1.77–1.93 Ma** ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)). Underlying site chronology: 178 paleomagnetic samples passing acceptance criteria and 14 cosmogenic burial ages spanning **2.37 ± 0.14 Ma to 1.24 ± 0.10 Ma** ([Shaar et al. 2021, *Quaternary Science Reviews*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277379121001141)).

**C. Dating method(s).** **Magnetostratigraphy** plus **cosmogenic-nuclide burial dating**; no radiocarbon, no U-series, no luminescence for these strata ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109); [Shaar et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277379121001141)). Fire identification methods are separate: micromorphological FTIR (mFTIR) indicating heating to ~500 °C, sediment clays heated 400–700 °C, 43.7% of 675 faunal specimens burned, potlid fractures in 61 of 633 excavation spits ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)); the 2026 study used **bone luminescence** (455 nm excitation with a 580 nm red long-pass filter) validated against FTIR hydroxyapatite indices, with 32 white/grey bones in Stratum 11 showing a 100% FTIR–luminescence match ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)).

**D. Type site.** Wonderwerk Cave, Northern Cape, **South Africa** ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)).

**E. Dispute status: largely-settled for the *presence* of burning; method-conflict / definitional for whether it means fire *control*.** The strength of Wonderwerk is that the burned material lies **30 m from the cave entrance**, where wildfire and guano combustion can be excluded ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)). The general skeptical framing of Early Pleistocene fire claims — that burning presence does not demonstrate fire *production* or *maintenance* — is set out by [Pickering, *South African Journal of Science* 2012](https://journals.sajs.aosis.co.za/index.php/sajs/article/view/1250/1230).

### 3b. Gesher Benot Ya'aqov (GBY), Israel

**A.** ~**790 ka** ([Goren-Inbar et al. 2004, *Science* 304(5671):725–727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15118160/)).
**B.** The magnetostratigraphic sequence spans the Matuyama–Brunhes boundary (reversed→normal) at **0.79 Ma**, and the whole sequence sits in **OIS 18–20**, roughly 100 kyr of deposition ([Feibel/GBY chronology paper, *Quaternary Science Reviews*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379108001480)).
**C. Magnetostratigraphy** (Matuyama–Brunhes boundary), no radiocarbon ([QSR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379108001480)).
**D.** Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Jordan Valley, **Israel**.
**E.** The fire evidence is burned seeds, wood and flint, with clustered burned flint microartifacts forming **"phantom hearths"**; because fewer than 2% of the flint and wood at the site is burned, a site-wide wildfire is ruled out ([Goren-Inbar et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15118160/); [Hebrew University GBY project page](https://gby.huji.ac.il/centers/arch/arch_project_fire.html)). **Largely-settled** as evidence of hominin fire use with spatial patterning.

### 3c. Chesowanja and Koobi Fora, Kenya

**Chesowanja GnJi 1/6E:** burnt clay associated with Developed Oldowan artefacts, **>1.42 ± 0.07 Ma** based on **K-Ar** dating of an overlying basalt; 40 discolored clay aggregates ([Gowlett, Harris, Walton & Wood 1981, *Nature* 294:125–129](https://www.nature.com/articles/294125a0); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29451266/)). **Contested-evidence.** Glynn Isaac argued the burnt clay came from a bush fire, and Gowlett himself later described the material as a few large clasts of baked clay possibly derived from an adjacent, now-lost natural burning feature ([Gowlett 2016, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4874402/)).

**Koobi Fora FxJj20:** thermally altered lithics, soil aggregates and bone identified by FTIR, plus rubified sediment, at **1.5–1.6 Ma**; the authors call it "the earliest securely documented evidence of fire in the archaeological context" ([Hlubik et al. 2017, *Current Anthropology*](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692530); follow-up [Hlubik et al. 2019, *JHE*](https://anthro.rutgers.edu/images/documents/publications/Feibel%20Hlubik%20et%20al%20-%20Hominin%20Fire%202019.pdf)). Earlier work at the site used TL and magnetometry (Bellomo 1994; Bellomo & Kean 1997) and phytoliths (Rowlett 2000), cited in [Hlubik et al. 2017](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692530). **Contested-evidence:** an independent SFU thesis on the site concluded that "results cannot conclusively demonstrate an anthropogenic or a natural origin of burned sediment" ([SFU Summit thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/38258)), and the core objection is that open-air sites cannot exclude wildfire — the point on which [Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109) rest their claim to primacy.

### Verdict for the app

**A (recommended headline value).** **~1.0 Ma**, Wonderwerk Cave Stratum 10, as the earliest *securely contextualised* fire ([Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)) — with a flag that a 2026 study extends the same cave's record to **1.79–1.07 Ma** ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)).

**E. Dispute status overall: method-conflict.** Cave sites (Wonderwerk) give secure context but no direct radiometric date on the burning; open-air sites (Koobi Fora, Chesowanja) are older but cannot exclude natural fire.

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Claim | Date | Method | Site / country | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wonderwerk St. 11 | 1.79–1.07 Ma, probably ~1.8 Ma ([PLOS ONE 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)) | Magnetostratigraphy + cosmogenic burial; bone luminescence + FTIR for burning | Wonderwerk, South Africa | New (2026), not yet absorbed into consensus |
| Koobi Fora FxJj20 | 1.5–1.6 Ma ([Current Anthropology 2017](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692530)) | FTIR on thermally altered lithics/bone/sediment; earlier TL & magnetometry | Koobi Fora, Kenya | Minority / contested (open-air wildfire cannot be excluded, [SFU thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/38258)) |
| Chesowanja GnJi 1/6E | >1.42 ± 0.07 Ma ([*Nature* 1981](https://www.nature.com/articles/294125a0)) | K-Ar on overlying basalt | Chesowanja, Kenya | Largely superseded / doubted ([Gowlett 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4874402/)) |
| Wonderwerk St. 10 | ~1.0 Ma ([*PNAS* 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)) | Magnetostratigraphy + cosmogenic burial | Wonderwerk, South Africa | Majority — the standard "earliest secure" citation |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** Chesowanja (>1.42 Ma) and Koobi Fora (1.5–1.6 Ma) both predate Wonderwerk St. 10 and are not generally accepted as *controlled* fire, because at open-air sites natural bush fire cannot be excluded, and at Chesowanja the burnt clay may be redeposited from a natural feature ([Gowlett 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4874402/); [Berna et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** Burned bone and ash deep inside a South African cave show that someone was using fire at least a million years ago, and probably far longer, but proving that early people *made* fire rather than just used a lucky wildfire is much harder.

**Attributed hominin.** Wonderwerk Stratum 11 burning is attributed to ***Homo erectus***, with no evidence of cooking ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)).

## 4. Pigment / ochre use

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~**300 ka** for the earliest *secure* ochre use, in both Africa and Europe. A dedicated review states that between 1.5 Ma and 300 ka the evidence is sparse and unconfirmable, and that ochre use becomes recurrent only from ~160 ka ([Rosso 2022, *Journal of Anthropological Sciences* 100:45–69](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)). Minimum bound. The single best-dated early instance is **Kapthurin GnJh-15 at 0.284 ± 0.012 Ma** ([Deino & McBrearty 2002, *JHE*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Deino-McBrearty-2002-JHE-date-of-Kapthurin.pdf)); Olorgesailie's worked pigments are ~**305–320 ka** ([Brooks et al. 2018, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao2646)).

**B. Uncertainty bounds.**
- Kapthurin: GnJh-15 lies immediately below the K4 Bedded Tuff, whose upper weighted mean is **0.284 ± 0.012 Ma** (from 0.271 ± 0.013 and 0.291 ± 0.010 Ma); the underlying Grey Tuff (K3) is **0.509 ± 0.009 Ma**, giving a bracket of ~509–284 ka for the member ([Deino & McBrearty 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Deino-McBrearty-2002-JHE-date-of-Kapthurin.pdf)).
- Olorgesailie BOK-1E: 86 manganese-oxide-bearing lumps below tephras dated ~305 ka; GOK-1 worked red ochre with an upper limit set by a U-series age of **277 ± 1.8 ka**; overall ~305–320 ka ([Brooks et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao2646)).
- Twin Rivers, Zambia: mass-spectrometric U-series flowstone at **195,000 ± 19,000 BP**; earlier conventional U-series at **230,000 +35,000/−28,000 BP**; corrected speleothem ages of 266,000 BP and two in excess of 400,000 BP; the pigment assemblage is reported as **270,000–170,000 BP** ([Barham 2002, *Current Anthropology*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf)).

**C. Dating method(s).**
- Kapthurin: **⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar**, using both single-crystal total fusion (SCTF) and laser incremental heating (LIH) on anorthoclase ([Deino & McBrearty 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Deino-McBrearty-2002-JHE-date-of-Kapthurin.pdf)).
- Olorgesailie: **⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar** (by A.L. Deino) on tephras plus **U-series** ([Brooks et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao2646)).
- Twin Rivers: **U-series** on speleothem/flowstone, both conventional (alpha-counting) and mass-spectrometric ([Barham 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf)).
- Blombos: **TL** on burnt lithics and **OSL** on the overlying dune (see entry 7 for the numbers).
- Qafzeh: **TL** on burnt flint ([Valladas et al. 1988, *Nature* 331:614–616](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)).
- **No radiocarbon** underpins any of the earliest ochre dates.

**D. Type site / key site.** For the earliest well-dated occurrence: **Kapthurin Formation site GnJh-15, Baringo, Kenya**, with >70 red ochre items totalling >5 kg plus ochre-stained grindstones ([Deino & McBrearty 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Deino-McBrearty-2002-JHE-date-of-Kapthurin.pdf)). For earliest *deliberately worked* pigment: **Olorgesailie, Kenya**, described as the "oldest-known clearly worked pigments," including a rock with grinding striations, anthropogenic chop marks and an attempted perforation ([Brooks et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao2646)). For the largest early assemblage: **Twin Rivers, Zambia** (302 pieces reported by [Barham 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf); 497 pieces per [Rosso 2022](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)); and **Blombos Cave, South Africa**, with >8,000 pieces totalling 5.8 kg, ochre-processing toolkits at 100 ka, and an ochre-crayon cross-hatch on silcrete at 73 ka ([Rosso 2022](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)).

**E. Dispute status: definitional.** The dating is solid; the argument is about what counts as *pigment use*. The presence of iron-oxide lumps at a site does not by itself prove symbolic or even utilitarian colour use — Rosso's review classifies the 1.5 Ma–300 ka record as sparse and unconfirmable precisely because anthropogenic modification cannot be demonstrated ([Rosso 2022](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)). A widely used three-phase model formalises this: an **initial** phase 500–330 ka, an **emergent** phase 330–160 ka, and **habitual** use from 160 ka ([Dapschauskas et al., *Journal of World Prehistory*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10963-022-09170-2)).

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Twin Rivers | 270,000–170,000 BP; flowstone 195,000 ± 19,000 BP ([Barham 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf)) | U-series (conventional + MS) | Twin Rivers, Zambia | Majority; often cited at ~250 ka by the [Smithsonian](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/art-music/rock-art/ancient-pigments) |
| Canteen Kopje | ~300 ka ([Rosso 2022](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)) | n.a. (not stated in fetched source) | Canteen Kopje, South Africa | Cited in review |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** Three are explicitly rejected in the review literature: **Gadeb, Ethiopia (1–1.5 Ma)**, red-streaked basalt; **Olduvai Gorge (1–1.5 Ma)**, two red ochre lumps; and **Isernia la Pineta, Italy (~610 ka)**, pebbles with possible ochre traces. All are rejected because the anthropogenic nature of the material cannot be demonstrated ([Rosso 2022](https://www.isita-org.com/jass/Contents/2022vol100/Rosso/35920761.pdf)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** By around 300,000 years ago people in Kenya were collecting red and black mineral chunks and grinding them into powder — the world's first paint — though we cannot tell whether they used it on their bodies, on hides, or as glue.

**Attributed hominin.** Twin Rivers pigment use is attributed to *Homo heidelbergensis*/*helmei* ([Barham 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf)). The Kenyan Middle Pleistocene sites are not confidently attributable to a named species from the fetched sources.

## 5. Deliberate burial / mortuary treatment

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~**430 ka** for deliberate accumulation of human bodies at Sima de los Huesos — a **minimum bound**, since the U-series speleothem was deposited *directly on top of* Cranium 4 ([Arsuaga et al. 2014, *Science* 344:1358–1363](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/321/1/Neandertal%20roots%20Cranial%20and%20chronological%20evidence%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos_Arsuaga_et_al_2014.pdf)). For unambiguous **grave-cutting burial of anatomically modern humans**, the figure is ~**92–100 ka** at Qafzeh ([Valladas et al. 1988, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0); [Smithsonian Human Origins](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/burial/qafzeh-oldest-intentional-burial)).

**B. Uncertainty bounds.**
- Sima de los Huesos: cave-raft speleothem on Cranium 4 (LU-6) = **434 +36/−24 ka** (n=5); pIR-IR on K-feldspar **428 ± 27 to 441 ± 25 ka**; quartz **396 ± 35 to 429 ± 32 ka**; ESR on quartz **443 ± 90 ka**; ESR/U-series on a bear tooth gives a minimum of **261 +26/−25 ka**; overall minimum ~430 ka ([Arsuaga et al. 2014](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/321/1/Neandertal%20roots%20Cranial%20and%20chronological%20evidence%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos_Arsuaga_et_al_2014.pdf)). Independently: pIR-IR K-feldspar **433 ± 15 ka**, TT-OSL single-grain quartz **416 ± 19 ka**, pooled weighted mean **427 ± 12 ka** (n=6) as a minimum age for the hominins, with palaeomagnetism confirming the Brunhes Chron (<780 ka) and thus a bracket of **427 ± 12 ka to ~780 ka** ([Arnold et al. 2014, *JHE*](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/315/1/Luminescence%20dating%20and%20palaeomagnetic%20age%20constraint%20on%20hominins%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos,%20Atapuerca,%20Spain_Arnold_et_al_2014.pdf)). A later TT-OSL study on the hominin-bearing clay gives **455,000–440,000 (mean 448,000)**, bracketing the deposit to **448–430 ka** in MIS 12 ([Demuro et al. 2019, *JHE* 131:76–95, summarised by Fundación Atapuerca](https://www.atapuerca.org/es/ficha/Z38F52AED-E5A7-63CB-947EF6317FA2BD38/acotando-la-edad-de-la-sima-de-los-huesos)). The site as a whole is given as **430,000–300,000** in the most recent synthesis ([Arsuaga et al. 2024, *The Anatomical Record* 307(7):2225–2245](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/6863e7bc-5cb1-4af2-9678-aa1928f13a1c)).
- Qafzeh: TL on 20 burnt flints from the hominid-bearing layers gives **~90–100 kyr BP**; no error is stated on the published page (**n.a.**) ([Valladas et al. 1988](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)). The Smithsonian gives ~100,000 years ([Smithsonian](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/burial/qafzeh-oldest-intentional-burial)).

**C. Dating method(s).** Sima de los Huesos: **U-series** on speleothem, **pIR-IR** (post-infrared infrared-stimulated luminescence) on K-feldspar, **TT-OSL** on single-grain quartz, **ESR** on quartz, **combined ESR/U-series** on tooth enamel, and **palaeomagnetism** ([Arsuaga et al. 2014](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/321/1/Neandertal%20roots%20Cranial%20and%20chronological%20evidence%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos_Arsuaga_et_al_2014.pdf); [Arnold et al. 2014](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/315/1/Luminescence%20dating%20and%20palaeomagnetic%20age%20constraint%20on%20hominins%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos,%20Atapuerca,%20Spain_Arnold_et_al_2014.pdf)). Qafzeh: **thermoluminescence** on burnt flint ([Valladas et al. 1988](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)). **No radiocarbon** in either case — both are beyond or at the edge of the ¹⁴C range.

**D. Type site / key site.** Sima de los Huesos, Sierra de Atapuerca, **Spain** — >7,000 hominin fossils from a minimum of 29 individuals ([Arsuaga et al. 2024](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/6863e7bc-5cb1-4af2-9678-aa1928f13a1c)). Secondary: Qafzeh Cave, **Israel** — at least 15 modern humans plus 71 pieces of red ochre and ochre-stained tools ([Smithsonian](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/burial/qafzeh-oldest-intentional-burial)).

**E. Dispute status: definitional (Sima) / contested-evidence (Rising Star).** At Sima, the fossils were not placed in dug graves; the claim is *deliberate disposal of bodies down a shaft*. Alternative explanations that have been advanced include carnivore transport, accidental falls into the shaft, and flood transport, and deliberate disposal is not universally accepted. The excavators' strongest support is the 2024 conclusion of "confirmation that the origin of the accumulation of human fossils was of an anthropic nature" ([Arsuaga et al. 2024](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/6863e7bc-5cb1-4af2-9678-aa1928f13a1c)), backed by the finding that Cranium 17 bears two perimortem frontal lesions produced by the same object, interpreted as lethal interpersonal violence and described as evidence for the earliest funerary behaviour in the human fossil record ([Sala et al. 2015, *PLOS ONE* 10(5):e0126589, per IPHES](https://www.iphes.cat/en/lethal-wounds-skull-may-indicate-430000-year-old-murder); [BBC coverage](https://www.bbc.com/news/science-environment-32890697)).

**The *Homo naledi* claim and its rebuttals.** Berger and colleagues claim deliberate burial in the Dinaledi Chamber of the Rising Star system, South Africa — three named features (the Hill Antechamber Feature, Dinaledi Feature 1, and Puzzle Box Feature) roughly 30 m below the surface and 80 m from the entrance — with US-ESR on two *H. naledi* teeth giving **139–335 ka** and a U/Th flowstone (RS18/RS68) giving a **244 ± 3 ka** minimum; they state this exceeds the earliest known *H. sapiens* cultural burials in Africa by up to 160,000 years ([Berger et al., eLife reviewed preprint 89106](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106)). Two formal rebuttals are on record and are cited on the paper's own review page: **Martinón-Torres, Garate, Herries & Petraglia 2023, "No scientific evidence that *Homo naledi* buried their dead and produced rock art," *Journal of Human Evolution*, 103464**, which notes that Robbins et al. 2021 showed that before ~241 ka — prior to the Dragon's Back block collapse — easier access existed via the Post Box Chamber, so the chamber's inaccessibility (a core plank of the burial argument) is an open question; and **Foecke, Queffelec & Pickering 2024, "No Sedimentological Evidence for Deliberate Burial by *Homo naledi*," *PaleoAnthropology*** ([both cited at eLife reviews](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106/reviews)).

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Claim | Date | Method | Site / country | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sima de los Huesos deliberate accumulation | min ~427 ± 12 ka; deposit 448–430 ka ([Arnold et al. 2014](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/315/1/Luminescence%20dating%20and%20palaeomagnetic%20age%20constraint%20on%20hominins%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos,%20Atapuerca,%20Spain_Arnold_et_al_2014.pdf)) | U-series, pIR-IR, TT-OSL, ESR, palaeomag | Atapuerca, Spain | Majority for anthropic accumulation; definitional debate over "burial" |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** The *Homo naledi* claim is not older than Sima, but it is the prominent *rejected-by-many* burial claim in this space; it is contested on sedimentological grounds (no evidence of grave-fill features) and on accessibility grounds (the chamber may have been easier to reach before ~241 ka) ([Foecke et al. 2024 and Martinón-Torres et al. 2023, both cited at eLife](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106/reviews)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** More than 400,000 years ago in northern Spain, people were putting dead bodies down a deep cave shaft rather than leaving them out in the open — the oldest sign anywhere that humans treated their dead differently from other animals.

**Attributed hominin.** Sima de los Huesos: early Neanderthal lineage ([Arsuaga et al. 2014, "Neandertal roots"](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/321/1/Neandertal%20roots%20Cranial%20and%20chronological%20evidence%20from%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos_Arsuaga_et_al_2014.pdf)). Qafzeh: anatomically modern *Homo sapiens*, described as "Proto-Cro-Magnons" ([Valladas et al. 1988](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)). Rising Star: *Homo naledi* ([eLife 89106](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89106)).

## 6. Figurative art

**A. Earliest accepted date.** **≥51.2 ka** — a minimum bound — for the narrative rock-art scene at **Leang Karampuang**, Maros-Pangkep, Sulawesi, Indonesia ([Oktaviana, Joannes-Boyau et al. 2024, *Nature* 631(8022):814–818](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11269172/)). Because the dated calcite sits *over* the paint, the true age of the painting is older.

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** Sample LK1, taken over human figure H2, gives **53.5 ± 2.3 ka**, hence a minimum of **51.2 ka**. Other samples from the same panel: LK4 (over figure H1) **45.9 ± 1.9 ka** → min 44.0 ka; LK3 (over the pig) **34.1 ± 2.2 ka** → min 31.9 ka; LK2 **19.2 ± 0.5 ka** → min 18.7 ka ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)). The 51,200-year figure is independently recorded as the oldest known painting ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/467702-oldest-painting-%E2%80%93-figurative-art)).

**Discrepancy to flag:** for the re-dated **Leang Bulu' Sipong 4** scene, the paper's abstract states a minimum age of **50.2 ± 2.2 ka** while the results text gives **51.2 ± 2.2 ka / min 48 ka** for sample BSP4.5. Treat the Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 minimum as ~48–50 ka and cite the abstract figure if a single number is needed ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)).

**C. Dating method(s).** **Laser-ablation U-series (LA-U-series) imaging by LA-MC-ICP-MS**, using a 44 × 44 μm spot, performed at the BIOMICS laboratory, Southern Cross University ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)). The earlier work used **solution U-series** on drilled calcite ([Aubert et al. 2019, *Nature* 576:442–445, as reported in the 2024 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)). **No radiocarbon** — so no calibration question. This matters, because the European comparanda (Chauvet) *are* radiocarbon-based.

**D. Type site / key site.** Leang Karampuang, Maros-Pangkep karst, Sulawesi, **Indonesia**. The scene consists of a red *Sus celebensis* (Sulawesi warty pig), 92 × 38 cm, plus at least three human-like figures (H1 42 × 27 cm, H2 28 × 25 cm, H3 35 × 5 cm) ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)).

**E. Dispute status: rival-chronologies (with a definitional edge).** The Sulawesi dates are minimum ages on overlying calcite, and the 2024 re-dating of Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 produced some samples *younger* than the 2019 solution U-series results, showing that method choice materially changes individual sample ages ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)). Separately, the older Iberian claims are the subject of an active method dispute (below). Southern Cross University's own statement of the standing is direct: "None of the famous European Ice Age art is anywhere near as old as 51,200 years, with the exception of some controversial finds in Spain" ([Southern Cross University](https://www.scu.edu.au/news/2024/oldest-cave-art-indonesia/)).

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Claim | Date | Method | Site / country | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 scene (re-dated) | min ~48 ka; abstract states 50.2 ± 2.2 ka; results text 51.2 ± 2.2 ka ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)) | LA-U-series | Sulawesi, Indonesia | Majority |
| Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 (original) | min 43.9 ka (BSP4.3 = 44.4 ± 0.5 ka) ([Aubert et al. 2019, per *Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)) | Solution U-series | Sulawesi, Indonesia | **Superseded** by the 2024 LA-U-series result |
| Hohle Fels Venus (ivory figurine) | at least **35,000 calendar years** ([Conard 2009, *Nature* 457:248–252](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07995)) | Basal Aurignacian stratigraphy; specific ¹⁴C dates and calibration status **not stated** on the fetched page (**n.a.**) | Hohle Fels, Swabian Jura, Germany | Majority as oldest *three-dimensional* figurative art; predates Gravettian Venuses by ≥5,000 years |
| Chauvet-Pont d'Arc parietal art | First occupation **37,000–33,500 cal BP** (95% range 37.0–36.2 to 34.4–33.5 ka cal BP), centred ~35.9 ka cal BP; second phase 31,000–28,000 cal BP ([Quiles et al. 2016, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)) | **Calibrated** ¹⁴C (IntCal13, OxCal 4.2, Bayesian, 259 ¹⁴C determinations of >350 total dates) + U-Th + TL + ³⁶Cl exposure dating | Chauvet, Ardèche, France | Majority; the best-dated Palaeolithic figurative cave art anywhere |
| El Castillo red disk | min **40.8 ka**; hand stencil min 37.3 ka; claviform-like symbol min 35.6 ka ([Pike et al. 2012, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1219957)) | U-series on overlying calcite crusts | El Castillo, Spain | Majority for the date, but the disk is **non-figurative**, so it does not compete on this behaviour |
| Iberian "Neanderthal art" | older than **64.8 ka** ([Hoffmann et al. 2018, *Science* 359:912–915](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap7778)) | U-Th on overlying carbonate crusts | La Pasiega, Maltravieso, Ardales, Spain | **Contested minority**, and non-figurative in any case |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** The Iberian >64.8 ka claim ([Hoffmann et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap7778)) is the main one, and it is rejected or heavily qualified for three distinct reasons: (i) **open-system behaviour in the dated calcite** — uranium loss can make U-Th ages far too old, demonstrated empirically at Nerja Cave and argued to apply to Ardales, Maltravieso and La Pasiega, where U concentration and apparent age are inversely correlated ([Pons-Branchu et al. 2020, *J. Archaeol. Sci.* 117:105120](https://hal.science/hal-02514905v1/file/S030544032030042X.pdf)); (ii) **the stratigraphic relationship between crust and pigment, and the identification of the marks as art at all**, is challenged by [Aubert, Brumm & Huntley 2018, *JHE* 125:215–217](https://www.iea.usp.br/eventos/aubertbrumm) and by a 44-author reply, [White et al. 2020, *JHE* 144:102640](https://univ-rennes.hal.science/hal-02365692v1/file/texto_completo.pdf), which states "there is still no convincing archaeological evidence that Neanderthals created Iberian cave art"; and (iii) a comment arguing the data better support an age of **~47,000 years** ([Slimak et al. 2018 comment, *Science*, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30237321/)). The original authors have rebutted all three ([response to Aubert et al.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/436042/1/Response_to_Aubert_et_al.pdf); [response to Slimak et al.](https://rodin.uca.es/bitstream/handle/10498/21511/Hoffmann%20et%20al.Science362..pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y); response to White et al., *JHE* 144:102810, [record](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1190888/response-to-white-et-als-reply-still-no-archaeological-evidence-that-neanderthals-created-iberian-cave-art-j-hum-evol-2020-102640)). Even if accepted, these motifs are **non-figurative**, so they would not displace Sulawesi for *figurative* art.

**H. Plain-language summary.** The oldest known picture of anything is a painting of a wild pig and three human-like figures on a cave wall in Indonesia, made at least 51,200 years ago.

**Attributed hominin.** *Homo sapiens* (the Sulawesi art). The Iberian claim, if valid, would be Neanderthal ([Hoffmann et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap7778)).

## 7. Abstract / symbolic engraving

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~**73 ka** for the Blombos ochre-crayon cross-hatch drawing ([Henshilwood et al. 2018, *Nature* 562:115–118](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0514-3)) and **77 ± 6 ka** for the engraved ochre pieces from the same cave ([Henshilwood et al. 2002, *Science* 295:1278–1280](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1067575)). Take **77 ± 6 ka** as the headline value for *engraving*; the 73 ka piece is a *drawn* rather than engraved mark. Both are minimum bounds for the behaviour.

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** Blombos: five burnt lithic specimens from the upper occupational phase BBC M1 give a TL mean age of **77 ± 6 ka**; the overlying dune gives an OSL age of **69 ± 5 ka** (synthetic-aliquot age 70 ± 5 ka), with equivalent doses of 47.9 ± 1.7 Gy (30 aliquots) and 48.5 ± 1.2 Gy (18 synthetic aliquots) ([*Science* 2002](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1067575)). The 73 ka drawing has **no error stated** on the fetched page (**n.a.**) ([*Nature* 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0514-3)).

**C. Dating method(s).** Blombos: **thermoluminescence (TL)** on burnt lithics for the engraved-ochre layer, plus **OSL** on the overlying dune sand ([*Science* 2002](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1067575)). The dating method for the 2018 drawing is **not stated** on the fetched page (**n.a.**). Trinil: **⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar** and **luminescence** — the fetched page explicitly does *not* report U-series or ESR for the age model ([Joordens et al. 2015, *Nature* 518:228–231](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13962)). Diepkloof: **TL and OSL** ([Texier et al. 2010, *PNAS* 107:6180–6185, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20194764/); [Texier & Porraz 2013, *Palethnologie* 5](https://journals.openedition.org/palethnologie/4818?lang=en)). **No radiocarbon** underpins any of these.

**D. Type site / key site.** Blombos Cave, Western Cape, **South Africa**. The 2002 engravings come from BBC M1 (samples BBC24 and BBC23 from unit CA/CB; BBC15, BBC20, BBC22 from unit CC) ([*Science* 2002](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1067575)); the 2018 cross-hatch is an ochre drawing on a silcrete flake from a Still Bay level ([*Nature* 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0514-3)).

**E. Dispute status: contested-evidence for the oldest claim (Trinil); largely-settled for Blombos.** Blombos is not seriously challenged as intentional engraving. The *Homo erectus* Trinil shell, by contrast, would push abstract engraving back ~430,000 years, and its own describers concede that the engraving's "function or meaning is not currently assessable" ([*Nature* 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13962)). Diepkloof illustrates a **rival-chronologies** problem within a single site: the collection was first published as ~60 ka, then re-dated.

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Claim | Date | Method | Site / country | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blombos engraved ochre | 77 ± 6 ka ([*Science* 2002](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1067575)) | TL on burnt lithics; OSL on overlying dune | Blombos, South Africa | Majority — the standard citation |
| Blombos ochre-crayon drawing | ~73 ka ([*Nature* 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0514-3)) | n.a. (not stated on fetched page) | Blombos, South Africa | Majority; described as pre-dating the earliest previously known abstract and figurative *drawings* by ≥30,000 years |
| Trinil *Pseudodon* engraved shell DUB1006-fL | max **0.54 ± 0.10 Ma**, min **0.43 ± 0.05 Ma** (1σ analytical) ([*Nature* 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13962)) | ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar + luminescence on shell infills | Trinil, Java, Indonesia | **Minority / contested** as symbolic behaviour, though the age model itself *revised the Hauptknochenschicht younger* than previously estimated |
| Diepkloof engraved ostrich eggshell (original) | ~60,000 years; 270 marked fragments; "the earliest reliable evidence of what is a widespread modern practice" ([Texier et al. 2010, *PNAS*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20194764/)) | TL + OSL | Diepkloof, South Africa | **Revised** |
| Diepkloof engraved ostrich eggshell (revised) | full collection spans **109 ± 10 to 52 ± 5 ka**; the 2010-published pieces bracketed **85 ± 9 to 52 ± 5 ka** (Tribolo et al. 2013) ([Texier et al. 2013 discussion](https://www.academia.edu/4180673/The_context_form_and_significance_of_the_MSA_engraved_ostrich_eggshell_collection_from_Diepkloof_Rock_Shelter_Western_Cape_South_Africa)) | New TL + OSL series | Diepkloof, South Africa | Contested — "controversy exists over the precise dating of the Diepkloof sequence" ([*PNAS* 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1910880117)); other Howiesons Poort work adopts ~64.8–59.5 ka ([*PLOS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0338509&type=printable)) and OSL-based syntheses put Howiesons Poort at ~65–59 ka ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61643/chapter/539817021)) |
| La Roche-Cotard non-figurative engravings | older than **57 ± 3 ka** ([*PLOS ONE* 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10284424/)) | n.a. (not extracted) | La Roche-Cotard, France | Presented as "the earliest unambiguous Neanderthal engravings on cave walls" |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** The **Trinil shell at 0.43–0.54 Ma** is the still-older claim. It is not generally accepted as evidence of symbolic behaviour because a single geometric groove pattern on one shell, from a 19th-century museum collection, cannot be shown to carry meaning — the authors themselves state its function or meaning is "not currently assessable" ([*Nature* 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13962)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** About 77,000 years ago in a South African cave, someone scratched a deliberate cross-hatched pattern into a lump of red ochre — the oldest design we can be confident a human made on purpose.

**Attributed hominin.** Blombos: early *Homo sapiens* ([*Nature* 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0514-3)). Trinil: *Homo erectus*, per the authors ([*Nature* 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13962)).

## 8. Personal ornaments / shell beads

**A. Earliest accepted date.** **≥142 ka** — explicitly a minimum bound — for perforated *Tritia gibbosula* shell beads from Bizmoune Cave, described as "the oldest shell beads yet recovered" ([Sehasseh et al. 2021, *Science Advances* 7(39):eabi8620](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8457661/)).

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** The dated speleothem 4c′ within layer 4c gives **142,290 +29,300/−22,060 years (2σ)** ([Table 1, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8457661/)) — i.e. a 95% window of roughly **120,000–171,000 years** for the bead-bearing layer ([*Science* news report](https://www.science.org/content/article/world-s-oldest-known-beads-found-morocco)). A less reliable U/Th age on an ungulate tooth deeper in layer 4c suggests **149,150 +63,840/−36,690** years. A broadly distributed flowstone at the top of layer 4 gives a minimum of **~103 ka** for the top of layers 4–4c. The whole MSA core sequence is bracketed between **~62 and ≥142 ka** ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620); [PMC Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8457661/)).

**C. Dating method(s).** Primarily **uranium-series (U/Th) on speleothems**, with a supporting (less reliable) U/Th age on a tooth, and **OSL** as a cross-check. **No radiocarbon.** Importantly, the OSL and U/Th results **conflict**: OSL ages from layer 4c are uniformly younger than the U/Th ages and are no older than OSL ages from overlying layers (e.g. layer 4c OSL of 57,800 ± 7,200 and 61,400 ± 7,400 from Max Planck, and 109,000 ± 18,000 / 77,100 ± 12,800 / 66,400 ± 10,800 from Sheffield). The authors' preliminary hypothesis is that post-depositional accumulation of radiogenic material above bedrock raised dose rates over time, causing luminescence ages to underestimate ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620); [PMC Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8457661/)).

**D. Type site / key site.** Bizmoune Cave, near Essaouira, southwest **Morocco**, ~12 km inland from the Atlantic. 33 marine shell beads recovered 2014–2018: 32 from layer 4c, one from layer 4a; all but one are *Tritia gibbosula*, the exception being *Columbella rustica* ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620)).

**E. Dispute status: method-conflict.** The behaviour itself is not in doubt — perforations are oval (n=22) or circular (n=8), most partly natural but many showing additional human chipping, with 75% of shells showing polish and striations in the aperture, 21% showing similar modification of the apex, and traces of red pigment on some ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620)). The dispute is the **U/Th vs OSL disagreement within the same layer**, which the authors themselves flag as unresolved and "a topic for future research." The date is also hedged in the primary literature's own coverage: "the world's oldest **if new dates hold up**" ([*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/world-s-oldest-known-beads-found-morocco)). Note also a stratigraphic argument in favour of the old age: most layer-4c beads were recovered at absolute depths *greater* than the dated speleothem, so a large part of the sample sits stratigraphically beneath it ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620)).

**F. Structured alternatives.**

|---|---|---|---|---|

**H. Plain-language summary.** At least 142,000 years ago, people in Morocco were drilling holes in tiny sea-snail shells and stringing them together as jewellery — the oldest known objects worn purely to send a signal about who you are.

**Attributed hominin.** Early ***Homo sapiens*** ([*Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abi8620); [*Science* summary](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.acx9133)).

## 9. Intentional seafaring / open-water crossing

**A. Earliest accepted date.** For **intentional, demonstrably repeated open-water voyaging** the accepted figure is ~**65 ka**, the colonisation of Sahul recorded at Madjedbebe: modelled start age **65.0 ± (3.7, 5.7) kyr** ([Clarkson et al. 2017, *Nature* 547:306–310, full PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). This is a **minimum age** for the crossing — the paper frames it as "a new minimum age for the human colonization of Australia." For the *earliest water crossing of any kind*, Early Pleistocene Wallacean island occupations are older: **≥1.04 Ma** on Sulawesi ([Hakim, Brumm et al. 2025, *Nature*, via Griffith University release](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-archaeologists-oldest-evidence-humans-hobbit.html)) and **1.02 ± 0.02 Ma** on Flores ([Brumm et al. 2010, *Nature* 464:748–752](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08844)).

**B. Uncertainty bounds.**
- Madjedbebe: the lowest dense artefact band (the zone of first occupation, Phase 2) has modelled mean start and end ages of **65.0 ± (3.7, 5.7)** and **52.7 ± (2.4, 4.3)** kyr, where the first error term is the modelled age uncertainty at **95.4% probability excluding** and the second **including** total systematic uncertainty. The authors give a conservative floor of **59.3 ka** (65.0 minus 5.7 at 95.4%) and total OSL age uncertainties of only 3–4 kyr at 68.2% ([Clarkson et al. 2017 PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). Restated in the reply paper as **65 ± 6 ka at 95.4%**, i.e. initial settlement between **c. 71 and 59 ka** ([Clarkson et al. 2018 reply, *Australian Archaeology*](http://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson-Roberts-Jacobs-Marwick-et-al-2018-AA-reply.pdf)). The dating team's own summary: 28,500 individual grains from 56 samples, giving 65,000 with a range of about **60,000–70,000 at 95% probability** ([*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/find-australia-hints-very-early-human-exit-africa)).
- Flores/Wolo Sege: the overlying ignimbrite erupted at **1.02 ± 0.02 Ma**, a minimum since the dated rock lies *above* the artefacts; reported ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar results ranged 1.02–1.08 Ma with errors no more than ±60,000 years, and the underlying formation goes back to 1.8 Ma or more ([*Nature* 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08844); [*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/hobbit-ancestors-arrived-flores-early)).
- Sulawesi/Calio: **at least 1.04 Ma**; **no numerical error margin is stated** in the fetched source (**n.a.**) ([Griffith University via Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-archaeologists-oldest-evidence-humans-hobbit.html)).

**C. Dating method(s).**
- Madjedbebe: **single-grain OSL** (52 ages) with **Bayesian modelling**, plus **AMS radiocarbon** (26 ages) in close agreement with the OSL chronology ([Clarkson et al. 2017 PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf); [Hayes et al. 2022, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15174-x)). **The radiocarbon component is calibrated** — the paper's ages are expressed as calendar-scale ka and the ¹⁴C series is reported as consistent with the OSL calendar-year chronology; the headline 65 ka figure is however driven by **OSL, not radiocarbon**, and OSL ages are inherently in calendar years, so no calibration issue attaches to the primary value ([Clarkson et al. 2018 reply](http://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson-Roberts-Jacobs-Marwick-et-al-2018-AA-reply.pdf)).
- Flores/Wolo Sege: **⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar** on an overlying ignimbrite ([*Nature* 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08844)). The previous Flores figure of 0.88 ± 0.07 Ma at Mata Menge came from **zircon fission-track** dating ([same source](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08844)).
- Sulawesi/Calio: **palaeomagnetic dating** of the artefact-bearing sandstone plus **direct dating of an excavated pig fossil** ([Phys.org / Griffith](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-archaeologists-oldest-evidence-humans-hobbit.html)).

**D. Type site / key site.** Madjedbebe (formerly Malakunanja II), a sandstone rock shelter in western Arnhem Land, Mirarr Country, **Australia** ([Clarkson et al. 2017 PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). For the Early Pleistocene crossings: Wolo Sege, So'a Basin, Flores, and Calio, southern Sulawesi, **Indonesia** ([*Nature* 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08844); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-archaeologists-oldest-evidence-humans-hobbit.html)).

**E. Dispute status: rival-chronologies (Madjedbebe) / definitional (Flores and Sulawesi).**
For Madjedbebe, critics prefer the **modelled end age of 53 ± 4 ka** over the modelled start age of 65 ± 6 ka; the excavators respond that these are the minimum and maximum ages of the Phase 2 material and that there is "no sound statistical basis for rejecting the latter age," while conceding that Phase 2 is not a snapshot but >11,000 artefacts accumulated over ~12 millennia ([Clarkson et al. 2018 reply](http://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson-Roberts-Jacobs-Marwick-et-al-2018-AA-reply.pdf)). Charcoal survival during chemical pretreatment was "a pervasive problem" at the site ([same reply](http://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson-Roberts-Jacobs-Marwick-et-al-2018-AA-reply.pdf)), and the 65 ka figure "has, as of 2017, been questioned by some experts" ([Wikipedia summary of the debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madjedbebe)). Note also that some artefacts in the deepest sediments were estimated at ~80,000 years, at least 95% likely older than 70,000, but the excavators did not treat this as evidence of occupation ([Guardian coverage](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2017/jul/19/dig-finds-evidence-of-aboriginal-habitation-up-to-80000-years-ago)).

For the ~1 Ma Wallacean sites the dispute is **definitional**: a hominin reaching an island does not prove *intentional* seafaring. A review of the field explicitly frames the pre-1 Ma Wolo Sege toolmaker as one that "presumably **rafted** to get there" ([Dennell et al., *QSR*, Nottingham repository copy](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/732520)), i.e. accidental passive drift rather than navigation. The maritime-dispersal literature calls the So'a Basin data "the most extensive—if still contentious" ([Antiquity, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maritime-hominin-dispersals-in-the-pleistocene-advancing-the-debate/88FE85D05C4B0CEA105FEE36065B4CC0)). Sahul is different in kind because it required crossing a permanent water gap that was never bridged at any sea level, at a scale requiring repeated, provisioned voyages.

**F. Structured alternatives.**

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** The **≥1.04 Ma Sulawesi** and **1.02 Ma Flores** occupations are far older than Sahul, and are not generally accepted as *intentional seafaring* because passive rafting on vegetation mats during storms or tsunamis is a sufficient explanation for one-off island arrivals ([Dennell et al., QSR](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/732520)); the maritime-dispersal debate remains explicitly unresolved ([Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/maritime-hominin-dispersals-in-the-pleistocene-advancing-the-debate/88FE85D05C4B0CEA105FEE36065B4CC0)). Separately, the **~80,000-year** estimates from the deepest Madjedbebe sediments are not claimed as occupation by the excavators themselves ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2017/jul/19/dig-finds-evidence-of-aboriginal-habitation-up-to-80000-years-ago)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** People reached Australia about 65,000 years ago, which meant crossing open ocean that never dried up even at the lowest sea levels — the first journey we can be sure was planned rather than a lucky accident on a floating raft of debris.

**Attributed hominin.** Madjedbebe: *Homo sapiens*. Flores/Wolo Sege: unknown — no hominin fossils were found with the million-year-old tools, though the working hypothesis is an ancestor of *Homo floresiensis* ([lead author quoted at Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2010-03-hobbit-island-colonised-earlier-thought.html)). Calio, Sulawesi: unknown — no hominin fossils, so the makers' identity remains unknown ([Griffith/Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-archaeologists-oldest-evidence-humans-hobbit.html)).

## 10. Earliest cooking of food

**Which I chose and why.** I chose **earliest cooking** over **earliest grave goods**. Cooking has a single, dedicated, primary-literature earliest-evidence claim with a named physical proxy and a published detection method (X-ray diffraction size–strain analysis of fish pharyngeal teeth), published in a Nature-family journal ([Zohar et al. 2022, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)). By contrast, the earliest grave-goods literature is thinner and largely definitional: the standard candidate, the fallow-deer (*Dama dama*) antlers laid on the chest of the adolescent Qafzeh 11 in a grave cut into bedrock, rests on excavation reports from the 1930s–1980s, and the only age I could verify from a primary fetched source is the site-level TL figure of ~90–100 kyr BP with no published error ([Valladas et al. 1988, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0); site framing at [Smithsonian Human Origins](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/burial/qafzeh-oldest-intentional-burial)). Cooking is the better-sourced "first."

**A. Earliest accepted date.** ~**0.78 Ma (780,000 years ago)**, early Middle Pleistocene ([Zohar et al. 2022, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)). Minimum bound.

**B. Uncertainty bounds.** The evidence comes from eight sequential archaeological horizons in Area B, Layer II-6 levels 1–7, correlated to **marine isotope stages 18–20** ([Zohar et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)). The site-level chronological anchor is the Matuyama–Brunhes boundary at **0.79 Ma**, with the sequence spanning OIS 18–20 over roughly 100 kyr ([GBY chronology, *Quaternary Science Reviews*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379108001480)). No ± figure is published on the 780 ka value itself (**n.a.** for a stated error).

**C. Dating method(s).** **Magnetostratigraphy** (Matuyama–Brunhes reversal) and marine-isotope-stage correlation ([QSR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379108001480)). **No radiocarbon** — the age is roughly 15 times beyond the radiocarbon limit. The *cooking* determination is a separate analytical method: **size–strain analysis using X-ray powder diffraction** on recovered pharyngeal teeth, showing exposure to low temperatures, **below 500 °C** — the signature of controlled cooking rather than incidental burning ([Zohar et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)).

**D. Type site / key site.** Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Jordan Valley, **Israel** ([Zohar et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)).

**E. Dispute status: definitional.** The species involved are *Luciobarbus longiceps* and *Carasobarbus canis*, freshwater carp; most pharyngeal teeth from horizons II-6 L1–7 were spatially associated with the same "phantom hearths" — clusters of burnt flint microartifacts — that anchor the site's fire evidence ([Zohar et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z); [Goren-Inbar et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15118160/)). The interpretive step under debate is whether sub-500 °C heating of fish teeth demonstrates *deliberate cooking* as opposed to fish being heated incidentally near a hearth; the authors argue the combination of archaeological, taphonomic and XRD data indicates fish were cooked and consumed on site ([Zohar et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)). Note the related negative result: the 2026 Wonderwerk study, despite pushing the fire record back to ~1.8 Ma, found **no cooking evidence** ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)).

**F. Structured alternatives.**

| Claim | Date | Method | Site / country | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earliest cooking (fish) | ~0.78 Ma ([*Nature Ecol. Evol.* 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-022-01910-z)) | Magnetostratigraphy for age; XRD size–strain analysis for cooking | Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Israel | Majority as earliest cooking claim |
| Earliest fire *without* cooking | Wonderwerk St. 11, 1.79–1.07 Ma ([*PLOS ONE* 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)) | Magnetostratigraphy + cosmogenic burial; bone luminescence + FTIR | Wonderwerk, South Africa | New; explicitly reports no cooking evidence, so does not displace GBY |
| Earliest grave goods (the road not taken) | Qafzeh 11, fallow-deer antlers on the chest in a grave cut into bedrock; site TL ~90–100 kyr BP ([*Nature* 1988](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)); ~100,000 years per [Smithsonian](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/burial/qafzeh-oldest-intentional-burial) | TL on 20 burnt flints | Qafzeh, Israel | Majority as earliest grave goods, but no published error and no primary-source date for the individual burial |

**G. Still-older claims not generally accepted.** No older cooking claim was confirmed from a fetched source (**n.a.**). The gap between the earliest fire (~1.0–1.8 Ma) and the earliest cooking (~0.78 Ma) is itself the substantive point: fire is much older than any demonstrated cooking, and the Wonderwerk team found fire without cooking ([Marin-Monfort et al. 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347480)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** About 780,000 years ago, people beside an ancient lake in Israel were heating carp gently enough to cook them rather than char them — the oldest known meal that was deliberately prepared with heat.

**Attributed hominin.** Not attributed to a named species in the fetched source (**n.a.** for GBY). The site's fire use is generally discussed in an *Homo erectus*-grade context, but the fetched pages do not state a species.

Dikika (2010) remains a claim in the same disputed state.

## Behaviours resting on UNCALIBRATED radiocarbon

**None.** Every earliest-evidence date in this set that could involve radiocarbon either does not use it at all, or uses it in explicitly calibrated form:

- Entries **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10** use no radiocarbon whatsoever — all are dated by ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar, K-Ar, U-series, ESR, TL/OSL/pIR-IR/TT-OSL, magnetostratigraphy, or cosmogenic burial dating. Most are also far beyond the ~50 ka radiocarbon ceiling, so radiocarbon is physically inapplicable.
- Entry **6 (figurative art)**: the headline Sulawesi value uses **laser-ablation U-series**, no radiocarbon ([*Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07541-7)). The European comparator, Chauvet, **is** radiocarbon-based and is **explicitly calibrated** — 259 ¹⁴C determinations calibrated with **IntCal13** in **OxCal 4.2**, reported as ka **cal BP** at 2σ ([Quiles et al. 2016, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)). Anyone building an app should render Chauvet as *37,000–33,500 cal BP*, never as an uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP figure.
- Entry **9 (seafaring)**: Madjedbebe combines 52 single-grain OSL ages (calendar-scale by construction) with 26 AMS ¹⁴C ages that the authors report as in close agreement with the OSL chronology; the headline 65 ± 6 ka is driven by **OSL, not radiocarbon** ([Clarkson et al. 2017 PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf); [2018 reply](http://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson-Roberts-Jacobs-Marwick-et-al-2018-AA-reply.pdf)).

**One caution:** several of these papers report ages as "BP" or "kyr BP" for non-radiocarbon methods — e.g. Twin Rivers U-series at "195,000 ± 19,000 BP" ([Barham 2002](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Barham-2002-CA-pigment-M-Ple-SC-Africa.pdf)) and Qafzeh TL at "~90–100 kyr BP" ([Valladas et al. 1988](https://www.nature.com/articles/331614a0)). These "BP" labels are **not** radiocarbon BP and require no calibration; do not treat them as ¹⁴C ages.

## Source-access notes for whoever extends this

- `sciencedirect.com` abstract pages frequently fail with a robots error. Working substitutes used here: PubMed Central (`pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`), the CENIEH institutional repository (`cir.cenieh.es`), `in-africa.org` PDF mirrors, HAL (`hal.science`), and university worktribe/eprints repositories.
- `nature.com/articles/nature13800` is **not** the Sima de los Huesos paper — it is an unrelated Alzheimer's paper. Do not cite it.
- The 2025 Sulawesi paper (`nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09348-6`) is not directly fetchable; the Griffith University release via Phys.org is the accessible substitute used here.
