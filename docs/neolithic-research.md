# Neolithic Transition — Research Notes for `history-and-prehistory`

**Purpose:** Source material to give children to the currently-empty "Agricultural Revolution" (10,000–4,500 BC) node and the global Mesolithic node, and to extend the "behavioural firsts" layer past African cereals into wheat, rice, maize, potato, pottery, textiles, the wheel, the plough, irrigation, and writing.

**Sourcing rules applied throughout:**
- No numeric date appears without the URL of a page actually fetched/searched in this research pass.
- Wikipedia is never cited as a dating authority. Where a Wikipedia page appears below, it is explicitly labeled **"locator only, not a dating authority"** and a non-Wikipedia source is sought alongside it wherever possible.
- Peer-reviewed papers, excavation/institutional reports, and primary research are preferred over popular summaries.
- Where a figure could not be confirmed against a fetched source, it is marked `n.a.` with a stated reason.
- This file favors fewer, well-sourced entries over broad unsupported coverage.
- **Already in the dataset — deliberately NOT re-researched here:** African cereal domestication (pearl millet, sorghum), cattle herding in Africa, Natufian, Kebaran, PPNA, PPNB, Jericho, Çatalhöyük, Göbekli Tepe, Ubaid, Halaf, Fayum Neolithic, Khartoum Mesolithic, Nabta Playa, Gobero, Chinese Neolithic cultures (Peiligang, Jiahu, Yangshao, Hongshan, Liangzhu), Jōmon phases, LBK, European megalithic, regional Chalcolithic entries.

Each section below is written as a standalone entity description so a later authoring pass can read it mechanically (name → dates → sources → recency flag).

## PART 2 — DOMESTICATION THRESHOLDS (highest priority)

### 2.1 Pottery — Xianrendong Cave, China (predates farming by ~10,000 years)

- **Entity:** Earliest known pottery.
- **Location:** Xianrendong Cave, Jiangxi Province, China.
- **Date:** Radiocarbon-dated to **20,000–19,000 cal BP**, roughly 2,000–3,000 years older than any other known East Asian pottery.
- **Significance:** Predates the emergence of agriculture in the same region by more than ten millennia. Explicitly demonstrates that ceramic technology is NOT causally or chronologically tied to farming or sedentism.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wu et al. 2012, "Early Pottery at 20,000 Years Ago in Xianrendong Cave, China," PubMed/Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22745428/) — peer-reviewed primary source.
  - [Science News summary, quoting archaeologist T. Douglas Price](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/oldest-pottery-comes-chinese-cave): *"Chinese pottery appeared long before animal domestication and has no obvious connection to the origins of agriculture or sedentary living."*
  - [American Ceramic Society summary](https://ceramics.org/ceramic-tech-today/oldest-known-pottery-dates-back-20000-years-and-may-have-changed-the-course-of-human-history/).
- **Note on Yuchanyan:** The task brief also flagged "Yuchanyan" (Hunan) as a candidate early-pottery site. This research pass did not turn up a directly fetched primary source confirming a date for Yuchanyan pottery independent of secondary summaries. **Mark Yuchanyan as `n.a.` pending a dedicated follow-up search** — do not assert a date for it from memory.
- **Flag:** Not a recent shift, but worth flagging as a **frequently-missed threshold** — many general timelines still imply pottery follows farming; the evidence base for pre-agricultural pottery in East Asia is now solid and over a decade old.

### 2.2 Textiles / fiber technology — two distinct thresholds

**(a) Earliest fiber technology (cordage, not woven cloth): Dzudzuana Cave, Georgia**
- **Date:** Wild flax fibers, some twisted/spun and dyed (turquoise, pink, black/gray), from Upper Palaeolithic layers. Oldest unit (D) dated **32,000–26,000 BP**, with further use-layers at 23,000–19,000 BP and 13,000–11,000 BP.
- **Significance:** Predates the Neolithic and agriculture by tens of thousands of years; evidence is for cordage/possible basketry/garments, not necessarily woven cloth.
- **Sources:** [Kvavadze, Bar-Yosef et al. 2009, Science, "30,000-Year-Old Wild Flax Fibers"](https://ghss.ug.edu.ge/storage/themes/June2025/3ev9ncSLyickvEe40dMm.pdf) (hosted PDF of the peer-reviewed paper); stratigraphic detail cross-checked against [ThoughtCo's summary of the site](https://www.thoughtco.com/dzudzuana-cave-early-upper-paleolithic-cave-170735) (used only as a secondary locator, not as the dating authority — the Science paper is the authority).

**(b) Earliest actual woven fabric: Çatalhöyük, Turkey**
- **Date:** ca. **6,700–6,500 cal BC**.
- **Significance:** Earliest site with preserved woven textiles (as opposed to cordage/fiber use).
- **Sources:** [Nature Scientific Reports 2021, "Multidisciplinary investigation reveals the earliest textiles"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-01349-5); [UCL Discovery, "From plants to textiles"](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10168971/1/1-s2.0-S2352409X23001153-main.pdf).

**(c) Earliest woven textile in China: Tianluoshan, Zhejiang**
- **Date:** Reed mat dated **6,775–6,645 cal BP**.
- **Source:** [Nature Scientific Reports 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep18664).

### 2.3 Wheat (einkorn and emmer) — Fertile Crescent

- **Date:** Earliest definite domesticated einkorn AND emmer: Çayönü and Cafer Höyük, ca. **10,600–9,900 cal BP** (Early PPNB).
- **Supporting date:** Tell Aswad emmer, AMS-dated to **~10,500 years ago** (Willcox 2005).
- **Sources:** [Current Anthropology, "Neolithic Foundations for the Evolution of Plant Phenotypic Diversity: A Case Study of Wheat and Barley Domestication" / SW Asian founder crops table](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/658367); [CiteSeerX-hosted PDF with the Tell Aswad AMS date](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?repid=rep1&type=pdf&doi=6f814ada00afb3c42b4499d21f7c98564aa4ddeb).
- **Framing note:** This single-site date describes only the *earliest confirmed archaeobotanical appearance*. The dominant current model (see Part 3) treats wheat domestication as a **protracted, multi-site process lasting on the order of 4,000 years**, not a punctual event — see [Fuller et al. 2012, Journal of Experimental Botany](https://academic.oup.com/jxb/article/63/12/4333/643435) and [ScienceDirect 2020, "Plant domestication in the Neolithic Near East"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379120303747).

### 2.4 Barley — Fertile Crescent, with an unresolved genetics-vs-archaeology gap

- **Date (archaeobotanical):** Nonbrittle (domesticated) barley earliest at Tell Aswad phase II, ca. **10,200–9,550 cal BP**; also Jarmo, Iraq, ~9,450–9,300 cal BP. Consensus figure ~10,500 BP for Fertile Crescent origin.
- **Sources:** [Current Anthropology founder-crops table](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/658367); [PMC, "Importance of Barley Genetics and Domestication"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2759206/).
- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2025):** A new *Nature* paper on wild-barley genetics estimates that the non-brittle (btr1) domestication-associated haplotype may be up to **27,000 years old** — roughly 17,000 years older than the archaeobotanical dates above. This is an open, unresolved discrepancy between genetic dating and archaeological dating, not a settled revision. **Source:** [Nature 2025, "A haplotype-based evolutionary history of barley domestication"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09533-7).
- **Additional 2025 note:** 9,200-year-old barley *foraging* (pre-domestication use) has been found far east of the Fertile Crescent in Central Asia (Toda-1 Cave, Uzbekistan), widening the known geography of early barley exploitation. **Source:** [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12435284/).

### 2.5 Rice — Yangtze Valley, China (protracted domestication, dates pushed earlier in 2024)

- **Sequence:**
  - Shangshan, **~10,000 cal BP**: earliest cultivation/exploitation (charred rice in potsherds; both wild and domesticated-type rachis present).
  - Kuahuqiao, 8,000–7,700 BP.
  - Tianluoshan, 7,000–6,500 BP.
  - Majiabang, 6,300–6,000 BP.
  - Culmination at Liangzhu (already in dataset), 5,300–4,300 BP.
- **Sources:** [Cambridge Antiquity Project Gallery, "The earliest rice domestication in China"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity-project-gallery/article/earliest-rice-domestication-in-china/0F8D71ECFBAE8E8A6201B558500A84AB); [Nature Scientific Reports 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136).
- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2024):** [Science 2024, "Rice's trajectory from wild to domesticated: An archaeobotanical perspective"](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.ade4487) places initial exploitation as early as **~24,000 BP**, pre-domestication cultivation **~13,000 BP**, and domestication proper **~11,000 BP** — meaningfully earlier than the older Shangshan-centered consensus above. Both date sets should be presented, with the 2024 paper flagged as the more recent and more aggressive claim.

### 2.6 Millets — China (phytolith vs. macrobotanical dating dispute)

- **Broomcorn millet:** Cishan site — phytolith evidence as early as **10,300–8,700 cal BP**, but this is contested; some reviews report only macrobotanical (seed) remains from as late as **~5,900 cal BC**.
- **Foxtail millet:** Later than broomcorn — ~8,700 BP at Cishan; ~8,000–7,500 cal BP at Xinglonggou, Inner Mongolia.
- **Sources:** [PNAS 2012, "Early millet use in northern China"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1115430109); [PNAS 2009, "Earliest domestication of common millet (Panicum miliaceum) in East Asia"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.0900158106); [Miller et al. 2016, The Holocene, flagging the phytolith-vs-macrobotanical discrepancy explicitly](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~nmiller0/papers/Miller%20&%20al.%202016%20millet.pdf).
- **Flag:** Methodologically contested — phytolith dates and macrobotanical dates for the same sites disagree by several millennia. Present both, do not silently pick one.

### 2.7 Maize — Balsas River Valley, Mexico

- **Genetic/starch/phytolith evidence of early maize:** Xihuatoxtla shelter, Central Balsas Valley, **~8,700 cal BP**.
- **Earliest directly AMS-dated maize macrofossils (cobs):** Guilá Naquitz Cave, Oaxaca — **6,250–6,230 cal BP** (two AMS dates: 5,420±60 and 5,410±40 ¹⁴C BP).
- **Sources:** [PMC, "Documenting domestication: the intersection of genetics and archaeology" (Guilá Naquitz AMS dates)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC33375/); [PNAS 2009, "Starch grain and phytolith evidence for early ninth millennium B.P. maize from the Central Balsas River Valley"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2664021/); [PNAS, "The Rio Balsas region is the most likely area for the origin of maize"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0900935106).
- **Note:** The starch/phytolith evidence (8,700 cal BP) and the direct AMS cob evidence (6,250–6,230 cal BP) are ~2,400 years apart. Both figures should be presented with their distinct evidentiary basis (indirect biochemical trace vs. direct macrofossil) made explicit — do not collapse them into a single date.

### 2.8 Squash — Guilá Naquitz Cave, Mesoamerica (earliest American domesticate)

- **Date:** AMS dates document cultivation of *Cucurbita pepo* **10,000–8,000 cal BP** at Guilá Naquitz — predating maize and beans in the Americas by more than 4,000 years.
- **Sources:** [Semantic Scholar-hosted PNAS paper, Benz, "Archaeological evidence of teosinte domestication"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Archaeological-evidence-of-teosinte-domestication-Benz/8268f28fcab4bcc6779a38ece5b0f5754e709b8c). A [Wikipedia page on Guilá Naquitz Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guil%C3%A1_Naquitz_Cave) was consulted only as a **locator, not a dating authority**.
- **Related independent squash domestication (Eastern North America, ~5,000 BP):** already covered under Part 1 (Eastern North America) below — not duplicated here.

### 2.9 Potato — Lake Titicaca Basin, Andes

- **Genetic model:** Single-domestication-event model (Spooner et al.), tracing modern potatoes to the *Solanum brevicaule* complex south of Lake Titicaca.
- **Earliest DIRECT archaeological evidence of potato processing:** Jiskairumoko site, Western Titicaca Basin, Peru — microbotanical (starch-grain) remains dated **3,400–2,200 cal BC** (Late Archaic–Early Formative).
- **Sources:** [PNAS 2016 (hosted at PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5137686/); [University of California news release on the same research](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/researchers-trace-roots-potato-farming-andes).
- **Caveat:** Broader popular claims that potato domestication began "8,000–5,000 BCE" are **not directly supported** by a fetched primary source in this research pass — flag any such earlier date as unconfirmed/secondary and prefer the Jiskairumoko direct-evidence date above.

### 2.10 Common bean (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) — two independent domestications

- **Genomic consensus:** Two geographically and genetically isolated wild gene pools (Mesoamerican and Andean) diverged **>100,000 years ago**; from these, **independent domestications began roughly ~8,000 years ago** in each region.
- **Direct archaeobotanical/AMS evidence — Andes:** Starch grains from teeth calculus, Ñanchoc Valley, Peru, **8,600–7,000 cal BP** (Piperno & Dillehay 2008); non-carbonized domesticated seed at Guitarrero Cave, Peru, ¹⁴C age 4,300 BP (≈5,000 cal BP); earlier stratum at Guitarrero Cave dated by ¹⁴C to 7,680±280 to 10,000±300 BP (this early range describes the deposit, not confirmed domesticated bean, per the primary reports below).
- **Direct archaeobotanical/AMS evidence — Mesoamerica:** Non-carbonized domesticated seed/pod, Oaxaca Valley, Mexico, ¹⁴C age 7,600 BP (≈8,300 cal BP), versus only ¹⁴C 2,285 BP (≈2,300 cal BP) for other Mesoamerican pod finds — a large internal spread depending on site.
- **Sources:** [Nature Genetics 2014, "A reference genome for common bean and genome-wide analysis of dual domestications"](https://www.nature.com/articles/ng.3008) (states ~8,000 years ago for both independent events); [Gepts 2014, "Phaseolus" encyclopedia chapter with a full date table by site](https://psfaculty.plantsciences.ucdavis.edu/gepts/Gepts%20Phaseolus%20Encycl%20Global%20Archaeol.%202014.pdf); [Kaplan & Lynch 1999 (PubMed), "Early Cultivated Beans from an Intermontane Peruvian Valley"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17731634/) (Guitarrero Cave stratum II, ¹⁴C 7,680±280–10,000±300 BP).
- **Flag:** There is a genuine tension in the literature between (a) genomic estimates converging on ~8,000 years ago for both centers and (b) archaeobotanical AMS dates that vary enormously by site and by whether charcoal-association dating or direct-seed dating is used (some Mesoamerican direct-seed AMS dates are as late as ~2,300 BP). Present both lines of evidence and do not average them into a single false-precision date.

- Taro exploited from **~10,000 cal BP**; banana cultivated intensively by **6,950–6,440 cal BP**, independent of Southeast Asian influence. Full citations under Part 1 §1.1 below — not duplicated here to avoid redundant sourcing.

### Animals

### 2.12 Dog — Palaeolithic, far predates agriculture (literature moved significantly 2020–2026)

- **Most recent (2026) genomic finding:** Dogs identified at UK and Türkiye sites dated **15,800–14,200 years ago** (Late Upper Palaeolithic) — oldest confirmed genetic evidence for domestic dogs to date.
- **Sources:** [Natural History Museum, London — press release](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world-s-oldest-genetic-evidence-for-domestic-dogs-identified-in-.html); [Nature 2026, "Dogs were widely distributed across western Eurasia during the Palaeolithic"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10170-x).
- **Earlier (2020) genomic model:** Suggested dog/wolf divergence 20,000–40,000 years ago, with five major dog ancestry lineages already diversified by 11,000 years ago. **Source:** [Science 2020, "Origins and genetic legacy of prehistoric dogs"](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aba9572).
- **Contested older claim:** A 33,000-year-old Altai "incipient dog" specimen was proposed as an early/dual origin but remains disputed. **Source:** [Science 2016, dual-origin hypothesis paper](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaf3161).
- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2020–2026):** Exact date and geographic origin remain contested, but a Palaeolithic origin — predating agriculture by well over 10,000 years — is now firmly established and has been pushed progressively earlier by successive ancient-genome studies.

### 2.13 Sheep and goat — Zagros Mountains

- **Date:** Goat domestication/management at Ganj Dareh, Zagros Mountains, Iran, by **~8,200 cal BC**; detectable morphological change in the herd appears roughly 1,000 years later. Broader caprine herding evident across the Near East by **~9,500 BP**.
- **Sources:** [PNAS 2021, "Herded and hunted goat genomes"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2100901118); [Science 2000, "Initial Domestication of Goats in the Zagros Mountains 10,000 Years Ago"](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.287.5461.2254); [Peking University archaeology department PDF summarizing Helmer 1989 and Zeder 1994 on ~9,500 BP herding](https://archaeology.pku.edu.cn/__local/B/59/9B/43D975355D4A3E27D2873D9E196_B4792283_1872B4.pdf).
- **⚑ Recent addition (2026):** Earliest sheep/goat dairy evidence in the Zagros now dated to the 7th millennium BC. **Source:** [Phys.org 2026, reporting on new dairy-residue findings](https://phys.org/news/2026-02-evidence-early-goat-sheep-dairy.html).

### 2.14 Pig — two independent centres (Near East and China), China date revised earlier in 2025

- **Near East:** Domestication in the Tigris Basin/Anatolia region by **~10,500 BP**.
- **China:** Independent domestication by **~8,000–9,000 BP**.
- **Sources:** [PMC 2021, "The beginning of pig domestication in China: clues from historical and archaeological data"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8214443/) — states Near East ~10,500 BP and China ~8,000 BP as two independent origins.
- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2025):** New evidence pushes Lower Yangtze pig management back to **8,000 cal BP** at Jingtoushan/Kuahuqiao. **Source:** [PNAS/PMC 2025, "Earliest evidence for domestic pigs in Neolithic China"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12260554/).
- A [Wikipedia "Pig" page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig) was consulted only as a **locator, not a dating authority**.

### 2.15 Cattle — Near East (taurine) and separately South Asia (zebu)

- **Taurine cattle (*Bos taurus*):** Domesticated from aurochs in the Near East **~10,500 years ago** (some sources say ~10,000); earliest signs at Dja'de, Middle Euphrates (Early PPNB), **10,800–10,300 cal BP**, and Çayönü, **~10,200 cal BP**.
- **Zebu/indicine cattle (*Bos indicus*):** Separate, independent domestication in South Asia, **~8,000–8,500 BP**.
- **Sources:** [Oxford Academic, Molecular Biology and Evolution 2012, "Modern Taurine Cattle Descended from Small Number of Near-Eastern Founders"](https://academic.oup.com/mbe/article/29/9/2101/1077727); [Nature Communications 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3755); [PMC 2023, "Ancient Mongolian aurochs genomes"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10441390/).

### 2.16 Horse — major reversal of the Botai consensus (2021)

- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2021), MAJOR REVISION:** The modern domestic horse lineage (DOM2) does **not** descend from the Botai culture horses of Kazakhstan, as long assumed (earlier evidence dated Botai domestication to ~5,500 years ago / ~3500 BC). Instead, DOM2 emerged in the **Western Eurasian steppes (lower Volga-Don region) ~4,200 years ago (~2200 BCE)**, spreading rapidly after ~2000 BC. Botai-lineage horses are now understood to represent Przewalski's-horse ancestry, not the ancestor of modern domestic breeds.
- **Sources:** [CNRS press release 2021, "The origin of domestic horses finally established"](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/origin-domestic-horses-finally-established); [Nature 2021, primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9.pdf); [Science 2021, "Ancient DNA reveals long-sought homeland of modern horses"](https://www.science.org/content/article/ancient-dna-reveals-long-sought-homeland-modern-horses).
- **Botai evidence still valid as EARLIEST HORSE HUSBANDRY (not ancestry):** [Science 2018, "Ancient genomes revisit the ancestry of domestic and Przewalski's horses"](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao3297) — Botai husbandry evidence (~5,500 years ago) remains the earliest known husbandry behavior even though it is a genetic dead end relative to modern horses.
- **Framing note for the dataset:** Present both figures with roles labeled clearly — "earliest known husbandry" (Botai, ~3500 BC) vs. "ancestral origin of modern domestic horses" (Volga-Don, ~2200 BC) — since they answer different questions and conflating them is the most common error in older secondary sources.

### 2.17 Donkey — East Africa, single domestication event

- **Date:** Genomic study of 238 modern and ancient donkeys places a single domestication event in East Africa (Kenya/Horn of Africa) **~7,000 years ago (~5000 BCE)**.
- **Earliest archaeological (skeletal) confirmation:** Egypt, 4th millennium BC (Ma'adi), **~4,500–4,000 BC** — roughly 500 years later than the genetic estimate.
- **Sources:** [Science 2022, "A genomic history of the origin and global expansion of domestic donkeys"](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo3503); [CNRS press release](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/origins-donkey-domestication); [Science News summary](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/donkey-domestication-dna-east-africa).
- **Flag:** Note explicitly the ~500-year gap between the genetic-estimate date and the confirmed archaeological date — do not present the genetic date as archaeologically confirmed.

### 2.18 Chicken — major reversal: Southeast Asia, not India (2022)

- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2022), MAJOR REVISION:** First unambiguous domestic chicken bones identified at **Ban Non Wat, central Thailand**, dated **1,650–1,250 BCE** — Southeast Asia, overturning the long-held assumption that chickens were first domesticated in India. Domestication process was underway by ~1,500 BC; chickens did not reach central China, South Asia, or Mesopotamia until the late 2nd millennium BCE, nor Ethiopia/Mediterranean Europe until ~800 BCE.
- **Sources:** [PubMed 2022, "The biocultural origins and dispersal of domestic chickens"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35666876/); [ScienceDaily 2022 summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/06/220606181159.htm).

### 2.19 Llama and alpaca — Andean highlands

- **Date:** Domesticated **6,000–7,000 years BP** at sites such as Telarmachay rockshelter, Junín highlands, Peru — alpaca from vicuña, llama from guanaco. Alpaca domestication is generally placed earlier (~6,000–5,500 years ago) than llama (~3,500–4,000 years ago).
- **Sources:** [eLife 2021, "Ancient DNA reveals the lost domestication history of South American camelids"](https://elifesciences.org/articles/63390); [Florida Museum, "Origins of animal domestication in the Andes"](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/envarch/research/peruvian-zooarchaeology/origins-of-animal-domestication-in-the-andes/).

### 2.20 Guinea pig — Andean highlands, possible additional domestication center identified in 2020

- **Date:** Domesticated roughly **6,000–2,000 BC** per zooarchaeological evidence, in the Andean highlands (Peru/Colombia/Ecuador/Bolivia); wild guinea pig remains from Colombia go back to ~9,000 BC.
- **Source:** [Nature Scientific Reports 2020, "Ancient DNA of Guinea Pigs Reveals a New Center of Domestication"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-65784-6).
- **⚑ LITERATURE MOVED (2020):** This paper's title finding is itself the flag — it proposes a **possible new/additional center of domestication** beyond the traditional single-Central-Andes model, meaning the geography of guinea pig domestication is actively being revised.

### 2.21 Water buffalo — two independently domesticated types

- **River buffalo:** Northwest/western India, **~6,300 years BP (~4,300 BCE)**.
- **Swamp buffalo:** Mainland Southeast Asia/China border region — dating far less certain, with published estimates ranging **~3,000–7,000 years ago** and explicitly contested.
- **Sources:** [PMC 2018, "New Insights on Water Buffalo Genomic Diversity and Post-Domestication Migration"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5841121/); [ScienceDirect, "Water Buffalo: A Review"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007193517317827).

### 2.22 Camels — dromedary (Arabia) and Bactrian (Central Asia)

- **Dromedary (one-humped):** Domesticated in the southeast Arabian Peninsula, generally placed **~3,000–4,000 years ago** (late 2nd millennium BCE); earliest secure evidence at Umm an-Nar, Abu Dhabi (early 3rd-millennium-BC context showing a juvenile cull pattern consistent with incipient domestication), confirmed via ancient and modern DNA.
- **Sources:** [PNAS 2016, "Ancient and modern DNA reveal dynamics of domestication and cross-continental dispersal of the dromedary"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1519508113); [Journal of the Agricultural and Life Sciences (jstage), "Domestication of Camel"](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jals/26/4/26_205/_pdf).
- **Bactrian camel:** Domesticated in southwest Central Asia. Popular estimates cluster around ~2,500 BC (Shahr-i Sokhta) or earlier (~5,000–6,000 BC per some sources), but this research pass did not confirm a Bactrian date against a fetched non-Wikipedia peer-reviewed source. **Mark the Bactrian camel date as `n.a.` pending direct confirmation** — the Wikipedia figure should be treated as a locator only, not used as the cited date.

### Technology (beyond pottery/textiles above)

### 2.23 The wheel — genuinely contested between Mesopotamia and Central/Eastern Europe

- **Mesopotamia:** Pictographic depictions of wheeled wagons at Uruk (Eanna district), dated **~3,500–3,350 BCE**.
- **Central/Eastern Europe:** The Bronocice pot (Funnelbeaker culture, Poland), depicting a four-wheeled wagon, dated **3,635–3,370 cal BCE** — essentially contemporaneous with the Uruk evidence.
- **Oldest securely dated PHYSICAL wheel:** The Ljubljana Marshes Wooden Wheel, Slovenia — wheel dated **3,340–3,030 cal BC**, associated axle dated **3,360–3,045 cal BC**.
- **Sources:** [Cambridge, Antiquity, "Earliest evidence of wheeled vehicles in Europe and the Near East"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/earliest-evidence-of-wheeled-vehicles-in-europe-and-the-near-east/4835B594180234DC116F6F0105771573); [LiveScience 2024, on a Carpathian-copper-miner modeling study suggesting an even earlier ~3,900 BC invention](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/1st-wheel-was-invented-6-000-years-ago-in-the-carpathian-mountains-modeling-study-suggests) — explicitly flagged here as a **modeling/inference claim, not direct physical evidence**, and should not be presented with the same confidence as the dated finds above.
- **Framing note:** Present as genuinely unresolved priority between Mesopotamia and Europe, with near-simultaneous appearance (~3,500–3,350 BCE) as the safest overall claim.

### 2.24 The plough (ard) — Near East and Mesopotamia; a sourcing gap remains

- **Mesopotamia:** Ards first depicted on Uruk clay-tablet pictographs, end of the 4th millennium BC (**~3,500–3,300 BCE**).
- **Near East (scratch-mark evidence, more general):** Evidence of ard use from the 6th millennium BC.
- **Oldest preserved wooden ard:** Lavagnone, Italy, dated **~2,300–2,000 BC** — much later than the scratch-mark evidence in Europe (3,500–3,000 BC), since it is a rare case of organic preservation rather than a marker of first use.
- **Sourcing gap:** This research pass relied on a [Wikipedia "Ard (plough)" page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ard_(plough)) as an initial locator only — **it is explicitly not used here as a dating authority**. A non-Wikipedia institutional or peer-reviewed source for the Uruk/Mesopotamia and Lavagnone dates specifically should be sought in a follow-up pass. **Treat the plough dates above as provisional (`n.a.` for citation purposes) until a primary source is fetched and confirms them.**

### 2.25 Irrigation — Choga Mami, Mesopotamia

- **Date:** First archaeological signs of canal/ditch irrigation in Mesopotamia at Choga Mami, associated with the Samarra culture, **~6,200–5,700 BC**, at a site occupied **~5,000 BC**.
- **Source:** [University of Chicago Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures, excavation report, "Excavations at Choga Mami, Iraq"](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/61-70/67-68a/67-68a_Choga_Mami.pdf) — a genuine excavation report, used as the primary dating authority here.
- A [Wikipedia "Choga Mami" page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choga_Mami) was consulted only as a **locator, not a dating authority**.

### 2.26 Writing — genuinely contested, independent development in Mesopotamia and Egypt

- **Mesopotamia:** Proto-cuneiform at Uruk, **~3,400–3,200 BCE** (some tablets ~3,200 BC; early true cuneiform ~3,100 BC).
- **Egypt:** Tomb U-j at Abydos (Umm el-Qaab), bone/ivory tags bearing early hieroglyphic signs, dated **~3,320 BCE** (Naqada III / "Dynasty 0"); the fuller hieroglyphic script is dated to origins around **~3,250 BCE**.
- **Source:** [Oxford Academic, "The Origins of Writing in Northeastern Africa"](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61663/chapter/553461749?searchresult=1) — peer-reviewed chapter stating these date ranges **overlap** and that the two systems developed **independently**.
- **Framing note:** Priority between Mesopotamia and Egypt is explicitly and currently contested in the peer-reviewed literature. Present both systems as independent inventions rather than assigning a "first."

### 2.27 Beer / fermentation — two distinct thresholds, one pre-agricultural

- **Earliest known fermented beverage (pre-agricultural):** Raqefet Cave, Mount Carmel, Israel — a Natufian ritual/burial site with stone mortars bearing residues of germinated/fermented wheat and barley starch, dated **13,700–11,700 cal BP (~13,000 years ago)**. This predates domesticated cereals and farming by several millennia (the Natufian is pre-agricultural).
- **Source:** [ScienceDirect 2018, Li Liu et al., "Fermented beverage and food storage in 13,000 y-old stone mortars at Raqefet Cave"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X18303468) — peer-reviewed.
- **Earliest chemically confirmed barley beer in pottery (post-farming):** Godin Tepe, Zagros Mountains, Iran — jar fragment dated **5,400–5,000 years ago (~3,500–3,100 BC)**, bearing calcium oxalate ("beerstone") residue.
- **Source:** [PMC 2021, discussing both Raqefet Cave and Godin Tepe together](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8360526/), which also notes early evidence of a mold/qu-type fermentation starter in Chinese beer-making — flagged here as an item needing its own precise date/site confirmation before being added to the dataset; not asserted as a dated fact in this file.

## PART 1 — INDEPENDENT CENTRES OF AGRICULTURAL ORIGIN

### 1.0 How many centres? — scholarly disagreement on the count itself

- Estimates of the number of independent domestication centres vary sharply by methodology and era of scholarship: Harlan (1971) counted **6**; Vavilov/Hawkes/Zeven-Zhukovsky counted **12**; Purugganan & Fuller (2009) count **24** separate independent domestication regions.
- **Sources:** [Springer, Genetic Resources and Crop Evolution review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10722-021-01114-7); [Meyer, DuVal & Jenson, Tansley review, New Phytologist](https://faculty.washington.edu/stevehar/New%20Phytologist%20Tansley%20review%20Meyer%20et%20al.pdf).
- **Framing note:** This disagreement is itself worth encoding in the dataset — "how many independent centres" is a live methodological question, not a settled count.

### 1.1 New Guinea — Kuk Swamp

- **Sequence:**
  - Phase 1 (plant exploitation/early cultivation): **10,220–9,910 cal BP**.
  - Phase 2 (mounded cultivation; taro and banana intensively cultivated): **6,950–6,440 cal BP**.
  - Phase 3 (ditched cultivation): **4,350–3,980 cal BP**.
- **Significance:** Independent of Southeast Asian influence — a genuinely separate centre of plant domestication (taro, banana).
- **Sources:** [Denham et al. 2003, Science, hosted PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Denham%20et%20al%202003.pdf); [UNESCO World Heritage nomination document for Kuk Swamp](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/887.pdf); [Cambridge, Antiquity 2004, "New evidence and revised interpretations of early agriculture in highland New Guinea"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/new-evidence-and-revised-interpretations-of-early-agriculture-in-highland-new-guinea/ED9F4A5DE31A96D8273A6B41E96F1BC3).

### 1.2 Eastern North America — independent domestication ~5,000 BP

- **Squash** (*Cucurbita pepo* ssp. *ovifera*): Phillips Spring, Missouri, dated **5,025 cal BP** (1σ range 5,290–4,870 cal BP) — earliest Eastern North American domesticate.
- **Sunflower** (*Helianthus annuus* var. *macrocarpus*): Hayes site, Tennessee, **4,840 cal BP** (1σ: 4,860–4,830).
- **Marsh elder:** Napoleon Hollow, Illinois, **~4,400 cal BP**.
- **Chenopod:** Riverton, Illinois, **~3,800 BP**.
- **Sources:** [PMC, "Ancient farming in eastern North America"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2672489/); [University of Chicago Press journal, "The Cultural Context of Plant Domestication in Eastern North America," with full data table](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdfplus/10.1086/659645); [Smith 2006, PubMed, "Eastern North America as an independent center of plant domestication"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16894156/).

### 1.3 Southwest Amazonia — manioc, squash, and maize much earlier than previously thought

- **Date:** Phytolith evidence shows manioc cultivated by **~10,350 years ago** and squash by **~10,250 years ago**; maize present by **~6,850 years ago**. These dates are substantially earlier than previous models of Amazonian agriculture.
- **Site:** Teotônio site, Madeira River.
- **Sources:** [PLOS ONE, primary paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0199868); [University of Bern press release](https://mediarelations.unibe.ch/media_releases/2020/media_releases_2020/agriculture_began_in_the_amazon_10_000_years_ago/index_eng.html); [Penn State News summary](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/amazonian-crops-domesticated-10000-years-ago).

## PART 3 — FRAMING QUESTIONS

### 3.1 "Agricultural Revolution" vs. the gradualist / protracted-autonomous model

- **Current consensus position:** The dominant model for Near Eastern plant domestication, developed principally by Fuller, Allaby, Purugganan, Willcox and colleagues, is the **"protracted-autonomous model"**: domestication was (a) a millennia-long process (~4,000 years), (b) geographically autonomous — occurring independently at several separate foci within the Near East rather than one "core area," and (c) largely unconscious/unintentional rather than a deliberate single invention.
- **Sources:** [ScienceDirect 2020, "Plant domestication in the Neolithic Near East"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379120303747); [hosted PDF of the same/related paper, Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://plantscience.agri.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/plantsciences/files/nct_2020.pdf); [Purugganan & Fuller 2009, Nature, "The nature of selection during plant domestication"](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/wpaper/hal-02145476.html) (cited via this working-paper summary of the Nature article); [Purugganan & Fuller 2011, Evolution, "Archaeological data reveal slow rates of evolution during plant domestication"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2010.01093.x).
- **Active counter-argument (genuinely contested, not settled):** Abbo, Gopher and colleagues have argued for years, in a sustained back-and-forth, that domestication was instead a **rapid, conscious, geographically-restricted "core area–one event"** process. The Fuller group has explicitly rebutted this most recently in 2022.
- **Sources:** [Academic OUP, Journal of Experimental Botany 2012, critical review of the protracted domestication model](https://academic.oup.com/jxb/article/63/12/4333/643435); [UCL Discovery, Fuller et al., "Progress in domestication research: a reply to Abbo and Gopher" (2022/2023)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10158703/1/Fuller_Reply%20to%20Abbo_v3.3.pdf) — explicitly frames this as an active, unresolved scholarly dispute, with the Fuller group asserting the weight of 20 years of accumulating data favors the protracted model, while Abbo and Gopher continue to dispute this characterization.
- **Terminology shift:** Multiple recent reviews explicitly state that the old "Neolithic Revolution" framing (V. Gordon Childe's original term) has been "replaced by a long and gradual process called the 'Neolithic transition'" as evidence has accumulated of mixed foraging-farming economies persisting for millennia, and even temporary reversion to hunting-and-gathering lifestyles in some regions.
- **Source:** [Journal of Historical Archaeology & Anthropological Sciences, "Persistent Controversies about the Neolithic"](http://www.bicga.org.uk/docs/Persistent_Controversies_about_the_Neolithic.pdf) — explicitly discusses the Childe-to-"transition" terminology shift and cites temporary reversion to foraging as part of the record.
- **Cross-regional reinforcement:** Rice domestication (§2.5 above) is likewise explicitly described in the literature as "a protracted process" spanning millennia (Shangshan to Liangzhu, ~10,000 to ~4,300 BP), and millet/pig/dog "productivity transitions" in Neolithic northern China are also framed as protracted-over-millennia rather than punctual events.
- **Source:** [Cambridge, Evolutionary Human Sciences 2024, "Millets, dogs, pigs and permanent settlement: productivity transitions in Neolithic northern China"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/evolutionary-human-sciences/article/millets-dogs-pigs-and-permanent-settlement-productivity-transitions-in-neolithic-northern-china/A294FE008F9499F006EF9AB8D79EA667).
- **Recommendation for the dataset:** Rename or re-frame the "Agricultural Revolution" node's label/description to reflect the gradualist/protracted consensus, while explicitly noting the active Abbo–Gopher minority position as a genuine, ongoing scholarly disagreement rather than a settled matter.

### 3.2 Sedentism before farming — order of thresholds in the Fertile Crescent

- **Consensus finding:** "It is now clear that sedentism preceded the domestication of plants and then of animals" in the Middle East. The first indications of sedentism are recognized in the Natufian culture of the Levant, dated by this source to **12,500–10,000 BC**; intentional cultivation appears later, "late in the cold Younger Dryas period, around 9,700 BC"; a full agricultural economy with settled farming based on cereals and pulses is established by **~9,500 BC** in the Levantine Corridor; pottery itself does not appear in this specific Levantine sequence until **~7,000 BC** (the end of PPNB), i.e., well after both sedentism and farming — illustrating that the pottery-before-farming pattern seen in East Asia (§2.1) is NOT universal and is region-specific.
- **Source:** [Comptes Rendus Palevol (Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle), "Inception of agriculture and rearing in the Middle East"](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2006v5f1-2a47.pdf) — peer-reviewed institutional source, used as the dating authority for this section.
- **Caveat / regional variability:** A 2023 paper notes that neolithization (transition to agriculture with a broadly concordant move to sedentism) occurred **asynchronously across different communities and regions of the Near East** — for example, southern Levant populations appear to have become sedentary earlier than communities in southeastern Anatolia (e.g., Nevali Çori, which shows sedentism only from the Middle PPNB per strontium isotope evidence).
- **Source:** [Nature Scientific Reports 2023, "Isotopic and proteomic evidence for communal stability at Pre-Pottery Neolithic Jericho in the Southern Levant"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-43549-1).
- **A German dissertation review explicitly cautions against a single unified model:** "there is no unified model to determine whether sedentism preceded agriculture or vice versa, necessitating further examination of archaeological contexts on-site."
- **Source:** [LMU Munich dissertation repository, "Mobility and Sedentism across the Pre-Pottery Neolithic Fertile Crescent"](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/32494/1/Wang_Xiaoran.pdf).
- **Recommendation for the dataset:** Present "sedentism generally precedes farming, farming generally precedes pottery" as the Fertile-Crescent-specific pattern, explicitly caveated as asynchronous by sub-region and NOT a universal global sequence — East Asia (§2.1 pottery at 20,000 BP, long before farming) is the clearest counter-example already in hand.

### 3.3 Domestication abandonment / reversal — not separately confirmed in this pass

- The [bicga.org.uk paper cited in §3.1](http://www.bicga.org.uk/docs/Persistent_Controversies_about_the_Neolithic.pdf) references "temporary reversion to hunting and gathering lifestyle" as part of the broader protracted-transition picture, but this research pass did not fetch a dedicated primary case-study source (e.g., a specific site or region documented as reverting from farming back to foraging).
- **Status: `n.a.`** — mark this as a gap requiring a dedicated follow-up search on specific abandonment/reversal case studies (e.g., proposed cases in the US Southwest, or Northern Europe) before adding any specific site or date to the dataset.
