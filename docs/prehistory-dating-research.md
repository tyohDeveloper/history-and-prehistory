# Contested Dating of Eight Prehistoric / Archaeological Subjects

Research report for a chronology dataset. Every date below is linked to the exact page the value was read from in this session. Where sources genuinely disagree, both figures are given with both citations. Values that could not be confirmed from a fetched page are marked `n.a.`

Field key: **A** consensus date · **B** earliest/latest plausible bounds · **C** dating methods · **D** superseded dates · **E** nature of dispute · **F** BP vs BCE convention.

## 1. Oldowan stone tool industry — earliest appearance

### A. Consensus date
**c. 2.6 Ma** (millions of years ago), based on Gona (Gonа/Gona River), Afar, Ethiopia. Semaw et al. 1997 (*Nature* 385:333–336) dated the East Gona 10 and 12 assemblages to **2.6–2.5 Myr**, describing them as "the oldest known artefacts from anywhere in the world" ([Semaw et al. 1997, PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9002516/)). Semaw 2000 restates the range as **2.6–2.5 Ma** and gives the underlying constraint: the AST-2.75 tuff, ~5 m above EG10/EG12, dated by single-crystal laser-fusion ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar to **2.517 + 0.075 Ma**, giving a minimum age of 2.52 Ma, with a maximum of ~2.6 Ma from the Gauss–Matuyama magnetic reversal below the artefacts ([Semaw 2000, J. Archaeol. Sci. PDF](http://www.ethiopianreview.com/pdf/001/Semaw2000.pdf)).

The most precisely dated single early Oldowan occurrence is Bokol Dora 1 (BD 1), Ledi-Geraru, Ethiopia, bracketed to **2.61–2.58 Ma** and conservatively assigned **~2.58 Ma**; the overlying Ali Toyta Tuff gives a K-feldspar ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar age of **2.584 ± 0.034 Ma (95% confidence)**, and the assemblage must predate the Gauss–Matuyama reversal at **2.581 Ma** ([Braun et al. 2019, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820177116)). That same paper states the previously earliest known Oldowan was **2.58–2.55 Ma at Gona, Ethiopia**.

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Oldest end (as Oldowan):** the Nyayanga site, Homa Peninsula, Kenya, is placed at **3.032–2.581 Ma** — i.e. potentially as old as ~3.0 Ma — and is described as extending the known Oldowan range by more than 400,000 years ([Plummer et al. 2023, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo7452); commentary: ["An older Oldowan", *Nature Ecology & Evolution*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-023-02285-5)).
- **Oldest end (stone tools generally, but *not* Oldowan):** Lomekwi 3, West Turkana, Kenya, at **3.3 Ma**, which the authors state predates the Oldowan by 700,000 years and assign to a proposed new industry, the **"Lomekwian"** ([Harmand et al. 2015, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464)).
- **Youngest / most conservative end:** ~2.52 Ma, the ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar minimum age from the AST-2.75 tuff above the Gona artefacts ([Semaw 2000](http://www.ethiopianreview.com/pdf/001/Semaw2000.pdf)).
- Working credible range for "earliest Oldowan": **~3.0 Ma (Nyayanga, wide bracket) to ~2.52 Ma (hard Gona minimum)**, with the well-supported figure at 2.6–2.58 Ma.

### C. Dating methods (and where they disagree)
- **⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar (single-crystal laser fusion) on tuffs** — Gona: AST-2.75 = 2.517 + 0.075 Ma ([Semaw 2000](http://www.ethiopianreview.com/pdf/001/Semaw2000.pdf)). Ledi-Geraru: Ali Toyta Tuff K-feldspar = 2.584 ± 0.034 Ma at 95% ([Braun et al. 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820177116)).
- **Magnetostratigraphy** — Gauss–Matuyama reversal at 2.581 Ma used as the upper bracket at Ledi-Geraru ([Braun et al. 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820177116)); the same reversal, identified stratigraphically below the Gona sites, supplies the ~2.6 Ma maximum there ([Semaw 2000](http://www.ethiopianreview.com/pdf/001/Semaw2000.pdf)). At Nyayanga, the C2An.1n subchron gives **3.032–2.595 Ma** ([Plummer et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo7452)).
- **(U-Th)/He on apatite** — Nyayanga: **2.87 ± 0.79 Ma** and **2.98 ± 0.50 Ma** (1σ; note the very wide uncertainties) ([Plummer et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo7452)).
- **Typological / technological attribution** — this is where the real conflict sits. Lomekwi 3 is radiometrically 3.3 Ma but is *typologically excluded* from the Oldowan by its excavators ([Harmand et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464)), whereas Nyayanga's tools *are* typologically assigned to the Oldowan, which is why the "earliest Oldowan" figure moves from ~2.6 Ma to potentially ~3.0 Ma ([Plummer et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo7452)).
- **Method conflict at Nyayanga specifically:** the (U-Th)/He ages centre on ~2.9–3.0 Ma while the magnetostratigraphic bracket permits anywhere in 3.032–2.595 Ma. The paper reports both rather than resolving to a point age.

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oldowan begins ~1.8 Ma (Olduvai Gorge type assemblages, Mary Leakey) | 2.6–2.5 Ma at Gona | 1997 | Discovery + ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar and paleomagnetic dating of the Gona assemblages; pushed tool manufacture back by at least 250,000 years and, per contemporary reporting, ~150,000+ years beyond the previous earliest date ([*Science* news 1997](https://www.science.org/content/article/first-tool-kit); [Archaeology Magazine 1997](https://archive.archaeology.org/9703/newsbriefs/tools.html)) |
| Earliest Oldowan = 2.58–2.55 Ma (Gona) | ~2.58 Ma with tighter brackets (Bokol Dora 1, Ledi-Geraru) | 2019 | New site with ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar + magnetostratigraphic bracketing ([Braun et al. 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820177116)) |
| Oldest stone tools = ~2.6 Ma | 3.3 Ma (Lomekwi 3), but classified as pre-Oldowan | 2015 | New excavation; authors propose the separate "Lomekwian" industry ([Harmand et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14464)) |
| Oldowan geographic/temporal range ends at ~2.6 Ma in Ethiopia | extended by >400,000 years and 1,300 km | 2023 | Nyayanga, Kenya ([*Nature Ecology & Evolution* commentary](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-023-02285-5)) |

Note also the hominin-attribution shift: the Nyayanga Oldowan tools are associated with ***Paranthropus***, not *Homo* ([Plummer et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abo7452)) — undercutting the older assumption that the Oldowan indexes the origin of *Homo* (the 1997 *Science* report framed Gona as dating to "a time when the first members of our genus, *Homo*, were thought to have been born in Africa", [*Science* 1997](https://www.science.org/content/article/first-tool-kit)).

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Definitional**, with a secondary method conflict. The radiometric ages of the candidate sites are not seriously contested; what is contested is *which assemblages count as Oldowan*. Lomekwi 3 at 3.3 Ma is excluded by its own excavators as "Lomekwian", while Nyayanga's flakes are admitted to the Oldowan — so the "earliest Oldowan" date is a function of typological definition rather than of geochronology. The subsidiary method conflict is at Nyayanga, where wide-uncertainty (U-Th)/He ages (~2.9–3.0 Ma, 1σ ±0.5–0.8 Myr) sit alongside a magnetostratigraphic bracket spanning 3.03–2.60 Ma.

### F. Convention
Quoted in **Ma / ka before present** (millions or thousands of years ago). BCE is never used at this timescale. Usage is not mixed. Note that both "Ma" and "Myr"/"million years" appear in the literature interchangeably ([Semaw et al. 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9002516/) uses "Myr"; [Braun et al. 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820177116) uses "Ma").

## 2. *Homo floresiensis* — youngest / most recent remains (Liang Bua, Flores, Indonesia)

### A. Consensus date
**~60,000 years ago for the youngest skeletal remains**, with associated stone artefacts continuing to ~50,000 years ago. Sutikna et al. 2016 (*Nature* 532:366–369) revised the chronology so that "the skeletal remains of *H. floresiensis* … are older than previously thought, at **~100–60 kyr ago**", while "stone artefacts attributable to this species … range from **~190–50 kyr** ago" ([Sutikna et al. 2016, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)). The Smithsonian summarises the resulting extinction estimate as **~50,000 years ago** ([Smithsonian Human Origins](https://humanorigins.si.edu/research/asian-research-projects/hobbits-flores-indonesia); [Smithsonian newsdesk release](https://www.si.edu/newsdesk/releases/smithsonian-and-international-team-scientists-reveal-new-evidence-pushing-back-time-disappe)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds for the *youngest* remains
- **Oldest end:** direct uranium-series ages on the LB1 ulna give modelled minimum ages of **86.9 ± 7.9 ka to 71.5 ± 4.3 ka (±2σ)** ([Oxford Handbook chapter, U-series table](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/42054/chapter/355842802)); *Science*'s report of the same work gives the LB skeletal U-Th range as **66,000–87,000 years** ([*Science* news, 2016](https://www.science.org/content/article/hobbit-was-separate-species-human-new-dating-reveals)).
- **Youngest end (skeletal):** LB6 direct U-series modelled minimum ages of **66.0 ± 4.3 ka to 54.6 ± 2.1 ka** ([Oxford Handbook](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/42054/chapter/355842802)) — i.e. the youngest credible bone age reaches ~55 ka.
- **Youngest end (species presence, artefact-based):** **~50 kyr** ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)).
- **Abandoned younger end:** ~13–11 kyr cal BP / ~12 kyr (see D).
- So: credible range for the last *H. floresiensis* = **~87 ka to ~50 ka**, with the working figure ~60–50 ka.

### C. Dating methods
- **Calibrated radiocarbon (AMS)** — the original, now-rejected ~18 kyr cal BP age for LB1, and the constraint that the pedestal deposits containing the remains are older than **~46 kyr cal BP** while the overlying sediments are younger than **~20 kyr cal BP** ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)).
- **Uranium-series (²³⁴U/²³⁰Th) directly on hominin bone** — LB1 **86.9 ± 7.9 → 71.5 ± 4.3 ka**; LB2 **71.4 ± 1.1 → 66.7 ± 0.8 ka**; LB6 **66.0 ± 4.3 → 54.6 ± 2.1 ka** (modelled minimum ages, ±2σ) ([Oxford Handbook](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/42054/chapter/355842802)). These are *minimum* ages because U uptake in bone is open-system.
- **Infrared-stimulated luminescence (IRSL), thermoluminescence (TL), ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar, and ²³⁴U/²³⁰Th on tephras** — tephras T1 and T3 bracketing the remains date to **~60 and ~50 ka**; one ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar determination is **79 ± 12 kyr (1σ)** ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)).
- **Method disagreement:** the radiocarbon-based chronology said ~18–12 kyr; the U-series/luminescence/Ar-Ar chronology says 100–60 kyr. The conflict was not a calibration problem — it was resolved stratigraphically (see D), and the radiocarbon dates themselves were not wrong, only mis-assigned.

### D. Superseded dates — **major revision**
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| LB1 skeleton **~18 kyr cal BP** (radiocarbon) | LB1 and other skeletal remains **~100–60 kyr** | 2016 (Sutikna et al., *Nature* 532:366–369) | Discovery of a previously undetected **erosional unconformity / pedestal** in the cave stratigraphy: the original dating samples came from a younger overlying layer that had been deposited against, not above, the fossil-bearing sediments ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)) |
| Last appearance of *H. floresiensis* **~17 kyr**, or **13–11 kyr cal BP** | **~50 kyr** | 2016 | Same restratification; the artefact sequence attributable to the species stops at ~50 kyr ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179); [Smithsonian](https://www.si.edu/newsdesk/releases/smithsonian-and-international-team-scientists-reveal-new-evidence-pushing-back-time-disappe)) |
| Liang Bua deposits spanning **95–12 kyr** | revised sequence with the hominin-bearing pedestal >~46 kyr cal BP and younger sediments <~20 kyr cal BP | 2016 | Same ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)) |

The revision also removed the widely repeated inference that *H. floresiensis* survived into the Holocene and possibly overlapped with modern humans for tens of millennia. Evidence of modern-human fire use at Liang Bua is dated **41,000–24,000 years ago** ([Morley et al. 2017, reported via Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2016-06-discovery-hobbit-demise.html)), i.e. after the revised *floresiensis* disappearance rather than alongside a Holocene survival.

For deep context, older hominin remains at Mata Menge on Flores are dated to **~700,000 years BP** ([Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-floresiensis/)).

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Largely settled following a stratigraphic correction** — this is the textbook case of a superseded date rather than an ongoing chronological argument. The 2016 revision was accepted by the original excavation team itself (Sutikna is the lead author of both the original and the revision), which is why it propagated quickly to institutional sources like the Smithsonian. Residual uncertainty is about *how much* younger than 60 ka the species persisted (the ~50 ka artefact figure rests on artefact attribution rather than on dated bone), and the U-series bone ages are minimum ages, so the true ages could be older. Separately, the *taxonomic* dispute over whether *H. floresiensis* is a distinct species is live but is not a dating dispute.

### F. Convention
Quoted in **ka / kyr BP** (thousands of years ago), and in **cal BP** where radiocarbon is involved. Never BCE. Usage is effectively uniform, but note the mixed unit types inside a single paper: [Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179) uses "kyr ago" for the luminescence/U-series results and "kyr cal BP" for the radiocarbon constraints — a distinction worth preserving in a dataset.

## 3. Chauvet Cave (Ardèche, France) — age of the paintings

### A. Consensus date
**Two human occupation/art phases: 37,000–33,500 cal BP and 31,000–28,000 cal BP.** Quiles et al. 2016 (*PNAS*) state the two phases of human activity as **37–33.5 ka cal BP** and **31–28 ka cal BP** ([Quiles et al. 2016, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

Modelled boundaries (95% probability), from the same paper:
- *Cave Floor Charcoal model:* Start Phase I **37.0–36.2 ka cal BP**; End Phase I **34.4–33.5**; Start Phase II **31.4–30.7**; End Phase II **29.7–27.9**.
- *Parietal (art) model:* Start Phase I **36.6–35.2**; End Phase I **35.8–34.0**; Start Phase II **31.9–30.5**; End Phase II **30.6–28.9 ka cal BP**.
- Interval between phases: **2.3–3.5 ka** (floor model) or **2.7–4.6 ka** (parietal model) ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Oldest end:** ~37,000 cal BP (start of Phase I, Aurignacian) ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).
- **Youngest end:** the sceptical position places the first drawings in the **Gravettian, ~26,000 BP**, with the latest in the early Magdalenian (Combier & Jouve 2012, *Quartär* 59:131–52, and 2014, *L'Anthropologie*; summarised in the [Pettitt & Bahn 2015 *Antiquity* supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0003598X15000216/resource/name/S0003598X15000216sup001.pdf)). Pettitt & Bahn argue the art is "no earlier than Gravettian" and extends to the Early Magdalenian ([Pettitt & Bahn 2015, *Antiquity* 89:542–53](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/an-alternative-chronology-for-the-art-of-chauvet-cave/723AB31F4E88629A2B7323BDD4845450)).
- **Hard youngest bound from an independent method:** the cave entrance was sealed by rockfall and is inaccessible after **~21 ka**, so the art is "most likely older than 21 ka" ([Sadier et al. 2012, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1118593109)).
- Credible full range across the literature: **~37,000 cal BP (Aurignacian, Quiles et al.) to ~21,000 BP (absolute closure limit)**, with the sceptics' preferred window ~26,000–21,000 BP.

### C. Dating methods
- **Calibrated AMS radiocarbon (IntCal13, OxCal 4.2 Bayesian modelling)** — the core of the accepted chronology: **259 ¹⁴C dates** out of **>350 total determinations**, of which **220 were modelled** ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)). An inter-laboratory comparison across **47 laboratories** produced **75 determinations** with a mean of **31,765 ± 136 BP**, corresponding to a calibrated density of ~35.9 ka cal BP ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).
- **Cosmogenic ³⁶Cl (rockfall / cave-sealing chronology)** — rockfall events dated to PAbr **34.5 ± 2.0 ka**, E1 **29.4 ± 1.8 ka**, E2 **23.5 ± 1.2 ka**, E3 **21.5 ± 1.0 ka** ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113); original: [Sadier et al. 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1118593109)).
- **Uranium-thorium and thermoluminescence** — included in the >350 determinations ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)); no separate figures were extracted.
- **Typological / stylistic attribution** — the basis of both the original (Solutrean) assignment and of the modern sceptical case. This is the method that disagrees most sharply: stylistic analysis places the art in the Gravettian–Early Magdalenian (~26,000 BP onward), while radiocarbon places its first phase at ~37,000–33,500 cal BP.
- Cave bear remains in the cave date **42–33 ka cal BP** with a modelled start of **48.3–41.5 ka cal BP** ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)), constraining the site's non-human occupation.

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solutrean, ~22,000–18,000 BP** (original stylistic attribution on discovery) | ~32,000–30,000 BP on the black drawings, later 37,000–33,500 cal BP | first ¹⁴C results in the late 1990s; consolidated 2016 | Direct AMS radiocarbon dating of charcoal pigment in the black drawings ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)) |
| First ¹⁴C determinations on black drawings **~32,000–30,000 BP** (uncalibrated) | **37,000–33,500 cal BP** for Phase I | 2016 | Greatly expanded dataset (259 ¹⁴C dates), IntCal13 calibration, and OxCal Bayesian phase modelling ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)) |
| A claimed humic/humin fraction discrepancy used to argue the dates were unreliable | withdrawn | 2005 | Retracted by Valladas et al. 2005:111, as noted by the sceptics themselves ([Pettitt & Bahn 2015 supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0003598X15000216/resource/name/S0003598X15000216sup001.pdf)) |

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Rival chronologies plus method conflict — genuinely unresolved.** Two full competing schemes exist for the same cave. Quiles et al. 2016 present a Bayesian radiocarbon chronology with two Aurignacian/Gravettian phases beginning ~37 ka cal BP ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)). Pettitt and Bahn argue that the entire early case rests on **six measurements from three images in the Hillaire Chamber**, that the art is stylistically Gravettian to Early Magdalenian, and that "the art of the Grotte Chauvet is not dated, and very possibly much younger than claimed" ([Pettitt & Bahn 2015, *Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/an-alternative-chronology-for-the-art-of-chauvet-cave/723AB31F4E88629A2B7323BDD4845450); [Pettitt 2008, *J. Hum. Evol.* 55:908–917](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18678392/)). The sceptical position was restated after the 2016 PNAS paper: Jouve, Pettitt & Bahn 2020, "Chauvet Cave's art remains undated" ([*L'Anthropologie*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003552120300418)). The disagreement is partly about method authority (does a ¹⁴C date on pigment charcoal date the *drawing*, or only the charcoal?) and partly about sampling adequacy.

### F. Convention
**Mixed.** The chronometric literature uses **ka cal BP** ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)), while the archaeological/stylistic literature frequently uses uncalibrated **BP** together with culture-period names (Aurignacian, Gravettian, Solutrean, Magdalenian) — e.g. the sceptics' "~26,000 BP" ([Pettitt & Bahn 2015 supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0003598X15000216/resource/name/S0003598X15000216sup001.pdf)). BCE is essentially never used. For a dataset, the cal BP vs uncal BP distinction here is a live source of ~3–5 kyr of apparent disagreement that is purely notational.

## 4. Madjedbebe (formerly Malakunanja II), Northern Territory, Australia — earliest occupation

### A. Consensus date
**~65,000 years ago.** Clarkson et al. 2017 (*Nature* 547:306–310) conclude that occupation began **~65,000 years ago** ([Clarkson et al. 2017, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968); [full PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). The uncertainty is commonly quoted as **65,000 ± 6,000 years**, and in press coverage as "65,000, give or take about 5,000 years" ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2017-07-20/aboriginal-shelter-pushes-human-history-back-to-65,000-years/8719314)). Britannica gives the site's OSL-based age and notes the associated scepticism ([Britannica: Madjedbebe](https://www.britannica.com/place/Madjedbebe)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Oldest end:** the deepest artefacts have been reported at up to **~80,000 years**, with the excavators stating the lowest artefacts are ≥95% likely to be older than 70,000 years ([*The Guardian*](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2017/jul/19/dig-finds-evidence-of-aboriginal-habitation-up-to-80000-years-ago)).
- **Phase-level figures:** Phase 2 dated **68.7–50.4 ka**; Phase 1 **>65.4 ka** ([Hayes et al. 2022, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15174-x)).
- **Youngest end:** the rival short chronology puts the first human arrival in Sahul at **~47,000 years ago**, treating 47 ka as an "outer bound for continental colonization" and explicitly discounting Madjedbebe ([O'Connell & Allen 2015, *J. Archaeol. Sci.*](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)).
- Credible full range: **~80,000 to ~47,000 years ago**, split between two incompatible camps rather than continuously distributed.

### C. Dating methods (and where they disagree)
- **Single-grain optically stimulated luminescence (OSL)** — 52 single-grain OSL ages underpin the 65 ka result ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968)). The OSL results were independently replicated blind by Lee Arnold at the University of Adelaide ([University of Wollongong release](https://www.uow.edu.au/media/2017/kakadu-site-dates-australias-human-history-back-to-at-least-65000-years-ago.php)).
- **AMS radiocarbon** — 26 AMS ¹⁴C ages, applying to the upper part of the sequence only ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968)). Critics point out that the radiocarbon data from Madjedbebe and Nauwalabila "are consistent with the termite-disturbance hypothesis" ([Williams et al., *Geoarchaeology*, abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/gea.21822?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,externalIds,venue)).
- **Thermoluminescence (TL)** — the earlier, now-superseded chronology ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968)).
- **Method conflict:** OSL of the *sediment* gives ~65 ka; radiocarbon of the *organics* gives younger ages. The dispute is about whether the artefacts are stratigraphically associated with the sediment grains that were dated, or have moved downward. Critics argue "luminescence data claimed to offer strong support for the early Madjedbebe estimate are also consistent with termite disruption" and conclude that "the early dates for human presence at Madjedbebe and Nauwalabila must be rejected" ([Williams, Spooner, McDonnell & O'Connell, *Geoarchaeology* 36(1):92–108, abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/gea.21822?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,externalIds,venue); [journal page](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gea.21822)).

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **60–50 ka**, with total uncertainties of **16–20 kyr at 95.4%** (Roberts et al. 1990, thermoluminescence at Malakunanja II) | **~65,000 years** (65 ± 6 ka) | 2017 (Clarkson et al., *Nature*) | New excavation, 52 single-grain OSL + 26 AMS ¹⁴C ages, replacing the imprecise TL chronology ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968)) |
| Continental colonization "not earlier than 47–48 ka" (Allen & O'Connell 2014) — still actively defended, not superseded | — | — | Not abandoned; this is a live rival position ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)) |

Also superseded within the sceptical literature: the earlier 50–60 ka luminescence claim at **Nauwalabila** was re-analysed and found to reflect post-depositional disturbance, with claims for human presence there earlier than ~40 ka judged "unsupportable" ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)).

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Rival chronologies plus method conflict — actively contested.** Two irreconcilable continental chronologies exist: a "long" chronology anchored on Madjedbebe at ~65 ka ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968)) and a "short" chronology at ~47 ka ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)). The short-chronology camp's core argument is not that the OSL measurements are wrong but that termite activity and trampling in the seasonally wet tropics moved artefacts downward into older sediment, breaking the artefact–sediment association ([Williams et al., *Geoarchaeology*](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/gea.21822?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,externalIds,venue)). They further note that "no other data from Australia support" a 65 ka age ([same abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/gea.21822?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,externalIds,venue)) — i.e. Madjedbebe is an isolated outlier, which O'Connell & Allen quantify as "separated by up to 13,000 years from the next earliest indication of human presence in Sahul" ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)). The stakes are explicit: a 65 ka date "would represent a 30% increase in the currently accepted length of human occupation of the continent" ([Williams et al. abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/gea.21822?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,externalIds,venue)).

### F. Convention
Quoted in **ka / years BP ("years ago")**, effectively uniformly. BCE is not used. Note that OSL ages are reported as years before the measurement date, not before 1950, but the literature does not usually flag this — a minor systematic offset that is negligible at 65 ka but worth noting in a dataset schema.

## 5. Neanderthal extinction / disappearance in Europe

### A. Consensus date
**Mousterian (Neanderthal) occupation ends 41,030–39,260 cal BP at 95.4% probability**, from a 40-site Bayesian analysis: Higham et al. 2014 (*Nature* 512:306–309) find that the Mousterian ended across Europe within this window ([Higham et al. 2014, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621); [University of Oxford release](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2014-08-20-neanderthals-and-modern-humans-%E2%80%98overlapped%E2%80%99-europe-5400-years)). The same study finds an overlap with anatomically modern humans of **2,600–5,400 years (95.4%)**. Chris Stringer's summary framing is that Neanderthals had largely or entirely vanished by **39,000 years ago** ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140820-neanderthal-dating-bones-archaeology-science)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Earliest (regional) disappearance:** in northwest Europe (Spy, Engis, Fonds-de-Forêt, Belgium), Neanderthals disappear at **44,200–40,600 cal BP (95.4%)** ([Devièse et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7999949/)).
- **Latest defensible figures under current methods:** an optimal-linear-estimation analysis places Châtelperronian extinction at **39,894–39,798 cal BP** and regional Neandertal extinction at **40,870–40,457 cal BP**, with an overlap of **1,400–2,900 years** ([*Scientific Reports* 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9561710/)).
- **Latest claims in the older literature (now largely rejected):** **28,000 and possibly 24,000 uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP** at Gorham's Cave, Gibraltar ([*Nature* news 2006 on Finlayson et al.](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060911/full/news060911-8.html); the 24,000 uncal claim is restated in [PNAS 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1903984116)). Zilhão et al. argued the most parsimonious reading of the Gorham's data is **ca. 32,000–30,000 ¹⁴C BP** ([Zilhão et al., University of Bristol PDF](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/archanth/staff/zilhao/gorhams.pdf)).
- Credible range: **~44,200 cal BP (earliest regional disappearance) to ~39,000 cal BP (last Neanderthals)**, with the Iberian "late survival" claims down to 28–24 ka uncal BP now treated as contamination artefacts.

### C. Dating methods
- **AMS radiocarbon with ultrafiltration pretreatment on bone collagen, plus Bayesian modelling** — the basis of the 41,030–39,260 cal BP figure, across 40 sites ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)).
- **Compound-specific radiocarbon dating of the amino acid hydroxyproline** — the most contamination-resistant method; used to redate Vindija G1 ([Devièse et al. 2017, PNAS, PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)).
- **Uncalibrated conventional radiocarbon on bulk bone (pre-ultrafiltration)** — the source of the discredited late dates. Devièse et al. quantify the problem: **1% modern carbon contamination shifts a 42,500-year date 8,455 years younger**, and they conclude that pre-2004 bone radiocarbon dates "should be used with great caution" ([PNAS/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)).
- **Optimal linear estimation (statistical extinction-time estimation applied to dated records)** — gives Châtelperronian extinction 39,894–39,798 cal BP ([*Scientific Reports* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9561710/)).
- **Method conflict, resolved directionally:** older and newer radiocarbon methods systematically disagree, and always in the same direction — the old dates are too young. In northwest Europe, previous dates were too young by **up to 10,000 years** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7999949/)).

### D. Superseded dates — **multiple major revisions**
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gorham's Cave, Gibraltar: Neanderthals to **28,000 and possibly 24,000 uncal ¹⁴C BP** (Finlayson et al. 2006) | Not sustained; Zilhão et al. read the data as ca. **32,000–30,000 ¹⁴C BP**, and the pan-European end is 41,030–39,260 cal BP | critique from 2006 onward; superseded framework 2014 | Contamination and stratigraphic association problems; Bayesian re-analysis with ultrafiltered dates ([*Nature* news 2006](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060911/full/news060911-8.html); [Zilhão et al.](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/archanth/staff/zilhao/gorhams.pdf); [Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)) |
| Zafarraya and Jarama VI, Spain: **~35,000** | **~50,000** | 2013 (Wood et al., PNAS) | Radiocarbon ultrafiltration pretreatment removing younger contaminant carbon ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/science-environment-21330194)) |
| Vindija G1, Croatia: **29,000–28,000** and **33,000–32,000 ¹⁴C yr** | **before 40,000 ¹⁴C BP** | 2017 (Devièse et al., PNAS) | Compound-specific hydroxyproline radiocarbon dating, eliminating contamination ([PNAS/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)) |
| Northwest European Neanderthal dates | too young by up to **10,000 years**; corrected disappearance **44,200–40,600 cal BP** | — | Improved pretreatment and direct dating ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7999949/)) |

The Vindija case matters beyond one site: Vindija G1 was the source of the Neanderthal genome and was the flagship evidence for late Neanderthal survival and long overlap with modern humans in central Europe. Its redating to >40,000 ¹⁴C BP removed that evidence ([PNAS/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)).

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Method conflict, now largely resolved in one direction.** Every improvement in radiocarbon pretreatment (ultrafiltration from ~2004; compound-specific hydroxyproline from ~2017) has made Neanderthal dates *older*, because the contaminant is younger carbon and even 1% of it produces a large young bias at these ages ([Devièse et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)). The "late survival in Iberia" school (Finlayson, Gorham's Cave) has therefore lost most of its chronometric support, though the 24 ka figure is still cited in the literature as a claim ([PNAS 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1903984116)). Residual live questions: the exact length of Neanderthal–AMH overlap (2,600–5,400 yr in [Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621) vs 1,400–2,900 yr in the [2022 OLE analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9561710/)), and whether disappearance was synchronous or staggered by region (NW Europe at 44.2–40.6 ka vs the continental 41.0–39.3 ka).

### F. Convention
**Mixed, and this is the single most dangerous case for a dataset.** Modern work uses **cal BP** ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)), but the older late-survival literature quotes **uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP** ([*Nature* news 2006](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060911/full/news060911-8.html)), and the Vindija redating is itself expressed in uncalibrated **¹⁴C BP** ([Devièse et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/)). Comparing a 24,000 uncal BP figure with a 40,000 cal BP figure without conversion produces spurious disagreement on top of the real one. BCE is not used.

## 6. Younger Dryas — start and end

### A. Consensus date
From the GICC05 Greenland ice-core timescale (Rasmussen et al. 2006; Vinther et al. 2006):
- **Onset of the Younger Dryas: 12,896 b2k**, with a maximum counting error of **138 years**, at NGRIP depth 1526.52 m ([Rasmussen et al. 2006, *JGR*, Niels Bohr Institute PDF](https://www.iceandclimate.nbi.ku.dk/publications/papers/pdfs/220.pdf); [DTU Orbit copy](https://orbit.dtu.dk/files/130083521/Rasmussen_et_al_2006_Journal_of_Geophysical_Research_Atmospheres_1984_2012_.pdf)).
- **YD–Preboreal termination: 11,703 b2k**, maximum counting error **99 years** ([same sources](https://www.iceandclimate.nbi.ku.dk/publications/papers/pdfs/220.pdf); [NOAA/NCEI GICC05 Holocene data file](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/paleo/icecore/greenland/summit/ngrip/vinther2006-gicc05-holocene-dye-20yr-noaa.txt)).
- **Duration: 1,186 years**, maximum counting error **44 years** ([Rasmussen PhD thesis](https://nbi.ku.dk/english/theses/phd-theses/sune-olander-rasmussen/PhDthesis_Sune_Olander_Rasmussen.pdf)).
- For context, the onset of the preceding Bølling is **14,692 b2k** (mce 186 yr) ([Rasmussen et al. 2006](https://www.iceandclimate.nbi.ku.dk/publications/papers/pdfs/220.pdf)).

The termination is also the **formally ratified base of the Holocene**: the GSSP at 1492.45 m in the NGRIP2 core, dated **11,700 calendar yr b2k with a maximum counting error of 99 yr**, defined by an abrupt shift in deuterium excess marking the end of the Younger Dryas / Greenland Stadial 1 ([Walker et al. 2009, *J. Quaternary Sci.* 24:3–17, PDF](https://timescalefoundation.org/references/HoloceneGSSP_JQuatSci090.pdf); [ICS GSSP page](https://stratigraphy.org/gssps/holocene); [NASA ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009EGUGA..1110397R/abstract)). Note the small discrepancy in stated counting uncertainty: 99 yr in Walker et al. and the ICS-linked sources, but "counting uncertainty is 69 years" on the ICS GSSP page itself ([stratigraphy.org](https://stratigraphy.org/gssps/holocene)), and "99 yr at the 2σ level" in the *Treatise*-style summary ([ScienceDirect NorthGRIP overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/northgrip)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Onset:** independent proxies span roughly **12,890 ± 20 to 12,770 ± 110 BP**. Cheng et al. 2020 give North Atlantic onset **12,870 ± 30 BP**, Asian Monsoon onset **~12,890 ± 20 BP**, and Antarctic onset **12,770 ± 110 BP** ([Cheng et al. 2020, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7519346/)). A Palawan stalagmite gives initiation at **12.92 ± 0.08 kyr** ([Partin et al. 2015, *Nature Communications*, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569703/)).
- **Termination:** **~11,900 ± 80 BP** (termination begins in Antarctica) to **11,700 ± 40 – 11,610 ± 40 BP** (North Atlantic) ([Cheng et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117)). Palawan recovery: **11.79 ± 0.08 kyr** ([Partin et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569703/)).
- Full credible range for the event: onset **12,920–12,770 BP**, termination **11,900–11,610 BP**.

### C. Dating methods
- **Ice-core multi-parameter annual layer counting (GICC05)** — the reference chronology; onset 12,896 b2k (mce 138 yr), termination 11,703 b2k (mce 99 yr) ([Rasmussen et al. 2006](https://www.iceandclimate.nbi.ku.dk/publications/papers/pdfs/220.pdf)). Cross-checked against tephra horizons: the Vedde Ash at 1506.14 m dated **12,171 b2k (counting uncertainty 114 yr)** and the Saksunarvatn tephra at 1409.83 m dated **10,347 b2k (counting uncertainty 89 yr)** ([Walker et al. 2009 PDF](https://timescalefoundation.org/references/HoloceneGSSP_JQuatSci090.pdf)).
- **Speleothem U-Th (absolutely dated stalagmites)** — Cheng et al. 2020 and Partin et al. 2015 figures above ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117); [*Nat. Commun.*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569703/)).
- **Agreement rather than conflict:** Cheng et al. 2020 report that the independently, absolutely dated speleothem record **confirms GICC05 to within ±20–40 years** ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117)). This is the strongest inter-method agreement of any entity in this report.
- Uncertainty quantification is also published as percentage-form uncertainty in the archived GICC05 dataset ([PANGAEA](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.943195)).

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| YD duration **1,150 ± 50 years** (Björck et al. 1998, GRIP-based event stratigraphy) | **1,186 years (mce 44 yr)** | 2006 (GICC05) | New multi-parameter annual layer counting on NGRIP + GRIP + DYE-3 ([Rasmussen PhD thesis](https://nbi.ku.dk/english/theses/phd-theses/sune-olander-rasmussen/PhDthesis_Sune_Olander_Rasmussen.pdf)) |
| Previous GRIP dating of the YD–Preboreal transition | **~150 years younger** than previously placed; GICC05 puts the transition **130 years earlier** than the previous GRIP dating | 2006 | GICC05 layer counting ([Rasmussen thesis](https://nbi.ku.dk/english/theses/phd-theses/sune-olander-rasmussen/PhDthesis_Sune_Olander_Rasmussen.pdf); [NOAA GICC05 file](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/paleo/icecore/greenland/summit/ngrip/vinther2006-gicc05-holocene-dye-20yr-noaa.txt)) |
| Holocene base as a radiocarbon-defined **10,000 ¹⁴C yr BP** Global Standard Stratigraphic Age (favoured in the 1960s) | GSSP at **11,700 cal yr b2k** in NGRIP2 | ratified 2008, published 2009 | Shift from a chronometric definition to a physical GSSP in an ice core — the first time an ice core defined a GSSP ([Walker et al. 2009](https://timescalefoundation.org/references/HoloceneGSSP_JQuatSci090.pdf); [Cambridge repository PDF](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/65d4b670-9c74-41bb-83a9-0a450cb5ed8a/content)) |

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Largely settled**, with a residual definitional wrinkle. The onset and termination are agreed to within a few decades across ice cores and absolutely dated speleothems ([Cheng et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117)), and the termination is formally ratified by the IUGS ([ICS](https://stratigraphy.org/gssps/holocene)). The remaining subtleties are definitional and geographic rather than chronometric: (i) the event is not globally synchronous — onset and termination differ between the North Atlantic, Asian Monsoon, and Antarctic records by ~100–200 years, with the termination *beginning* in Antarctica ~200 years before the North Atlantic ([Cheng et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117)); (ii) "Younger Dryas" and "Greenland Stadial 1 (GS-1)" are used for the same interval but are defined on different evidence (biostratigraphic vs ice-core isotopic); (iii) the base of the Holocene is defined at the *end* of the YD, not at the termination of the last cold stage, which is at ~14,500 cal yr BP ([Cambridge repository PDF](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/65d4b670-9c74-41bb-83a9-0a450cb5ed8a/content)).

### F. Convention
**Mixed, and the units are not interchangeable.** Ice-core work uses **b2k** (years before AD 2000), which is offset by **50 years** from radiocarbon's BP zero of AD 1950 — Walker et al. state this explicitly ([Walker et al. 2009](https://timescalefoundation.org/references/HoloceneGSSP_JQuatSci090.pdf)). Speleothem work uses **BP** ([Cheng et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2007869117)). BCE conversions do appear: Rasmussen's thesis gives the YD as **10,897–9,704 years B.C.** ([Rasmussen PhD thesis](https://nbi.ku.dk/english/theses/phd-theses/sune-olander-rasmussen/PhDthesis_Sune_Olander_Rasmussen.pdf)). For a dataset, store b2k, BP, and BCE separately with the 50-year offset applied explicitly.

## 7. Göbekli Tepe — earliest monumental construction (Layer III)

### A. Consensus date
**Late 10th millennium BCE, c. 9600–9500 calBC.** The most secure direct date on Layer III architecture is from wall plaster in Enclosure D, sample **KIA-44149: 9984 ± 42 ¹⁴C BP = 9745–9314 calBC at 95.4% probability** ([Dietrich 2016, DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)); the same date is elsewhere quoted as 9675–9314 calBC at 93.9% using IntCal09. Charcoal samples immediately adjacent to the Enclosure D ring wall (UGAMS-10795, -10796, -10799) cluster at **9664–9311 calBC (95.4%)** ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).

Recalibrated with IntCal20, KIA-44149 and UGAMS-10796 both yield **9,530 ± 200 BCE**; the earliest date from Dietrich et al. 2013 is also quoted as **9530 ± 215 BCE** ([*Journal of Skyscape Archaeology* / Taylor & Francis 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1751696X.2024.2373876)).

The site as a whole spans **c. 9600–8000 calBC**, about 1,600 years, with the PPNA phase at **9600–8700 calBC** ([Gresky et al. 2017, *Science Advances*, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5489262/)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Oldest end:** ~9,745 calBC (the 95.4% upper bound of KIA-44149) ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)). Kinzel & Clare go further, stating that a **Younger Dryas age** for the earliest phase (i.e. before ~11,700 b2k) **cannot be ruled out** ([Kinzel & Clare, *Open Archaeology*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2022-0317/html?lang=en)).
- **Youngest end for Layer III enclosures:** Enclosure C gives **UGAMS-10797: 9700 ± 30 BP = 9261–9139 calBC (91.6%)**, and Enclosure D fill gives **UGAMS-10798: 9540 ± 30 BP = 9127–8763 calBC** and **KIA-44701 (tooth collagen): 9800 ± 120 BP = 9746–8818 calBC** ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).
- **Terminus ante quem for Layer II:** **IGAS-2658 (humic acids): 8880 ± 60 BP = 8241–7795 calBC** ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)); a related paper gives the Layer II end as **8860 ± 60 BP = 8240–7780 calBC** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493732/)).
- Credible range for earliest monumental construction: **~9,750 to ~9,300 calBC**, i.e. ~11,700–11,250 cal BP, with a minority view allowing something earlier.

### C. Dating methods
- **Calibrated AMS radiocarbon on wall plaster (organic inclusions)** — the direct architectural dates, e.g. KIA-44149 ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).
- **Calibrated AMS radiocarbon on charcoal and on bone/tooth collagen** — the UGAMS series and KIA-44701 ([same source](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).
- **Radiocarbon on pedogenic carbonate** — explicitly rejected as too young: these dates push Layer III into the 9th millennium and Layer II into the 8th/7th millennium and can serve only as a **terminus ante quem** ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).
- **Radiocarbon on humic acids** — IGAS-2658, used only as a terminus ante quem ([same source](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)).
- **Calibration curve versions** — IntCal09 vs IntCal20 shift the same measurements: KIA-44149 reads 9675–9314 calBC on IntCal09 and 9,530 ± 200 BCE on IntCal20 ([DAI](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/); [Taylor & Francis 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1751696X.2024.2373876)).
- **Method conflict:** direct plaster/charcoal dates say late 10th millennium BCE; pedogenic carbonate dates say 9th millennium or later. The plaster dates are also affected by the **old-wood effect** ([Kinzel & Clare](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2022-0317/html?lang=en)), meaning the direct dates may be too old.
- **Sample-size caveat:** at the time of Dietrich's synthesis, there were **only eleven radiocarbon dates** for the entire site ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)). This is a small chronometric base for a site of this fame.

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Schmidt-era determinations **Hd-20025: 9452 ± 73 BP** and **Hd-20036: 9559 ± 53 BP** (Kromer & Schmidt 1998, *Neo-Lithics* 3/98:8–9) | superseded by the direct plaster and adjacent-charcoal dates, 9745–9314 calBC | 2010–2013 (Dietrich & Schmidt 2010; Dietrich et al. 2013) | New direct sampling of Layer III architecture rather than of loose fill ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)) |
| Calibration on **IntCal09** (9675–9314 calBC) | **IntCal20** recalibration → **9,530 ± 200 BCE** | 2020 (Reimer et al. IntCal20) / applied 2024 | Calibration curve revision ([Taylor & Francis 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1751696X.2024.2373876)) |
| The **three-layer model (Layer III / II / I)** with Layer III as a single monumental phase | model abandoned in favour of **at least eight building phases**, with Phase 1 in the second half of the 10th millennium BCE | 2020 (Kinzel & Clare) | Re-analysis of the built sequence; the enclosures are shown to be multi-phase constructions, not single-episode monuments ([Kinzel & Clare, *Open Archaeology*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2022-0317/html?lang=en); [Clare 2020, *e-Forschungsberichte*](https://publications.dainst.org/journals/index.php/efb/article/view/2596); summary at [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe)) |
| Pedogenic-carbonate dates placing Layer III in the 9th millennium BCE | rejected as too young, used only as terminus ante quem | — | Recognised method bias in pedogenic carbonate ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)) |

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Definitional, with a method conflict underneath.** The absolute radiocarbon range for the site (c. 9600–8000 calBC) is not seriously contested ([Gresky et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5489262/)), but "the date of Layer III" has become an ill-posed question: Kinzel & Clare abandoned the Layer III/II/I scheme entirely in favour of at least eight phases, so there is no longer a single "Layer III" to date ([*Open Archaeology*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2022-0317/html?lang=en)). Layered on that: only eleven ¹⁴C dates existed at the time of the main synthesis ([DAI](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)), the direct plaster dates are vulnerable to the old-wood effect (biasing old), and the pedogenic carbonate dates are biased young. For a chronology dataset, the defensible entry is "earliest monumental construction c. 9600–9500 calBC, from a small ¹⁴C base, under a superseded stratigraphic model".

### F. Convention
Quoted in **calBC / BCE**, uniformly, with the underlying measurements given in **uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP** ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)). This is a Near Eastern Neolithic convention and contrasts sharply with the BP convention used for every Palaeolithic entity in this report. Cal BP figures are almost never quoted for Göbekli Tepe, so converting for dataset consistency requires adding 1,950 years and flagging the conversion as derived.

## 8. Monte Verde II (Chile) — occupation date and the fall of "Clovis First"

### A. Consensus date
**~14,500 cal BP** (equivalently, ~12,500 uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP). The 1997 site-visit verification paper (Meltzer et al. 1997, *American Antiquity* 62(4):659–663) concluded that MV-II is "both archaeological and **12,500 years old**", based on 11 radiocarbon ages of ~12,500 BP (uncalibrated) ([Meltzer et al. 1997, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/on-the-pleistocene-antiquity-of-monte-verde-southern-chile/C2881B00F15E74B27780CD989E6AB595); [Meltzer 1999, "On Monte Verde", SMU PDF](https://www.smu.edu/~/media/site/dedman/departments/anthropology/pdf/meltzer/da_1999_on%20monte%20verde.ashx)).

Direct dating of marine algae from MV-II hearths gave **14,220–13,980 calendar years BP (≈12,310–12,290 ¹⁴C years)** ([Dillehay et al. 2008, *Science* 320:784–786, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18467586/); [Vanderbilt release](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2008/05/08/new-evidence-from-earliest-known-human-settlement-in-the-americas-supports-coastal-migration-theory-58122/)). Dillehay's own later summary figure is **14,600–14,200 cal BP** ([tDAR record](https://core.tdar.org/browse/site-name/10536/monte-verde-i)), and the widely cited round figure is **14,500 cal BP** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Verde)).

### B. Earliest / latest plausible bounds
- **Oldest end:** Dillehay et al. 2015 (*PLOS ONE*) report ephemeral human activity in the Monte Verde area from **~18,500 to 14,500 cal BP** ([summary](http://thefuzzysasquatch.blogspot.com/2015/12/monte-verde-18500-bp.html)). OSL on river sands at the base of the site gives ages of **18,100–14,300 years** ([El Ciudadano, reporting the 2026 eLetters](https://www.elciudadano.com/actualidad/chile-at-the-heart-of-the-debate-on-american-colonization-scientists-confirm-monte-verdes-age-at-14500-years/05/17/)). MV-I, a separate lower component, has yielded ~33,000 BP dates that have never been resolved ([Meltzer et al. 1997](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/on-the-pleistocene-antiquity-of-monte-verde-southern-chile/C2881B00F15E74B27780CD989E6AB595)).
- **Consensus band:** MV-II radiocarbon ages of **12,700–12,000 rcbp ≈ 14,500–14,000 cal BP**; discounting outliers leaves **12,700–12,200 rcbp** ([Fiedel, *Archaeology* online feature](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/clovis/fiedel.html)).
- **Youngest end (2026 challenge):** Surovell et al. 2026 argue Monte Verde "cannot be older than the Middle Holocene (**8200 to 4200 years B.P.**)" ([Surovell et al. 2026, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)), with Surovell suggesting the human activity likely occurred **6,000–8,000 years ago** ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/study-suggests-younger-age-chiles-important-monte-verde-archaeological-site-2026-03-19/)) and the surface on which the material rests forming **after 8,600 years ago** ([University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)).
- Credible range as of August 2026: **~14,600 cal BP (defended by the excavators and ~30 co-signatories) vs 8,200–4,200 BP (Surovell et al.)** — not a continuum but two mutually exclusive positions.

### C. Dating methods
- **Uncalibrated conventional and AMS radiocarbon** — the original 11 ages of ~12,500 BP ([Meltzer et al. 1997](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/on-the-pleistocene-antiquity-of-monte-verde-southern-chile/C2881B00F15E74B27780CD989E6AB595)).
- **Calibrated radiocarbon, including direct dating of seaweed/marine algae** — 14,220–13,980 cal BP ([Dillehay et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18467586/)). Caveat: marine samples carry a marine reservoir effect, which would bias them **old**; the seaweed dates are noted as slightly younger than the other dates from the site ([Surovell interview transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=czGIhnrK6iI)).
- **Bayesian modelling of sediment cores** — earliest sealing sediments at **c. 14,550 cal BP**; a combustion structure at **12,440–12,550 cal yr BP**; and per the 2026 rebuttal, basal peat sealing the occupation at **~14,500–14,600 cal BP** ([Dillehay et al. rebuttal PDF, Fundación Monte Verde](https://fundacionmonteverde.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Dillehayetal.RebuttaltoSurovellet.al2026-1.pdf)).
- **Luminescence (OSL)** — used both by Dillehay et al. 2015 for the 18,500–14,500 cal BP activity range and by Surovell et al. 2026 to argue for a Holocene landform age ([Surovell et al. 2026, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)). OSL on basal river sands gives 18,100–14,300 years per the rebuttal ([El Ciudadano](https://www.elciudadano.com/actualidad/chile-at-the-heart-of-the-debate-on-american-colonization-scientists-confirm-monte-verdes-age-at-14500-years/05/17/)).
- **Tephrochronology** — the crux of the 2026 dispute: a tephra dated to **11,000 years B.P.** identified as stratigraphically *underlying* the archaeological component, which would make the occupation younger than 11,000 BP ([Surovell et al. 2026, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)). Surovell et al. identify it as the Lepué Tephra from Michinmahuida ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/first-people-south-america-clovis)). Dillehay et al. respond that "there is no [around 11,000-year-old] Lepué Tephra below the MV-II archaeological site" and that the sampled layer is instead a fungus and iron-oxide-rich pyroclastic bead layer ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile); [Dillehay et al. rebuttal PDF](https://fundacionmonteverde.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Dillehay-PinoM.-Lara-L._-Abarzua-A.M.-et-al.-Rebuttal-to-Surovell-et.al-2026-.pdf)). Waters et al. add that available geochemical analyses do not confirm the deposit is Lepué Tephra at all ([El Ciudadano](https://www.elciudadano.com/actualidad/chile-at-the-heart-of-the-debate-on-american-colonization-scientists-confirm-monte-verdes-age-at-14500-years/05/17/)).
- **Method conflict:** radiocarbon on wood/organics says ~14,500 cal BP; Surovell et al.'s OSL on landform sediments plus tephra correlation says Holocene. Surovell's framing is that dating the wood does not date the occupation, because Ice Age wood was redeposited from creek banks into younger deposits ([University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)). Notably, Surovell et al.'s own new radiocarbon dates on charcoal and wood came out at **13,400–16,500 years**, in line with previous studies — they attribute this to redeposition rather than to occupation ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile)).

### D. Superseded dates
| Old value | New value | Revision published | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12,500 BP** (uncalibrated ¹⁴C), the figure of the 1997 verification | **~14,500 cal BP** — the *same measurements* expressed on the calendar scale | progressive, from the late 1990s | Radiocarbon calibration, not new evidence. This is a notational revision that has caused widespread confusion; the Clovis baseline likewise shifts from 11,500–11,000 rcbp to 13,500–13,000 cal BP ([Fiedel, *Archaeology*](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/clovis/fiedel.html)) |
| **"Clovis First"** — the Americas peopled no earlier than ~11,500 rcbp / ~13,500 cal BP | pre-Clovis occupation at ~14,500 cal BP, i.e. at least 1,000 years before Clovis | 1997 (site visit and Meltzer et al. verification) | A delegation of prominent archaeologists, including former sceptics, visited the site and endorsed its Pleistocene antiquity, which is why 1997 rather than 1979 is the pivot year ([Meltzer et al. 1997](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/on-the-pleistocene-antiquity-of-monte-verde-southern-chile/C2881B00F15E74B27780CD989E6AB595); Dillehay's original 14,000+ BP report in 1979 "stirred up a major controversy because it appeared to conflict with archaeological evidence of the settlement of North America", [Vanderbilt](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2008/05/08/new-evidence-from-earliest-known-human-settlement-in-the-americas-supports-coastal-migration-theory-58122/)). Wikipedia describes "Clovis first" as "now discredited" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Verde)) |
| Occupation limited to ~14,500 cal BP | ephemeral human activity extended back to **~18,500 cal BP** | 2015 (Dillehay et al., *PLOS ONE*) | New excavations plus ¹⁴C and luminescence dating ([summary](http://thefuzzysasquatch.blogspot.com/2015/12/monte-verde-18500-bp.html)) |
| **~14,500 cal BP** | **claimed** Middle Holocene, 8,200–4,200 BP — *contested, not accepted* | 19 March 2026 (Surovell et al., *Science* 391(6791):1283–1288) | Radiocarbon and luminescence dating of nine alluvial exposures along Chinchihuapi Creek plus tephra identification; argument that Pleistocene organics were fluvially redeposited ([Surovell et al. 2026, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)) |

**Important status note:** the 2026 revision has *not* been accepted. Three eLetters published in *Science* on 4–5 May 2026, first-authored by Dillehay, Waters, and Meltzer and signed by around 30 experts, totalling more than 100 pages with supplements, describe the Surovell paper as containing "substantive errors and misrepresentations" and its claims as "categorically false and found to be unsupported" ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile); [the three critiques, Texas A&M CSFA PDF](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/csfa/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2026/05/Three-Critiques.pdf); [Dillehay et al. rebuttal](https://fundacionmonteverde.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Dillehay-PinoM.-Lara-L._-Abarzua-A.M.-et-al.-Rebuttal-to-Surovell-et.al-2026-.pdf); [Meltzer et al. rebuttal](https://fundacionmonteverde.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Meltzer-et-al-Rebuttal-to-Surovell-et-al.-2026.pdf)). Surovell and colleagues published counter-responses in the same eLetters section in June 2026 and stand by their conclusions ([University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)). **For a chronology dataset as of August 2026, MV-II should be recorded at ~14,500 cal BP with an active dispute flag, not revised to the Holocene.**

### E. Nature of the dispute
**Contested/undecided — the evidence itself is questioned.** This is not a case of competing calibration schemes or of two methods disagreeing on the same stratum; it is a dispute over whether the dated material has anything to do with the occupation. Surovell et al. argue the Late Pleistocene wood was redeposited by Chinchihuapi Creek into younger archaeological deposits, and that an 11,000-year-old ash layer underlies the occupation surface, which therefore formed after 8,600 years ago ([Surovell et al. 2026](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217); [University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)). The critics counter on three grounds: that Surovell's team did not excavate within the original site and sampled exposures **50 to 4,000 metres away** over fewer than four days, so their stratigraphy is not equivalent to the MV-II sequence ([Dillehay et al. rebuttal, tDAR/CSFA compilation](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/csfa/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2026/05/Three-Critiques.pdf); [El Ciudadano](https://www.elciudadano.com/actualidad/chile-at-the-heart-of-the-debate-on-american-colonization-scientists-confirm-monte-verdes-age-at-14500-years/05/17/); ["Reevaluation Is Not Novel", Fundación Monte Verde PDF](https://fundacionmonteverde.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Reevaluation-Is-Not-Novel-and-Not-All-Reevaluations-Are-Equal-A-Response-to-Tune-and-Miller.pdf)); that no evidence was presented that any dated wood or bone had actually moved, which Waters et al. call "speculation" and identify the stratigraphic mismatch as the study's "most egregious failure" ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile)); and that numerous radiocarbon ages come directly from MV-II artefacts and features, including seaweed and boldo from hearths and directly dated bone ([Dillehay presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lutKzLje55k); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile)). Meltzer et al. add that genetic evidence supports 14,500 years ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile)), while Surovell's group replies that genetics cannot independently date the site because no American human remains older than ~12,900 years have been genetically analysed ([University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)). The dispute is live and unresolved as of mid-2026.

On the "Clovis First" role specifically: Monte Verde is described as "the only securely dated Late Pleistocene archaeological site in South America" ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/americas/speculation-and-egregious-failure-30-researchers-publish-scathing-critiques-of-study-that-questioned-date-of-early-human-occupation-of-monte-verde-in-chile)) and as having placed occupation "roughly 1,500 years before what was until then thought to be the oldest evidence of people in the Americas" ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/first-people-south-america-clovis)). Its 1997 verification is what broke the Clovis-First paradigm, which is why the 2026 challenge is framed as potentially forcing a rethink of how and when the Americas were first settled ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/first-people-south-america-clovis)) and, in Surovell's words, "essentially eliminates Monte Verde as a pillar of the theory of the coastal colonization of the Americas" ([University of Wyoming](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2026/06/uws-surovell-colleagues-defend-findings-at-famous-south-american-archaeological-site.html)).

### F. Convention
**Mixed — the worst case in this set.** The famous 1997 figure of **12,500 years** is *uncalibrated* radiocarbon BP ([Meltzer et al. 1997](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/on-the-pleistocene-antiquity-of-monte-verde-southern-chile/C2881B00F15E74B27780CD989E6AB595)); the modern figure of **14,500** is *calibrated* cal BP ([Fiedel, *Archaeology*](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/clovis/fiedel.html)). These are the same evidence. Dillehay et al. 2008 helpfully give both: "between 14,220 and 13,980 calendar years before the present (approximately 12,310 and 12,290 carbon-14 years ago)" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18467586/)). Popular coverage almost always uses bare "years ago" without specifying which, and Surovell et al. use "years B.P." ([*Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)). BCE is not used. Any dataset entry for Monte Verde must store the calibration status explicitly or it will silently encode a 2,000-year error.

## Cross-cutting notes for the dataset

**1. The unit is part of the datum.** Three entities in this set (Neanderthal extinction, Monte Verde, Chauvet) generate apparent 2,000–16,000-year disagreements that are partly or wholly artefacts of uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP vs cal BP. Monte Verde's "12,500 vs 14,500" is entirely notational ([Fiedel](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/clovis/fiedel.html)); Neanderthal "24,000 vs 40,000" mixes uncalibrated claims with calibrated ones ([*Nature* news 2006](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060911/full/news060911-8.html); [Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)). The Younger Dryas adds a third scale, **b2k**, offset 50 years from BP ([Walker et al. 2009](https://timescalefoundation.org/references/HoloceneGSSP_JQuatSci090.pdf)). Recommend separate fields for value, scale (uncal BP / cal BP / b2k / BCE / Ma), calibration curve version, and uncertainty type (±1σ / ±2σ / 95.4% HPD / maximum counting error).

**2. Convention splits by period, not by discipline.** Palaeolithic and Quaternary entities are quoted in BP/ka/Ma; Near Eastern Neolithic (Göbekli Tepe) is quoted in calBC/BCE ([DAI Tepe Telegrams](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/06/22/how-old-ist-it-dating-gobekli-tepe/)). Ice-core work uses b2k. There is no single house style to normalise to without loss.

**3. Revisions have a direction.** Radiocarbon methodological improvements systematically push dates **older** (contaminant carbon is young): Neanderthals, Vindija, Zafarraya ([Devièse et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635937/); [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/science-environment-21330194)). Stratigraphic re-analyses can push either way: *H. floresiensis* got much older ([Sutikna et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17179)), Monte Verde is being argued much younger ([Surovell et al. 2026](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adw9217)).

**4. Dispute-type distribution across these eight cases.** Definitional: Oldowan, Göbekli Tepe. Rival chronologies + method conflict: Chauvet, Madjedbebe. Method conflict now resolved directionally: Neanderthal extinction. Contested/undecided: Monte Verde II. Largely settled after a correction: *Homo floresiensis*. Largely settled: Younger Dryas.

**5. Recency flag.** Monte Verde is the only entity here with an unresolved dispute opened within the last six months of this report's date (March–June 2026). Any dataset built now should carry a review date on that row.
