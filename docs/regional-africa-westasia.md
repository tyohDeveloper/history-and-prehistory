**Dating-method vocabulary used:** `radiocarbon-calibrated`, `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`, `argon-argon`, `potassium-argon`, `uranium-series`, `luminescence`, `esr`, `layer-counting`, `magnetostratigraphy`, `typological`, `calendar`.

**Critical reading note on "BP":** In this brief, "ka" and "BP" attached to luminescence, ESR and U-series ages are **not** radiocarbon and carry **no calibration** — they are absolute elapsed-time ages before present. # AFRICA

## 1. Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania) — archaeological locality

**A. Start date.** Bed I, the base of the archaeological sequence, begins immediately above the Naabi Ignimbrite, dated **2.038 ± 0.005 Ma** ([Deino 2012, ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar dating of Bed I, *Journal of Human Evolution*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22809744/)). The compiled bed sequence gives Bed I as **~2.04–1.8 Ma** ([Pante et al., *Journal of Human Evolution*](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The occupation sequence continues upward through Bed II (~1.8–1.2 Ma), Bed III (~1.15–0.93 Ma) and Bed IV (~0.93–0.8 Ma), then the Masek Beds, whose younger part preserves the Brunhes–Matuyama reversal at **781 ka** ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)). No absolute terminal age for the Masek/Ndutu/Naisiusiu Beds was confirmed from a fetched source: `n.a.`

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Published error terms are unusually tight for a site of this age: Naabi Ignimbrite **2.038 ± 0.005 Ma**, Tuff IB **1.848 ± 0.003 Ma**, Tuff IF **1.803 ± 0.002 Ma** ([Deino 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22809744/)). Bed III and Bed IV boundaries are cited as approximate with no ± term ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** Primary: **argon-argon** (⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar on tuffs and lavas) ([Deino 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22809744/)). Secondary: **magnetostratigraphy** (Brunhes–Matuyama reversal at 781 ka in the Masek Beds) ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)). No radiocarbon is involved in the Bed I–IV chronology, so calibration is not applicable.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single ~50 km gorge system in northern Tanzania on the edge of the Serengeti Plain. It is the type locality for the **Oldowan** industry; localities within it include HWK EE (Bed II, ~1.7 Ma, Oldowan), SC (Bed II, 1.4–1.2 Ma, Developed Oldowan/Acheulean), and JK (Bed III, 1.15 Ma, Acheulean) ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** The radioisotopic framework is among the best-constrained in African prehistory and errors are at the few-thousand-year level ([Deino 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22809744/)). The live arguments concern industry labels rather than dates — specifically whether "Developed Oldowan" is a real entity distinct from early Acheulean, since the two are reported side by side in Bed II at 1.4–1.2 Ma ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)). No named challenger to the argon-argon ages was found in a fetched source.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bed I base (Naabi Ignimbrite), Deino 2012 recalibration | 2.038 ± 0.005 Ma | argon-argon | majority | [Deino 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22809744/) |
| Rounded compilation figure "~2.04 Ma" used downstream | ~2.04 Ma | argon-argon | majority | [Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf) |

No competing chronology of different standing was located.

**H. Plain-language summary.** Olduvai Gorge is a deep ravine in Tanzania where layers of volcanic ash let scientists put very precise dates — roughly two million to under one million years ago — on some of the oldest stone tools and human ancestors ever found.

**Hominins attributed:** *Homo habilis*, *Homo erectus*, *Paranthropus boisei*; Bed III/IV bone tools attributed to *H. erectus* ([Pante et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02998313/file/pagination_YJHEV_102885.pdf)).

## 2. Omo Kibish (Ethiopia) — early *Homo sapiens*

**A. Start date.** The Omo I fossil is **older than 233 ± 22 kyr**. The KHS Tuff at the base of Member II, which overlies the horizon containing Omo I, is dated to **233 ± 22 kyr (2σ)** by single-crystal ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar on sanidine, correlated to the Shala volcano Qi2 eruption. Omo I was recovered ~1.4 m below the tuff, so this is explicitly a **minimum age** ([Vidal et al. 2022, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

**B. End date.** Not applicable — this is a fossil-bearing horizon, not a culture. No robust **maximum** age for Omo I exists ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** **±22 kyr at 2σ** on the weighted mean; the inverse-isochron age is **219 ± 27 kyr** (⁴⁰Ar/³⁶Ar = 314 ± 24, MSWD 1.1, P = 0.19, n = 71). 113 crystals were analysed and 71 accepted. Alternative decay-constant calibrations give 234 ± 22 kyr (Renne et al. 2011) and 233 ± 22 kyr (Kuiper et al. 2008) ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

**D. Dating method.** **argon-argon** — single-crystal ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar on sanidine feldspar from volcanic tuff. Not radiocarbon; calibration is not applicable ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Omo-Kibish Formation, lower Omo valley, southern Ethiopia. Omo I came from the surface of a siltstone near the top of Member I. Type site for the "Kibish" *H. sapiens* fossils ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The date changed because the anchoring tephra changed, not because the method changed. Vidal et al. rejected the previously standard **196 ± 4 kyr (2σ)**, recalculated to **197 ± 4 kyr** against the Fish Canyon 28.201 Ma monitor, on the grounds that the Nakaa'kire Tuff feldspars it rested on had uncertain age, uncertain stratigraphic placement, and heterogeneous lithology and geochemistry. They also reject a ~184 kyr age based on correlation to Gademotta unit D and a ~172 kyr age argued from sapropel flux ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)). The counter-position is the pre-2022 consensus built on the Nakaa'kire correlation, i.e. the McDougall/Brown/Fleagle line of work whose ~197 kyr figure Vidal et al. displace.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KHS Tuff = Shala Qi2 (current) | ≥233 ± 22 kyr | argon-argon | majority | [Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8) |
| Nakaa'kire Tuff feldspar age | 196 ± 4 kyr, recalc. 197 ± 4 kyr | argon-argon | superseded | [Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8) |
| Gademotta unit D correlation | ~184 kyr | argon-argon | superseded | [Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8) |
| Sapropel-flux argument | ~172 kyr | correlative (not radiometric) | minority | [Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Omo Kibish in Ethiopia holds one of the oldest skeletons of our own species, and a layer of volcanic ash lying above it shows the person lived at least about 233,000 years ago — some 36,000 years earlier than researchers had thought before 2022.

**Hominin attributed:** *Homo sapiens* (Omo I) ([Vidal et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04275-8)).

## 3. Middle Stone Age (sub-Saharan Africa) — industry period

**A. Start date.** The African MSA began **at least 300,000 years ago**; the overall span is given as roughly **300,000 to 30,000 years ago**, about 270,000 years' duration ([Bader et al. 2022, *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Anthropology*](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **~30,000 BP**, approximately the end of MIS 3 (57,000–29,000 BP) ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)). The transition is regionally staggered: final MSA at Sibhudu has an OSL mean of **38,600 ± 1,900 BP**, Umbeli Belli runs 40,300 ± 3,500 to 29,000 BP, Rose Cottage's final MSA is ~28,000 BP, Strathalan B 29,000–22,000 BP, and Sibebe 31,400–22,000 BP; early LSA appears at many sites 25,000–20,000 BP ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Very large at the early end. Kathu Pan 1 Stratum 3 is **291,000 ± 145,000 BP** (OSL) — a ±50% error. Other early anchors: Florisbad BS layer **279,000 ± 47,000 BP**; the Florisbad hominin **259,000 ± 35,000 BP** (ESR); Haaskraal Pan **244,000 +38,000/−44,000 BP** (U-series); Wonderwerk Cave **238,000 ± 13,000 to 153,000 ± 15,000 BP** (OSL); Canteen Kopje ~300,000 BP ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** Mixed: **luminescence** (OSL and TL), **esr**, **uranium-series** at the early end; **radiocarbon-calibrated** only for the terminal MSA/early LSA, and only within the last ~50 ka. Note the trap: the ages quoted as "BP" above from OSL, ESR and U-series are **not radiocarbon and are not calibrated** — they are elapsed-time ages ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Sub-Saharan Africa broadly; the literature reviewed here is centred on South Africa, with key sites at Blombos, Klasies River, Border Cave, Sibhudu, Diepkloof, Pinnacle Point, Rose Cottage, Wonderwerk, Florisbad and Kathu Pan. The MSA is an industry period defined by technology, not by a single type site ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Two incompatible chronologies exist for the MSA's diagnostic sub-industries. **Jacobs & Roberts** (maximum-likelihood modelling of single-grain OSL) compress Still Bay to ~73,000–70,000 BP and Howiesons Poort to ~66,000–58,000 BP, "just a few millennia" each. **Tribolo et al.** at Diepkloof, using OSL plus TL, produce a "long chronology" in which Still Bay begins as early as **109,000 ± 10,000 BP**, early HP appears at 109,000 and 105,000 ± 10,000 BP, intermediate HP runs 77,000 ± 5,000 to 65,000 ± 8,000 BP, and late HP reaches **52,000 ± 5,000 BP**. The Jacobs & Roberts short chronology has been criticised on methodological grounds by **Guérin et al. 2013** and on layer-attribution grounds by **Porraz et al. 2013**; conversely, the Diepkloof long chronology has not been reproduced at any other southern African site ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short chronology (Jacobs & Roberts 2017) | SB ~73–70 ka BP; HP ~66–58 ka BP | luminescence (single-grain OSL + modelling) | majority | [Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) |
| Long chronology (Tribolo et al., Diepkloof) | SB from 109 ± 10 ka BP; HP to 52 ± 5 ka BP | luminescence (OSL + TL) | minority | [Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) |
| Fauresmith at Kathu Pan 1 Stratum 4 as earliest MSA | ~500,000 BP | esr + luminescence | minority (contested; would require *H. heidelbergensis*) | [Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Middle Stone Age is the long stretch of African prehistory, roughly 300,000 to 30,000 years ago, when people made flake and blade tools and first left behind clear signs of art, ornament and symbol use.

**Requested note on Still Bay and Howiesons Poort as separate carried industries.** The app's existing values — **Still Bay 75.5–67.8 ka** and **Howiesons Poort 64.8–59.5 ka** — sit as two short episodes near the *late-middle* of a ~270,000-year MSA, occupying together under 6% of its duration. Their placement is consistent with the short-chronology camp: Blombos Still Bay is bracketed by an OSL age of **76.8 ± 3.1 ka** on M2 and a sterile hiatus sand at **67.8 ± 4.2 ka** ([Jacobs et al. 2006, *Journal of Human Evolution*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)), and 67.8 ka is exactly the app's Still Bay end value. Stratigraphically, Still Bay begins at the end of MIS 5 (130,000–71,000 BP) and peaks in MIS 4 (71,000–57,000 BP), with Howiesons Poort also within MIS 4 ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20all.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) — see corrected link [here](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)). **Caveat for the app:** carrying these two as narrow fixed bands silently adopts the Jacobs & Roberts short chronology and hides the Diepkloof long chronology, under which Still Bay would begin ~109 ka and Howiesons Poort would extend to ~52 ka ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)). There is also a documented gap between the end of Howiesons Poort (~59.5 ka in the app) and the end of the MSA (~30 ka) that neither carried industry covers.

## 4. Blombos Cave (South Africa) — MSA site

**A. Start date.** Occupation in the dated sequence begins in **M3 at 98.9 ± 4.5 ka**; the sterile basal dune sand beneath it is **143.2 ± 5.5 ka** ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)). An ochre-processing workshop in layer CP has a single-grain OSL age of **101 ± 4 ka** ([Henshilwood et al. 2011, *Science*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Henshilwood-et-al-2011-100000-yr-old-ochre-processing-Blombos_Science.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The MSA sequence ends by **67.8 ± 4.2 ka**, the age of the sterile hiatus sand (ZB15) that separates MSA from LSA. The cave was later reoccupied in the LSA, dated to **290 and 2000 ¹⁴C years BP** ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Single-grain OSL ages with 1σ errors of 3–6 ka: M1 (ZB4) **72.7 ± 3.1 ka**; M2 ZB10 **76.8 ± 3.1 ka**, ZB7 **82.2 ± 3.6 ka**, ZB8 **81.1 ± 4.2 ka**, ZB6 **84.6 ± 5.8 ka**; M3 ZB42 **98.9 ± 4.5 ka**. The basal dune sand age used 2-mm multiple-grain aliquots rather than single grains ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)). The two LSA radiocarbon figures are quoted **with no error terms at all** ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** **luminescence** — single-grain OSL on quartz, the primary method ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)); supplemented by **luminescence** (TL on burnt lithics) with M1 ages of 74 ± 5 ka and 78 ± 6 ka ([Tribolo et al. 2006, *Archaeometry*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4754.2006.00260.x)). The LSA layers are **radiocarbon-uncalibrated** — reported as "290 and 2000 ¹⁴C years BP" ([Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)). The MSA "ka" ages are OSL elapsed time, not radiocarbon.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single coastal cave on the southern Cape coast of South Africa, ~300 km east of Cape Town. It is the reference site for Still Bay engraved ochre and shell-bead symbolism ([Henshilwood et al. 2009, *Journal of Human Evolution*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Henshilwood-et-al-2009-JHE-Engraved-ochres-from-Blombos.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Blombos itself is internally coherent — TL on burnt lithics (74 ± 5 / 78 ± 6 ka, [Tribolo et al. 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4754.2006.00260.x)) agrees with single-grain OSL on M1 (72.7 ± 3.1 ka, [Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf)). The dispute is inherited: Blombos anchors the **short** Still Bay chronology, against which **Tribolo et al.**'s Diepkloof long chronology pushes Still Bay back to ~109 ka, and the Jacobs & Roberts modelling behind the short chronology is attacked by **Guérin et al. 2013** and **Porraz et al. 2013** ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blombos single-grain OSL sequence | M1 72.7 ± 3.1 ka; M2 76.8 ± 3.1 ka; M3 98.9 ± 4.5 ka | luminescence | majority | [Jacobs et al. 2006](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Jacobs-et-al-2006-JHE-OSL-dates-for-Blombos.pdf) |
| TL on burnt lithics, M1 | 74 ± 5 ka and 78 ± 6 ka | luminescence (TL) | majority (concordant) | [Tribolo et al. 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4754.2006.00260.x) |
| Diepkloof long chronology applied to Still Bay generally | from 109 ± 10 ka BP | luminescence (OSL + TL) | minority | [Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Blombos Cave on South Africa's coast preserved deliberately engraved pieces of red ochre and shell beads from roughly 100,000 to 70,000 years ago, some of the earliest solid evidence that people were using symbols.

**Hominin attributed:** *Homo sapiens* (implied by the modern symbolic behaviour and MSA context; no direct fossil attribution was confirmed from a fetched source): `n.a.` for direct fossil attribution.

## 5. Klasies River Mouth (South Africa) — MSA site

**A. Start date.** The base of the sequence is dated by **uranium-disequilibrium to ~110,000 years**; the LBS member corresponds to MIS 5e, the Last Interglacial (~130,000–118,000), and is placed at ~110 ka ([Klasies River excursion guide, Stellenbosch University Department of Archaeology, 2001](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)). The whole sequence is fixed between **120,000 and more than 50,000 years ago** ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The top of the MSA sequence is radiocarbon-dated as **older than 50 ka**, i.e. it is at or beyond the radiocarbon limit and is effectively an infinite age. Post-Howiesons Poort units at the top sit at ~60 ka. LSA burials occur in a **2,000–4,000-year-old** midden capping Cave 1 ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The guide gives ranges rather than ± terms: SAS member U-disequilibrium **~100,000–78,000**; oxygen-isotope correlation of SAS to MIS 5c–b (**~103,000–90,000**); Howiesons Poort lower units ~70 ka with U-disequilibrium ~65 ka; upper member 2.0–5.0 m thick spanning MIS 5a–MIS 4 ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)). An independent luminescence chronology gives Klasies River MSA I as **~115,000–110,000 BP** and MSA II as **~108,000–63,000 BP** (Feathers 2002) ([Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** Primary: **uranium-series** (uranium-disequilibrium, John Vogel) and oxygen-isotope stratigraphy on turban shells (two independent studies; profile by Siep Talma). Also applied "with varying success": **esr**, **luminescence**, and amino-acid racemisation. **radiocarbon-uncalibrated** applies only as a limiting statement ("older than 50 ka") ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)). Note: the "~110,000 years" figure is a U-series elapsed-time age, **not** calibrated radiocarbon.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A cluster of caves and shelters at the mouth of the Klasies River on the Tsitsikamma coast, Eastern Cape, South Africa. It is the type site for the **Klasies River sub-stage** (~110 ka) and for the **Mossel Bay** phase (mid/lower SAS, ~100 ka) in the local culture-stratigraphic scheme ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The chronology is broadly accepted; what is contested is the interpretation of the human remains. The guide states that the fragmentary human bones — mostly skull parts, the oldest ~110,000 years — carry cut and tear marks, percussion damage and burning, and that "episodic cannibalism seems to be indicated." Rightmire's reading of the robust, strongly dimorphic population as candidate San ancestors is a further interpretive claim built on the same fragments ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)). No named opponent of the U-series chronology itself was found in a fetched source.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U-disequilibrium + oxygen-isotope sequence | base ~110 ka; SAS ~100–78 ka; top >50 ka | uranium-series + isotope stratigraphy | majority | [Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf) |
| Feathers 2002 luminescence chronology | MSA I ~115–110 ka BP; MSA II ~108–63 ka BP | luminescence | majority (concordant) | [Bader et al. 2022](https://www.hadw-bw.de/sites/default/files/documents/Bader%20et%20al.%202022_MSA%20of%20South%20Africa.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Klasies River Mouth is a group of South African sea caves whose layers cover roughly 120,000 to 50,000 years ago and contain some of the earliest bones of anatomically modern people, along with the earliest good evidence that people were systematically eating shellfish.

**Hominin attributed:** anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* — "among the oldest morphologically modern people" ([Stellenbosch guide](http://academic.sun.ac.za/archaeology/krguide2001.pdf)).

## 6. Border Cave (South Africa) — MSA/LSA transition site

**A. Start date.** The sequence spans **~200 ka to the present**; a revised ESR-based scheme places MSA 1 / Pietersburg (members 5WA, 5BS, 4WA, 4BS) at **227–77 ka** ([Villa et al. 2012, *PNAS*](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf); [Backwell et al. 2018, *Journal of Field Archaeology*](https://sahris.sahra.org.za/sites/default/files/heritagereports/File%207a.%20Backwell%20et%20al%202018%20JFA%20paper.pdf)).

**B. End date.** Occupation continues into the Holocene. The critical horizon is the Early Later Stone Age at **~44–42 ka cal BP**: 1WA at **44.2–43.0 ka cal BP** (Bayesian ¹⁴C) and 1BS Lower B+C at **43.0–42.5 ka cal BP** ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)). The revised scheme places the ELSA after **~39 ka** ([Backwell et al., Border Cave chapter](https://hal.science/hal-04722241v1/file/Backwell%20et%20al_Border%20Cave%20chapter.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** 2WA **60 ± 3 ka by ESR**; 2BS Lower C ~56 ka; 2BS Lower A+B **>49 ka cal BP**; 2BS UP **49–45 ka cal BP**. Grass bedding from Member 1BS Lower C is published in both forms: **36,700 ± 900 B.P. (42,533–39,362 CAL B.P.)**, which makes the calibration offset explicit ([Backwell et al., Border Cave chapter](https://hal.science/hal-04722241v1/file/Backwell%20et%20al_Border%20Cave%20chapter.pdf)). The BC3 infant burial's perforated *Conus* shell is **74 ka by ESR** ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** — explicitly "ka cal BP", AMS run at Oxford and Kiel on digging-stick wood, an ostrich-eggshell bead, pitch and beeswax, modelled in a Bayesian framework ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)). **esr** provides the older part of the sequence (2WA 60 ± 3 ka; MSA 1 227–77 ka) ([Backwell et al. 2018](https://sahris.sahra.org.za/sites/default/files/heritagereports/File%207a.%20Backwell%20et%20al%202018%20JFA%20paper.pdf)). Border Cave is also the reference site for pushing radiocarbon into the 40–60 ka window ([Bird et al. 2003, *Quaternary Science Reviews*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277379103000052)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single rock shelter in the Lebombo Mountains on the KwaZulu-Natal/Eswatini border, South Africa. It is the reference site for the claimed earliest Later Stone Age and for the earliest known plant bedding ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** Villa and Beaumont date the onset of the LSA at Border Cave to **44–42 ka cal BP**, roughly 20,000 years earlier than the conventional view. Critics reject the stratigraphic association of the diagnostic organic artefacts with the dated layers and instead place the MSA–LSA transition at **32–22 ka**, with the LSA proper beginning ~22 ka and the Robberg Industry running ~22–21 to 12 ka BP; the comparison sequences they rely on are Rose Cottage (final MSA ~30.8–27 ka), Strathalan Cave B (29–25.7 ka) and Boomplaas (BP 34–32 ka, LSA in LPC ~21 ka) ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)). Note that the later ESR-anchored scheme moves the ELSA to after **~39 ka**, partly conceding ground ([Backwell et al., Border Cave chapter](https://hal.science/hal-04722241v1/file/Backwell%20et%20al_Border%20Cave%20chapter.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early LSA at Border Cave (Villa et al. 2012) | 44–42 ka cal BP | radiocarbon-calibrated (Bayesian AMS) | minority-to-contested | [Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Border Cave in South Africa has layers running back roughly 200,000 years, and its excavators argue that the toolkit and organic artefacts of the Later Stone Age appear here around 44,000 years ago — much earlier than most researchers accept.

**Hominin attributed:** *Homo sapiens*; the BC3 infant burial with a perforated *Conus* shell dated to 74 ka by ESR ([Villa et al. 2012](http://in-africa.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/Villa-et-al-2012-PNAS-Border-Cave-and-the-beginning-of-the-LSA-in-S-Africa.pdf)).

## 7. Green Sahara / African Humid Period

**A. Start date.** The African Humid Period began abruptly at **~14.5 ka**, coincident with the Bølling–Allerød warming; some records place onset at ~15 ka and Nile records at ~14 ka ([McGee & deMenocal 2017, *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Climate Science*](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)). The ODP Site 658C terrigenous-flux record puts the onset step at **14.8 cal ka BP** ([deMenocal, ODP 658C dataset, PANGAEA](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.742958)).

**B. End date.** The AHP ends **~5,000 years ago**, but termination is time-transgressive: the northern and eastern Sahara became arid after ~8–7 ka while equatorial sites persisted to 5–3 ka. Quantified termination estimates: ODP 658 dust doubling ~5.5 ka; McGee et al. 2013 weighted-mean dust rise **4.9 ± 0.2 ka**; Tierney et al. 2017 abrupt δD drying **5.2 ± 0.3 ka** ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)). One record places initiation of termination at **8.4–8.5 ka** ([Quaternary Science Reviews, ODP 658 reanalysis](https://hal.science/hal-02406506/file/S0277379119305931.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Published error terms on the termination: **4.9 ± 0.2 ka** (dust flux) and **5.2 ± 0.3 ka** (leaf-wax δD) ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)). The overall span is given as a round "~15,000–5,000 years ago" with no ± term. The Younger Dryas interrupts the AHP at **12.9–11.7 ka**. Peak humidity is ~10–8 ka, with the June–August insolation peak at 20°N at 10–11 ka reaching ~8% above present and staying ≥4% above present from 15–5 ka ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** Mixed, and this entity is unusual in resting mostly on non-archaeological methods: **radiocarbon-calibrated** (marine-core age models; recalibrated archaeological date compilations), **layer-counting** (Lake Yoa annually laminated sediments covering the last 6.1 ka), **luminescence** (Lake Mega-Chad and Fazzan shoreline deposits), plus non-chronometric proxies (pollen, leaf-wax δD, dust flux, bioturbation modelling) ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Not a site but a climatic window across North Africa. Lakes and wetlands extended from **4°N to 28°N**, with large lakes as far north as the Fazzan basin at ~26°N and monsoon rainfall reaching **31°N**. At ~6 ka, savanna reached ~20°N and steppe ~30°N. Hoelzmann et al. 1998 estimate lakes and wetlands covered **~7.4% of the land** between 10–30°N and 17°W–60°E during ~9.5–5.5 ka — 14% in the eastern Sahara, 2% in the western ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)). Reference records: ODP Site 658C off Mauritania and Lake Yoa in Chad.

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** The core disagreement is whether the termination was **abrupt** or **gradual**. The abrupt camp — **deMenocal et al. 2000, Adkins et al. 2006, McGee et al. 2013, Bradtmiller et al. 2016, Egerer et al. 2017, Tierney et al. 2017** — reads marine dust-flux and leaf-wax records as a step change at ~5 ka. The gradual camp — led by **Kröpelin et al. 2008** on the Lake Yoa laminated record, with **Niedermeyer et al. 2010, Cockerton et al. 2014, Shanahan et al. 2015** and Weldeab et al. — sees a vegetation succession spread over ~6,000 years: drying from 5.6 ka, xeric conditions after 4.7 ka, salinity rise 4.2–3.8 ka, and true desert flora only after 2.7 ka. The abrupt camp's counter is that bioturbation and age-model smoothing in marine cores can make sharp transitions look gradual and vice versa ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abrupt termination (dust flux) | 4.9 ± 0.2 ka | radiocarbon-calibrated age models on marine cores | majority | [McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf) |
| Abrupt termination (leaf-wax δD) | 5.2 ± 0.3 ka | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf) |
| Gradual termination (Kröpelin et al. 2008, Lake Yoa) | drying from 5.6 ka; desert flora only after 2.7 ka | layer-counting (annual laminations) | minority | [McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf) |
| Early initiation of termination | 8.4–8.5 ka | radiocarbon-calibrated | minority | [QSR ODP 658 reanalysis](https://hal.science/hal-02406506/file/S0277379119305931.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** For about ten thousand years after the last ice age, a stronger monsoon turned the Sahara green, with lakes, grassland and people living deep inside what is now desert — and it dried out again around five thousand years ago.

**Human occupation signal.** Manning & Timpson 2014 recalibrated radiocarbon dates from **1,011 sites and 3,287 dates**, finding a population peak ~9–5 ka BP and a rapid depopulation step at **6.3–5.2 ka BP**. Kuper & Kröpelin 2006 compiled radiocarbon from >150 eastern Saharan sites; the northernmost sites were abandoned ~7–6 ka and the southernmost were occupied to ~3–2 ka. Lake Chad dropped more than 100 m in the Bodélé and 34 m in the Chad basin between ~5 and 4.7 ka ([McGee & deMenocal 2017](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~peter/site/Home_files/McGee.deMenocal2017.pdf)).

## 8. Nabta Playa (Egypt) — Neolithic ceremonial and astronomical site

**A. Start date.** The Early Neolithic at Nabta Playa begins at **10,800 cal B.P.**, subdivided into El Adam **10,800–9,800 cal B.P.**, El Ghorab **9,600–9,200 cal B.P.** and El Nabta **9,100–8,900 cal B.P.**, with a later El Nabta phase at ~8,800–8,700 cal B.P. ([Wendorf & Schild 1998, *Journal of Anthropological Archaeology* 17:97–123](https://www.kar.zcu.cz/studium/materialy/egy/texty-pro-studenty-2012/NabtaPlaya.pdf)). Monsoons entered Egypt ~11,000 years ago ([Malville et al. 1998, *Nature* 392:488–491](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131)).

**B. End date.** The Late Neolithic runs **7,500–6,200 cal B.P.**, and the Eastern Sahara was abandoned to hyperaridity at **~6,200 cal B.P.** ([Wendorf & Schild 1998](https://www.kar.zcu.cz/studium/materialy/egy/texty-pro-studenty-2012/NabtaPlaya.pdf)). *Nature* gives the onset of hyperaridity as **4,800 radiocarbon years before present** ([Malville et al. 1998](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131)) — see field D, this is an uncalibrated figure.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Wendorf & Schild present phase boundaries as rounded calibrated ranges with no ± terms. The Middle Neolithic is **8,300–7,600 cal B.P.**; cattle tumuli in the "Wadi of Sacrifices" (7 excavated) are ~**7,500–7,400 cal B.P.** ([Wendorf & Schild 1998](https://www.kar.zcu.cz/studium/materialy/egy/texty-pro-studenty-2012/NabtaPlaya.pdf)). Individual dates with error terms appear only in the astronomy literature: a tamarisk roof at **7,270 ± 270 cal BP** and a hearth at **6,800 ± 60 BP** ([Malville, "Astronomy at Nabta Playa" chapter](https://sci-hub.se/tree/0d/9d/0d9d5f6a6886bcdabc6a0d081c704ab0.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** for the cultural phase scheme — Wendorf & Schild label every boundary "cal B.P." ([Wendorf & Schild 1998](https://www.kar.zcu.cz/studium/materialy/egy/texty-pro-studenty-2012/NabtaPlaya.pdf)). **radiocarbon-uncalibrated** for the *Nature* hyperaridity figure, which is explicitly "4,800 radiocarbon years before present (years bp)" with no calibration stated ([Malville et al. 1998](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131)). The alignment dates are **not chronometric at all** — they are **calendar**/archaeoastronomical retrodictions from precession models, and the megalith alignments themselves have no independent date in the *Nature* paper ([Malville et al. 1998](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single internally drained playa basin approximately **10 km × 7 km** in the Egyptian Western Desert, ~100 km west of Abu Simbel. It is the type site for the El Adam, El Ghorab and El Nabta Early Neolithic phases ([Malville et al. 1998](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131); [Wendorf & Schild 1998](https://www.kar.zcu.cz/studium/materialy/egy/texty-pro-studenty-2012/NabtaPlaya.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The archaeological chronology is reasonably settled; the **astronomical** dating of the megaliths is not, and it is a dispute about measurement, not about radiocarbon. Malville et al. reported five megalith lines radiating from Complex Structure A at azimuths of 24.3°, 25° and 28° (northerly), 126° (southeasterly) and a due-east line at 90.02° ([Malville et al. 1998](https://www.nature.com/articles/33131)), and Wendorf & Malville 2001 correlated them to Sirius at **4820 BC** and to precessional tracking of Ursa Majoris (4742, 4423, 4199 BC) and Orion's Belt (4176, 3786 BC). **Brophy & Rosen 2008** re-measured the megalith positions from 60 cm QuickBird satellite imagery plus ground GPS and concluded that the published Sirius and Ursa Majoris rising azimuths were significantly in error, that correcting them did not improve the fit, and that the whole scheme implausibly requires Neolithic observers to have tracked some stars through precession over 1,000+ years while ignoring others. They propose instead Sirius at **~6088/6000 BC**, Orion's Belt and shoulder stars at **~6270 BC** (scatter 6400–5200 BC), and Vega over the Calendar Circle from **~6400 BC** ([Brophy & Rosen 2008](https://www.eixdelmon.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/brophyrosen2008.pdf)). Brophy & Rosen also report the radiocarbon context differently from Wendorf & Schild: most intense activity ~6000 BC, steady activity to the end of the last humid interphase ~**5100 BC**, and less intense occupation until hyperaridity at ~**3800 BC** ([Brophy & Rosen 2008](https://www.eixdelmon.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/brophyrosen2008.pdf)) — a ~1,300-year discrepancy with the 6,200 cal B.P. (≈4250 BC) abandonment date. **The Malville et al. 2007 rebuttal could not be retrieved** (see retrieval failures below).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wendorf & Malville 2001 Sirius alignment | 4820 BC | calendar (precession retrodiction) | majority (as originally published) | [Brophy & Rosen 2008, reporting it](https://www.eixdelmon.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/brophyrosen2008.pdf) |
| Brophy & Rosen Sirius alignment | ~6088 BC / ~6000 BC | calendar (precession retrodiction on re-measured satellite/GPS azimuths) | minority | [Brophy & Rosen 2008](https://www.eixdelmon.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/brophyrosen2008.pdf) |
| Brophy & Rosen Orion's Belt alignment | ~6270 BC (scatter 6400–5200 BC) | calendar | minority | [Brophy & Rosen 2008](https://www.eixdelmon.com/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/brophyrosen2008.pdf) |
| Malville alignment table (later chapter) | Sirius 6,640–6,400 BP; Orion's Belt 6,300–6,100 BP; Arcturus 6,530–6,320 BP | calendar | minority | [Malville, "Astronomy at Nabta Playa"](https://sci-hub.se/tree/0d/9d/0d9d5f6a6886bcdabc6a0d081c704ab0.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Nabta Playa is a dried-up lake basin in Egypt's Western Desert where cattle-herding people left burials, huts and lines of standing stones between about 10,800 and 6,200 years ago — though exactly which stars the stone lines point at, and when, is still argued over.

# WEST ASIA

## 9. Natufian culture (Levant)

**A. Start date.** **c. 13,000 cal BC**. Early Natufian runs **13,000–11,500 cal BC** ([Nilsson Stutz et al., Oxford Handbook chapter on the Natufian](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)). A compilation of >600 calibrated dates places the early Natufian at **~13,400–11,800 BC** ([Benz, PPND summary, ex oriente](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**B. End date.** **c. 10,000 cal BC**, when the Natufian gives way to the PPNA ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)). The PPND compilation is blunter: the end of the Late Natufian "can only be placed at **10,000 BC ± 500 years**" ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The end boundary carries an explicit **± 500 years** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Site-level dates are tighter: Shubayqa 1 was first occupied ~**14,400–14,200 cal BP (1σ)** / ~14,600–14,200 cal BP (2σ), with Late Natufian reoccupation ~13,300–13,100 cal BP (1σ) and a final phase at 12,160–11,650 cal BP (1σ) ([Richter et al. 2017, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-17096-5)). Beidha's Natufian dates have deviations greater than **±150 years** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** — AMS radiocarbon calibrated against IntCal20 (Reimer et al. 2020) in the current literature ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)). **Flag: much of the older Natufian literature is `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`.** Belfer-Cohen & Bar-Yosef's 1991 *Annual Review of Anthropology* scheme gives the Natufian as **12,500–10,200 BP uncalibrated**, with Early 12,500–11,000 BP, Late 11,000–10,200 BP and Final 10,500–10,200 BP ([Belfer-Cohen & Bar-Yosef 1991](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/podzim2015/PAPVA_02/Belfer-Cohen_-Natufian.pdf)); Munro's 2004 *Current Anthropology* table lists site dates in an explicit "Uncal BP" column, e.g. el-Wad **12,950 ± 200** ([Munro 2004](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/422084)). Additionally, PPND states that **many Epipalaeolithic dates were not calibrated at all** because their ± deviations were too large ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Type site: **Shukbah Cave in Wadi en-Natuf**, from whose Layer B (7+ burials, lunate microliths) Dorothy Garrod coined the term "Natufian industry"; she initially called it Mesolithic, and Jean Perrot later placed it in the terminal Pleistocene. The distribution runs throughout the Levant — Sinai to the Middle Euphrates, Mediterranean coast to the Black Desert — and possibly into Anatolia (Dederiyeh Cave, Pınarbaşı rock shelter). Early hamlets include el-Wad, 'Ain Mallaha and Wadi Hammeh 27 ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Two disagreements. First, the position of the Early/Late boundary: the conventional changeover is ~13,500 cal BP, but radiocarbon dating of burials at **Raqefet Cave** pushes the Late Natufian at Mount Carmel back to ~**14,000 cal BP**, while el-Wad's Early Natufian runs continuously 15,000–13,200 cal BP — implying the two "phases" overlapped regionally rather than succeeding one another ([Yeshurun et al., *Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-human-burials-from-raqefet-cave-and-contemporaneous-natufian-traditions-at-mount-carmel/24BABEC97480D3CC9CFD157E8ADC7747)). Second, the overall span: the Cambridge *Quaternary of the Levant* chapter gives **15,000–11,500 cal BP** ([Cambridge, "The Natufian Culture"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/quaternary-of-the-levant/natufian-culture/6F12693C5C41F0807986481C45691351)), which is wider at both ends than the Oxford Handbook's 13,000–10,000 cal BC (≈14,950–11,950 cal BP). There is also a definitional problem: the **Khiamian cannot be separated from the Late Natufian by radiocarbon alone** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxford Handbook scheme | 13,000–10,000 cal BC; Early 13,000–11,500; Late 11,500–10,000 | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944) |
| Cambridge *Quaternary of the Levant* | 15,000–11,500 cal BP | radiocarbon-calibrated | minority | [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/quaternary-of-the-levant/natufian-culture/6F12693C5C41F0807986481C45691351) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Natufians were hunter-gatherers in the Levant between about 13,000 and 10,000 BC who built the first stone-founded villages, ground wild cereals, and buried their dead with ornaments — the immediate forerunners of the first farmers.

### Requested note: Early vs Late Natufian, and the Younger Dryas

**Early Natufian, 13,000–11,500 cal BC.** Diagnostics: Helwan bifacially backed lunates; *Dentalium* and other scaphopod shell beads; deep groundstone mortars; circular single-room stone-founded structures; burials placed under or immediately beside structures, frequently decorated ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)).

**Late Natufian, c. 11,500–10,000 cal BC.** Diagnostics: abruptly backed lunates, with Helwan retouch often absent altogether; grinding slabs replace deep mortars; decorated burials virtually disappear, the notable exception being the Hilazon Tachtit woman ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)).

**Relation to the Younger Dryas.** The chapter dates the Younger Dryas to **11,900–9,700 cal BC**, with a possibly rapid onset at 10,900–10,700 cal BC and cool, dry conditions after c. 10,900 BC. The **Early→Late Natufian transition occurred *before* the Younger Dryas began**, but most *dated* Late Natufian occupations fall **within** it. Critically, the chapter explicitly rejects the classic "Younger Dryas forced farming" narrative: there was no substantial moisture decline, no retreat of the Mediterranean biome, the eastern wetlands remained habitable, and there was no widespread population collapse — Natufian societies are described as **resilient**. Instead, pre-domestication cultivation, storage, and new ideologies of kinship and land tenure at the Holocene onset drove a complex multiregional transition, with Harifian intensification helping to shape the conditions for PPNA settlements after **c. 9,700 cal BC** ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)).

**A dissenting radiocarbon signal.** The PPND compilation of >600 calibrated dates *does* show a strong decrease in dates during the Younger Dryas, with the clearest hiatus in the central southern Levant at **~10,400–9,700 BC**, though Mallaha and Hatula are exceptions ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Whether this is a real demographic decline or an artefact of the calibration curve is the same methodological question that recurs at the PPNA/PPNB boundary (below).

**Related facies.** The **Harifian** dates to 10,500–9,900 cal BC (central 67% of the summed probability), is confined to the southern steppe-desert, is characterised by Harif and Shunera points, and is treated as a later Natufian facies. The Late/Final Ramonian is penecontemporaneous with the Early Natufian at c. 13,000–12,000 cal BC ([Oxford Handbook chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59635/chapter/505050944)).

## 10. Pre-Pottery Neolithic A (PPNA)

**A. Start date.** **9800/9700 cal BC** — the beginning of PPNA Phase 1, following the late Proto-Neolithic ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Corroboration at the site level: activity at WF16 commenced **12.02–11.64 ka cal BP** and had certainly started by **c. 11.84 ka cal BP** ([Wicks et al. 2016, *Proceedings of the Prehistoric Society*](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/23519/6/Dating%20WF16%20Wicks%20et%20al%20%202016%20PPS.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **8800/8700 cal BC**, the end of PPNA Phase 2, after which a distinct "transitional phase" runs 8800–8600 BC before the Early PPNB ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). WF16 activity ceased by **c. 10.24 ka cal BP** after lasting at least 1,590 years ([Wicks et al. 2016](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/23519/6/Dating%20WF16%20Wicks%20et%20al%20%202016%20PPS.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** PPND expresses uncertainty as double boundary values rather than ± terms: Phase 1 **9800/9700–9300/9200 BC**; Phase 2 **9300/9200–8800/8700 BC** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Blockley & Pinhasi 2011 give the PPNA start as **11.78–11.42 ka cal BP** ([Wicks et al. 2016](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/23519/6/Dating%20WF16%20Wicks%20et%20al%20%202016%20PPS.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** — PPND compiles ~800 radiocarbon dates of which about three-quarters were calibratable, and its phase scheme is expressed in calibrated BC ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Individual publications routinely present both forms side by side; Motza's EPPNB measurements, for instance, appear as raw values such as **RTT 4579, 8890 ± 45 BP (uncalibrated)** alongside a calibrated 8500–8000 cal BC range ([Motza EPPNB report](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/cafc/45ce822a9ca78123c03077c0b1ceaf341f96.pdf)). The 2013 PLOS ONE synthesis gives PPNA both ways: **c. 10,300–9,600 BP (radiocarbon years)** = **10,200–8,800 cal BC** ([PLOS ONE 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3716651/)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** The Levant and Middle Euphrates. Type site: **Jericho / Tell es-Sultan**, where Kenyon's "Proto-Neolithic" layers were shown by Crowfoot-Payne's 1983 lithic analysis to be indistinguishable from other PPNA assemblages, after which the industry was renamed **"Sultanian"** ([Bar-Yosef, "The Walls of Jericho: An Alternative Interpretation"](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). Other key sites: WF16 (Jordan), Nemrik 9, Tell Qaramel, Mureybet, Hatula ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The internal PPNA break at **~9300/9200 BC** dividing early from late PPNA is one of three breaks in the dataset that PPND notes correlate with steep slopes and plateaus in the radiocarbon calibration curve — meaning they **may be dating artefacts rather than real cultural or demographic events** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). Laboratory problems compound this: Gif-sur-Yvette conventional dates run ~500 years too old at Aswad; dates produced only at Gliwice for Nemrik 9 and Tell Qaramel are considered doubtful; and Mithen & Finlayson discarded the WF16 "old-wood" dates outright ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPND scheme (Benz) | 9800/9700–8800/8700 cal BC | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php) |
| PLOS ONE 2013 synthesis | 10,200–8,800 cal BC | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority (broader) | [PLOS ONE 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3716651/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Pre-Pottery Neolithic A, roughly 9800 to 8700 BC, is the period when Levantine villages became permanent and people began deliberately planting cereals — but still made no pottery.

## 11. Pre-Pottery Neolithic B (PPNB)

**A. Start date.** **8600 cal BC** for the Early PPNB in the PPND scheme, following the 8800–8600 BC "transition phase." PPNB as defined by Hours et al. (2004) begins **~8700/8600 BC** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). The Middle Euphrates-based standard, Aurenche et al. 1981, dates the Early PPNB to **8700–8200 cal BC** ([Ibáñez et al. 2024, "The Early Pre-Pottery Neolithic B in the Southern Levant"](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **6900? cal BC** — the end of Late PPNB, and PPND marks it with a question mark ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). The PLOS ONE synthesis gives the PPNB as ending **6,900 cal BC** ([PLOS ONE 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3716651/)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** PPND sub-phases, again as double boundaries: Early PPNB **8600–8300/8200 BC**; Middle PPNB **8300/8200–7800/7500 BC**; Late PPNB **7800/7500–6900? BC** ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated**, from the same ~800-date PPND compilation ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). PLOS ONE gives PPNB in both forms: **c. 9,600–8,600 BP (radiocarbon years)** = **8,800–6,900 cal BC** ([PLOS ONE 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3716651/)). The standard periodisation reference for the region is Aurenche, Galet, Regagnon-Caroline & Evin 2001, "A Calibrated ¹⁴C Chronology 12,500–5500 cal BC," *Radiocarbon* 43/3:1191–1202 ([Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Levant, Middle Euphrates and southeastern Anatolia. Type site: **Jericho / Tell es-Sultan** (Kenyon's PPNB layers). Key EPPNB sites: Mureybet (Mureybetian, from which Cauvin derived the PPNB origin ~8800 cal BC), Tell Qarassa North (8600–8200/8100 cal BC), Kharaysin (from ~8500 cal BC, main occupation ending abruptly ~8300 cal BC, Structure 21 used 8300–8000 cal BC), Motza (8500–8000 cal BC) ([Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf); [Motza report](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/cafc/45ce822a9ca78123c03077c0b1ceaf341f96.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** See the extended note below. The two internal PPNB breaks at ~8300/8200 BC (toward Middle PPNB) and ~7600/7500 BC (toward Late PPNB) both coincide with steep slopes and plateaus in the calibration curve and may therefore be dating artefacts rather than cultural transitions ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)). A separate substantive dispute concerns the geography of the PPNB's spread: Ibáñez et al. document a **~500-year gap** between the end of the Early PPNB in the north and the appearance of the first southern (Middle) PPNB at ~8200 cal BC ([Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPND scheme (Benz) | Early PPNB from 8600 cal BC; PPNB to 6900? cal BC | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php) |
| Hours et al. 2004 | PPNB begins ~8700/8600 BC | radiocarbon-calibrated + typological | majority | [Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php) |
| Aurenche et al. 1981 (Middle Euphrates) | EPPNB 8700–8200 cal BC | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf) |
| Proposed revision pushing PPNB start earlier | up to 9200 BC | radiocarbon-calibrated | minority (judged doubtful) | [Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Pre-Pottery Neolithic B, roughly 8600 to 6900 BC, is when Near Eastern farming villages got big and rectangular, domesticated sheep and goats, and produced elaborate ritual objects like plastered skulls and life-size plaster statues — still without pottery.

### Requested note: the PPNA/PPNB boundary — real horizon or typological convention?

**The boundary date.** PPND places it at **8800/8700–8600 cal BC**, with an intervening **"transition phase" (*phase de transition*, Abbès & Stordeur) from 8800 to 8600 BC** that belongs cleanly to neither period ([Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php)).

**Verdict: it is substantially a typological convention overlaid on a probable dating artefact, not a demonstrated horizon.** The evidence for this reading, all from the PPND compilation:

1. There is a **clear break in the radiocarbon data between 8700 and 8600 BC** — but this break **coincides with a steep drop in the calibration curve**, and PPND states outright that "it is unclear whether this is a true settlement decline or a methodological artefact."
2. The 8800–8600 BC transition phase is one of **three phases for which there is no methodological or obvious climatic explanation** — i.e. it is a period the scheme has to accommodate rather than one the data produce.
3. The two adjacent internal breaks (~9300/9200 BC within the PPNA, ~8300/8200 BC toward the Middle PPNB) **also correlate with steep slopes and plateaus in the calibration curve**, which means the whole boundary structure of the PPNA/PPNB sequence may be a signature of the curve rather than of prehistory.
4. A proposed revision moving the PPNB start back to **9200 BC is judged doubtful** precisely because it would then overlap almost entirely with PPNA Phase 2 — showing that the boundary's position is being set by the need to keep the two type-categories separate, not by an independent horizon.

([All four points: Benz, PPND summary](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_summary.php))

Ibáñez et al. add a substantive complication: the Early PPNB as defined from the Middle Euphrates (8700–8200 cal BC) is followed by a **~500-year gap** before the first southern Levantine PPNB appears at ~8200 cal BC, so "the PPNA/PPNB boundary" does not fall at the same calendar moment across the region ([Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf)).

**Recommendation for the app:** if the app displays a single PPNA/PPNB boundary, it should be **8700 BC with an explicit note that a 8800–8600 BC transitional interval exists and that the boundary may be an artefact of the radiocarbon calibration curve.**

## 12. Çatalhöyük (Turkey)

**A. Start date.** East Mound: **c. 7100 cal BC**. Mellaart's original estimate of c. 7100 BC "has proved remarkably accurate" when tested against Bayliss et al.'s (2015) Bayesian model ([Orton et al., "A tale of two tells: dating the Çatalhöyük West Mound," *Antiquity*](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)). West Mound: start **6015–5965 cal BC at 68.2%** / **6040–5935 cal BC at 95.4%** ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**B. End date.** East Mound: cessation of occupation **5975–5865 cal BC at 95% probability**; the latest known domestic building, Building 33, was abandoned **6015–5905 cal BC at 95%** (Marciniak et al. 2015). West Mound: end **5615–5570 cal BC at 68.2%** / **5625–5530 cal BC at 95.4%** ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Explicitly modelled and stated at two confidence levels throughout. West Mound trench-level detail: Trench 1 start **6010–5935 BC (68.2%)** / 6055–5900 BC (95.4%), end 5865–5780 BC (68.2%) / 5890–5720 BC (95.4%); Trench 5 start 5950–5860 BC (68.2%) / 6010–5845 BC (95.4%), end 5840–5760 BC (68.2%) / 5875–5710 BC (95.4%); Trench 7 start boundary 5920–5715 BC (68.2%), described as very uncertain. The authors caution that the overall West Mound chronology is a **significant underestimate** of the mound's antiquity because metres of unexcavated deposits underlie trenches 1 and 5 ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** — 33 new AMS determinations from 32 short-life samples (34 attempted, 32 successful) from trenches 1, 2, 5 and 7, plus 16 previously published by Higham et al. (2007), modelled in **OxCal 4.3** against **IntCal13** ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)). Note that Mellaart's original West Mound estimate of "perhaps from c. 5600 BC" was **uncalibrated** ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A twin-mound settlement on the Konya Plain, south-central Anatolia, Turkey. It is its own type site — the reference site for large agglomerated Neolithic settlement in Anatolia. The two mounds are interpreted as parts of a **single settlement** whose focus shifted gradually from east to west over one or two centuries around the turn of the seventh to sixth millennia BC ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`** (the East/West hiatus question is now resolved; the residual issue is undated depth). Orton et al. model an **overlap of 20–100 years at 68.2% probability**, ranging from 150 years of overlap to 10 years of hiatus at 95.4% — i.e. **no hiatus**. This directly overturns **Weninger et al. (2009)**, who maintained there was a "glaring 200-year gap" between the mounds; earlier West Mound dates had calibrated to ~6000–5800 BC, and four previously available samples, some described as problematic, fell between 6100 and c. 5800 BC ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)). Cessford (2005) had already argued for only a brief interval or none at all. The unresolved issue is that the West Mound start is an underestimate of unknown size because of unexcavated basal deposits; Orton et al. offer a conservative alternative start of **c. 6100 BC**, which would give up to two centuries of overlap ([Orton et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/132693/1/tale_of_two_tells_dating_the_catalhoyuk_west_mound.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

## 13. Halaf culture (northern Mesopotamia)

**A. Start date.** **6100 cal BC** for the Proto-Halaf, or **5950 cal BC** for the Early Halaf proper ([Anatolian journal article, "Kuzey Mezopotamya ve Kuzey Levant'ta 'Halaf Dönemi' (MÖ 6100–5300/5100)", DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). In the Late Neolithic framework of Bernbeck & Nieuwenhuyse 2013, Late Neolithic 5 = Halaf Early–Late runs **5900–5300 cal BC**, with LN4 (Hassuna/Samarra/Proto-Halaf) at 6000–5900 cal BC ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). A parallel scheme places the Halaf at **5800–5300 BC** in northern Mesopotamia, contemporary with southern Ubaid 1–2 ([Stein, Surezha 2013–14 annual report, ISAC](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **5300 cal BC** for the Late Halaf, followed by a **Halaf–Ubaid transition at 5300–5100 cal BC** (= Late Neolithic 6) ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). This is the value under the strongest challenge — see F.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Presented as sharp round boundaries with no ± terms: Proto-Halaf **6100–5950**, Early Halaf **5950–5850**, Early–Middle transition **5850–5700**, Middle Halaf **5700–5550**, Late Halaf **5550–5300 cal BC** (all after Cruells & Nieuwenhuyse 2004) ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). Site-level precision can be much finer: Fıstıklı Höyük was occupied very briefly, **5850–5750 BC** ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-calibrated** — the framework table is labelled "corrected radiocarbon dating" and all phase boundaries are cal BC ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). The competing end-date argument is likewise based on calibrated radiocarbon, explicitly written as "(calibrated) b.c." ([Carter & Philip et al., *Beyond the Ubaid*, ISAC/Oriental Institute SAOC 63](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). Underlying the whole exercise is **typological** ceramic seriation, which the *Beyond the Ubaid* volume warns is circular without radiometric control ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Type site: **Tell Halaf**, northeastern Syria near the modern Syrian–Turkish border, opposite Ceylanpınar; excavated 1911–1913, briefly in 1929, then after a 77-year interruption from 2006; occupied from the Pre-Halaf period to the Halaf–Ubaid transition, c. **6500–5300 BC**. The core region is a **600–650 km** corridor of northern Mesopotamia between the Euphrates and the Tigris along the southern slopes of the Taurus, comprising three adjoining basins — the **Balikh Valley**, the **Upper Khabur Triangle** and the **Upper Tigris Basin** — across northeastern Syria, northern Iraq and southeastern Anatolia. The wider interaction sphere is a half-circle on a **1,000 km base** from Hatay to the Zagros, or alternatively an **Aleppo–Diyarbakır–Mosul triangle of at least 60,000 km²**, extending west to Cilicia and the northern Levant (Ras Shamra, Tell Kurdu), north to the Muş–Elazığ Plain and Lake Van (Tilkitepe), east to the Central Zagros and Shahrazor Plain, and south to Central Mesopotamia. Halaf material-culture elements span ~950 km from Yumuktepe to Tilkitepe ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** The conventional end date of 5300 cal BC is challenged by roughly two centuries. The compilation in *Beyond the Ubaid* argues that **"the Halaf period cannot be conclusively shown to extend beyond a date somewhere between ca. 5500 and ca. 5400 (calibrated) b.c."** and that Ubaid ceramics with calibration results support a start "certainly after 5300 (calibrated) b.c. and might be as late as 5100 (calibrated) b.c." — concluding that "there is no positive indication that the Halaf ended after 5500 (calibrated) b.c. or that the Ubaid started until 5100 or 5000 (calibrated) b.c." This makes the Halaf–Ubaid transition potentially **as long as 5500–5000 cal BC**, i.e. five centuries rather than two ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). **Stuart Campbell and Alexandra Fletcher** press a stronger, definitional version of the attack in their chapter "Questioning the Halaf-Ubaid Transition": they question the directionality of the conventional south-to-north model and "the reality of a transitional phase between two supposedly more meaningful entities (Halaf and Ubaid)," suggesting the period terminology is poorly suited to what is better understood as **continuous incremental change** at the level of local communities. Their Domuztepe evidence shows Ubaid ceramic elements as early as the **first half of the sixth millennium cal BC** — and those sherds compare with **Ubaid 3 or 4** material while showing none of the expected Ubaid 1 or 2 parallels, which inverts the assumed sequence ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)).

**Attribution caveat:** the widely circulated Proto-/Early/Middle/Late Halaf dates are frequently credited to **Campbell 2007, "Rethinking Halaf Chronologies," *Paléorient* 33/1:103–136**. The DergiPark article that reproduces them explicitly cites **Cruells & Nieuwenhuyse 2004** for those figures, with Campbell 2007 in the bibliography but not the source for those specific dates ([DergiPark article](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1184094)). Campbell 2007's own numerical scheme could not be retrieved — the Persée record returns only the abstract, which states that the article treats the 7th–5th millennia cal BC in northern Mesopotamia, focuses on the distortions in previous sequences, and proposes "a new approach to the chronology of this period, less rigid," but gives no dates ([Campbell 2007, Persée record](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_2007_num_33_1_5209)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

## 14. Ubaid period (Mesopotamia) — hands off to Sumer

**A. Start date.** **c. 6500 BC** in southern Mesopotamia, counting Ubaid 0 (the Oueili phase) ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf); [Ubaid lecture, Masaryk University](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)). **Ubaid 1 begins c. 5750 BCE** ([Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)). In northern Mesopotamia the Ubaid begins only at **c. 5300 BC** ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)).

**B. End date and handoff to Uruk/Sumer.** In southern Mesopotamia the Ubaid ends at **c. 3800 BC**, spanning nearly three millennia ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)); the Masaryk lecture gives **4200/4100 BCE** for the same terminus ([Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)). In northern Mesopotamia it ends at **c. 4300 BC** ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). **The most usable single handoff date is 4200 BC**: Stein's synchronised table gives Terminal Ubaid (south) / Late Chalcolithic 1 (north) at **4500–4200 BC**, and **Early Uruk beginning at 4200 BC**, with Middle Uruk and the first cities in the south at 3850 BC and Late Uruk 3400–3100 BC ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)). Sumer proper — the historical, literate phase — follows from the Late Uruk period; the app should treat the Ubaid→Uruk handoff at **4200 BC** and note that the region does not become historical (written) until c. 3350–3200 BC.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** This is the weakest chronology in the brief. *Beyond the Ubaid* states flatly that **"in the case of absolute chronology the term 'Ubaid period' is not consistently defined,"** and that the same label covers a nearly three-millennium southern span and a ~1,000-year northern one. Oates, in the same volume, notes that reliable Ubaid sequences are "regrettably scarce," that there is **no agreed mechanism for comparing recent dates with dates obtained thirty or forty years ago**, and no agreed mechanism for comparing dates from samples of different materials ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). The Masaryk lecture simply states **"Calendrical dates – problematic"** ([Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)). Stein's own table flags the Ubaid 3–4 end with a literal question mark: "5300–4500(?)" ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)). No ± terms are attached to any phase boundary in any fetched source.

**D. Dating method.** Predominantly **typological** — ceramic seriation from long stratigraphic sequences at Eridu, Ur, Tell al-'Ubaid and Tepe Gawra, with **radiocarbon-calibrated** control that is patchy in the south and better in the north. *Beyond the Ubaid* states that using ceramic typology to assign Ubaid occupations to relative chronological positions is **circular without the control provided by radiometric dating techniques**, and that radiocarbon dates have already **undermined some core assumptions** of Ubaid chronology — notably the "apparently anomalous dating" at **Kenan Tepe**, where radiocarbon diverges from typological expectation and thereby throws the assumed direction of influence into question ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). The northern radiocarbon control is calibrated AMS: six samples from two operations at Surezha date the start of LC-1 there ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Type site: **Tell al-'Ubaid**, excavated by H. Hall and C. L. Woolley in 1919, which gave its name to a black-on-buff painted pottery style found in all "Ubaid" contexts; similar pottery was soon found at Ur, and "Ubaid" came into use as a period label around 1930, carrying with it the notion of an Ubaid "culture" and an Ubaid "people." The key stratigraphic reference site is **Eridu**, excavated by S. Lloyd and F. Safar around World War II, which yielded the temple sequence, the Ubaid cemetery and the "Hut sounding" on which Joan Oates built her 1960 chronology ([Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)). The interaction zone is enormous — a phenomenon of the sixth and fifth millennia BC reaching from southern Iraq into northern Mesopotamia, the Arabian shores of the Persian Gulf (Ubaid 2–3, some Ubaid 4), and beyond ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf); [Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** The primary problem is not competing dates for a shared entity but that "the Ubaid period" denotes different things in different regions and different literatures. The same label covers **6500–3800 BC in the south** and **5300–4300 BC in the north**, a threefold difference in duration ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). Beneath that, the direction of spread is contested: the traditional model has northern communities adopting southern Ubaid styles in Ubaid 3 and 4, perhaps beginning in Ubaid 2, but Campbell & Fletcher's Domuztepe ceramics show Ubaid elements in the **first half of the sixth millennium cal BC** resembling **Ubaid 3/4 rather than Ubaid 1/2** — which the volume's editors concede "raises questions about the direction of influence" ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). Regional endings also diverge: at Surezha on the Erbil plain, LC-1 began about **4800 BC if not before** — at least **300 years earlier** than the 4500 BC LC-1 start in the Upper Khabur or the Balikh — so the Ubaid ended earlier there than elsewhere in the interaction sphere, and at ~4800 BC Ubaid pottery was still in use at Tepe Gawra near Mosul while it had already vanished from Surezha ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Ubaid period is the long stretch, from roughly 6500 to 4200 BC in southern Iraq, when irrigation farming, temples and a shared style of painted pottery spread across Mesopotamia and the Gulf, setting the stage for the first Sumerian cities.

### Requested note: Ubaid 0–4 sub-phases — are they securely dated?

**Short answer: the phase *names and sequence* are secure; the *absolute dates* attached to them are not, and the app should not present them as fixed.**

The phase scheme is stable and well attributed. Joan **Oates (1960)** used the long stratigraphic sequence from **Eridu** to build a four-phase Ubaid chronology; **Lebeau (1987)** added **Ubaid 0 (Oueili period)** at the front, and **Oates (1976: 26–28)** and **Forest (1996: 387)** added a Terminal Ubaid or **Ubaid 5** at the back ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)):

| Phase | Style / period name | Absolute date — status |
|---|---|---|
| Ubaid 0 | Oueili period (Samarran) | `n.a.` from a fetched primary source; span begins the c. 6500 BC southern sequence ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)) |
| Ubaid 1 | Eridu style and period | **begins c. 5750 BCE** ([Masaryk lecture](https://is.muni.cz/el/phil/jaro2013/PAPVA_49/um/39400680/Ubaid_Lec_1.pdf)); grouped as **Ubaid 1–2: 5800–5300 BC** ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)) |
| Ubaid 2 | Hajji Muhammad style and period | grouped as **Ubaid 1–2: 5800–5300 BC** ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)) |
| Ubaid 3 | Tell al-'Ubaid style and period | grouped as **Ubaid 3–4: 5300–4500(?) BC** ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)) |
| Ubaid 4 | formerly "Late Ubaid" | grouped as **Ubaid 3–4: 5300–4500(?) BC** ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)) |
| Ubaid 5 | Terminal Ubaid | **4500–4200 BC**, itself flagged "Terminal Ubaid?" ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)) |

**Why individual phase dates should be treated as insecure.** *Beyond the Ubaid* provides no separate absolute date for any of Ubaid 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 — it gives only the overall southern span (c. 6500–3800 BC) and the shorter traditional northern span (c. 5300–4300 BC) ([*Beyond the Ubaid*](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc63.pdf)). That is the most authoritative single fetched source in this section, and its silence on per-phase dates is itself the finding. Only the paired groupings Ubaid 1–2 and Ubaid 3–4 are datable from fetched primary material, and even the Ubaid 3–4 terminus carries a published question mark ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)).

**Recommendation for the app:** carry the Ubaid as **two or three coarse bands** (Ubaid 1–2, Ubaid 3–4, Terminal Ubaid) rather than six phases with sharp dates, and label the southern and northern spans separately. Hand off to the Uruk period at **4200 BC** ([Surezha annual report](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/ar/11-20/13-14/ar2013-14_Surezha.pdf)).

## 15. Jericho / Tell es-Sultan (West Bank) — the Neolithic tower and walls

**A. Start date.** The tower and the first perimeter wall ("Town Wall" I, Kenyon's Stage III) were constructed between **8300 B.C. and 7800 B.C.**, possibly around 8000 B.C. as Kenyon estimated. The single Stage III determination is **BM-105: 8300 ± 200 B.C.**, and the underlying "Proto-Neolithic" Stage I gives **BM-106: 8350 ± 200 B.C.** ([Bar-Yosef, "The Walls of Jericho: An Alternative Interpretation"](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). Occupation of the site is older: Natufian dates at Jericho extend back to **11170–10980 BC** ([Benz, PPND site entry for Jericho](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=36)).

**B. End date.** The wall and tower went out of use by approximately **7400–7300 B.C.** ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). PPND's independent reading: the tower was certainly in use until Jericho Stage VI (PPNA) and was abandoned at the latest during Stage IX (Middle PPNB), by which point fill almost reached its top ([Benz, PPND Jericho](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=36); [Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). The site itself continues into the historical period as a Bronze and Iron Age town — this entity's dates cover only the Neolithic tower-and-wall complex.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The published error terms are large and, at one point, extreme: **BM-105 8300 ± 200**, **BM-106 8350 ± 200**, **BM-250 8350 ± 500** (Stage V), **BM-251 7440 ± 150** (Stage VI), **P-378 7825 ± 110**, **BM-1327 7610 ± 65**, **BM-1322 7430 ± 85**, **P-379 7705 ± 84**, **BM-1323 7430 ± 85**, **BM-1787 7330 ± 100**, **BM-1321 7280 ± 80**, **BM-1326 7280 ± 220**, **BM-152 7330 ± 150**, **BM-110 8230 ± 200**, **BM-1789 7250 ± 70** ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). Note that the stratigraphy and the dates conflict: Stage V's 8350 ± 500 and Stage IX's 8230 ± 200 are *older* than Stage III's 8300 ± 200 despite being stratigraphically later. PPND's Bayesian-style summed ranges are: earliest PPN Stage IV/IVA **9300–8700 BC**; Stage VI/VIA **9150–9000 BC (11.6%)** or **8950–8450 BC (56.6%)**; Stage VIIIA **8620–8320 BC** ([Benz, PPND Jericho](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=36)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-uncalibrated** — this is the single most important caveat for this entity. Bar-Yosef presents the whole stage table as "available ¹⁴C dates," "¹⁴C readings" and "radiocarbon dates" in **B.C.** and **nowhere states that they are calibrated** ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). The internal evidence confirms they are raw: PPND's *calibrated* estimates for the same stages are systematically **500–900 years older** — Stage IV/IVA at 9300–8700 BC calibrated versus 7825 ± 110 / 7610 ± 65 uncalibrated, and Stage VIIIA at 8620–8320 BC calibrated versus 7330 ± 100 / 7280 ± 80 uncalibrated ([Benz, PPND Jericho](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=36) vs. [Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). **The widely quoted "tower built around 8300 BC" figure is therefore an uncalibrated radiocarbon date and is roughly 1,000 years too young in calendar terms.** PPND adds that **the tower's construction start date is unresolved and the Proto-Neolithic layers were never radiocarbon dated at all** ([Benz, PPND Jericho](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=36)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single 40,000 m² tell in the Jordan Valley near the Ein as-Sultan spring, West Bank. It is the **type site for both the PPNA and the PPNB**, and the source of the "Sultanian" industry label after Crowfoot-Payne's 1983 lithic reanalysis showed Kenyon's "Proto-Neolithic" was not distinguishable from other PPNA assemblages ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). The first perimeter wall stood **3.6 m high**, **1.8 m thick at the base** and 1.1 m at the preserved top; the tower is **8.2 m high**, ~9 m in diameter at the base and 7 m at the top, built of undressed stones with a 22-step dressed-slab internal staircase, no interior rooms, and an estimated mass of **~1,000 tons** ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The dispute is about **function, not date**. **Kathleen Kenyon** interpreted the free-standing wall and tower as a communal fortification, making Jericho a walled "town" comparable to later fortified Near Eastern towns, with Dorrell calculating the wall as roughly a week's work for 200 men. **Ofer Bar-Yosef** argues instead that the walls were built as a **defence system against floods and mudflows**, not enemies — the PPNA inhabitants lived beside a copious spring on a moderately sloping plain subject to mudflows, sheetwash, alluvial accumulation and wadi flooding, and responded by building a wall and then digging a ditch. His key evidence is differential wall thickness: during Stages IV–VI the wall was ~**3.5 m thick in the west** but only **1.4–1.6 m thick in the north and south**, consistent with a response to flowing water and uneven alluviation rather than to an enemy. He raises five problems for Kenyon: who the enemies were; why no new town walls were built immediately when the PPNA walls went out of use; why a mere terrace wall sufficed in the PPNB; why there is no record of any other fortified Near Eastern site until ~**5500 B.C.**; and why the tower was built *inside* the wall rather than projecting outside it where defenders could enfilade attackers. He reinterprets the tower as possibly publicly owned, at the service of the community, and a centre for **ritual activities** — noting Kenyon's own attribution of "ceremonial significance" to structures in Square FI immediately north of it — and predicts there was only **one** tower in the PPNA settlement ([Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). A third position, from **Ran Barkai and Roy Liran**, adds an archaeoastronomical function: computer reconstruction of ancient sunsets indicates that at summer solstice the shadow of nearby mountains struck the tower first and then spread across the settlement, which Barkai reads as making the tower a symbol of communal strength and cohesion during the adjustment to settled life ([Tower of Jericho, Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/tower_of_jericho)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kenyon's estimate for wall and tower | around 8000 B.C. | radiocarbon-uncalibrated + stratigraphic estimate | majority (as conventionally quoted) | [Bar-Yosef](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-1ce8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** At Jericho, around ten thousand years ago, villagers built a thick stone wall and an 8-metre stone tower with a staircase inside — the oldest structures of their kind anywhere — though archaeologists still argue over whether they were meant to stop attackers, stop floodwater, or serve as a ceremonial monument.

## 16. Ain Ghazal (Jordan) — PPNB site of the plaster statues

**A. Start date.** The oldest occupation phase dates to **8400–8000 BCE**, the time frame of a lentil seed (sample AA 1164) that the excavators assigned to the Middle PPNB ([Benz, PPND site entry for 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). Compare the Early PPNB horizon in the region generally: 8700–8200 cal BC in the Middle Euphrates, with Kharaysin in Jordan starting ~8500 cal BC ([Ibáñez et al. 2024](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/141768/1/Iba%C3%B1ez-2024-Early.pdf)).

**B. End date.** Occupation continues into the Pottery Neolithic. All the late dates significantly overlap **from 7500 BC onward to around 6700/6600 BC**, with one probably-too-recent date starting around 6600 BC treated as aberrant. A Yarmoukian sample from a clay-lined pit (AA983965, **6873 ± 64 BP**) provides the site's first Yarmoukian radiocarbon date ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The sequence resolves into two occupation phases with a short interruption: oldest phase **8400–8000 BCE**; a second phase from slightly after **8000 BCE to 7600 BCE**; a short hiatus **7600–7500 BCE**; reoccupation around **7510 BCE** ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). The hiatus has since been closed: until 2012, GrN 12971 was the only sample falling in the ~100-year gap, and in 2012 a domesticated cattle bone directly AMS-dated to **7607–7531 cal BCE (2σ)** attested continuous occupation (Zielhofer et al. 2012; Jacobsson 2017) ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). The **statue caches** are dated far less precisely and in raw radiocarbon: charcoal in immediate association with Cache 1 gave **6750 ± 80 B.C. (uncalibrated)** and **6710 ± 80 B.C. (uncalibrated)**, and charcoal beneath Cache 2 gave **6570 ± 110 B.C. (uncalibrated)**, making Cache 2 the later of the two ([Grissom, "Neolithic Statues from 'Ain Ghazal: Construction and Form," Smithsonian repository](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/73bd63c6-4d5d-42a0-a679-b530a8d81b88/content)).

**D. Dating method.** **radiocarbon-uncalibrated for the statues; mixed for the site.** PPND lists **61 ¹⁴C dates** and states explicitly that its Fig. 1 comparison uses **uncalibrated radiocarbon dates**, reported in BP with deviations; only the 2012 cattle bone is given as a calibrated BCE range ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). The statue dates are unambiguously raw: Grissom writes them as "6750 + 80 B.C. (uncalibrated)," "6710 + 80 B.C. (uncalibrated)" and "6570 + 110 B.C. (uncalibrated)," describes the caches as "solidly dated to the seventh millennium B.C. (uncalibrated)," and gives a single calibrated equivalent of **7580 ± 110 B.C.** — an offset of roughly **830 years** ([Grissom, Smithsonian](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/73bd63c6-4d5d-42a0-a679-b530a8d81b88/content)). The underlying AMS measurements on charcoal from between the tightly packed statues, run at the Oxford Radiocarbon Accelerator Unit, were **8700 ± 80 and 8660 ± 80 bp** (Hedges et al., *Archaeometry* 31, datelist 9) ([Tubb & Grissom, "The statues of 'Ain Ghazal"](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/9bda/1b5c6f2483da98ca4261c0243b3c7224e40d.pdf)). Also **typological** for the PPNB/PPNC attribution of individual layers, which is precisely where the problem lies (see F).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A single large settlement along the Zarqa River on the edge of the dry-farming zone in what is now suburban Amman, Jordan, coordinates **31°59′13″ N, 35°58′34″ E** ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). It is the type site for the **PPNC** (Final PPN) and the reference site for PPNB lime-plaster statuary. In its prime it spanned three major periods of cultural development: PPNB, the Final PPN or "PPNC," and the Yarmoukian phase of the early Pottery Neolithic ([Rollefson, "Neolithic 'Ain Ghazal (Jordan): Ritual and Ceremony, II," *Paléorient* 12/1](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_1986_num_12_1_4397)).

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** The radiocarbon dates cannot separate the site's two late aceramic phases. PPND states that **it is not possible to separate the latest pre-pottery Neolithic layers (PPNC) from the other late PPNB dates**: some PPNC dates significantly overlap the interval conventionally attributed to the late PPNB, some samples from late PPNB layers are too recent and fall in the PPNC range, and some aberrant dates are probably explained by old-wood effects or strays from more recent layers. Some samples simply do not correlate with the phases the excavators assigned them to. PPND frames the open question sharply: it should eventually be determined whether **PPNB and PPNC represent an actual chronological sequence, or whether two different social groups occupied 'Ain Ghazal, differing in material culture and burial rites** — i.e. whether the "PPNC" is a period at all or a contemporaneous community ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)). The stratigraphy PPND adopted is that of the excavators (Grindell 1998 and an unpublished Rollefson manuscript) ([Benz, PPND 'Ain Ghazal](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=10)).

## R6. Implementation notes for the app

1. **Do not store a single number for Jericho's tower.** Store the stage table with lab numbers and an explicit `calibration_status: "unstated"` flag. Any UI that renders "8300 BC" without that flag is asserting something the primary source does not.
2. **Ain Ghazal statues need two date fields**, uncalibrated (6750 ± 80 B.C.) and calibrated (7580 ± 110 B.C.), because both circulate widely and they differ by ~830 years.
3. **Still Bay and Howiesons Poort:** the app's existing SB 75.5–67.8 ka and HP 64.8–59.5 ka are OSL-based short-chronology values. They sit in the last third of an MSA that runs ~300–30 ka, i.e. they occupy roughly 8 kyr and 5 kyr respectively inside a ~270 kyr industry — under 5% of it combined. Store the Jacobs & Roberts 2017 maximum-likelihood alternative (SB ~73–70 ka, HP ~66–58 ka) and the Tribolo/Diepkloof long chronology (SB from 109 ± 10 ka) as competing entries, not as corrections.
4. **Ubaid should not carry a single phase table.** Carry the two spans that are actually defensible — southern ~6500–3800 BC and northern ~5300–4300 BC — plus a `phase_schemes[]` array holding Pournelle 2003, Harris 2021, Carter 2010 and Forest 1996 side by side with their disagreements visible.
5. **Green Sahara needs a `time_transgressive: true` flag.** Its termination is not a date; it is a north-to-south gradient running from ~8–7 ka in the north/east Sahara to ~5–3 ka at equatorial sites.
6. **Nabta Playa's alignment dates are retrodictions, not measurements.** They should live in a different field type from radiocarbon dates, or the app will imply an archaeometric precision that does not exist.
7. **PPNA/PPNB, Halaf sub-phases and Ubaid phases are typological boundaries first.** PPND itself warns that its three internal breaks correlate with steep slopes and plateaus in the calibration curve and may be **dating artefacts** rather than settlement history. Mark them `boundary_type: "typological-convention"`.
