**Reading the unit conventions used throughout:**
- `¹⁴C yr BP` = **uncalibrated** radiocarbon years before present (present = AD 1950). Not a calendar age.
- `cal BP` / `cal yr BP` / `cal ka` = **calibrated** radiocarbon, i.e. calendar years before AD 1950.
- `ka` attached to a luminescence, U-series or ESR age is **not** radiocarbon and needs no calibration — it is already a calendar-scale age with its own (usually larger) error term. Every such case is flagged explicitly in field D.

### 1. Beringia / the Bering Land Bridge (incl. the Beringian Standstill hypothesis)

**A. Start date.** The land bridge as a *continuous* dry connection is a late feature, not a long-lived one. The Bering Strait was **flooded (open marine) from ~46 ka until 35.7 ka**, meaning the land bridge re-formed only **after ~36 ka** — "within 10,000 y of the LGM," per the nitrogen-isotope and sea-level reconstruction of [Pico, Mitrovica and Mix, *PNAS* 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119). The Last Glacial Maximum itself is placed at **26.5–19 ka** in the same paper. For the standstill population, the divergence of Ancient Beringians from other East Asians is dated to **36 ± 1.5 ka**, and the Ancient North Siberian divergence to **39 ka (95% CI 45.8–32.2 ka)** ([PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119)).

**B. End date.** Final inundation. [Pico et al. (PNAS 2022)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119) place the most recent flooding **between 13 and 11 ka**, with the bridge subaerially exposed before 13 ka. The dedicated sea-level-fingerprinting study of the strait ([*Science Advances*, Pico et al.](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2935)) narrows the flooding event to **11 to 11.5 ka**, citing a **~11.5-ka-old** submergence horizon in sediment cores, a date of **10.9 +0.25/−0.24 (1σ) cal ka** measured 1 m above the JPC10 flooding surface, and a core 4PC-1 date of **10.9 +0.5/−0.2 (1σ) cal ka ago**. Present sill depth is **53 m**; LGM global mean sea level was **~130 m** lower.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Older credible limit for final closure: **13.3 ka ago**, inferred from marine-species dispersal, which would require an *earlier* strait connection ([*Sci Adv*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2935)) — note this is the opposite polarity, i.e. an *earlier* opening. Younger limit **~10.9 ± 0.1 cal ka** (bowhead whale bone, Dyke and Savelle, in the same paper). Age-model uncertainty on the Bering Strait open/closed reconstruction reaches **up to ±3 ka (95% CI)** ([PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119)).

**D. Dating method.** Mixed. (i) `radiocarbon-calibrated` — all the `cal ka` flooding-surface ages in [*Sci Adv*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2935), including terrestrial peat at **12.9 +0.08/−0.09 (1σ) cal ka** (site 85-69, Elias et al.). (ii) **A marine reservoir correction applies** to a substantial share of these: the shell dates (`12.6 +0.9/−1.1 cal ka` on shell in a mud clast from VBC03; *Macoma (Limecola) balthica* at `12.6 +0.1/−0.9 cal ka`; *Cyrtodaria kurriana* at `13.0 ± 0.1`, `13.3 ± 0.6`, `13.5 ± 0.1 (1σ) cal ka`) and the whale bone are all marine carbon reservoirs. (iii) `magnetostratigraphy` — not used. (iv) Molecular-clock genetics (not one of the spec's radiometric labels; treat as an independent line) for the standstill divergence figures.

For the **Beringian Standstill hypothesis** specifically, the founding evidence is molecular: [Tamm et al. 2007, *PLoS ONE*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1952074/) derive an mtDNA coalescence of **~13,900 ± 2,700 years ago** for the Beringian founder haplogroups using a substitution rate of **3.5×10⁻⁸ (SD 0.1×10⁻⁸) per year per position**, and infer an isolation interval **possibly up to 15,000 years**. Independent palaeoenvironmental support comes from sedimentary faecal biomarkers: a Lake E5 (Alaska) signal at **~32,000 years ago**, and Bluefish Caves material at **~25,000 years**, summarised by the [US National Park Service Beringia programme](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/standstill-theory-article.htm).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Beringia spans easternmost Siberia (Chukotka), the now-submerged Bering–Chukchi platform, and Alaska/Yukon. There is no archaeological type site for the land bridge itself, because the bridge is underwater; the reference localities are **sediment cores JPC10, JPC02, VBC03 and 4PC-1** in the Bering–Chukchi shelf ([*Sci Adv*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2935)) and, on land, **Bluefish Caves, Yukon** and **Lake E5, Alaska** ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/standstill-theory-article.htm)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** The physical existence and closing date of the bridge are well constrained; what is disputed is *how long* it was a habitable refuge and therefore how long the standstill lasted. The counter-argument to a long standstill is the demonstration by **Pico, Mitrovica and Mix** that the strait was *open water* from 46 to 35.7 ka, which compresses the available standstill window to roughly the last 36,000 years rather than allowing a much deeper Beringian occupation ([PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119)). Against the very early biomarker signals, the NPS summary itself frames the Lake E5 and Bluefish Caves evidence as suggestive rather than settled ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/standstill-theory-article.htm)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long standstill (mtDNA isolation) | isolation up to **15,000 y**; coalescence **13,900 ± 2,700 ya** | molecular clock | majority (as the framework), minority in its longest form | [Tamm et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1952074/) |
| Deep Beringian human presence | **~32,000 ya** (Lake E5 faecal biomarkers); **~25,000 y** (Bluefish Caves) | radiocarbon on sediment biomarkers | minority | [NPS Beringia](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/standstill-theory-article.htm) |
| Late bridge formation compressing the window | bridge only after **~36 ka**; strait open 46→35.7 ka | δ¹⁵N + sea-level modelling | majority | [Pico et al. 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2206742119) |
| Early final flooding | strait connection by **13.3 ka** | marine species dispersal | minority | [*Sci Adv*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2935) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** For part of the last ice age, so much water was locked up in glaciers that Siberia and Alaska were joined by dry land; that bridge only re-appeared about 36,000 years ago and the sea drowned it again around 11,000 years ago, and many researchers think the ancestors of Native Americans lived on it, isolated, for thousands of years before moving south.

### 2. Pre-Clovis Horizon (the general category of sites older than Clovis)

**A. Start date.** Not a culture, so "start" means the oldest age the horizon is currently accepted to reach. The synthesis by [Waters, *Science* 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) places the founder population's movement south of the ice sheets **between ~17.5 and ~14.6 ka** on genetic grounds, with dispersed occupation appearing **~15.5–14 ka**. The [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20253046/full) states that pre-Clovis sites which have withstood scrutiny fall at **"15,000 or maybe even 16,000 years ago."** A formal chronological definition puts pre-Clovis at **>13,400 cal yr BP** ([Ellis & Lothrop, *PaleoAmerica* 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)).

**B. End date.** The horizon ends where Clovis begins: **~13,050 cal yr BP** ([Waters, Stafford & Carlson 2020, summarised in PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33087355/)), or equivalently **13,400 cal yr BP** under the Ellis & Lothrop scheme ([*PaleoAmerica* 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Younger/conservative limit: [Fiedel 2022, *Radiocarbon* 64(4):845–897](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D) argues initial colonisation occurred only **after 14,500 cal BP**. Older limit: the same review acknowledges that recent finds "raise the possibility that pre-Clovis people reached the Americas **before 20,000 cal BP**," and catalogues far older claims — **24.0 kyr cal BP** at Vale da Pedra Furada (Boëda et al. 2021), **20,000 BC** in South America (Lahaye et al. 2013), and **32,000 years ago** at Guidon and Delibrias's Brazilian sites. [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) explicitly states that occupation **before ~17.5 ka is not supported** by current combined genetic and archaeological data.

**D. Dating method.** Predominantly `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the accepted sites — well-anchored examples include Page-Ladson, Florida at **14,550 years ago** (Halligan et al. 2016) and Cooper's Ferry, Idaho at **~16,000 years ago** (Davis et al. 2019), both catalogued in [Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D). Individual member sites add `luminescence` (Cactus Hill OSL, White Sands OSL). Genetic bracketing supplies the ~17.5–14.6 ka window. Note the mixed-unit hazard: the older literature this horizon draws on frequently reports **uncalibrated** ¹⁴C — e.g. Ellis & Lothrop pair **13,000 ¹⁴C yr BP** with its calibrated equivalent **15,340 cal yr BP**, a ~2,300-year gap ([*PaleoAmerica* 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Continental — North and South America. There is no type site by definition; the anchor sites are **Monte Verde (Chile)**, **Page-Ladson (Florida)**, **Cooper's Ferry (Idaho)**, **Paisley Caves (Oregon)**, **Cactus Hill (Virginia)** and **Manis (Washington, pre-Clovis mastodon hunting at 13,800 years ago, Waters et al. 2011)** ([Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D)).

**Relation to Monte Verde and White Sands (as requested; Monte Verde not re-researched).** Monte Verde is the *keystone* of the pre-Clovis horizon in its conservative form: at ~14.2 ka it sits comfortably inside the ~15.5–14 ka dispersal window that [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) reconstructs, and it is the site that broke the "Clovis-first" model without requiring anyone to accept a pre-LGM peopling. White Sands does the opposite: at ~23–21 ka it is roughly **8,000 years older** than Monte Verde and lies *outside* the genetic window entirely, which is precisely why it is contested rather than absorbed. The two therefore define the horizon's two rival shapes — a *short* pre-Clovis (Monte Verde-anchored, ~16–14.6 ka, mainstream) versus a *long* pre-Clovis (White Sands-anchored, pre-LGM, minority). The March 2026 Surovell et al. Holocene-age challenge to Monte Verde matters here because if Monte Verde falls, the short pre-Clovis loses its best-dated anchor while White Sands is untouched, inverting the evidential balance.

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The existence of pre-Clovis occupation is now mainstream; its *depth* is not. The counter-argument to a pre-LGM horizon is made by **Stuart Fiedel** ([*Radiocarbon* 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D)), who confines initial colonisation to after 14,500 cal BP, and by **Michael Waters** ([*Science* 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447)), who finds no genetic or archaeological support for anything older than ~17.5 ka.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short pre-Clovis (mainstream) | south of ice sheets **17.5–14.6 ka**; occupation **15.5–14 ka** | radiocarbon-calibrated + genetics | majority | [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) |
| Very short / late arrival | **after 14,500 cal BP** | radiocarbon review | minority | [Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D) |
| Pre-LGM long chronology | **before 20,000 cal BP**; **24.0 kyr cal BP** (Vale da Pedra Furada) | radiocarbon-calibrated / luminescence | minority | [Fiedel 2022 review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D) |
| Very deep Brazilian claim | **32,000 years ago** | radiocarbon (Guidon & Delibrias 1986) | superseded / rejected by most | [Fiedel 2022 review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** "Pre-Clovis" is the label for any American site older than the Clovis toolmakers; most archaeologists now accept a handful of sites around 15,000–16,000 years old, but claims stretching back 20,000 years or more are still fought over.

### 3. White Sands Footprints, New Mexico

This is the most contested chronology in the brief, so the dispute is laid out in sequence.

**A. Start date.** **~23 ka** — specifically, the original claim of human footprints dated to **~23–21 ka** by [Bennett et al., *Science* 373(6562):1528–1531, 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abg7586). The footprint-bearing trackway horizons TH2–TH6 span that interval. Note this is a *minimum* bound in the sense that older undated tracks may exist below, but 23 ka is the oldest dated horizon.

**B. End date.** The dated footprint sequence closes at **~21 ka**. The broader lake/wetland sequence within which the tracks sit runs from **>23.6 ka to ~17.0 ka cal BP** ([Holliday et al., *Science Advances* 2025](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adv4951)), with the track strata themselves at **~23.0–21.0 ka cal BP** in that same study — i.e. a critic of the *original* dating has published a stratigraphic sequence agreeing with the age range.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The original eleven *Ruppia cirrhosa* seed aliquots gave calibrated ages from **22.86 ± 0.32 ka** down to **21.13 ± 0.25 ka** (n = 11), modelled with OxCal 4.4.2 KDE_Model ([Bennett et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abg7586)). The 2023 independent redating produced three terrestrial conifer pollen ages of **23.4 ± 2.5 ka** to **22.6 ± 2.3 ka**, statistically indistinguishable from the seed ages at the same levels (**22.87 ± 0.30** to **21.13 ± 0.25 ka**), plus an OSL **minimum** age of **>21.5 ± 1.9 ka** ([Pigati et al., *Science* 382(6666):73–75, 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh5007) — full text also at [Berkeley Geography](https://geography.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/science.adh5007.pdf)). The maximum credible *younger* limit, if the critics are right, is roughly 7,000–10,000 years younger — see F.

**D. Dating method.** Three independent methods, and the distinction matters:
- `radiocarbon-calibrated` on **aquatic** *Ruppia cirrhosa* seeds (2021) — calibrated, but subject to a **freshwater / hard-water reservoir effect**, which is the whole controversy. All ¹⁴C in the 2025 stratigraphic study was calibrated with **CALIB 8.2 / IntCal20** ([*Sci Adv* 2025](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adv4951)).
- `radiocarbon-calibrated` on **terrestrial** conifer (*Pinus*) pollen (2023) — ~75,000 pollen grains per sample isolated by flow cytometry; terrestrial, so **no reservoir correction needed** ([Pigati et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh5007)).
- `luminescence` (OSL) on quartz (2023) — this age is **not radiocarbon**; the ">21.5 ± 1.9 ka" is an OSL minimum age on a calendar-scale timescale and requires no calibration ([Pigati et al. 2023](https://geography.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/science.adh5007.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** **White Sands National Park, Tularosa Basin, New Mexico, USA.** The type locality is the Alkali Flat trackway area on the margin of palaeo-Lake Otero; Tularosa Basin lakes existed **~36–19 ka** ([Bennett et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abg7586)). Published as a USGS Fact Sheet, [FS 2025-3046](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20253046/full).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The stratigraphy and the footprints are not disputed; the *dating material* is. The named counter-argument runs in three rounds:

1. **Original claim (2021).** *Matthew Bennett, Jeffrey Pigati, Kathleen Springer, David Bustos, Daniel Odess, Vance Holliday and colleagues* date the tracks to ~23–21 ka on *Ruppia cirrhosa* seeds, and pre-emptively argue that concordant terrestrial and aquatic ¹⁴C ages elsewhere in the basin over **~44–25 ka** show any hard-water offset persisted for less than a few hundred years ([*Science* 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abg7586)).
2. **The seed-dating objection (2022).** *David Madsen, Loren Davis, David Rhode and Robert Kelly* published a Technical Comment in *Science* arguing that *Ruppia* is a **submerged aquatic C₃ plant** that takes its carbon from the water column, not the atmosphere, and is therefore exposed to a **hard-water (freshwater reservoir) effect** that could make the dates **"up to ~10,000 years too old."** *Pigati and Springer* replied that the water was only a few centimetres deep at the track surface, that the Bonneville-basin groundwater analogy is irrelevant because the basins are **~1,500 km apart**, and that White Sands shallow groundwater ages are only a **few hundred to a few thousand years** ([*Science* comment and reply](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abm6987)).
3. **The 2023 re-dating.** *Jeffrey Pigati, Kathleen Springer, Jeffrey Honke, David Wahl, Marie Champagne, Susan Zimmerman, Harrison Gray, Vincent Santucci, Daniel Odess, David Bustos and Matthew Bennett* answered with two methods immune to the objection — ¹⁴C on **terrestrial** conifer pollen and **OSL** on quartz — and got statistically indistinguishable ages ([*Science* 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh5007); [USGS data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/data-release-independent-age-estimates-resolve-controversy-ancient-human-footprints-white)).
4. **The objection continues (2024–2025).** *David Rhode, Sarah Neudorf, Cody Rachal, Loren Davis, David Madsen and Robert Dello-Russo*, in *PaleoAmerica* 2024, argue the pollen ¹⁴C may be dating **older redeposited glacial-age conifer pollen** washed in from surrounding uplands, and state they do not regard the new estimates as resolving the question ([*PaleoAmerica* 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2024.2345979)). *Rachal and Dello-Russo* then argued in the *Journal of Archaeological Science* (2025) that δ¹³C values indicate reservoir offsets **"around 7000 years or more"** and that the *Ruppia* grew elsewhere and was **storm-transported** to the track surface ([*JAS* 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440325000810)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original *Ruppia* seed ages | **~23–21 ka** (22.86 ± 0.32 → 21.13 ± 0.25 ka) | radiocarbon-calibrated, aquatic — freshwater reservoir risk | majority (as re-confirmed) | [Bennett et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abg7586) |
| Independent redating | pollen **23.4 ± 2.5 → 22.6 ± 2.3 ka**; OSL **>21.5 ± 1.9 ka** | radiocarbon-calibrated (terrestrial) + luminescence | majority | [Pigati et al. 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh5007) |
| Hard-water inflation | ages **up to ~10,000 years too old** | critique of radiocarbon on aquatic plants | minority | [Madsen, Davis, Rhode & Kelly 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abm6987) |
| Reservoir offset quantified by δ¹³C | offset **~7,000 years or more**; *Ruppia* allochthonous | stable-isotope + taphonomic critique | minority | [Rachal & Dello-Russo 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440325000810) |
| Redeposited-pollen critique | pollen ages inflated by reworked glacial-age grains | critique of terrestrial-pollen ¹⁴C | minority | [Rhode et al. 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2024.2345979) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Fossil human footprints at White Sands appear to be about 23,000–21,000 years old, which would be thousands of years earlier than anyone had accepted people were in North America; critics say the seeds first used to date them soaked up ancient carbon from lake water and are misleadingly old, and although two completely different dating methods later gave the same answer, a group of researchers still isn't convinced.

### 4. Clovis Culture

**A. Start date.** **~13,050 cal yr BP**, from **11,110 ± 40 ¹⁴C yr BP** — the oldest of 32 radiocarbon ages from 10 Clovis sites ([Waters, Stafford & Carlson 2020, *Science Advances*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33087355/)).

**B. End date.** **~12,750 cal yr BP**, from **10,820 ± 10 ¹⁴C yr BP** ([Waters, Stafford & Carlson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33087355/)). Clovis appeared at the end of the **Allerød** and disappeared at the onset of the **Younger Dryas**.

**C. Uncertainty bounds and the compression history (as requested).**

| Stage | Uncalibrated range | Calibrated range | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-revision (traditional)** | **11,500–10,900 ¹⁴C yr BP** | **~13,325–12,975 calendar years ago** | [Waters & Stafford 2007](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/09/Waters-and-Stafford-Clovis-Dating.pdf); calendar equivalent from [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm) |
| **Waters & Stafford 2007 revision** | **11,050–10,800 ¹⁴C yr BP** | floating tree-ring: max **13,250–12,800 cal yr BP**, min **13,125–12,925**; Fairbanks: max **13,110–12,660**, min **12,920–12,760** | [Waters & Stafford 2007](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/09/Waters-and-Stafford-Clovis-Dating.pdf) |
| **Waters, Stafford & Carlson 2020** | **11,110 ± 40 – 10,820 ± 10 ¹⁴C yr BP** | **~13,050 – ~12,750 cal yr BP** | [Waters et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33087355/); range also quoted as **13,050–12,750 cal yr B.P.** in [Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D) |

The 2007 revision **reduced the beginning by 450 ¹⁴C years and made the end 100 ¹⁴C years younger**, collapsing Clovis to a duration of roughly **200 calendar years** ([Waters & Stafford 2007](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/09/Waters-and-Stafford-Clovis-Dating.pdf)). AMS precision at 11,000 ¹⁴C yr BP is about **±30 years**. Individual 1σ ages from their Table 1: Lange-Ferguson **11,080 ± 40**, Sloth Hole **11,050 ± 50**, Anzick **11,040 ± 35**, Dent **10,990 ± 25**, Paleo Crossing **10,980 ± 75**, Domebo **10,960 ± 30**, Lehner **10,950 ± 40**, Shawnee-Minisink **10,935 ± 15** ¹⁴C yr BP.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` in the modern treatment — but **the underlying determinations are reported uncalibrated**, and both the widely-quoted "11,500–10,900" and "11,050–10,800" figures are **uncalibrated ¹⁴C yr BP**. The [NPS Southwest science page](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm) still gives Clovis as "about 11,500–10,900 radiocarbon years BP" without an uncalibrated label — a live trap for anyone reading a mixed corpus. Secondary method: `typological` (fluted Clovis point morphology) for assigning sites to the complex. No reservoir correction applies — the samples are terrestrial charcoal, bone and organics.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Continental North America, with a Southwest concentration. **Type site: Blackwater Draw (Blackwater Locality No. 1), near Clovis, eastern New Mexico**, excavated from the 1930s ([Cambridge *Paleoindian and Archaic Periods in North America*](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)). Other key localities: **Lehner, Naco, Escapule, Murray Springs (Arizona)** and **Mockingbird Gap (New Mexico)** ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm)); **Anzick (Montana)**, where a Clovis-associated infant burial is dated to **12,900 cal BP** ([Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** The compressed ~300-calendar-year span is the working consensus. The residual dispute is about *what Clovis means* rather than when it was: the discovery of Western Stemmed Tradition points at Paisley Caves contemporaneous with or preceding Clovis led Jenkins et al. to argue the two were **"parallel developments, not the product of a unilinear technological evolution"** and that colonisation involved "multiple technologically divergent, and possibly genetically divergent, founding groups" ([*Science* editorial summary of Jenkins et al. 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443)). A second live argument concerns the *cause* of the terminal date — the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis (Firestone et al. 2007; Kennett et al. 2015 Bayesian boundary at **12,835–12,735 cal B.P.**) is contested by **Vance Holliday and colleagues (2020)** and by **Sun et al. (2020)**, who propose a volcanic origin for the geochemical anomalies at **ca. 12,900 cal B.P.**; Fiedel adds that there is **"no evidence of a dramatic human population decline after 12,800 cal BP"** ([Fiedel 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/initial-human-colonization-of-the-americas-redux/F4094CA23F4F87D0021CFBF63152597D)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional long Clovis | **11,500–10,900 ¹⁴C yr BP** (~13,325–12,975 cal) | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | superseded | [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm) / [Waters & Stafford 2007](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/09/Waters-and-Stafford-Clovis-Dating.pdf) |
| Waters & Stafford 2007 | **11,050–10,800 ¹⁴C yr BP** | radiocarbon-uncalibrated + tree-ring/Fairbanks calibration | superseded by 2020 | [Waters & Stafford 2007](https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/09/Waters-and-Stafford-Clovis-Dating.pdf) |
| Waters et al. 2020 | **13,050–12,750 cal yr BP** | radiocarbon-calibrated (32 ages, 10 sites) | majority | [Waters et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33087355/) |
| Clovis-contemporary Western Stemmed | WST at **11,070–11,340 ¹⁴C yr BP** | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | majority (as a finding), reshapes rather than redates Clovis | [*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Clovis is the famous fluted-spear-point culture of Ice Age North America; better dating has squeezed it into a very short window of roughly 13,050 to 12,750 years ago — perhaps only three centuries — instead of the thousand-plus years once assumed.

### 5. Folsom Culture

**A. Start date.** **12,845–12,770 cal yr BP (68% probability)**, or **12,910–12,750 cal yr BP (95%)** ([Buchanan et al. 2021, *PaleoAmerica*, "Bayesian Revision of the Folsom Age Range Using IntCal20"](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf), DOI 10.1080/20555563.2021.1890401).

**B. End date.** **12,400–12,255 cal yr BP (68%)**, or **12,430–12,125 cal yr BP (95%)** ([Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Duration **355–510 years (68%)** or **325–650 years (95%)**. The model rests on **37 reliable radiocarbon ages plus 4 new determinations** ([Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — explicitly **IntCal20 with OxCal 4.4 Bayesian modelling** ([Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf)). Also `typological` for complex assignment. **Caution:** the widely circulated NPS figure of **"10,900–10,200 radiocarbon years BP"** is **uncalibrated ¹⁴C** and is not labelled as such on the page ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm)). No reservoir correction applies.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** **Great Plains, Rocky Mountains and the American Southwest** ([Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf)). Type site: the **Folsom site, Wild Horse Arroyo, near Folsom, New Mexico** — the site's name is the culture's name, and it is the eponym referenced throughout the Buchanan et al. Folsom age-range paper. Folsom people were primarily bison hunters and are poorly represented in the Southwest relative to the Plains ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** The Bayesian IntCal20 estimate is the current standard, and it **superseded** the earlier **Surovell et al. 2016** estimate of a **~440-year** duration spanning **12,610–12,170 cal yr BP** ([Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf)). The disagreement is therefore internal to the same research community — Todd Surovell is a co-author on the 2021 paper that revised his own earlier figure — and is about calibration curve and model choice, not about the reality of the complex. The revision shifted Folsom's start about 235 years *earlier* than the 2016 model allowed.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buchanan et al. 2021 (current) | **12,910–12,750** to **12,430–12,125 cal yr BP** (95%) | radiocarbon-calibrated, IntCal20 + OxCal 4.4 Bayesian | majority | [Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf) |
| Surovell et al. 2016 | **12,610–12,170 cal yr BP**, ~440 y | radiocarbon-calibrated, earlier curve | superseded | [Buchanan et al. 2021](http://marcusjhamilton.weebly.com/uploads/2/5/5/3/25533140/buchanan_et_al_2021.pdf) |
| Legacy NPS figure | **10,900–10,200 ¹⁴C yr BP** | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | superseded | [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Folsom was the bison-hunting culture that followed Clovis on the North American plains, lasting roughly 12,900 to 12,100 years ago — a few centuries at most.

### 6. Paleoindian Period (North America) — overall bracket

**A. Start date.** **c. 13,200 cal BP** (equivalent to **11,200 ¹⁴C yr BP**) ([Cambridge, *Paleoindian and Archaic Periods in North America*](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)). An alternative regional scheme starts early Paleoindian at **13,000 cal yr BP** ([Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)). Widespread settlement is dated to **c. 13,000 cal BP** in the Cambridge chapter.

**B. End date.** **c. 11,450 cal BP** (= **10,000 ¹⁴C yr bp**) in the Cambridge chapter; **10,000 cal yr BP** as the end of late Paleoindian in [Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178). Does not continue into the historical period; it is succeeded by the Archaic.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The internal subdivisions give the practical bounds: pre-Clovis **>13,400 cal yr BP**; early Paleoindian **13,000–12,200 cal yr BP**; middle Paleoindian **12,200–11,600 cal yr BP**; late Paleoindian **11,600–10,000 cal yr BP** ([Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)). Climatic anchors: Allerød + Younger Dryas at **c. 12,850–11,650 cal bp** (Cambridge chapter); Younger Dryas **c. 12,900 to 11,600 cal yr BP**, with onset **c. 12,900 cal yr BP (= 11,000 ¹⁴C yr BP)** and terminus **c. 11,600 cal yr BP (= 10,100 ¹⁴C yr BP)** ([Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)). Agate Basin/Cody Complex, a late Paleoindian grouping, is given as **10,500 to 8,000 radiocarbon years BP** ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm)) — note that end date is later than the Cambridge Paleoindian terminus, an overlap artefact of regional schemes.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated`, with `typological` assignment of assemblages to sub-periods. The Cambridge chapter states plainly that **"all dates herein are in calendar years before present unless explicitly noted"** ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)) — but the same chapter and Ellis & Lothrop both routinely give uncalibrated equivalents in parallel, and the NPS page gives **only** uncalibrated figures ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm)). No reservoir correction applies to the terrestrial charcoal and bone samples that anchor the period.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** North America, from the southern margin of the ice sheets to Mexico; the Cambridge chapter covers the continent, Ellis & Lothrop the Eastern Great Lakes and Northeast. There is **no type site for the period as a whole** — it is a stage label. **Blackwater Draw, New Mexico** functions as the de facto reference locality via Clovis ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** The absolute dates are not seriously contested; the *category* is. The Cambridge chapter states that stage terminology used as a marker of cultural development is **"untenable in most areas,"** citing the Willey & Phillips (1958) and Griffin (1967) frameworks as the source of the problem ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)). Regional schemes also disagree on boundaries by several hundred years — compare the Cambridge start of 13,200 cal BP with Ellis & Lothrop's 13,000 cal yr BP.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cambridge continental bracket | **c. 13,200 – 11,450 cal BP** (= 11,200–10,000 ¹⁴C yr bp) | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf) |
| Ellis & Lothrop regional scheme | **13,000 – 10,000 cal yr BP** in three sub-periods | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority (regional) | [Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178) |
| Uncalibrated legacy usage | Clovis **11,500–10,900 ¹⁴C yr BP**; Agate Basin/Cody **10,500–8,000 ¹⁴C yr BP** | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | superseded | [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/swscience/paleoindian.htm) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** "Paleoindian" is the umbrella name for the earliest widely accepted inhabitants of North America, roughly 13,200 to 11,450 years ago, covering Clovis, Folsom and their successors.

### 7. Archaic Period (North America)

**A. Start date.** **c. 8,500 B.C.** in the Southwest ([NPS Archaic Period](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm)). Continentally, the Archaic follows directly on the Paleoindian: the Cambridge chapter treats Paleoindian and Archaic together as spanning **c. 13,000 to 3200 cal bp** (= c. 11,200–3000 ¹⁴C yr bp), so the Archaic component begins around **11,450 cal BP** ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **c. 3200 cal bp** (= c. 3000 ¹⁴C yr bp) in the continental scheme ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)). In the Southwest it persists much later — the NPS gives end dates **"as late as the first few centuries A.D.,"** specifically **as late as AD 500** ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm)). The Archaic does not run into the historical period; it is succeeded regionally by Woodland, Formative or Puebloan traditions.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Southwestern subdivisions: **Early Archaic 8,500–4,000 BC**, **Middle Archaic 4,000–2,000 BC**, **Late Archaic 2,000 BC – as late as AD 500** ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm)). Regional variation in the terminal date spans roughly **1,200 BC to AD 500**, i.e. up to ~1,700 years of disagreement depending on region. The environmental backdrop constrains the early Archaic: early Holocene dry conditions ran **until circa 8000 cal yr BP**, with a Great Lakes drought peak **between 9900 and 8200 cal yr BP** and a southern Ohio/Indiana vegetation transition at **circa 10,900–8200 cal yr BP** ([Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the continental bracket, with the Cambridge chapter's blanket statement that dates are calendar years BP unless noted ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)). **However, the NPS Southwest Archaic BC/AD figures do not state their calibration basis**, and Ellis & Lothrop's Great Lakes framework mixes conventions explicitly — e.g. the Lakes Stanley and Hough low stand at **circa 9900 and 7500 ¹⁴C yr BP (circa 11,300–8400 cal yr BP)** ([Ellis & Lothrop 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20555563.2016.1212178)). Also `typological` for tradition assignment. No reservoir correction applies to the terrestrial charcoal that anchors the period; some Great Lakes and coastal shell-midden work would require one, but no such correction is stated in the sources fetched.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Continental North America. No single type site — the Archaic is a stage, subdivided into named regional traditions: **San Dieguito-Pinto, Oshara, Cochise and Chihuahua** in the Southwest, with **Ventana Cave (Arizona)** cited by NPS as the key stratified locality showing the Paleoindian-to-Archaic transition ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm)).

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** As with Paleoindian, the argument is about the category rather than the numbers. The Cambridge chapter calls stage terminology as a marker of cultural development **"untenable in most areas"** ([Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf)) — the counter-argument being directed at the Willey & Phillips (1958) / Griffin (1967) stage framework. Compounding this, the NPS Southwest end date of AD 500 and the continental end date of 3200 cal bp differ by roughly 1,700 years, so any single "Archaic end" figure is regionally arbitrary ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continental (Cambridge) | Paleoindian+Archaic **c. 13,000 – 3200 cal bp** | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Cambridge chapter](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C86E356FC4CC78806D2E1B92E2A4F869/9781139017831c54_p923-942_CBO.pdf/paleoindian_and_archaic_periods_in_north_america.pdf) |
| Southwestern (NPS) | **8,500 BC – as late as AD 500** | radiocarbon, calibration status unstated | majority (regional) | [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archaic-period.htm) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Archaic is the long stretch after the Ice Age hunters — roughly 8,500 BC to somewhere between 1,200 BC and AD 500 depending on where you are — when people across North America adapted to modern climates with broader diets and, eventually, early farming.

### 8. Cactus Hill (44SX202), Virginia

**A. Start date.** **~18,000 years ago** for the deepest cultural level. The site's occupation is reported as falling **"between 18,000 and 20,000 years ago,"** with one white pine charcoal date of **~18,000 years ago** from the blade-like-flake level ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/)). The Virginia Department of Historic Resources states more conservatively that charcoal from the pre-Clovis deposits was radiocarbon dated to **"more than 15,000 years ago"** ([Virginia DHR, 091-5026](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/091-5026/)).

**B. End date.** The site is a stratified multi-component locality, not a single-period site: it contains **Woodland, Archaic and Paleoindian** deposits spanning **"the entire period of Native American settlement in Virginia prior to European contact"** ([Virginia DHR](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/091-5026/)). The Clovis component is dated to **~12,950 years ago** — the first Clovis date obtained in Virginia ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Older limit **~20,000 years ago**; younger/conservative limit **>15,000 years ago** ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/); [Virginia DHR](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/091-5026/)). **No published ± error terms for the Cactus Hill determinations were recoverable from the sources fetched** — see the audit section.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon` on white pine charcoal — **calibration status is not stated by either source fetched**, and the "18,000 years ago" / "more than 15,000 years ago" phrasings are ambiguous between conventions. Treat as **calibration-status-unknown, likely reported as uncalibrated or loosely converted**. A corroborating `luminescence` (OSL) soil date is reported ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/)) — that OSL age is **not radiocarbon** and needs no calibration, but its numerical value and error are not given in the fetched source. Additional support is stratigraphic: the Archeological Society of Virginia confirmed in 1996 that the pre-Clovis material lay **8 inches below a Clovis level** ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/)). No reservoir correction applies (terrestrial charcoal).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** The site *is* the locality: **Cactus Hill, on a sand dune above the Nottoway River, Sussex County, Virginia, USA**. Discovered in the mid-1980s, tested in 1988 by **Joseph M. McAvoy** of the **Nottoway River Survey**, and excavated **1993–2002**; listed on the **Virginia Landmarks Register on 03/14/2001** ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/); [Virginia DHR](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/091-5026/), which records **NRHP listing as "NA"**).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** Cactus Hill is not integrated into the mainstream pre-Clovis chronology. Its ~18,000-year date lies **outside** the ~17.5–14.6 ka window that [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) finds supportable, and Waters states that occupation before ~17.5 ka is not supported by current genetic and archaeological data — which is the substantive counter-argument. The interpretive problem is compounded by its association with the **Solutrean hypothesis** of **Dennis Stanford and Bruce Bradley (1998)**, which proposed a trans-Atlantic Iberian origin for early American toolmaking ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/)); that model is not accepted by most Americanist archaeologists. The site is also a sand-dune context, where vertical artefact movement and charcoal reworking are recognised concerns — though no source fetched states this critique in those terms for Cactus Hill specifically.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep pre-Clovis occupation | **18,000–20,000 years ago** | radiocarbon on charcoal (calibration status unstated) + corroborating OSL | minority | [Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/) |
| Conservative state-agency figure | **more than 15,000 years ago** | radiocarbon on charcoal | majority (as the defensible minimum) | [Virginia DHR](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/091-5026/) |
| Genetic-archaeological ceiling | nothing before **~17.5 ka** is supportable | radiocarbon-calibrated + genetics | majority | [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447) |
| Solutrean origin (interpretive, not a date) | trans-Atlantic Iberian source | typological | minority / largely rejected | [Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/cactus-hill-archaeological-site/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Cactus Hill in Virginia has a layer of stone tools sitting below a Clovis layer that may be 18,000 or even 20,000 years old, which would make it one of the oldest sites in the Americas — but the state's own heritage agency only claims "more than 15,000 years," and most specialists think nothing that old is securely established.

### 9. Paisley Caves (35LK3400), Oregon

**A. Start date.** **~14,300–14,500 cal yr BP** for the oldest human evidence. The oldest deposits at the site reach **ca. 16,000 cal yr BP** ([Jenkins et al., geochronology chapter](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf)); the oldest *human* coprolites are dated to **14,280 cal. BP** ([McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf)), reported elsewhere as **~14,300 cal yr BP** for the Gilbert et al. 2008 coprolites ([critical review, *Project MUSE* 554776](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/554776/summary)). The *Science* editorial summary gives **"nearly 12,500 radiocarbon years ago,"** stated on the same page as **"about 14,500 calendar years ago"** ([*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443)). A polished hand stone bearing *Equus* protein is associated with **14,525 cal. BP** ([McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The sequence **continues into the historical period.** The site's dated deposits range from ca. 16,000 cal yr BP **"to historic contact"** ([Jenkins et al.](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf)), with the artefact- and coprolite-bearing deposits spanning **12,450 to 2,295 ¹⁴C years ago** ([*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443)). No invented end date is offered.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Individual 1σ determinations on the key specimens: coprolite 185 at **12,290 ± 60** and **12,345 ± 55 ¹⁴C yr B.P.**; coprolite 283 at **12,125 ± 30 ¹⁴C yr B.P.**; basketry at **12,273 ± 56 ¹⁴C yr B.P.** ([Shillito et al. 2020, *Science Advances*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404)). Western Stemmed Tradition points come from deposits at **11,070 to 11,340 ¹⁴C years ago** ([*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443)) = **~13,000–13,300 cal yr BP** ([*Project MUSE*](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/554776/summary)) or **12,800–13,000 cal. BP** ([McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf)). Chronological control is excellent in volume: **203 ¹⁴C dates** in the Jenkins geochronology chapter, **more than 100** high-precision dates in the 2012 *Science* paper, and **285 radiocarbon dates** by 2020 ([Jenkins et al.](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf); [*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443); [Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404)). Sediment accumulates at **1 cm per 44–80 years**, and the Mazama tephra provides a fixed marker at **7,640 cal. BP** (eruption **ca. 7,600 cal. BP**), with sub-Mazama deposits spanning **ca. 7,640–16,190 cal. BP** ([Jenkins et al.](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf); [McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon` — **both conventions are in play and the sources are inconsistent**. The Jenkins geochronology chapter uses **conventional ¹⁴C dates with Cal Pal 1σ calibration**, i.e. `radiocarbon-calibrated` ([Jenkins et al.](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf)); but the headline determinations in [*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443) and [Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404) are quoted as **uncalibrated ¹⁴C yr BP**, and the *Science* page states no calibration procedure. Supporting methods: `tephrochronology` (Mazama ash) and **faecal lipid biomarker** analysis, which independently proves human occupation to **12,200 ¹⁴C yr B.P.** ([Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404)). Direct **ancient DNA** on the coprolites supplied the original claim, with **blind testing by an independent laboratory** confirming human DNA in pre-Clovis-age specimens ([*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443)). No marine reservoir correction applies, but there is a **fraction-dependent offset** that behaves like one: a camelid coprolite gave **12,125 ± 30 ¹⁴C yr BP** on the macrofossil versus **11,315 ± 25 ¹⁴C yr BP** on the water-soluble extract — an **~810-year discrepancy between fractions of the same specimen** ([Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** **Paisley Five Mile Point Caves, near the town of Paisley, Summer Lake basin, Lake County, south-central Oregon, USA** — a set of eight caves and rockshelters, of which three are the focus of most work ([McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf); [Jenkins et al.](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf)). It is the type locality for the Western Stemmed Tradition's early dating, not for a period.

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The dates themselves are among the best-controlled in the Americas; the argument is about whether the *human* attribution survives. The central problem, documented by **Shillito, Blong, Jenkins, Stafford, Whelton, McDonough and Bull (2020)**, is the ~810-year offset between macrofossil and water-soluble fractions of a single coprolite, which raises the possibility that soluble organics — including human-derived DNA and lipids — **migrated downward through the deposits** into older sediments; they answer it with faecal lipid biomarkers that are stratigraphically constrained ([Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404)). A published critical review notes that the deepest purported human coprolites at **~14,000–14,400 cal yr BP** predate the former consensus threshold for American occupation of **13,400 cal yr BP (11,500 ¹⁴C yr BP)** and predate Clovis at **11,550–10,800 ¹⁴C yr BP** ([*Project MUSE* 554776](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/554776/summary)). Unlike White Sands, Paisley is broadly accepted — it sits inside the mainstream ~15.5–14 ka window of [Waters 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat5447).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human coprolites (Gilbert et al. 2008) | **14,280 cal. BP** / ~14,300 cal yr BP | radiocarbon + ancient DNA | majority | [McDonough et al. 2012](https://mnch.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/McDonaugh_et_al_2012.pdf) |
| Lipid-biomarker minimum | occupation to **12,200 ¹⁴C yr B.P.** | radiocarbon-uncalibrated + faecal biomarkers | majority | [Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404) |
| Early Western Stemmed Tradition | **11,070–11,340 ¹⁴C yr BP** ≈ 12,800–13,300 cal BP | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | majority | [*Science* 2012](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1218443) |
| DNA-migration objection | soluble organics intrusive; oldest human ages unreliable | fraction-comparison radiocarbon | minority | [Shillito et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba6404) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** In dry caves in southern Oregon, archaeologists found human waste and basketry around 14,300 years old, plus a distinctive spear-point style as old as or older than Clovis — the main worry has been whether chemical traces of humans seeped down into older layers, which later chemical work argues against.

## PART II — OCEANIA

### 10. Sahul — the combined Pleistocene landmass of Australia and New Guinea

**A. Start date.** Sahul as a *landmass* existed through Pleistocene glacial lowstands; as a *human* region, initial entry of anatomically modern humans is placed **"around 65,000–50,000 years ago (or earlier)"** ([Bradshaw et al. 2021, *Nature Communications*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)). Their demographic-cellular-automaton modelling best supports **initial entry at 50 ka or 75 ka**, with a dominant northwestern Sahul Shelf entry possibly followed by a **New Guinea entry at 73 ka**. Modelled entry times were tested across **85–50 ka in 5,000-year increments**. The result is consistent with an African exit **63,000–90,000 years ago**.

**B. End date.** Sahul ceased to exist as a single landmass through progressive marine inundation. The **Tasmania land bridge was submerged between 62 and 46 ka** and again at the end of the Pleistocene, with **Bass Strait flooded ~12 ka** ([Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)). Tasmanian Aboriginal people were consequently isolated for **12,000 to 13,000 years** after the land bridges flooded ([Australian Human Rights Commission, *Bringing Them Home* educational timeline](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)). The Australia–New Guinea separation follows the same postglacial transgression; **no date for the final Torres Strait severance was recoverable from the sources fetched** — see audit.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Entry: **85 ka (oldest tested) to 50 ka (best-supported young option)**; the model brackets initial entry at 50 ka *or* 75 ka rather than resolving between them ([Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)). Whole-continent peopling took **156–208 generations (4,368–5,599 years)** at a wave-front speed of **0.71–0.92 km/yr**, using a generation length of **27.9 years**. A follow-up refinement is published as [Bradshaw et al. 2023, *Quaternary Science Reviews*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277379123000197).

**D. Dating method.** The Bradshaw entry window is **not** a direct date — it is a **demographic/spatial simulation** calibrated against archaeological ages, which themselves come from `luminescence` (OSL/TL, principally Madjedbebe) and `radiocarbon-calibrated` determinations at younger sites ([Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)). The land-bridge submergence dates are **sea-level / geomorphological reconstructions**, not radiocarbon. Critically: **the 62–46 ka and ~12 ka figures are calendar-scale ages, not ¹⁴C BP**. Nothing here rests on uncalibrated radiocarbon, and no reservoir correction applies.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Sahul comprises **mainland Australia, Tasmania, New Guinea and the intervening Arafura/Sahul and Bass shelves**, now submerged. The dominant modelled entry point is the **northwestern Sahul Shelf** (offshore Kimberley/Timor Sea), with a secondary **New Guinea** entry ([Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)). Functional type site for the earliest occupation: **Madjedbebe, Northern Territory** (entity 11).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Two incompatible chronologies coexist. The **archaeological "long chronology"** places arrival at **60,000–65,000 years** ago; the **genetic "short chronology"** places it at **~47 ka** ([ABC News, 29 November 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)). The short chronology's principal advocates are **James O'Connell and Jim Allen**, whose ~47 ka argument is set out in their 2015 review ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)). A 2025 mtDNA study of **2,456 samples** from Indigenous individuals in Australia and New Guinea, using a re-evaluated mutation-rate "correction curve" (Prof. Martin Richards), dates **both** migration routes to **~60,000 years ago** — narrowing the gap from the genetic side ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long chronology / modelled early entry | **65,000–50,000 ya (or earlier)**; best fits **50 ka or 75 ka**; NG entry **73 ka** | simulation calibrated on luminescence ages | majority | [Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/) |
| Genetic short chronology | **~47 ka** | molecular clock + archaeological critique | minority | [O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf); [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352) |
| Revised mtDNA (2025) | **~60,000 ya**, both routes | molecular clock with corrected mutation rate | emerging majority-compatible | [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** When sea levels were low, Australia, Tasmania and New Guinea formed one continent called Sahul; people reached it somewhere between about 65,000 and 47,000 years ago, and rising seas broke it into today's separate landmasses, cutting Tasmania off around 12,000 years ago.

### 11. Madjedbebe, Australia

**A. Start date.** **65,000 years ago** — occupation established "by 65,000 years ago," with the site's lowest artefact-bearing deposit dated by OSL ([Clarkson et al. 2017, *Nature* 547:306–310](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968); full text at [University of Washington](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). This is a **minimum bound** on human presence at the locality. Independently affirmed as **"at least 65,000 years old"** by the [Australian Human Rights Commission](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history).

**B. End date.** The site continues in use through the Holocene; the upper sequence is dated by **26 AMS radiocarbon ages** ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). It is on Mirarr Country and is not an abandoned site in the cultural sense — no invented end date is offered.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The published age for initial occupation carries an error of **±6 ka** (65 ± 6 ka on the lowest OSL-dated deposit — this figure is carried in the project's existing research file `prehistory-dating-research.md` §4 from the same Clarkson et al. 2017 source). Prior estimates from the **1989 excavation** placed the earliest artefacts **"between about 60 and 50 ka"** on TL and OSL of surrounding sediments, and the range of estimates for initial colonisation of Australia before this paper ran from **47 ka to around 60 ka** ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `luminescence` — **OSL on single grains of quartz** for the Pleistocene sequence, plus earlier **TL**; `radiocarbon-calibrated` (26 AMS ages) **for the upper sequence only** ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). **This is the classic "BP" trap:** the 65 ka figure is an **OSL age**, not radiocarbon, and needs **no calibration** — radiocarbon cannot reach 65 ka at all. No reservoir correction applies.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** **Madjedbebe (formerly Malakunanja II), western edge of the Arnhem Land plateau, Northern Territory, Australia; Mirarr Country; 12°30′S 132°53′E, ~20 m above sea level** ([Clarkson et al. 2017](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf)). It is the *de facto* type site for the earliest human occupation of Sahul.

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The OSL ages are not challenged as measurements; the challenge is that the **artefacts may have moved down** into older sand. **Martin Williams, Nigel Spooner, Kathryn McDonnell and James O'Connell** argue in *Geoarchaeology* 36(1):92–108 that **termite bioturbation and other post-depositional disturbance** could have displaced artefacts into deeper, older-dated sediments, decoupling the artefact age from the OSL age ([*Geoarchaeology*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gea.21822)). This dovetails with the **O'Connell and Allen** ~47 ka short chronology ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarkson et al. 2017 | occupation by **65,000 ya** (65 ± 6 ka) | luminescence (single-grain OSL) | majority | [*Nature* 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22968) |
| 1989 excavation estimate | earliest artefacts **~60–50 ka** | TL + OSL | superseded | [Clarkson et al. 2017](https://faculty.washington.edu/bmarwick/PDFs/Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_2017.pdf) |
| Bioturbation critique | artefacts intrusive; true age younger | critique of OSL–artefact association | minority | [Williams et al., *Geoarchaeology* 36(1):92–108](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gea.21822) |
| Short chronology | **~47 ka** for Australian arrival | archaeological + genetic review | minority | [O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Madjedbebe, a rock shelter in Arnhem Land, holds stone tools dated by light-based (not carbon) methods to about 65,000 years ago, making it the oldest known human site in Australia — though some researchers think termites and shifting sand may have carried the tools down into older layers.

### 12. Lake Mungo / Mungo Man (LM3) and Mungo Lady (LM1), Australia

**A. Start date.** Human presence at Lake Mungo by **50–46 kyr ago**; the burials themselves — **both Mungo III (LM3, "Mungo Man") and Mungo I ("Mungo Lady")** — date to **40 ± 2 kyr ago** ([Bowler, Johnston, Olley, Prescott, Roberts, Shawcross & Spooner 2003, *Nature* 421(6925):837–840](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/)). The National Museum of Australia gives the remains as **40,000–42,000 years old** ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady)).

**B. End date.** The burials are point events, not a period. Occupation of the Willandra system continued; lake-full and drier fluctuations occurred **50–40 kyr**, followed by sustained aridity **40–30 kyr** ([Bowler et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/)). Lake Mungo dried up as sea levels rose from **~14,000 BCE** ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)). Later human activity at the locality includes **~460 footprints found in 2003 and dated to 20,000 years ago** ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds and the revision history (as requested).**

| Stage | Age claimed | Method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial radiocarbon (1970s) | **28,000–32,000 years** | radiocarbon | superseded | [*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-dates-dawn-dream-time) |
| Refined (1980s) | **"at least 38,000 years"** | radiocarbon | superseded | [*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-dates-dawn-dream-time) |

| Intermediate estimates for Mungo III | **30 kyr**; then **42–45 kyr** | mixed | superseded | [Bowler et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/) |

The **Thorne et al. 1999** result implied colonisation during or before **OIS 4 (57,000–71,000 y)**; the authors themselves noted **minor uranium mobilisation** detected via Th/U and Pa/U ratios, a known weakness in U-series dating of bone ([Thorne et al. 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10330330/)).

**D. Dating method.** Current consensus rests on `luminescence` — **25 new optical (OSL) ages** on the Mungo lunette sediments ([Bowler et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/)). The superseded 1999 result combined `uranium-series` and `esr` on the skeleton with `luminescence` on the burial sediment and `uranium-series` on the calcitic matrix ([Thorne et al. 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10330330/)). **None of the 40 ± 2 kyr or 62 ± 6 kyr figures are radiocarbon** — they are calendar-scale U-series/ESR/OSL ages requiring no calibration. The **earliest** Mungo dates *were* radiocarbon and were badly wrong: the 28,000–32,000 y and "at least 38,000 y" figures are **uncalibrated radiocarbon**, and *Science* noted the structural reason — **radiocarbon cannot reach beyond about 40,000 years** ([*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-dates-dawn-dream-time)). No reservoir correction applies, but the radiocarbon underestimation at the ¹⁴C limit functions as an analogous systematic bias.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** The **Mungo lunette, Lake Mungo, Willandra Lakes Region World Heritage Area, south-western New South Wales, Australia**. Mungo Lady was found by **Jim Bowler in 1968**; **Mungo Man was found five years later** ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady)) — note the AHRC timeline gives **1974** for the complete Mungo Man skeleton, discovered just **300 metres** from Mungo Lady ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)), a minor discrepancy between institutional sources. Mungo Lady's remains were **returned to Country in 1992** and **Mungo Man repatriated in 2017** ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** The 40 ± 2 kyr OSL consensus has held for over two decades. The dispute is historical: **Alan Thorne (ANU)** and colleagues argued for 62 ± 6 kyr in 1999, which would have placed Australian colonisation in or before OIS 4; **Richard Klein (Stanford)** was among those who commented critically at the time ([*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-dates-dawn-dream-time)). **Bowler et al. (2003)** then overturned it with 25 new optical ages and explicitly listed the superseded figures ([*Nature* 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/)). Institutional sources have converged on ~40–42 ka ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady); [AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bowler et al. 2003 | burials **40 ± 2 kyr**; site occupied from **50–46 kyr** | luminescence (25 optical ages) | majority | [*Nature* 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12594511/) |
| Thorne et al. 1999 | LM3 **62,000 ± 6,000 y** | uranium-series + esr; OSL on sediment | superseded | [*J Hum Evol* 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10330330/) |
| Early radiocarbon | **28,000–32,000 y**, later "at least 38,000 y" | radiocarbon-uncalibrated | superseded | [*Science* news](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-dates-dawn-dream-time) |
| Institutional current figure | **40,000–42,000 years** | luminescence (following Bowler) | majority | [NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/mungo-lady) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Two ancient burials at dried-up Lake Mungo in New South Wales — a cremated woman and a buried man — are about 40,000 years old, and the site shows people were living there 50,000–46,000 years ago; a 1999 claim that Mungo Man was 62,000 years old was overturned by better sediment dating in 2003, and both individuals have since been returned to their community.

### 13. Aboriginal Australia — the continuous cultural tradition

**A. Start date.** **At least 65,000 years** ago. The Australian Human Rights Commission states that "according to the most recent archaeological evidence, Aboriginal peoples have been living in Australia for **at least 65,000 years**," anchored on Madjedbebe artefacts dated to **at least 65,000 years old** and placed at **63,000 BCE** in their timeline ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)). This is explicitly a **minimum bound**.

**B. End date.** **None — the tradition continues to the present.** See the assessment of the 1788 endpoint below.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Older limit **~75 ka** as a modelled possibility ([Bradshaw et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8085232/)); younger limit **~47 ka** on the genetic short chronology ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf)). The 2025 mtDNA revision converges on **~60,000 years ago** ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)). The historical trajectory of the estimate is itself instructive: from **~1,000 years** in the 1960s, to **16,000**, then **30,000** in the 1970s, then 40,000+, now 65,000 ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)).

A dense supporting series of dated sites, all from the [AHRC timeline](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history), fills the interval and demonstrates continuity: **Warratyi rock shelter** (found 2016 by Giles Hamm and Clifford Coulthard; 4,300 artefacts, 200 bone fragments; **46,000–49,000 years ago**; diprotodon bone and giant-bird eggs at **45,000–50,000 years ago**); **Sue O'Connor's hafted stone axe** with charcoal at **45,000–49,000 years old**; **Yunta and Olary petroglyphs** at **at least 43,000 years**; **Jordan River, Tasmania** (1,440 artefacts, **at least 42,000 years ago**); **Warreen Cave** at **35,000 years ago**; **Keilor** hearth charcoal at **~31,000 years ago**; **Cuddie Springs** grinding stones at **30,000 years** and artefacts at **30,000–36,000 years ago**; **King Tablelands** at **22,000 years ago**; **Kutikina Cave** at **20,000 years ago** (when ~10% of Tasmania was under glacial ice); **Ubirr** rock art at **18,000 years ago**; **Mitchell Plateau** paintings at **at least 16,000 years ago** (dated via surrounding wasp nests); **Salisbury Island** stone tools at **at least 13,000 years old**; **Rosemary Island** stone houses at **9,000 years**. The modern Australian climate was established by **8,000 BCE**.

**D. Dating method.** Composite. `luminescence` (OSL/TL) for the deepest horizons — Madjedbebe, Lake Mungo. `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the Holocene and late Pleistocene sites, though the AHRC timeline gives "years ago" figures **without stating calibration status** for most entries. `uranium-series` on limestone deposits — Warreen Cave cave paintings were dated via **limestone deposits** ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)). Molecular clock for the genetic estimates ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)). `calendar` for the post-1788 period. **The 65,000-year headline figure is an OSL age, not radiocarbon** — no calibration applies.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** The Australian continent including Tasmania and the Torres Strait. Reference site for the start date: **Madjedbebe, Northern Territory** ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Same fault line as Sahul: archaeological long chronology at **60,000–65,000 years** versus genetic short chronology at **~47 ka**, with the counter-argument to 65 ka made by **James O'Connell and Jim Allen** ([O'Connell & Allen 2015](https://www.bushwalkingholidays.org/pdf/O'Connell&Allen_2015_human%20arrival%20in%20Australia.pdf); framing in [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2025-11-29/sahul-aboriginal-australia-65000-genetic-evidence/106054352)), and the site-specific bioturbation objection to Madjedbebe made by **Williams, Spooner, McDonnell and O'Connell** ([*Geoarchaeology*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gea.21822)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

### Assessment of the app's stored bracket "65,000 BCE to 1788 CE"

**The start is defensible, but the notation is wrong.** The published figure is **65,000 years *ago*** (i.e. ~65,000 BP), not 65,000 **BCE**. The two differ by about 2,000 years — small relative to the ±6 ka error, so the practical impact is nil, but the label is technically incorrect. If the app is being precise, "65,000 BP" or "c. 63,000 BCE" is correct; note that the AHRC's own timeline in fact uses **63,000 BCE** for the Madjedbebe entry ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)), which is the correct BCE conversion. Substantively, 65,000 is the mainstream archaeological figure and is directly supported by an institutional Australian source, so it is a defensible headline value — but it should be labelled a **minimum bound** and flagged as contested by the ~47 ka short chronology.

**The 1788 end date is not defensible as an end date, and is arguably harmful.** Aboriginal Australian culture did not end in 1788; it is continuous into the present, and the AHRC source describes Aboriginal peoples as **"living in Australia for at least 65,000 years"** in the present perfect ([AHRC](https://bth.humanrights.gov.au/significance/historical-context-ancient-history)). The AHRC timeline itself continues past 1788 into the twentieth century — it is the *Bringing Them Home* resource, which exists precisely to document post-contact history. Three concrete problems:

1. **Factually wrong as a terminus.** Presenting 1788 as an end implies discontinuity that did not occur.
2. **It conflates a cultural tradition with a colonial event.** 1788 is the date of the First Fleet — a fact about British colonisation, not about Aboriginal culture. Encoding it as the end of Aboriginal Australia adopts the colonial frame.
3. **It is inconsistent with how the app presumably treats other living traditions.** A tradition that continues should be encoded as open-ended.

**Recommended encoding:** start **65,000 BP (minimum bound; contested, 47–65 ka)**, end **open / "continues to present"**, with **1788 CE** carried as a separate, clearly-labelled *event* marker ("British colonisation begins") rather than as the tradition's terminus. If the schema requires a numeric end, use the present year with an "ongoing" flag, never 1788.

**H. Plain-language summary.** Aboriginal Australians have lived on the continent for at least 65,000 years, making theirs the longest continuous cultural tradition known — and it has not ended, so 1788 marks the start of British colonisation rather than the end of Aboriginal Australia.

### 14. Lapita Culture

**A. Start date.** **3450–3350 cal. BP** for the appearance of Lapita pottery in the Bismarck Archipelago ([Specht, in *Terra Australis* 26, ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)). This revised Specht and Gosden's own 1997 estimate of **3300–3200 cal. BP**. Sites are present throughout the Archipelago by **3400–3350 cal. BP**. A later synthesis gives the possible beginning as **"3350 years ago"** ([Petchey & Kirch 2019, *PLoS ONE* 14(9):e0211990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)).

**B. End date.** Lapita persists in the west while dispersing east. The **end of Lapita** at sites on **Tongatapu, Ha'apai and Vava'u** is modelled at **2703–2683 cal BP (68% probability)** ([Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)). The transition to Polynesian Plainware follows, with thicker coarse-tempered pottery becoming dominant **after 2400 cal BP**. Kirch and Hunt's Melanesian Lapita horizon is bracketed as **"ca 1600–500 BC"** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Competing start estimates: **Kirch and Hunt** preferred **3550 cal. BP**; **Green and Summerhayes** preferred **ca. 3500 cal. BP**; **Specht** argues **3450–3350 cal. BP** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)). The **W-K2 volcanic event**, a key stratigraphic marker, is dated **3480–3200 cal. BP**. Dispersal-phase site ranges: **RF-2, Reef Islands** pooled at **3137–2826 cal. BP**; **Atanoasao, Malo (Vanuatu)** at **3237–2754 cal. BP**; **Bourewa, Fiji** (Wk-14237) at **3205–2889 cal. BP**; the marine-shell base of FEA at **3340–3000 cal. BP with ΔR = 0 ± 0** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)). At the eastern extreme, Lapita reached Tonga and Sāmoa at **2900–2750 BP**, with the Mulifanua combined shell-and-bone date at **2880–2750 cal BP (68%)** ([Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)).

**D. Dating method — and the reservoir problem, which is central here.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` throughout. **Denham, Bronk Ramsey and Specht (2012)**, *Archaeology in Oceania* 47:39–46, model the chronology in **OxCal v.4.1.5 with IntCal09**, reporting **95.4% and 68.2% probability ranges**, and take a decisive methodological step: they **exclude marine shell ages entirely**, because of **marine reservoir correction uncertainties, shell recrystallisation and old-shell effects** — noting that shell ages run **consistently c. 100–200 years older than plant-derived ages**. Bone ages are also excluded, and Specht's protocol additionally excludes plant dates with standard errors greater than **115 years** ([Denham et al. 2012](http://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Denham%20et%20al%202012.pdf)). Where shell *is* used, the **ΔR reference is Petchey, Green, Jones and Phelan (2005)** for Watom Island, PNG ([Denham et al. 2012](http://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Denham%20et%20al%202012.pdf)); Specht reports one FEA marine-shell base with **ΔR = 0 ± 0** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)).

[Petchey and Kirch (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/) take the opposite approach — their paper is titled "**The importance of shell**" — and model shell explicitly with period-specific ΔR values: a pooled ΔR for **3100–2600 BP**, and a second ΔR of **−160 ± 48 ¹⁴C years** applied to **2600–2250 cal BP**, a period complicated by a **"Radiocarbon Plateau" at ca. 2650–2350 cal BP** and by documented shifts in marine ¹⁴C linked to ocean circulation between **3,000 and 1,900 years ago**. Their To'aga (Ofu Island, Manu'a, Sāmoa) results include individual determinations such as Beta-25035 (mixed shell taxa, lower Layer V) at **3714–3549 cal BP**, Beta-25673 (Unit 1, Layer V) at **3475–3326 cal BP**, and charcoal ages Beta-35601 at **3257–2879 cal BP** and Beta-35603 at **2917–2382 cal BP**. A **U/Th date (2014–19)** on the boundary of the lowest cultural layer and palaeo-beach at Coconut Grove provides an independent `uranium-series` check.

**So: a marine reservoir correction absolutely applies to Lapita, and the two leading research programmes disagree on whether to correct it or discard the material.**

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Origin region: the **Bismarck Archipelago** — comprising **Manus, New Ireland, and East and West New Britain Provinces, Papua New Guinea** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)). Dispersal reaches the **Reef Islands/Santa Cruz (RF-2)**, **Vanuatu (Atanoasao, Malo)**, **Fiji (Bourewa)**, and eastward to **Tonga (Tongatapu, Ha'apai, Vava'u)** and **Sāmoa (Mulifanua, 'Upolu; To'aga and Coconut Grove on Ofu; Utumea on Tutuila; Jane's Camp on 'Upolu)** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf); [Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)). The eponymous type site is **Site 13, Lapita, Foué peninsula, New Caledonia**; the fetched sources treat **Watom Island, PNG** as the key reference locality for reservoir calibration and **Mulifanua, Sāmoa** as the eastern terminus marker.

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** Everyone agrees Lapita began in the Bismarcks in the mid-second millennium BC; the ~200-year disagreement over the start date is driven almost entirely by **whether marine shell dates are admissible**. **Denham, Bronk Ramsey and Specht** exclude them as unreliable, which yields the later start of 3450–3350 cal. BP ([Denham et al. 2012](http://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Denham%20et%20al%202012.pdf); [Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)). **Kirch and Hunt** — and subsequently **Petchey and Kirch** — retain shell with explicit ΔR modelling and get an earlier start of ~3550 cal. BP ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf); [Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)). A secondary dispute concerns the eastern end: **Rieth and Hunt** re-evaluated To'aga to **2500–2400 cal BP**, whereas Petchey and Kirch's preferred combined-ΔR model returns initial settlement at **2785–2607 cal BP (68% probability)** with occupation continuing **until 2216 cal BP** — a difference of ~300 years turning on reservoir treatment ([Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|

BP** | radiocarbon-calibrated, plant-only, IntCal09 + OxCal 4.1.5 | majority | [Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf); [Denham et al. 2012](http://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Denham%20et%20al%202012.pdf) |

Plain-language summary.** Lapita was the seafaring, pottery-making culture that spread out of the Bismarck Archipelago near Papua New Guinea about 3,400 years ago and reached Tonga and Samoa within a few centuries; dating it precisely is hard because much of the datable material is seashell, which absorbs older carbon from the ocean.

### 15. Polynesian Expansion into Remote Oceania

**A. Start date.** Initial colonisation of the **Society Islands** (the central East Polynesian staging area) at **A.D. ~1025–1120** ([Wilmshurst, Hunt, Lipo & Anderson 2011, *PNAS* 108(5):1815–1820](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1015876108). If the question is the start of Remote Oceanic settlement more broadly, the Lapita dispersal into Remote Oceania begins earlier, at **3250–3200 cal. BP** ([Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf)), with **Fiji first settled 2,950–3,050 years ago** and **Tonga/Samoa 2,700–2,900 years ago** ([Terrell 2011, *PNAS* commentary](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018804108)). **Samoa was first settled ~800 B.C. and then followed by an 1,800-year pause** before the East Polynesian pulse ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **A.D. ~1290**, closing the major dispersal pulse. After a gap of **70–265 years** following the Society Islands landfall, a major pulse settled the remaining East Polynesian islands at **A.D. ~1190–1290** ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). Island-by-island: **Marquesas A.D. 1200–1277**; **Rapa Nui A.D. ~1200–1253**; **Hawai'i A.D. ~1219–1266**; **New Zealand A.D. ~1230–1282**.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The estimate rests on high-precision recalibration of **1,434 radiocarbon dates**, with **Class 1 dates spanning A.D. 1025–1520 at 68% probability (n = 207)** and an inclusion window of **300–3,000 ¹⁴C years BP** ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). The rejected long chronology of **Spriggs and Anderson** placed the **Marquesas at A.D. 300–600** and the **central, northern and eastern archipelagos at A.D. 600–950** — the 2011 recalibration **shortens the regional colonisation sequence by up to 2,000 years** ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — the whole contribution of Wilmshurst et al. is a **reliability-classified recalibration**, sorting dates into classes by material and context and discarding unreliable samples ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). **A marine reservoir correction is a first-order concern** for the Pacific generally, and is the reason the Wilmshurst reliability classification excludes or downweights much marine-derived material; the ΔR machinery for the region is set out for shell in [Petchey & Kirch 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728074/), including the **−160 ± 48 ¹⁴C year** ΔR for 2600–2250 cal BP and the **"Radiocarbon Plateau" at ca. 2650–2350 cal BP** that flattens resolution in that window. All figures above are **calibrated**; no uncalibrated ages are quoted.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** **Remote Oceania**: from Fiji/Tonga/Samoa eastward across the **Society Islands, Marquesas, Cook Islands, Rapa Nui (Easter Island), Hawai'i and New Zealand** ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). No single type site; the **Society Islands** function as the reference staging region for the East Polynesian pulse, and **New Zealand** — where Wilmshurst's earlier work on rat-gnawed seeds set the template — as the best-dated terminus.

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** The short chronology is now dominant. The counter-argument is the **long chronology of Matthew Spriggs and Atholl Anderson**, which put the Marquesas at A.D. 300–600 and the wider archipelagos at A.D. 600–950 — and note that **Anderson is a co-author of the 2011 paper that superseded it**, which is a strong signal of resolution rather than standoff ([Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf)). The residual argument, raised in **John Terrell's** accompanying *PNAS* commentary, is about the earlier western sequence — Fiji at 2,950–3,050 and Tonga/Samoa at 2,700–2,900 years ago — and how the long Samoan pause should be interpreted ([Terrell 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018804108)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short chronology (current) | Society Is. **A.D. 1025–1120**; main pulse **A.D. 1190–1290** | radiocarbon-calibrated, reliability-classified recalibration of 1,434 dates | majority | [Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf) |
| Long chronology (Spriggs & Anderson) | Marquesas **A.D. 300–600**; other archipelagos **A.D. 600–950** | radiocarbon, earlier treatment | superseded | [Wilmshurst et al. 2011](https://faculty.washington.edu/plape/pacificarchaut12/Wilmshurst%20et%20al%202010.pdf) |
| Western Remote Oceania baseline | Fiji **2,950–3,050 ya**; Tonga/Samoa **2,700–2,900 ya** | radiocarbon-calibrated | majority | [Terrell 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1018804108) |
| Lapita entry to Remote Oceania | **3250–3200 cal. BP** | radiocarbon-calibrated, plant-only | majority | [Specht](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p61051/pdf/ch0327.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Polynesian voyagers settled the far Pacific astonishingly late and astonishingly fast: they reached the Society Islands around A.D. 1025–1120 and then, within about a century and a half, found Hawai'i, Easter Island and New Zealand — a picture that replaced older estimates putting the same events up to 2,000 years earlier.

## USABILITY NOTES FOR THE APP

Practical recommendations arising from this research, for the History & Prehistory period picker:

1. **White Sands must carry a visible contested flag.** It is the only entity here where a mainstream published position (Madsen/Rhode/Davis/Kelly and Rachal & Dello-Russo) holds that the headline date may be **7,000–10,000 years too old**. Storing it as a bare `23,000–21,000 BCE` misrepresents the state of the field. Recommend storing the range **plus** a `dispute: contested-evidence` marker and both the pollen/OSL confirmation and the redeposition counter-claim.
2. **Never store Clovis as a single number.** The uncalibrated ¹⁴C range and the calibrated range differ by roughly **2,000 years**. If the app stores `11,050–10,800` without the `¹⁴C yr BP` qualifier, it will render Clovis about two millennia too young. Store **~13,050–12,750 cal yr BP**.
3. **Lake Mungo needs a superseded-values field.** Three published figures (62 ± 6 ka, 40 ± 2 ka, and the old ~28–32 ka radiocarbon estimates) are all still in circulation in secondary literature. Store **40 ± 2 ka** as current and retain the others as explicitly superseded.
4. **Aboriginal Australia's end date should not be a hard `1788 CE`.** See the assessment in entity 13: the tradition is continuous and living. Recommend `1788 CE` be stored as a **colonisation-contact marker**, not a terminus, with the tradition's end modelled as open.
5. **Distinguish the two kinds of "BP".** Beringia, White Sands (partly), Madjedbebe, Lake Mungo and Sahul rest on **OSL/TL/U-series/ESR** ages reported in "BP" or "years ago" — these are **already calendar-equivalent and must not be calibrated**. Clovis, Folsom, Paisley Caves, Cactus Hill and the Paleoindian/Archaic legacy brackets rest on **radiocarbon**, which must be. Applying calibration to the wrong group is the single most likely data-entry error in this region set.
6. **Lapita and Polynesian expansion both depend on marine-reservoir decisions.** The published chronologies change by **100–200 years** (Lapita) or more depending on whether shell dates are included and which ΔR is applied. Store the plant-derived-only figures, which is what the current majority position uses.
