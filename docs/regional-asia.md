## EAST ASIA — JAPAN

### 1. Jomon period (overall span)

**A. Start date.** ca. **16,000 cal BP**. Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui state: "Most scholars agree that the date of the oldest pottery in Japan (ca. 16000 cal. B[P]) marks the beginning of the Jomon Period" ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017, PDF](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)). This is effectively a **minimum bound** on the period start: it is defined by the oldest *dated* pottery, so earlier pottery would push it back.

An alternative, lower start figure is published by the Japanese World Heritage consortium: "the Jomon period began approximately 13,000 BCE" (≈15,000 cal BP), with calibration status not stated ([Jomon Prehistoric Sites — About the Jomon period](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-culture)).

**B. End date.** Regionally diachronous, not a single line: "In Kyushu, the transition from the Jomon to the Yayoi Period may have occurred as early as 2900 cal. B[P] … but in northern Honshu … the end of the Jomon Period is likely to have been as late as 2400 cal. B[P]" ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)). A separate Bayesian study gives the period as "approximately 16–2.3 k BP (2380 BP in East Japan)" ([Radiocarbon / Cambridge — Bayesian modelling of Middle Jomon Kanto](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/optimal-bayesian-chronological-modeling-for-high-resolution-chronology-for-the-middle-jomon-of-kanto-region-honshu-island-japan-approaching-a-generational-scale/4B1717D6D82FFA1CF8A63E6B1EBCCC8D). The end **does** run into the protohistoric/historical sequence: the Jomon is followed directly by the agricultural Yayoi period, whose start is placed "approximately 2,400 years ago" ([Jomon Prehistoric Sites](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-culture)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The period boundaries are quoted as round "ca." figures without ± error terms in the fetched sources. The only published error terms recovered are on individual site determinations, e.g. Odai Yamamoto I charred residues at 12,680–13,780 BP on five potsherds, with two charred-wood dates of 7070±40 and 7710±40 BP ([Radiocarbon 43(2B):1129–1138](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-charred-residues-on-the-earliest-pottery-in-japan/D2DE167CFF67D9B361D5D2E3AF198BD8)). Regional Bayesian modelling in Kanto used 140 AMS dates for the Middle Jomon and 108 for the Late Jomon ([Cambridge/Radiocarbon Bayesian paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/optimal-bayesian-chronological-modeling-for-high-resolution-chronology-for-the-middle-jomon-of-kanto-region-honshu-island-japan-approaching-a-generational-scale/4B1717D6D82FFA1CF8A63E6B1EBCCC8D)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the modern framework — the sub-phase table is explicitly headed "Approximate dates (calibrated BP)" ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)). **However**, much of the working Japanese literature and the widely used regional chronology tables are `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`, and mix in `luminescence` (TL), fission-track and obsidian-hydration ages ([Keally, Jomon chronology tables](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)). The same paper demonstrates the trap directly by giving paired values for tephra markers: the Sakurajima Pt3 ash at "ca. 9400 uncal. bp or 10,600 cal. BP" and the Kikai Caldera eruption "at around 6400 uncal. bp or 7300 cal. BP" — a ~1,000-year offset in both cases ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)). Beneath the six-phase scheme, the actual archaeological subdivision is `typological`: roughly 46 pottery phases nationwide ([Cambridge/Radiocarbon](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/optimal-bayesian-chronological-modeling-for-high-resolution-chronology-for-the-middle-jomon-of-kanto-region-honshu-island-japan-approaching-a-generational-scale/4B1717D6D82FFA1CF8A63E6B1EBCCC8D)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** The Japanese Archipelago, from Kyushu to northern Honshu and Hokkaido; the period "follows the Paleolithic Period and precedes the agricultural Yayoi Period" ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)). There is no single type site — the name derives from cord-marked ("jōmon") pottery. The reference site for the period's *beginning* is **Ōdai Yamamoto I**, Aomori Prefecture ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)); other anchor sites include Higashi-Kurotsuchida (Kagoshima), Okinoshima (Chiba), Sannai Maruyama and Terano-higashi (Tochigi) ([same](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf); [Keally tables](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).

**F. Dispute status:** `method-conflict`.
The disagreement is not mainly about which sherds are oldest but about **which age scale is being quoted**. Uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP, calibrated cal BP, and BCE figures circulate side by side, producing apparent conflicts of 2,000–3,000 years: 16,000 cal BP ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)) versus "approximately 13,000 BCE" ([Jomon Prehistoric Sites](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-culture)) versus the uncalibrated ">13,000–8,500 BP" Tohoku Incipient bracket ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)). Compounding this, the counter-argument specifically raised by **Charles T. Keally** is that a portion of the Japanese Palaeolithic/early-Jomon dating record was compromised by fabricated finds and requires re-audit ([Keally, "fakery" pages](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Reports/Fakery2/fakery2.html)). The end date is also `definitional` in part, because the Jomon→Yayoi transition is 500 years apart in Kyushu versus northern Honshu ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Jomon begins ca. 13,000 BCE" (World Heritage consortium framing) | ca. 13,000 BCE (≈14,950 cal BP) | `radiocarbon-calibrated` status not stated by source | minority | [jomon-japan.jp](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-culture) |
| Uncalibrated regional brackets (Incipient Tohoku >13,000–8,500 BP) | >13,000 BP uncal. (≈15,000+ cal BP) | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` | superseded for period-level use | [Keally tables](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Jomon is a ~13,500-year-long hunter-fisher-gatherer period on the Japanese islands, conventionally started at about 16,000 calibrated years ago by the world's oldest well-dated pottery and ended between about 2,900 and 2,400 years ago depending on region.

Widely reproduced older regional tables are `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` plus TL/`luminescence`, fission-track and obsidian hydration ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Nationwide sub-periods, but they are **not synchronous across regions** — Keally's tables are organised region by region (Tohoku, Kanto, Kyushu, Ishikawa) precisely because the boundaries shift; the Kyushu Incipient bracket is 12,800–10,500 BP against a Tohoku bracket of >13,000–8,500 BP ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)). No type site per sub-phase.

**F. Dispute status:** `rival-chronologies`.
The six-phase national scheme coexists with region-specific schemes whose phase boundaries differ by 1,000–2,500 years; the counter-position, set out in **Keally's** regional tables, is that a single nationwide six-phase calendar is an artefact of convenience and that the Kyushu, Kanto and Tohoku sequences must be dated separately ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)). A worked illustration is a widely reproduced Ishikawa Prefecture table that gives the Early Jomon as 5200–3400 BC in calibrated BC columns, which does not map cleanly onto 7,000–5,300 cal BP ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Jomon is split into six named stages from Incipient to Final, dated in calibrated years before present, but the boundaries drift by up to two millennia between Kyushu, Kanto and northern Honshu.

### Requested note — earliest secure Japanese pottery (Ōdai Yamamoto I) and whether it is calibrated

**The earliest secure Japanese pottery date is ca. 16,000 cal BP, and it IS calibrated — but the underlying laboratory numbers are uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP and must not be quoted as calendar years.**

- The five AMS determinations on charred residues adhering to early Incipient Jomon plain pottery at Ōdai Yamamoto I (Aomori Pref.) are **13,480–12,680 BP uncalibrated**, equivalent to **16,140–14,920 cal BP** ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).
- The primary publication reports ¹⁴C ages of **12,680–13,780 BP** on the five potsherd residues, with two associated charred-wood dates of 7070±40 and 7710±40 BP; the assemblage is assigned to the Chōjakubō Culture ([Radiocarbon 43(2B):1129–1138](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-charred-residues-on-the-earliest-pottery-in-japan/D2DE167CFF67D9B361D5D2E3AF198BD8)).
- The site is bracketed as **ca. 15,000–16,000 cal BP** in the synthetic literature ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)).
- Stratigraphic support: the pottery-bearing horizon predates the Hachinohe Pumice, dated ~12,500–13,000 BP ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).
- The site's own heritage documentation gives only "around 13,000 BCE" and "potsherds from c. 15,000 years ago", with **no calibration status stated and no ¹⁴C BP figures at all** ([Odai Yamamoto Site — World Heritage Jomon Prehistoric Sites](https://jomon-japan.jp/en/learn/jomon-sites/odai-yamamoto)). The two charred-wood dates in the 7,000-BP range at the same site are also a live caution that not all the site's carbon is contemporaneous with the pottery ([Radiocarbon 43(2B)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-charred-residues-on-the-earliest-pottery-in-japan/D2DE167CFF67D9B361D5D2E3AF198BD8)).

**Verdict:** the *secure* figure to quote is **16,140–14,920 cal BP (calibrated)**; the raw **13,480–12,680 ¹⁴C BP is uncalibrated** and is the origin of the circulating "13,000 BCE" figure.

## EAST ASIA — CHINA

### Requested note (applies to entities 3–8) — calibration status and the Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project

**Calibration.** Chinese Neolithic culture dates are quoted inconsistently and the calibration status frequently is not stated. A review of radiocarbon practice in Chinese archaeology states plainly that "**Radiocarbon dates are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP**" while itself giving "Yangshao cultures ca. 6900 BP – 4500 BP" and "Longshan cultures ca. 4500 BP–3800 BP" ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). Institutional Chinese publications likewise give bare "BP" without a calibration label — e.g. "The Yangshao Culture … dates from ca. 6000–4500 BP" and "The Hongshan Culture … chronologically ranges from 6000 to 5000 BP" ([Institute of Archaeology, CASS PDF](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)). **Treat every bare-"BP" Chinese culture range in this brief as calibration-unstated, and therefore possibly uncalibrated.**

There is, however, a recent corrective: the 2025 Chinese archaeological radiocarbon databank calibrates **all** dates with **OxCal v4.4.4 + IntCal20**, records median, σ and 95.4% range, and covers determinations older than 1830 BP ([Qiu et al., *Scientific Data* 12:1665 — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/); [Nature journal record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05956-z)).

**Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project (夏商周断代工程).** Commissioned 1996, completed 2000; it produced more than 200 dates, improved liquid-scintillation-counting precision to 2–3% and AMS to ~5%, and yielded the dynastic figures Xia ca. **2070–1600 BC** and Shang ca. **1600–1046 BC** ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). It is described as the first national programme to combine radiocarbon, archaeology, textual sources and astronomy ([Qiu et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/)).

**It is contested, and the Neolithic culture dates in entities 3–7 are largely NOT products of it.** The XSZ Project's output is dynastic (Xia onward), not Neolithic; the Neolithic ranges above come from separate site-level radiocarbon programmes and from typological seriation. The XSZ Project itself has been criticised specifically on its **astronomical retro-calculation methodology** ([Informath critique, AH207a](https://www.informath.org/pubs/AH207a.pdf)) and on its **Western Zhou chronology** in a dedicated review of the project report ([Cambridge review of the *Xia Shang Zhou Duandai Gongcheng Baogao*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/78B7D45FE8B93DD70AB440B44A5FF2CA/S0362502824000026a.pdf/xia_shang_zhou_duandai_gongcheng_baogao_and_its_chronology_of_the_western_zhou_dynasty.pdf)). A magnetic-susceptibility-plus-Bayesian study places the Xia start at "∼2100 BC", close to but not identical with the XSZ figure ([Radiocarbon / Cambridge, Shangluo area](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)).

### 3. Peiligang culture

**A. Start date.** **9,000 cal BP** — the culture is dated "9000–7000 cal. yr BP" ([Zhang et al., PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). An institutional Chinese source gives the type-site period as **5,500–4,900 BC** with no calibration status stated ([China Culture — Peiligang Site](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)); the discrepancy is discussed under F.

**B. End date.** **7,000 cal BP**, with a Late Peiligang stage at 8,000–7,000 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). It does not continue into the historical period; it is succeeded in the Central Plains by the Yangshao ([Institute of Archaeology, CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf); [China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Nine ¹⁴C ages on charcoal-rich palaeosoil at the Tanghu site span 7,840–3,500 cal yr BP, with one stratigraphic outlier (F39) at 5,843±64 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). The published ± term on the culture boundaries themselves is not given; the ±64 yr figure is a single-sample error.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — the source labels the ranges "cal. yr BP" and states the dates were calibrated with **CalPal** software; the calibration *curve* is not named ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). The competing 5,500–4,900 BC figure has **no stated calibration status** ([China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)) and should be treated as unverified on that point. Framework is also partly `typological` (Early/Late Peiligang stages).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Type site is the **Peiligang Site**, at Peiligang Village of Xincun Town, **Xinzheng, Henan Province**, central Henan between Zhengzhou, Kaifeng, Xuchang and Luoyang; "Peiligang Culture is named after the Peiligang Site" ([China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)). Distribution is the Central Plains of Henan; the site is explicitly linked to "the development of the early Neolithic cultures in the Central Plains, as well as their relationships with the Yangshao Culture" ([same](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)). Key excavated site beyond the type site: **Tanghu** ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)).

**F. Dispute status:** `method-conflict`.
The two ranges differ by roughly 1,500 years at the start, and the difference is a units/calibration artefact rather than an archaeological one: 9,000–7,000 **cal yr BP** ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)) is ≈7050–5050 BC, whereas the Chinese institutional figure is 5,500–4,900 **BC** with calibration status unstated ([China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)). The counter-argument here is not authored polemic but the general methodological caution stated in the Chinese radiocarbon-practice review — that Chinese dates "are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP", so BC conversions of them are systematically too young ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type-site period, Chinese institutional figure | 5,500–4,900 BC | radiocarbon, calibration status not stated | minority | [China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Peiligang is the earliest well-defined farming culture of the central Henan plain, running roughly 9,000–7,000 calibrated years ago, with the oldest broomcorn millet in the sequence appearing around 7,800 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)).

End date.** **3000 BC** (Late Yangshao 3500–3000 BC) ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.662391/full)); alternatively ca. 4500 BP ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full); [CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)), ca. 2900 BCE ([PMC12218936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218936/)), or ∼2900 BC in the Shangluo Bayesian model ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)). Does not continue into the historical period; succeeded by Longshan ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)).

Internal subdivision: Early 5000–4200 BC (first phase 5000–4500 BC), Middle 4200–3500 BC, Late 3500–3000 BC ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.662391/full)); Middle Yangshao is independently given as 6,000–5,500 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Direct AMS on charred seeds returned **3314–2891 cal BC** (Beta Analytic) ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.662391/full)). At Zhanmatun, a Late Yangshao sample (H217) gave **5451–5054 cal BP**, with layer dates of 2706–2474 BC and 3132–2913 BC ([PMC12218936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218936/)). The Xipo cemetery mid-phase is placed at "5300 BP" with no error term and no calibration label ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** Mixed. Individual determinations are `radiocarbon-calibrated` where reported as "cal BC" / "cal BP" ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.662391/full); [PMC12218936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218936/); [PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). The Shangluo framework is Bayesian-modelled AMS ¹⁴C but does **not** state calibration explicitly ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)). The bare-"BP" culture ranges are **calibration-unstated and possibly `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`** ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full); [CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)). Phase subdivision is `typological` (painted-pottery seriation).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** "Principally distributed in Shaanxi, Henan, Shanxi, Hebei and Gansu in the Yellow River Valley" ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)). Type site is Yangshao village, Henan (the culture is name-derived); anchor sites recovered from fetched sources include **Xipo** (Lingbao County, Henan; 34 tombs, mid-phase) ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)), **Zhanmatun** ([PMC12218936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218936/)) and **Donglongshan / Zijing** in the Shangluo area ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)).

**F. Dispute status:** `method-conflict`.
The start date ranges from ∼4200 BC to ~5000 BC to ca. 6900 BP across fetched sources, a spread of ~1,000 years driven by whether the number is a calibrated calendar date, a Bayesian regional model, or a bare uncalibrated BP figure. The explicit counter-position is the methodological warning in the Chinese radiocarbon review that dates in this literature "are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP" ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)); the corrective programme is Qiu et al.'s recalibration of the entire Chinese archaeological ¹⁴C corpus with OxCal 4.4.4 + IntCal20 ([Qiu et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/)). Regionally, the Shangluo Bayesian model narrows Yangshao to ∼4200–2900 BC, materially later at the start than the 5000 BC convention ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shangluo-area Bayesian model | ∼4200–2900 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (Bayesian AMS; calibration not explicitly labelled) | minority (regional) | [Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8) |
| Chinese institutional bare-BP range | ca. 6000–4500 BP | radiocarbon, calibration not stated | majority in Chinese literature | [CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Yangshao is the great painted-pottery farming culture of the middle Yellow River, conventionally about 5000–3000 BC, though regional Bayesian modelling compresses it to roughly 4200–2900 BC.

### 5. Hongshan culture

**A. Start date.** **6,000 BP** — "The Hongshan Culture is distributed mainly in present-day southeastern Inner Mongolia and western Liaoning and chronologically ranges from 6000 to 5000 BP" ([Institute of Archaeology, CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)). A widely reported alternative places emergence at **6,500 years ago** ([Bastille Post / Xinhua-sourced report, Dec 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). UNESCO's tentative-list dossier gives its Hongshan sites as "6,000 – 5,000 years ago" ([UNESCO tentative list 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)).

**B. End date.** **5,000 BP** in the standard scheme ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf); [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)), but **revised to 4,800 years ago** by findings reported in 2025: "Previously it was believed that the Hongshan culture declined 5,000 years ago. Now, we see it lasted for at least 200 more years until 4,800 years ago" (Jia Xiaobing), on the basis of the **Zhengjiagou site** in Zhangjiakou, Hebei, "placed between 5,300 and 4,800 years ago by radiocarbon dating" ([Bastille Post 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** **n.a.** — no published ± error terms for Hongshan were recovered from any fetched source. UNESCO states only "Based on a carbon-fourteen dating analysis of the relics, as well as scientific examinations on the unearthed animal bones and plant remains, the sites could be dated back to 6,000-5[,]500 years ago" ([UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)).

**D. Dating method.** Radiocarbon, **calibration status not stated in any fetched source**. UNESCO says "carbon-fourteen dating analysis" and gives ranges only in "years ago", never using "cal BP", "BCE", "calibrated" or "uncalibrated" ([UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)). The CASS figure is bare "BP" ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)). The Zhengjiagou revision is attributed to "radiocarbon dating" with calibration status not stated ([Bastille Post 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). Given the review statement that Chinese dates "are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP" ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)), **Hongshan must be flagged as possibly resting on uncalibrated radiocarbon.** Stage division into three stages is `typological` ([Bastille Post](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Type site is the **Hongshanhou Archaeological Site**, on Hongshan Mountain (the culture is named from it), dated 6,000–5,500 years ago ([UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)). Range: "southeastern Inner Mongolia and western Liaoning" ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)); more precisely "the basins of Xar Moron River, Laoha River, and Daling and Xiaoling rivers north of the Yanshan Mountains", now extended westward and southward into Hebei ([Bastille Post 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). Principal ceremonial centre: **Niuheliang** (goddess temple, altars, stone mounds; a burial and sacrificial centre of the late Hongshan, 5,500–5,000 years ago; discovered 1981) ([UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/); [Bastille Post](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). Other sites: Weijiawopu (6,000–5,500 years ago) ([UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)), Dongshanzui ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)), Dongshantou (Hebei), Sanjia Dongbei (Liaoning), Yuanbaoshan (Inner Mongolia) ([Bastille Post](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
The end date was actively revised in 2025 on new evidence, not on re-analysis of old dates. The counter-argument to the long-standing 5,000-BP terminus is made by **Jia Xiaobing** and colleagues, who argue on the Zhengjiagou material that the culture did not decline at 5,000 BP but "moved westward and southward" and persisted to 4,800 years ago ([Bastille Post 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). Note also an internal inconsistency in the CASS text itself, which gives Hongshan as 6000–5000 BP in one place and then asserts that "The Hongshan, Liangzhu and Yangshao cultures … almost all existed in the chronological frame of 5500-4500 BP" ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extended Hongshan (Jia Xiaobing, Zhengjiagou) | 6,500 – 4,800 years ago; Zhengjiagou 5,300–4,800 years ago | radiocarbon, calibration status not stated | emerging majority (2025) | [Bastille Post](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture) |
| Classic scheme | 6,000 – 5,000 BP | radiocarbon, calibration not stated | superseded at the end boundary | [CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf); [UNESCO 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Hongshan is the jade-working, temple-building Neolithic culture of the West Liao River region, traditionally 6,000–5,000 years ago, whose end has just been pushed to about 4,800 years ago by new Hebei finds.

### 6. Longshan culture

**A. Start date.** Conventional: **2600 BCE** ([Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports / Bayesian Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). Bayesian-modelled: the Haidai analysis "places Longshan culture between approximately 2900–2500 BCE and 2100–1700 BCE", i.e. the *start boundary* itself is a 2900–2500 BCE probability range ([same](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). In the Shangluo area of central China the Bayesian figure is **∼2900 BC** ([Radiocarbon / Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)). A general Chinese-archaeology review gives ca. **4500 BP** ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). A reference encyclopedia gives development "from around 3000 BCE" out of early Dawenkou ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Longshan_Culture/)).

**B. End date.** Conventional **1900 BCE** ([Haidai Bayesian study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)); modelled end boundary **2100–1700 BCE** ([same](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)); Shangluo **∼2100 BC**, coinciding with the point where "Magnetic susceptibility values then decreased from ∼2100 BC when the Xia Dynasty started" ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)); ca. **3800 BP** ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). **Yes — it runs into the historical/protohistoric period**: "By around 1700 BCE the culture had morphed with other regional cultures into a wider Chinese Bronze Age culture", evolving toward the Shang dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BCE) ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Longshan_Culture/)), and the Longshan-period walled sites at Taosi, Wangchenggang and Xinzhai are used to argue that "the history of Xia period previously considered as legendary times actually is a reliable history" ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The Bayesian boundary ranges themselves are the uncertainty statement: start 2900–2500 BCE, end 2100–1700 BCE, computed with **OxCal 4.2**; the calibration curve is not named ([Haidai Bayesian study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). No per-date ± terms were recovered.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the modelled chronologies (Bayesian OxCal treatment of ¹⁴C) — though note that the Haidai paper does not print a calibration *curve* and the Shangluo paper "does not explicitly label any quoted BC range as 'calibrated' or 'uncalibrated'", using high-precision AMS ¹⁴C at Donglongshan and Zijing ([Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648); [Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)). The pre-existing culture sequence is `typological`, described as "existing unmodelled archaeological chronology" only "loosely supported by radiometric dating evidence" ([Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). The ca. 4500–3800 BP figures are calibration-unstated and possibly `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). `magnetostratigraphy` is not used, but magnetic susceptibility is used as a correlative proxy at Shangluo ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** "The culture is named after the Longshan site in Shandong province"; distribution "spread over the Henan, Shandong, and Shanxi provinces", with more than ten major sites; **Chengziya** has the earliest walls at the site group, averaging 6 m high ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Longshan_Culture/)). The Haidai (Shandong) region is the core sequence Houli → Beixin → Dawenkou → Longshan → Yueshi ([Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). Major Longshan-period walled centres: **Taosi** (Xiangfen, Shanxi), **Wangchenggang** (Dengfeng, Henan), **Xinzhai** (Xinmi) ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)); **Donglongshan** and **Zijing** in Shangluo ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)).

**F. Dispute status:** `rival-chronologies`.
The Bayesian re-analysis of the Haidai sequence pushes Longshan's start up to 300 years earlier than the conventional 2600 BCE and, more damagingly, finds that "The overlaps of Dawenk[ou], Long[shan], and Yueshi cultures **call into question the widely accepted linear cultural chronology of the region**" — that is, the counter-argument, made by the authors of that Bayesian study, is that the standard Shandong culture succession is not a clean sequence at all but a set of overlapping traditions ([Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)). Regionally the Shangluo model gives ∼2900–2100 BC ([Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)), also earlier than 2600 BCE. The terminus is additionally entangled with the contested Xia question, since Longshan-period walled sites are the primary archaeological argument for a historical Xia ([CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)), and the XSZ Project's dynastic dates are themselves criticised ([Cambridge review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/78B7D45FE8B93DD70AB440B44A5FF2CA/S0362502824000026a.pdf/xia_shang_zhou_duandai_gongcheng_baogao_and_its_chronology_of_the_western_zhou_dynasty.pdf); [Informath](https://www.informath.org/pubs/AH207a.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haidai Bayesian model (overlapping, not linear) | start boundary 2900–2500 BCE; end boundary 2100–1700 BCE | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (OxCal 4.2 Bayesian) | minority but methodologically strongest | [Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648) |
| Unmodelled conventional chronology | 2600–1900 BCE | `typological` + loose radiometric support | majority | [Haidai study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648) |
| Shangluo regional Bayesian | ∼2900–2100 BC | AMS ¹⁴C, Bayesian, calibration not explicitly labelled | minority (regional) | [Radiocarbon/Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Longshan is the walled-town, black-pottery late Neolithic of the Yellow River basin, conventionally 2600–1900 BCE, which Bayesian modelling stretches earlier and shows overlapping messily with its supposed predecessors and successors.

### 7. Liangzhu culture

**A. Start date.** **5,300 cal BP** — the culture is dated "5,300–4,300 years cal B.P." ([Liu et al., *PNAS* 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)), corroborated as "c. 5.3 ka BP" ([Zhang et al., PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)) and as 5,500–4,200 cal yr BP in one regional sequence ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). UNESCO gives the inscribed property as "about 3,300–2,300 BCE" ([UNESCO World Heritage List 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/)).

**B. End date.** **4,300 cal BP** ([PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114); [PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)), i.e. 2,300 BCE ([UNESCO 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/)). Does not continue into the historical period; the collapse is attributed to hydroclimatic catastrophe (see F/G).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The best-constrained figures are the speleothem uranium-series ages bracketing the collapse, each with explicit ± terms: a megadrought at **4423±42 yr BP**; pluvial transitions 4423±42 → 4404±38 and 4345±32 → 4324±30 yr BP; a dry interval 4554±54 – 4335±30 yr BP; with COPRA age modelling giving 2σ uncertainty of ~25 yr ([PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)). Flood sediment layers date to **4510±100** and **4365±80 yr BP** from combined ¹⁴C and single-grain OSL ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)).

**D. Dating method.** Three distinct methods, and the distinction matters:
- `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the culture and the hydraulic works: direct AMS on annual grassy plants inside the dams' sandbags plus charcoal, from 10 dams and 1 levee, run at Peking University and calibrated with **OxCal v4.3.2 + IntCal13**, Libby 5568-yr half-life ([PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)).
- `uranium-series` (²³⁰Th) for the speleothem collapse record. **These "yr BP" values are NOT radiocarbon and require no calibration** — they are already absolute ([PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)). This is exactly the "which kind of BP" trap: 4423±42 yr BP is a U-series age, not a ¹⁴C age.
- `luminescence` (single-grain OSL) combined with ¹⁴C for the flood sediment layers ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)).
- UNESCO's BCE figures are `calendar` presentations of the above ([UNESCO 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Yangtze Delta, Zhejiang. The type site and centre is **Liangzhu City**, ~300 ha (1.9 × 1.7 km), dated 4,900–4,600 cal BP, with levees ≥5 km long, 20–50 m wide and 2–7 m high, a 1,300-ha protective screen and a ~10,000-ha zone of influence ([PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)). The Maoshan paddy fields date to 4,900–4,600 and 4,600–4,300 cal BP ([same](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)). The predecessor sequence is Majiabang 7,000–6,000 → Songze 6,000–5,300 → Liangzhu 5,500–4,200 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)), with Shangshan far earlier at c. 10–8.5 ka BP ([PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)).

**F. Dispute status:** `largely-settled`.
Liangzhu is the best-dated entity in this brief: direct AMS on short-lived annual plants from within the dam construction fabric, calibrated with a named curve and software, cross-checked by independent U-series speleothem ages ([PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114); [PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)). The remaining tension is over the *cause* of collapse rather than the dates, and over the small offsets between the ¹⁴C dam chronology (dam construction terminating ~4.4 ka BP, hydraulic complexes built 5.3–4.7 ka BP) and the U-series megadrought at 4423±42 yr BP ([PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)). One minor definitional wrinkle: UNESCO's 3,300–2,300 BCE is ~50 years wider at the start than the 5,300 cal BP research figure ([UNESCO 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UNESCO inscribed-property span | ca. 3300–2300 BCE | `calendar` presentation of radiocarbon-calibrated work | majority (official) | [UNESCO 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/) |
| Regional sequence variant | 5,500–4,200 cal yr BP | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Liangzhu is a walled Yangtze-delta city-state of about 3300–2300 BCE with the world's earliest large-scale water-management system, and it is one of the few Neolithic entities anywhere whose dams are dated directly, to the century.

#### Requested note — 2019 UNESCO inscription and the dating of the hydraulic/dam system

**UNESCO 2019.** The site was inscribed in 2019 as "**Archaeological Ruins of Liangzhu City**", reference **1592**, spanning "about 3,300–2,300 BCE", in **four component areas**: the Yaoshan Site; the High-dam at the Mouth of the Valley; the Low-dam on the Plain; and the City Site ([UNESCO 1592](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1592/)). The inclusion of the high-dam and low-dam areas as distinct components is itself the formal recognition of the hydraulic system.

**Dam chronology (all `radiocarbon-calibrated`, OxCal v4.3.2 + IntCal13, Peking University):**

### 8. Jiahu (Henan)

**A. Start date.** **c. 9,000 cal yr BP** ([Zhang et al., PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)); the *Nature* report of the flutes states "Jiahu was occupied from 7000 BC to 5700 BC" ([Zhang et al., *Nature* 401:366–368, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/)).

**B. End date.** **c. 7,800 cal yr BP** ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)) / 5700 BC ([Nature 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** **n.a.** — no ± error terms for Jiahu determinations were recovered from the fetched sources. The two independent brackets (9,000–7,800 cal yr BP versus 7000–5700 BC, i.e. ≈8,950–7,650 cal BP) agree to within ~150 years, which is itself a useful consistency check ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/); [Nature 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — the site range is given explicitly in "cal yr BP", and the rice at Jiahu is dated c. 9,000–8,000 cal yr BP ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)). The *Nature* flute paper reports that the layers were radiocarbon-dated but the fetched record does not state calibration for its 7000–5700 BC figure ([Nature 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Jiahu is itself the site: Wuyang County, Henan, in the upper Huai River system, associated with the Peiligang cultural horizon of the Central Plains ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
The **dates** are largely uncontroversial; what is contested is the **interpretation** of two claims. (i) The flutes: six playable bone flutes made from red-crowned crane ulnae, with 5, 6, 7 and 8 finger holes, plus roughly 30 further fragments — the *Nature* report's claim that these are genuinely playable, tuned instruments rather than fortuitously perforated bones ([Nature 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/)). (ii) The "proto-writing": incised marks on tortoise shells and other objects have been proposed as ancestral to Chinese script, a claim widely regarded as unproven — **no fetched source in this session substantiates the Jiahu signs as writing**, so that specific claim is recorded here as `n.a.` for verification purposes.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nature flute-paper occupation span | 7000–5700 BC | radiocarbon, calibration not stated | majority (popularly cited) | [Nature 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16862110/) |
| Calibrated site span | c. 9,000–7,800 cal yr BP | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | majority (technical) | [PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/) |
| Jiahu incised marks as proto-writing | n.a. | n.a. | unverified in this session | `n.a.` |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Jiahu is a ~9,000-year-old village in Henan that produced the world's oldest still-playable musical instruments — tuned bone flutes made from crane wing bones — along with early rice and much-debated incised signs.

## SOUTH ASIA

### 9. Mehrgarh (Pakistan)

**A. Start date.** This is the most substantially revised date in this brief. The current directly-dated figure for the aceramic Neolithic (Period I) is **5250–4650 cal BCE**, with the burial ground beginning at **5223–4914 cal BCE** ([Mutin, Zazzo, Bondioli, Coppa et al., *Scientific Reports* 15:11345, 2025 — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/); [Nature record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-92621-5)). The classic figure, which this supersedes, was ca. **7000 BCE** (with some formulations reaching 8000 BCE) ([Nature news framing](https://www.nature.com/articles/d44151-025-00060-y); [Harappa.com summary](https://www.harappa.com/content/new-radiocarbon-dates-human-tooth-enamel-reveal-late-appearance-farming-life-indus-valley)).

**B. End date.** The aceramic Neolithic cemetery **ended 4769–4679 cal BCE**, giving a duration of only **186–531 years** — against the classic estimate of roughly three millennia ([Sci Rep 2025 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/)). Pottery-bearing Period IIA is "no earlier than ca. 4650 BCE", on three dates of 4704, 4494 and 4443 BCE ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/)). The site sequence continues into the later Neolithic/Chalcolithic and thus into the pre-Harappan sequence, but not into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** A Bayesian model of **23 radiocarbon dates on human tooth enamel** from aceramic Neolithic burials; raw determinations run from **6092±27 BP** (T514, Layer 1) to **5801±25 BP** (T105, Layer 9) ([Sci Rep 2025 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/); [preprint PDF](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/68551cb7-8fdb-463d-98ed-9dd114c12276/Mutin_New-radiocarbon-dates_2025.pdf)). The boundary estimates 5223–4914 and 4769–4679 cal BCE are modelled probability ranges, not point estimates.

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated`, Bayesian-modelled, on **human tooth enamel** — a material choice that is the technical heart of the revision, because enamel bioapatite avoids the inbuilt-age and diagenesis problems of the charcoal and bone-collagen samples underpinning the older chronology ([Sci Rep 2025 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Mehrgarh is the type site: Kachi Plain, Balochistan, Pakistan, at the foot of the Bolan Pass; it is on Pakistan's UNESCO tentative list ([UNESCO tentative list 1876](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1876/)). It is the reference site for the earliest farming and herding in the Indus borderlands ([Harappa.com](https://www.harappa.com/content/new-radiocarbon-dates-human-tooth-enamel-reveal-late-appearance-farming-life-indus-valley)).

**F. Dispute status:** `rival-chronologies`.
This is a live, unresolved clash between two dating programmes. The classic chronology, built by **Jean-François Jarrige** and the French mission, put the aceramic Neolithic start at roughly 8000/7000 BCE and gave it a ~3,000-year duration; **Mutin, Zazzo, Bondioli and Coppa** directly contradict this, arguing from 23 enamel dates that Period I is 5250–4650 cal BCE and lasted only 186–531 years ([Sci Rep 2025 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/); [Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/d44151-025-00060-y); [Harappa.com](https://www.harappa.com/content/new-radiocarbon-dates-human-tooth-enamel-reveal-late-appearance-farming-life-indus-valley)). If accepted, farming life in the Indus Valley appears roughly **two millennia later** than the textbook figure. Because the paper is from 2025, the classic chronology remains embedded in most secondary literature and should be shown as an explicit alternative rather than silently replaced.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic Jarrige chronology, aceramic Neolithic | ca. 7000 BCE (some formulations 8000 BCE), ~3,000-yr duration | `radiocarbon-calibrated` on charcoal/bone (older programme) | majority in secondary literature, but challenged | [Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/d44151-025-00060-y); [Harappa.com](https://www.harappa.com/content/new-radiocarbon-dates-human-tooth-enamel-reveal-late-appearance-farming-life-indus-valley) |
| Mutin et al. 2025 enamel chronology | Period I 5250–4650 cal BCE | `radiocarbon-calibrated`, Bayesian, human tooth enamel | new, methodologically strongest | [Sci Rep 2025 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Mehrgarh is the site that has long anchored the origins of South Asian farming at about 7000 BCE, but a 2025 programme of radiocarbon dates run directly on human tooth enamel argues its earliest, pottery-free village is only about 5250–4650 BCE and lasted a few centuries rather than three millennia.

#### Requested note — the dispute over the aceramic Neolithic start date

The argument that Mehrgarh's aceramic Neolithic is **younger than the classic 7000 BCE figure** is made explicitly by Mutin, Zazzo, Bondioli, Coppa and colleagues in *Scientific Reports* (2025). Their case rests on three points: (i) direct dating of **human tooth enamel** from the burials themselves rather than associated charcoal or bone collagen; (ii) a Bayesian model over **23** such dates, yielding a cemetery start of **5223–4914 cal BCE** and an end of **4769–4679 cal BCE**; (iii) a resulting duration of only **186–531 years**, which is incompatible with the multi-millennial Period I of the classic scheme ([PMC12000376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-92621-5); [preprint PDF](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/68551cb7-8fdb-463d-98ed-9dd114c12276/Mutin_New-radiocarbon-dates_2025.pdf)). The consequence stated by the authors and by the accompanying commentary is a **late appearance of farming life in the Indus Valley** ([Harappa.com](https://www.harappa.com/content/new-radiocarbon-dates-human-tooth-enamel-reveal-late-appearance-farming-life-indus-valley); [Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/d44151-025-00060-y)). A knock-on effect: Period IIA pottery is pushed to no earlier than ca. 4650 BCE ([PMC12000376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12000376/)).

### 10. Bhirrana (India)

**A. Start date.** Contested by a wide margin. The claim from the isotope study is that the Pre-Harappan Hakra phase at Bhirrana runs from **~9.5 ka BP**, that the base of the trench section at 355 cm is "equivalent to ~9 ka Pre-Harappan Hakra level", and that "the settlement at Bhirrana has been inferred to be the oldest (>9 ka BP) in the Indian sub-continent" ([Sarkar et al., *Scientific Reports* 6:26555](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). The directly reported earliest determination, however, is more modest: "At Bhirrana the earliest level has provided mean ¹⁴C age of **8.35 ± 0.14 ka BP (8597 to 8171 years BP)**" ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). The competing position is that the earliest occupation of the Ghaggar-Hakra basin is only **fourth millennium BCE** ([Archaeological Survey of India-hosted review PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The Bhirrana phase sequence given is Hakra ~9.5–8 ka BP → Early Harappan ~8–6.5 ka BP → Early Mature Harappan ~6.5–5 ka BP → Mature Harappan ~5–2.8 ka BP ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). So the site's occupation continues down to ~2.8 ka BP, i.e. **into the early historic period**. Note that this internal scheme is markedly older at every boundary than the conventional Indus periodisation quoted in the same paper: Early Ravi ~5.7–4.8, Transitional Kot Diji ~4.8–4.6, Mature ~4.6–3.9, Late (Painted Grey Ware) 3.9–3.3 ka BP ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** ¹⁴C: **8.35 ± 0.14 ka BP**, range 8597–8171 years BP. OSL on pottery: **4.8 ± 0.3 (1σ) ka BP**, range 5120–4520 years BP at 42 cm (Mature Harappan level); **5.9 ± 0.25 (1σ) ka BP**, range 6185–5695 years BP at 143 cm (Early Mature Harappan) ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)).

**D. Dating method.** Two methods, and the distinction is critical here. The paper uses "a compilation of **calibrated** radiocarbon dates of the charcoal samples and **OSL** dates of pottery"; the radiocarbon dates are explicitly described as `radiocarbon-calibrated`, though **no calibration curve or software is named anywhere on the page** ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). The OSL dates are `luminescence` — **their "ka BP" figures are not radiocarbon and are not calibrated, and must not be read as calibrated ¹⁴C ages**; they were run explicitly "to check the validity of the radiocarbon dates and the antiquity of the Bhirrana settlement" ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). Regional comparanda are "conventional and AMS radiocarbon dates" with **calibration status not stated for the individual dates**: Girawad Pit-23 at 6.2 ka BP, Mithathal Trench A-1 at 8.2 ka BP, Kalibangan TF-439 at 7.6 ka BP, Rakhigarhi S-4173 at 6.4 ka BP ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Bhirrana, Fatehabad district, Haryana, in the **Ghaggar-Hakra (Sarasvati) palaeochannel tract**; grouped with Kunal and Girawad as Hakra-culture sites ([ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)), and among the Sothi-Siswal and later Harappan sites of Haryana/Rajasthan alongside Kalibangan, Banawali, Siswal, Rakhigarhi, Farmana and Mitathal ([same](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
Two incompatible chronologies are in print, roughly 4,000–5,000 years apart at the start. Sarkar et al. present Bhirrana as ">9 ka BP" and the oldest settlement in the subcontinent, and go further: "the Harappan civilization originated during the 8–9th millennium BP", with the pre-Harappans inhabiting the Ghaggar-Hakra "from 9 to 7 ka BP" ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)). The counter-argument is stated bluntly in an ASI-hosted review, which credits **Shinde et al. (2011)** for dating Girawad to the late fourth millennium BCE and then says of Bhirrana: "**for Bhirrana some very early dates have been proposed, which, however are not consistent with fourth millennium BCE dates. Thus, the earliest phase of human occupation in the Ghaggar-Hakra basin can be traced to fourth millennium BCE**" ([ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)). The same review dates the Hakra culture generally to the mid-fourth millennium BCE on radiocarbon and stratigraphic correlation ([same](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)). Note that Sarkar et al.'s own OSL results (4.8 and 5.9 ka BP) are far younger than their ~9 ka claim and depend on extrapolating down the section to reach it ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shinde et al. / ASI-review position | earliest Ghaggar-Hakra occupation = fourth millennium BCE (~5 ka BP) | radiocarbon + stratigraphic correlation, calibration not stated | majority among archaeologists | [ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf) |
| Sarkar et al. 2016 "oldest in subcontinent" | >9 ka BP; Hakra phase ~9.5–8 ka BP | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (curve unnamed) + `luminescence` (OSL) | minority | [Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555) |
| Conventional Indus periodisation (same paper) | Early Ravi ~5.7–4.8 ka BP; Mature ~4.6–3.9 ka BP | radiocarbon | majority | [Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Bhirrana is a Haryana mound whose excavators and an isotope study claim occupation from more than 9,000 years ago — which would make it the oldest settlement in South Asia — but mainstream archaeological opinion places the earliest occupation of that river tract in the fourth millennium BCE, some four millennia later.

### 11. Early Harappan / Ravi phase

**A. Start date.** **c. 3300 BC** — Harappa Periods 1A and 1B, the Early Harappan/Ravi Phase, are tabulated as "c. 3300 - 2800 BC", and the earliest occupation is placed "from the earliest phase of occupation c. 3300 BC" ([Kenoyer, "The Ravi Phase: A New Cultural Manifestation at Harappa", PDF](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2000_The%20Ravi%20Phase%20A%20New%20Cultural%20Manifestation%20at%20H.pdf)). The paper also frames the broader interval as "the period between about 3500 and 2800 BC" ([same](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2000_The%20Ravi%20Phase%20A%20New%20Cultural%20Manifestation%20at%20H.pdf)). An independent figure for the Hakra/Ravi horizon at Harappa is **3700–2800 BCE**, attributed to Kenoyer 2013 ([ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Type site is **Harappa**, Punjab, Pakistan, on the Ravi River (the phase is named from the river). The phase is compared with contemporaneous manifestations at other Indus Valley sites "i.e., c. 3300-2800 BC" ([Kenoyer, Ravi Phase PDF](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2000_The%20Ravi%20Phase%20A%20New%20Cultural%20Manifestation%20at%20H.pdf)). In the eastern Ghaggar-Hakra domain the corresponding sites include Kunal, Bhirrana and Girawad ([ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)).

Second, the phase's dates conflict with the much older Ghaggar-Hakra chronology claimed for Bhirrana, where the "Early Ravi Phase" is given a wholly different bracket of ~5.7–4.8 ka BP in the conventional scheme and ~9–6.3 ka BP in the alternative older scheme ([Sarkar et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)); the ASI-hosted review's counter-position is that such very early dates "are not consistent with fourth millennium BCE dates" ([ASI-hosted PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82/uploads/2023/02/2023021535.pdf)). Separately, a shorter overall Harappan chronology has been argued in the literature ([Possehl-type "shorter chronology" paper, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17743929/)).

### 12. Lahuradewa (India) — early rice

**A. Start date.** The site's palaeoenvironmental sequence begins at **~10,899 cal yr BP** (pre-agricultural phase ~10,899 to ~7,451 cal yr BP; given elsewhere in the same paper as 10,999–7,451) ([Patel & Agnihotri, *Journal of the Palaeontological Society of India* 67(1), PDF](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). Wild rice is abundant from **~10 ka BP** ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). The archaeological occupation proper is younger — the excavated Neolithic-to-early-historic sequence is given as "Neolithic ~4450 BP to early historic ~2150 BP" ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The core sequence runs to the present: early-agricultural ~7,451–2,623 cal yr BP → deteriorating monsoon ~2,623–1,228 cal yr BP → modern ~1,228 cal yr BP to present ([Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). **Yes, it continues into the historical period** — the occupation sequence explicitly reaches "early historic ~2150 BP", and rice-only agriculture is recorded "after ~2600 BP" ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)).

**Rice dates specifically:**

| Claim | Date | Calibration as printed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earliest rice cultivation | ~8.3 ka BP / ~8.35 ka BP | bare "ka BP"; calibration not stated for these figures | [Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf) |
| Earliest agriculture (conclusion) | between ~8,830 and ~8,141 cal yr BP | **explicitly calibrated** | [Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf) |
| Cultivated diatoms appear | ~9,250 years ago | calibration not stated | [Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf) |
| Charred rice grains (Tewari et al. 2008) | 6409 BCE (**8359 cal BP**) | "radio-carbon dated"; cal BP given | [PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/) |
| Earliest exploitation of wild rice | c. 6000 BCE | method and calibration not stated | [PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/) |

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Six new AMS determinations, all with ± terms, e.g. LRD-27 at **9638±105** BP calibrating to **10,966±274 BP** (range 10,692–11,240 BP; 9290–8743 BC; median 9017±274 BC), and LRD-25 at **6196±112** BP → **7102±308** BP (6794–7410 BP; 5376–4844 BC) ([Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). Six earlier conventional LSC dates are recalibrated in the same table, e.g. BS-2211/LRD-27 at **9210±170** BP → **10,487±583** BP (8868–7954 BC) and BS-2215/LRD-26 at **8710±170** BP → **9838±383** BP (8271–7586 BC) ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). Bayesian uncertainties were estimated with **clam v2.4.0** in R ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated`, with the protocol fully stated: "Five AMS ¹⁴C dates and six earlier reported radiometric dates were calibrated using **Oxcal 4.2 (IntCal20)**", and the modern sample LRD-2 calibrated with **CALIBomb (IntCal20)** ([Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). Importantly, the authors report that "All six AMS ¹⁴C ages were found to be in excellent agreement with earlier reported radiometric ¹⁴C dates", so the revision is a recalibration and tightening rather than an overturning ([same](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)). No OSL is reported for Lahuradewa ([PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)). Caution: the headline "~8.3 ka BP" rice figure is printed as bare "ka BP" without a calibration label, even though the underlying chronology is calibrated ([Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Lahuradewa is the site: a lake-margin settlement in the **Middle Ganges plains, north India** (Sant Kabir Nagar district, Uttar Pradesh) ([PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)). Comparanda in the same region are Mahagara (c. 1800–1600 BCE) and Senuwar ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
The dates are not seriously disputed; the **botanical identification** is. Tewari et al. (2008) recovered charred rice grains radiocarbon-dated to 6409 BCE (8359 cal BP) and argued from grain length/width/thickness ratios that the rice was **domesticated** ([PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)). The counter-argument is made explicitly by **Dorian Fuller and colleagues (2010)**, who "noted that the morphometrics for these grains from Lahuradewa overlap significantly with those of wild grains, and have therefore argued that Lahuradewa could instead represent the beginnings of a long history of **cultivation of wild rice** that continues throughout the site's occupation" ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)). The stakes are large: on Fuller's reading there is "a significant gap (c. 4200 years) between earliest evidence for the exploitation of wild rice (Lahuradewa c. 6000 BCE) and earliest dated evidence for the utilisation of fully domesticated rice (Mahagara c. 1800 BCE)" ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fuller et al. 2010 — wild-rice cultivation, not domestication | c. 6000 BCE onward; full domestication only by c. 2000–1800 BCE | `radiocarbon-calibrated` dates, morphometric reinterpretation | majority among archaeobotanists | [PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/) |
| Tewari et al. 2008 — domesticated rice at 6409 BCE | 6409 BCE (8359 cal BP) | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/) |
| Patel & Agnihotri revised chronology | rice cultivation begins ~8.35 ka BP; earliest agriculture 8,830–8,141 cal yr BP | `radiocarbon-calibrated`, OxCal 4.2 + IntCal20, clam Bayesian | current best-constrained | [Patel & Agnihotri](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Lahuradewa in the middle Ganges plain has rice remains dated to roughly 8,300–8,800 calibrated years ago, making it South Asia's earliest rice site — but specialists disagree over whether that rice was domesticated or simply wild rice being harvested and encouraged.

## SOUTHEAST ASIA

### 13. Hoabinhian

**A. Start date.** Two answers, differing by ~20,000 years. The conventional Southeast Asian figure is that the oldest dates are "**no earlier than about 18,500 to 23,000 BP (non-calibrated)**", from Lang Rongrien (Anderson 1990) ([Forestier, Zeitoun et al., "Chronological perspectives for the Hoabinhian", *C. R. Palevol* 12(1), 2013, PDF](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)). Against this, Ji et al. report the **oldest Hoabinhian in Asia at 43.5 ka** from **Xiaodong rockshelter, Yunnan**, with calibrated ¹⁴C intervals including 42,695–44,085 BP and 43,500 BP ([Ji et al., *Quaternary International* 400, 2016, PDF](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)).

**B. End date.** The technocomplex persists "from the end of Pleistocene until some **3700 years ago**", the youngest secure date being Huai Hin HH3 at 3700±30 cal BP (Poz-10063) ([Forestier, Zeitoun et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)). Ji et al. instead put the terminus at the Neolithic transition "~6th millennium BC" ([Ji et al. 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)). It does not continue into the historical period, though the Log Coffin Culture in the same north-west Thai caves runs c. 350 BC–AD 1000 ([Antiquity — re-evaluating NW Thailand cave sites](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)).

**D. Dating method.** **Predominantly `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`** — this is the single most important flag in this brief. Forestier, Zeitoun et al. mark date after date "(non-calibrated)" ([Forestier et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)). `luminescence` (TL) is used at Tham Lod ([same](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)) and OSL/TL/IRSL at Steep Cliff Cave and Banyan Valley Cave ([Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)). Recent work is `radiocarbon-calibrated` to a stated standard: 27 new ¹⁴C plus 4 luminescence dates, all standardised to the Libby 5568-yr half-life and calibrated at 95.4% with **OxCal v4.4.2 + IntCal20** ([Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)). Definition of the technocomplex itself is `typological` (unifacially flaked "Sumatraliths" and related pebble tools).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Mainland Southeast Asia — northern Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Malaysia — and now extending into Yunnan, southern China ([Ji et al. 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)). **There is no formal type site.** Madeleine Colani defined the industry in 1927/1929 from roughly **60 caves in Hoa Binh province, northern Vietnam**; the Lang Kay rockshelter is named among them ([Forestier et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status:** `method-conflict`.
The reliability of the entire early Hoabinhian date corpus is directly challenged. **Higham & Higham (2009)** argue that the old radiocarbon dates are unreliable ([Forestier et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)), and the Antiquity re-dating programme vindicates that concern spectacularly at Spirit Cave: Gorman's conventional dates of 11,346±560 to 7,397±320 BP are replaced by new crab-dactyl AMS dates of 9839±30 (L1) to 8551±25 BP which come out **in reverse stratigraphic order**, with a freshwater reservoir offset of ~970 years requiring correction ([Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)). The most striking correction concerns rice: rice spikelets at Banyan Valley Cave, once treated as very early, date to **280±20 to 220±20 BP** ([same](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)). A second, separate dispute is the **"Hoabinhian Homeland" hypothesis (Dai 1988)**, revived by Ji et al. on the Xiaodong 43.5 ka dates, which would place the origin in Yunnan and roughly double the technocomplex's antiquity ([Ji et al. 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)). A third is definitional: at Tam Hua Pu, Sayavongkhamdy, Bellwood and Bulbeck suggested a Hoabinhian assemblage at 4000–3500 BP, but "no clear link" was established ([Forestier et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xiaodong / Yunnan "Hoabinhian Homeland" (Ji et al., after Dai 1988) | 43.5 ka; oldest interval 42,695–44,085 BP | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [Ji et al. 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Hoabinhian is a long-lived pebble-tool tradition of mainland Southeast Asian caves, conventionally spanning roughly 23,000 to 3,700 years ago, but most of its published dates are uncalibrated and the early end is disputed between ~23 ka and a much older 43.5 ka claim from Yunnan.

### 14. Ban Chiang (Thailand)

**A. Start date.** Current chronology: the earliest burial phase, **EP I, dates to 1600–1450 BC** (68.2% probability) ([Higham, Douka & Higham, *PLOS ONE* 2015 — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)). The old chronology had the site's early Bronze Age starting at **3600 BC** ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)).

**B. End date.** The site sequence runs into the Iron Age: later Bronze Age **EP V begins 890–835 BC**, and Iron Age **MP VII begins 760–505 BC** ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)). So yes, it continues into the protohistoric/early historical period.

**E. Geographic range + type site.** Ban Chiang, Udon Thani province, **northeastern Thailand (Khorat Plateau)**; it is the type site of the debate. The regional model incorporates Ban Non Wat, Non Nok Tha and Ban Lum Khao ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)).

**F. Dispute status:** `rival-chronologies`.
Two named, explicitly opposed models. **Joyce White and Elizabeth Hamilton** advance the **Long Chronology Model (LCM)**, placing the Bronze Age at Ban Chiang at **2000–1800 BC** on the basis of six AMS dates of organic temper in crushed potsherds plus one rice-phytolith date, with a Seima-Turbino origin for the metallurgy. **Charles Higham, Katerina Douka and Thomas Higham** advance the Short Chronology Model (SCM) at **1050–955 BC**, and argue the LCM's dating materials are systematically too old: unspeciated charcoal carries unknown inbuilt age, and crushed-potsherd clay contains old carbon ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)). The decisive internal evidence they cite is a single EP III pot at burial 47 that yielded **4810±90 BP from crushed-sherd organic temper but 2910±90 BP from rice fragments in the same vessel** — a ~1,900-year discrepancy ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)). White and Hamilton have published rebuttals ([Allard review PDF, ISEA Archaeology](https://iseaarchaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Allard-review.pdf); [White chapter PDF](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/bookchapters/2008_White.pdf); [Penn repository record](https://repository.upenn.edu/entities/publication/47ffa5e8-6c30-4a48-9549-4335ccb14a81)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White & Hamilton Long Chronology Model | Bronze Age 2000–1800 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` on organic temper in crushed potsherds + 1 rice phytolith date | minority | [PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/) |
| Gorman & Charoenwongsa 1976 | early Bronze Age from 3600 BC; Iron Age from ≥1600 BC | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated`-era charcoal dating | superseded | [PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/) |
| Solheim, Non Nok Tha | 4th millennium BC bronze | early radiocarbon | superseded | [PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Ban Chiang was once thought to show bronze metallurgy by 3600 BC, which would have made Southeast Asia an independent early centre; direct dating of the buried people themselves now places its Bronze Age at about 1050–955 BC, roughly 2,500 years later.

All figures from [Higham, Douka & Higham, *PLOS ONE* 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/).

**What caused the revision — three linked methodological changes:**
1. **Abandoning unspeciated charcoal.** Charcoal of unknown species carries an unknown "inbuilt age" (heartwood from a long-lived tree can be centuries older than the fire that burned it) ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)).
2. **Rejecting crushed-potsherd organic temper.** The clay used in the pottery contains old carbon, biasing results old. The demonstration case is burial 47's EP III pot: 4810±90 BP from crushed sherd versus 2910±90 BP from rice fragments in the same vessel ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)).
3. **Direct dating of human bone collagen** — 105 determinations at ORAU, Bayesian-modelled in OxCal v4.2.4 with IntCal13, dating the interred individuals themselves rather than associated organics ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)). In the popular account, Tom Higham re-dated pig bones and human bone at Oxford to break the deadlock ([Current World Archaeology — "Charles Higham: the dating game and the saga of Ban Chiang"](https://www.world-archaeology.com/world/asia/thailand/charles-higham-the-dating-game-and-the-saga-of-ban-chiang/)).

### 15. Niah Caves / Deep Skull (Sarawak, Borneo, Malaysia)

**A. Start date (age of the fossil).** The Deep Skull is now related to human activity **ca. 46,000 to ca. 34,000 years ago** ([Barker et al., *Journal of Human Evolution* 52:243–261, 2007 — PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/)). A second synthesis gives the period **c. 45–39 ka**, "and most likely around 37 ka", with an estimated geological age "of c. 37 ka" ([Curnoe et al., *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 4:75, 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)). The original claim, made in 1958–60, was an association with "radiocarbon dates of ca. 40,000 years before present" ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/)); the specific early charcoal date is **c. 39,600 ± 1,000 BP (GRO1339)** ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)).

**B. End date.** Not applicable to a single fossil. The West Mouth deposits contain later material including Neolithic burials, which is the source of the intrusion dispute (see F). The cave sequence continues into the Neolithic and later, and hence into the historical period at the site level ([Peter Brown, "Niah Cave Deep Skull"](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** The charcoal date carries an explicit **±1,000 BP** term (39,600±1,000 BP, GRO1339) ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)). The **uranium-series age on the cranium itself is ~35 kyr**, and the authors caution that it **may be an underestimate** ([Peter Brown](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)). The 46,000–34,000 and 45–39 ka figures are bracket estimates rather than single dates with ± terms ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/); [Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)).

**D. Dating method.** Three methods, and the "BP" units differ in kind:
- `radiocarbon-calibrated` / AMS with three pre-treatments including **ABOX** (acid-base-wet oxidation) on charcoal from sediments adjacent to the skull ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/)).
- `uranium-series` **directly on bone fragments of the Deep Skull itself**, using the **Diffusion-Absorption model** ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/)); reported as ~35 kyr ([Peter Brown](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)). **This is not a radiocarbon age and requires no calibration** — a U-series "kyr" is already an absolute age.
- The 39,600±1,000 BP charcoal figure is a ¹⁴C age whose **calibration status is not stated** by the source that reports it ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)); treat it as possibly uncalibrated. Note that a ~39.6 ka uncalibrated ¹⁴C age would calibrate substantially older, which is consistent with the 45–39 ka bracket.
- Supporting `typological`/stratigraphic work: detailed re-excavation and chronostratigraphy of the West Mouth deposits ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** The **Great Cave of Niah**, West Mouth, Niah National Park, **Sarawak, Malaysian Borneo**. This is the find site of the Deep Skull, "the oldest anatomically modern human recovered from island Southeast Asia" ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
The core question for fifty years was whether the Deep Skull was **intrusive** — a much later Neolithic burial that had sunk or been dug into Pleistocene deposits. That argument was made by **Kenneth Kennedy (1979)**, **Milford Wolpoff (1999)** and **Wilhelm Solheim (1983)**, and it had real force because the Harrissons excavated the cave from 1954 onward without formal archaeological training and left the deposits disturbed ([Peter Brown](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)). Barker et al.'s re-investigation shows the skull is **not** intrusive and is of Late Pleistocene age ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/); [Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)), so the dispute is now largely resolved in favour of Pleistocene antiquity — but a residual method question remains, since the direct U-series age on the cranium (~35 kyr) is younger than the charcoal-based bracket and its own authors flag it as a possible underestimate ([Peter Brown](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)). A secondary dispute concerns the individual: Brothwell (1960) estimated 15–17 years of age at death, whereas Brown re-estimates 30–40 years from tooth wear ([same](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)).

**Attributed hominin species: anatomically modern *Homo sapiens*** ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intrusive Holocene/Neolithic burial (Kennedy 1979; Solheim 1983; Wolpoff 1999) | Holocene, Neolithic | stratigraphic / typological argument, no independent absolute date | superseded | [peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html) |
| Direct U-series age on the cranium itself | ~35 kyr (its own authors say it may be an underestimate) | `uranium-series` (Diffusion-Absorption model on bone) | minority — younger than the charcoal-based bracket | [peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html) |
| Original 1958–60 association | ca. 40,000 BP | `radiocarbon` on charcoal, calibration status not stated | superseded by the 2007 re-dating | [Barker et al. 2007, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The Deep Skull is the oldest anatomically modern human skull from island Southeast Asia; after fifty years of arguing about whether it was a much later burial that had sunk into old deposits, re-excavation plus uranium-series dating of the bone itself placed it firmly in the Late Pleistocene, roughly 46,000–34,000 years ago ([Barker et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17161859/); [Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)).

### 16. Earliest rice domestication in the Yangtze (a process, not a site)

**A. Start date.** Domestication is bracketed rather than pinned. Phytolith evidence indicates that "domestication was underway between **12,500 and 7,500 BP**", and molecular-clock work brackets the domestication of *Oryza sativa* to **13,500–8,200 BP** ([Zheng, Fuller et al., *Scientific Reports* 6:28136](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). The earliest anchor site, **Shangshan**, is dated to **ca. 10,000 BP**, and rice cultivation "could have begun **9,400 to 9,000 BP** at the Shangshan and Hehuashan sites" ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023 review of radiocarbon in Chinese archaeology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)); another study gives Shangshan culture as **c. 10–8.5 ka BP** ([Zhang et al., PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)). These are **minimum bounds for the appearance of morphologically detectable domestication traits**, not for the beginning of human rice exploitation.

**B. End date (i.e. when domestication was complete).** Selection had already shifted the population toward **reduced shattering by 9,000–8,400 BP** at Huxi, and domestication was "well under way by **8,000–5,500 BP**" ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). The trait-by-trait sequence continues into the Neolithic: the **qSH2 and qSH5** shattering loci had mutated **by 8,400 years ago** while **qSH1 had not**; *japonica* rice carrying qSH1 is present **by at least 7,000 years ago**; and paddy-field engineering runs **7,000–4,000 BP** ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). So the process **continues into the fully agricultural Liangzhu period (5,300–4,300 BP)** and thus effectively into the proto-historical horizon ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136); [Liu et al., *PNAS* 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** This entity is reported as **ranges rather than dates with ± terms**; the source publishes no explicit error terms for the headline figures. Individual site brackets from the same paper: Kuahuqiao **8,000–7,700 BP** (58% shattering, i.e. wild type), Tianluoshan and Luojiajiao **c. 7,000–6,500 BP** (49% shattering), Majiabang **6,300–6,000 BP**, Liangzhu **5,300–4,300 BP**; rice at Jiahu **9,000–8,000 BP**; the Houli sites Yuezhuang and Xihe **8,000–7,700 BP** ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon` — AMS determinations run by Prof. Wu Xiaohong at Peking University ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). **Calibration status is a problem for this entity.** The paper reports its ages in plain "BP" and **does not state whether they are calibrated or uncalibrated**; the figures must therefore be treated as ambiguous and are flagged below. The other lines of evidence are not radiochronometric at all and need no calibration: `typological`/morphometric analysis of spikelet-base shattering scars, phytolith morphometrics, and molecular-clock estimation from modern rice genomes ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). By contrast, the 2023 Chinese-archaeology radiocarbon review that gives 9,400–9,000 BP for Shangshan/Hehuashan explicitly warns that in Chinese practice "radiocarbon dates are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP" ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)), while Zhang et al. give Shangshan as **c. 10–8.5 ka BP** within a paper that uses calibrated ¹⁴C, single-grain OSL and ²³⁰Th uranium-series ages ([PMC8626068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8626068/)).

**E. Geographic range + type site.** The **lower Yangtze valley**, Zhejiang province, China. There is no single type site because this is a process; the key sequence runs **Shangshan → Huxi (Shangshan culture, Yongkang County, Zhejiang) → Kuahuqiao → Tianluoshan / Luojiajiao → Majiabang → Liangzhu** ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). Contemporary comparanda outside the Yangtze include **Jiahu** in Henan and the **Houli** sites Yuezhuang and Xihe in Shandong ([same](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)).

**F. Dispute status:** `contested-evidence`.
The disputes are about whether the *proxies* actually record domestication, not about the radiocarbon. Three named arguments: (i) the grain length/width ratio of rice husks used as pottery temper at Shangshan is debated as a domestication indicator; (ii) the shattering ratio at Kuahuqiao is disputed because **immature spikelets** can mimic a wild signature and bias the count; and (iii) whether *japonica* and *indica* represent a **single domestication** with later introgression or **separate domestications** remains open ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). Fuller and colleagues elsewhere argue for a **protracted, non-centred** domestication in which "cultivation" long precedes "domestication" — the same argument they apply against the Lahuradewa claim, where they note the grain morphometrics overlap wild rice ([Murphy/Fuller-line argument reported in PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early Shangshan/Hehuashan onset of cultivation | 9,400–9,000 BP (source warns Chinese dates are usually uncalibrated) | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` (per that source's own caveat) | majority for "cultivation begins" | [Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full) |
| Wide phytolith bracket | domestication underway 12,500–7,500 BP | phytolith morphometrics, not a radiometric method | minority (very wide, treated as an outer bound) | [Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136) |
| Molecular-clock bracket | 13,500–8,200 BP | genomic molecular clock — **not** radiocarbon, no calibration applicable | minority / independent cross-check | [Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136) |
| Reduced-shattering threshold (this paper's own conclusion) | selection achieved by 9,000–8,400 BP at Huxi | `radiocarbon` (AMS, calibration status unstated) + spikelet-base morphology | majority for "domestication detectable" | [Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136) |
| Kuahuqiao still wild-type | 8,000–7,700 BP, 58% shattering | `radiocarbon` (calibration unstated) + morphology | contested (immature-spikelet bias) | [Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Rice in the lower Yangtze was not domesticated at a moment but over several thousand years: people were harvesting and managing wild rice by around 10,000 years ago at Shangshan, the key non-shattering trait had been selected for by roughly 9,000–8,400 years ago, and the full domesticated package was only assembled gene by gene down to about 7,000 years ago ([Zheng, Fuller et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)).

### 1. Entities whose dates rest on UNCALIBRATED radiocarbon (or on ¹⁴C of unstated calibration status)

**Explicitly uncalibrated:**

1. **Hoabinhian (entity 13).** The single most uncalibrated entity in this set. The synthesis states the oldest dates are "no earlier than about **18,500 to 23,000 BP (non-calibrated)**", and then reports site after site in non-calibrated ¹⁴C: Spirit Cave pre-pottery 11,690±280 to 8,760±135 BP non-calibrated; the cord-impressed pottery level 8,806±200 to 7,622±300 BP non-calibrated; Tham Phaa Chan 7,500–5,100 BP non-calibrated; Ban Rai eight dates 10,600–7,250 BP non-calibrated; Tham Lod 32,400–12,100 BP non-calibrated; Tam Hua Pu 1,340±70 to 32,500±900 BP non-calibrated ([Forestier, Zeitoun et al., *Comptes Rendus Palevol* 12(1), 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf)). Lang Kamnan Cave is likewise given as "uncalibrated 27,110 years" ([Ji et al., *Quaternary International* 400, 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)).
2. **Jomon regional sub-phase chronologies as compiled by Keally (entity 2, secondary framework).** The regional tables are "mostly UNCALIBRATED ¹⁴C" alongside fission-track, TL and obsidian-hydration ages ([C.T. Keally, Jomon dates](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).
3. **Ōdai Yamamoto I raw determinations (entity 1/2 note).** The five AMS dates on charred residue are **13,480–12,680 BP uncalibrated**, equivalent to 16,140–14,920 cal BP ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)); the published version gives 12,680–13,780 BP ([Nakamura et al., *Radiocarbon* 43(2B):1129–1138](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-charred-residues-on-the-earliest-pottery-in-japan/D2DE167CFF67D9B361D5D2E3AF198BD8)). The calibrated equivalents exist, so the entity itself is secure — but the raw numbers are uncalibrated and are frequently quoted as if they were calendar years.
4. **Sannai Maruyama and Kanto Jomon site series (entity 2 supporting data).** Reported in paired form: Early Jomon 5,050–4,570 BP uncalibrated = 5,800–5,295 cal BP; Middle Jomon 4,470–3,880 BP uncalibrated = 5,045–4,285 cal BP ([Keally](http://www.t-net.ne.jp/~keally/Chronologies/jomon-dates.html)).
5. **Kyushu tephra-linked Jomon horizons.** Sakurajima Pt3 ash "ca. **9,400 uncal. bp** or 10,600 cal. BP"; Kikai Caldera "around **6,400 uncal. bp** or 7,300 cal. BP" ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)).

**Chinese Neolithic dates where the source itself warns the convention is uncalibrated:**

6. **Yangshao culture (entity 4)** and **Longshan culture (entity 6)** as quoted in "ca. 6,900 BP – 4,500 BP" and "ca. 4,500 BP – 3,800 BP" form, on a page that states outright that "Radiocarbon dates are usually reported in uncalibrated years BP" in Chinese archaeological practice ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). The calibrated alternatives exist and are preferred in the entity entries above.
7. **Hongshan culture (entity 5).** Every source retrieved gives it in plain "years ago" or plain "BP" — 6,000–5,000 BP ([Institute of Archaeology, CASS](http://kaogu.cssn.cn/ywb/research_work/other_topics/201411/W020180124632420705403.pdf)); 6,000–5,000 years ago and Niuheliang 5,500–5,000 years ago ([UNESCO tentative list 5804](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5804/)); Zhengjiagou 5,300–4,800 years ago "by radiocarbon dating" with **no calibration statement** ([Bastille Post / Xinhua, Dec 2025](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5463782-new-findings-extend-duration-of-chinas-hongshan-culture)). **Hongshan is the weakest-grounded Chinese entity in this set on calibration.**
8. **Longshan culture in the Haidai and Shangluo studies.** The Shangluo Bayesian framework gives Yangshao ~4200–2900 BC and Longshan ~2900–2100 BC **without stating calibration status or naming a curve** ([*Radiocarbon* 52(2):466–478](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)); the Haidai study names OxCal 4.2 but no curve ([*JAS: Reports*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X16305648)).
9. **Peiligang culture (entity 3), partially.** The core dates are calibrated (CalPal), but the **calibration curve is not named** ([Zhang et al., PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)), and the institutional 5,500–4,900 BC figure carries no calibration statement at all ([China Culture](http://en.chinaculture.org/library/2003-09/24/content_39079.htm)).

**Calibration status unstated (treat as unreliable until checked):**

10. **Yangtze rice domestication (entity 16).** The primary paper reports all its ages in plain "BP" and never says whether they are calibrated ([Zheng, Fuller et al., *Scientific Reports* 6:28136](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28136)). This affects the headline 9,000–8,400 BP figure.
11. **Niah Deep Skull charcoal date (entity 15).** 39,600±1,000 BP (GRO1339) is reported with **no calibration status** ([Curnoe et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)). The ~35 kyr uranium-series age is **not** radiocarbon and needs no calibration.
12. **Bhirrana (entity 10), the older "9 ka" claims.** The alternative long chronology (Early Ravi ~9–6.3 ka BP etc.) is given with **calibration status not stated**, and the site's headline 8.35±0.14 ka BP figure sits in a paper that describes a "compilation of calibrated radiocarbon dates" but never names a curve or software ([Sarkar et al., *Scientific Reports* 6:26555](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26555)).
13. **Lahuradewa (entity 12), the widely quoted 6409 BCE figure.** Reported as "radio-carbon dated to 6409 BCE (8359 cal BP)" without saying whether 6409 BCE is itself calibrated ([PMC7773629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7773629/)). The **revised** Lahuradewa chronology is properly calibrated (OxCal 4.2 with IntCal20, plus CALIBomb for the modern sample) ([Patel & Agnihotri, *J. Palaeontological Society of India* 67(1)](https://palaeontologicalsociety.in/vol67_1/10.%20JPSI-IBSV-Patel-Agnihotri.pdf)).
14. **Ravi phase (entity 11), partially.** The dates are calibrated, but with the **University of Washington Calibration Program rev. 3.0.3c**, a 1990s curve superseded several times over; the raw ages are given in lower-case "bp" (e.g. 4980±60 bp) with calibrated BC in a separate column ([Kenoyer 2000](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2000_The%20Ravi%20Phase%20A%20New%20Cultural%20Manifestation%20at%20H.pdf)).

**Entities that are securely calibrated (for contrast):** Jomon overall and sub-phases in the Habu framework (explicit "calibrated BP") ([Matsumoto, Habu & Matsui 2017](https://junkohabu.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/matsumoto-habumatsui-2017.pdf)); Liangzhu and its dams (OxCal v4.3.2 + IntCal13) ([Liu et al., *PNAS* 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710516114)); Jiahu (cal yr BP) ([PMC3524165](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524165/)); Mehrgarh 2025 re-dating (Bayesian, cal BCE) ([Mutin et al., *Scientific Reports* 15:11345](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-92621-5)); Ban Chiang (OxCal v4.2.4 + IntCal13) ([Higham, Douka & Higham, *PLOS ONE* 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4575132/)); the north-west Thailand cave re-dating (OxCal v4.4.2 + IntCal20) ([*Antiquity*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reevaluating-pleistoceneholocene-occupation-of-cave-sites-in-northwest-thailand-new-radiocarbon-and-luminescence-dating/E9E63C4655B25B5A1AB4EDACA0DC91FD)); the 2025 Chinese national radiocarbon databank (OxCal v4.4.4 + IntCal20) ([Qiu et al., *Scientific Data* 12:1665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/)).

### 3. Entities attributed to a specific hominin species

Only **one** of the sixteen entities is attributed to a named hominin taxon:

- **Niah Caves / Deep Skull (entity 15): anatomically modern *Homo sapiens*.** The Deep Skull is described as "the oldest anatomically modern human recovered from island Southeast Asia" ([Curnoe et al., *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 4:75, 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)). The same source discusses whether the individual relates to the Negrito populations, whose molecular divergence from other East Asians is put at 30–10 ka — **younger** than the ~37 ka skull ([same](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2016.00075/full)). A secondary, non-taxonomic dispute concerns age at death: Brothwell (1960) estimated 15–17 years; Brown re-estimates 30–40 years from tooth wear ([Peter Brown](https://www.peterbrown-palaeoanthropology.net/Niah.html)).

All fifteen other entities are **archaeological cultures, sites, periods or processes**, not hominin fossils, and none of the fetched sources attributes them to a species other than modern humans. The **Hoabinhian** (entity 13) is a lithic technocomplex, not a taxon, and none of the retrieved sources assigns it to a specific hominin ([Forestier et al. 2013](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/comptes-rendus-palevol2013v12f1a06.pdf); [Ji et al. 2016](https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/QI/2016_400_Jietal.pdf)).

### Method note on the Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project (as required)

The Xia-Shang-Zhou Chronology Project was commissioned in 1996 and completed in 2000, produced more than 200 dates, improved liquid-scintillation-counting precision to 2–3% and AMS to 5%, and delivered the now-standard dynastic figures of Xia ca. 2070–1600 BC and Shang ca. 1600–1046 BC ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1064717/full)). The 2025 national radiocarbon databank describes it as the first national programme to combine radiocarbon, archaeology, textual scholarship and astronomy ([Qiu et al., *Scientific Data* 12:1665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/)).

**It is contested.** Its astronomical retrocalculations have been attacked directly ([Informath critique, AH207a](https://www.informath.org/pubs/AH207a.pdf)), and its Western Zhou chronology has been reviewed critically in Sinological literature ([Cambridge review of the *Baogao*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/78B7D45FE8B93DD70AB440B44A5FF2CA/S0362502824000026a.pdf/xia_shang_zhou_duandai_gongcheng_baogao_and_its_chronology_of_the_western_zhou_dynasty.pdf)). Crucially for this report: **the Project's dates bear on the dynastic horizon (Xia, Shang, Zhou), not on the Neolithic cultures in entities 3–8.** None of the Peiligang, Yangshao, Hongshan, Longshan, Liangzhu or Jiahu dates cited above is drawn from the Project. Longshan is the only entity that abuts it, since the Longshan-to-Xia transition at ~2100 BC is where the two frameworks meet ([Shangluo study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/establishing-a-firm-chronological-framework-for-neolithic-and-early-dynastic-archaeology-in-the-shangluo-area-central-china/8E67FAD0B90114132F0102B46A887DB8)). The safest current practice for Chinese Neolithic dates is to use the 2025 databank's uniform OxCal v4.4.4 / IntCal20 recalibration rather than any figure inherited from the Project era ([Qiu et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540757/)).
