# EUROPE

## 1. Neanderthal Europe — occupation span of Neanderthals in Europe

**A. Start date.** Minimum bound, not an origin date. The Sima de los Huesos hominins — the oldest reliably dated hominins with clear Neanderthal apomorphies — are constrained to a minimum age of **~430 ka**, from a cave-raft speleothem deposited directly on Cranium 4 dated **434 +36/−24 ka** ([Arsuaga et al. 2014, *Science*, CNRS HAL full text](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-03739291/document)). Institutional summaries put the Neanderthal lineage as "already well-established in Europe by 400,000 years ago" ([Natural History Museum, London](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/who-were-the-neanderthals.html)). Treat 430 ka as a **minimum bound** on lineage presence, not on "classic" Neanderthals, which the same source dates to **c. 130,000–40,000 years ago** ([NHM](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/who-were-the-neanderthals.html)).

**B. End date.** The Mousterian in Europe ends at **41,030–39,260 cal BP** ([Higham et al. 2014, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)). Does not continue into the historical period. Smithsonian gives the species span as "about 400,000 – 40,000 years ago" with all traces gone by ~40 ka ([Smithsonian Human Origins](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-neanderthalensis)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.**
- End: **41,030–39,260 cal BP at 95.4% probability**; succeeding transitional industries (incl. Châtelperronian) end at a similar time; overlap with anatomically modern humans (Uluzzian-associated) **2,600–5,400 years at 95.4% probability** ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)).
- Start: U-series **434 +36/−24 ka** (n=5) on the cranium concretion; TT-OSL and pIR-IR on overlying LU-7 **428 ± 27 to 441 ± 25 ka** (K-feldspar) and **396 ± 35 to 429 ± 32 ka** (quartz); one ESR quartz age **443 ± 90 ka** ([Arsuaga et al. 2014](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-03739291/document)).
- Later bracketing work: LU-6 deposited **455 ± 17 to 440 ± 15 ka**, mean **448 ± 15 ka** (68.2%, ±1σ); four single-grain TT-OSL ages **453 ± 56, 437 ± 38, 457 ± 41, 460 ± 39 ka** ([Demuro et al. 2019, *QSR*, CENIEH copy](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/1194/1/New%20bracketing%20luminescence%20ages%20constrain%20the%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos%20hominin%20fossils%20(Atapuerca,%20Spain)%20to%20MIS%2012_Demuro_et_al_2019.pdf)).
- Only reliable maximum for the SH fossils is palaeomagnetic: red clays deposited in the Brunhes chron, **<780 ka** ([Demuro et al. 2019](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/1194/1/New%20bracketing%20luminescence%20ages%20constrain%20the%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos%20hominin%20fossils%20(Atapuerca,%20Spain)%20to%20MIS%2012_Demuro_et_al_2019.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** End date: `radiocarbon-calibrated` — AMS ¹⁴C, Bayesian-modelled, explicitly **CALIBRATED** ("calibrated years before present"), 40 sites, and the paper notes the radiocarbon method reaches its limit at ~50,000 years ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)). Start date: `uranium-series` + `luminescence` (TT-OSL, pIR-IRSL) + `esr` + `magnetostratigraphy` ([Arsuaga et al. 2014](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-03739291/document)). **Trap flagged:** the U-series, TT-OSL/pIR-IR and ESR ages at Sima are *not* radiocarbon; "ka" here means elapsed years before analysis and requires no calibration. Only the 41,030–39,260 figure is calibrated radiocarbon BP.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Europe from **Russia to Spain** in the Higham dataset ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)); species range "from Portugal and Wales in the west across to the Altai Mountains of Siberia in the east" ([NHM](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/who-were-the-neanderthals.html)). Type site: Kleine Feldhofer Grotte, Neander Valley, Germany (Neanderthal 1) — **n.a.** from a fetched primary source; the fetched sources do not designate a type site. Key European chronological site for the start bound: **Sima de los Huesos, Sierra de Atapuerca, Burgos, Spain** ([Arsuaga et al. 2014](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-03739291/document)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`** for the extinction date, `rival-chronologies` for the start. The ~40 ka disappearance and the 2.6–5.4 ka overlap with modern humans are the majority position from a single large AMS/Bayesian programme ([Higham et al. 2014](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13621)). The start bound was genuinely contested: **Bischoff and colleagues** argued from U-series on speleothem that the Sima hominins lay beyond U/Th equilibrium, giving "a firm lower limit of 350 kyr" and a likely age of **400–600 kyr** ([Bischoff et al., USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70025902)). **Arsuaga's** 2014 team explicitly overturned this, resolving the fossils "at 100,000 years younger than previously reported" ([University of Adelaide release](https://www.adelaide.edu.au/news/news71242.html)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sima de los Huesos >530 kyr / 400–600 kyr | >350 kyr firm minimum; likely 400–600 kyr | `uranium-series` (speleothem, growth-rate extrapolation) | superseded | [USGS/JAS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70025902) |
| Sima LU-6 slightly older than 430 ka | 455 ± 17 to 440 ± 15 ka | `luminescence` (single-grain TT-OSL) | minority (refinement, not rival) | [Demuro et al. 2019](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/1194/1/New%20bracketing%20luminescence%20ages%20constrain%20the%20Sima%20de%20los%20Huesos%20hominin%20fossils%20(Atapuerca,%20Spain)%20to%20MIS%2012_Demuro_et_al_2019.pdf) |
| Neanderthals appear in Europe after 300,000 years ago | ~300 ka | `typological`/fossil summary | minority | [Nature timeline](https://www.nature.com/news/minds-at-work-7.10495?article=1.12974) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Neanderthal-looking people were living in Europe by at least 430,000 years ago, and the last of them vanished from the continent about 40,000 years ago, after sharing it with incoming modern humans for a few thousand years.

## 2. Chauvet Cave (France) — parietal art

**A. Start date.** Human occupation Phase I begins **37.0–36.2 ka cal BP (95%)**; the parietal-art model specifically begins **36.6–35.2 ka cal BP (95%)** ([Quiles et al. 2016, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

**B. End date.** Phase II human occupation ends **29.7–27.9 ka cal BP (95%)**; parietal Phase II ends **30.6–28.9 ka cal BP (95%)**. Cave-bear occupation runs **48.3–41.5 to 33.5–32.7 ka cal BP (95%)**. Does not continue into the historical period; the cave was sealed by rockfalls ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Two-phase model, all at 95% (2σ), IntCal13 + OxCal 4.2: Phase I **37.0–36.2 → 34.4–33.5 ka cal BP**; Phase II **31.4–30.7 → 29.7–27.9 ka cal BP**. Parietal sub-model (33 dates: 19 black drawings + 14 charcoal marks): Phase I **36.6–35.2 → 35.8–34.0**; Phase II **31.9–30.5 → 30.6–28.9 ka cal BP**. Independent ³⁶Cl cosmogenic ages on rockfalls sealing the entrance: PAbr **34.5 ± 2.0 ka**, E1 **29.4 ± 1.8 ka**, E2 **23.5 ± 1.2 ka**, E3 **21.5 ± 1.0 ka**. Intercomparison anchor: **31,765 ± 136 BP uncalibrated** (75 determinations, 47 labs) ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` as the primary chronology — >350 dates in total, 259 radiocarbon determinations, 220 retained after selection criteria, **CALIBRATED** with IntCal13 in OxCal 4.2 and reported as cal BP. Supported by `uranium-series` (U-Th), `luminescence` (TL on two wall fires, which fell within Phase I), and ³⁶Cl cosmogenic nuclide dating of the rockfalls ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)). **Trap flagged:** the ³⁶Cl and TL ages ("34.5 ± 2.0 ka") are exposure/luminescence ages, not radiocarbon, and need no calibration; the **31,765 ± 136 BP** intercomparison value is explicitly an *uncalibrated* conventional radiocarbon age.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Grotte Chauvet-Pont d'Arc, Vallon-Pont-d'Arc, Ardèche, southern France — the site is itself the reference locality ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** Radiocarbon places the black "series" in the Aurignacian, more than 20,000 years earlier than traditional stylistic models, which had assigned the art to the Solutrean (~22,000–18,000 BP). The counter-argument — that the radiometric results should not be trusted and that stylistic comparanda, palaeontological remains and stratigraphy point to a much younger age — was made by **Christian Züchner** ("The Chauvet Cave: Radiocarbon versus Archaeology"; "La cueva Chauvet datada arqueológicamente") and by **Paul Pettitt and Paul Bahn** ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)). Pettitt and Bahn set out the case formally in ["An alternative chronology for the art of Chauvet cave", *Antiquity* 89(345)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/an-alternative-chronology-for-the-art-of-chauvet-cave/723AB31F4E88629A2B7323BDD4845450), questioning the validity of the radiometric dating, cautioning against relying solely on charcoal dates, and proposing an alternative based on stylistic comparanda, palaeontological remains and stratigraphy. The 2016 PNAS synthesis is the majority position.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solutrean/Gravettian stylistic attribution (Züchner; Pettitt & Bahn) | c. 22,000–18,000 BP for the black series | `typological` (style, comparanda, stratigraphy) | minority | [Pettitt & Bahn, *Antiquity* 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/an-alternative-chronology-for-the-art-of-chauvet-cave/723AB31F4E88629A2B7323BDD4845450); framing in [Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Hundreds of scientific dates show people painted in Chauvet Cave in two visits, roughly 37,000–33,500 and 31,500–28,000 years ago, even though some art historians long insisted the paintings looked far younger.

## 3. Lascaux (France) — parietal art

**A. Start date.** Best current estimate: occupation **between 21,500 and 21,000 years before present**, from the LAsCO project's new ¹⁴C programme on reindeer-bone fragments ([French Ministry of Culture, "Dating the figures at Lascaux"](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)).

**B. End date.** Same interval — one or more occupations within **21,500–21,000 BP**, falling between the Badegoulian (23,000–21,000 BP) and the Magdalenian (21,000–14,000 BP) ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Published individual determinations, with error terms as given:
- 1951 (Libby, Chicago), charcoal from the Shaft: **15,516 ± 900 BP**
- André Glory's charcoal, Passage and Shaft: **17,190 ± 140 BP** and **16,000 ± 500 BP**
- 1998/2002 AMS on reindeer-antler fragments: **18,600 ± 190 BP** and **18,930 ± 230 BP**, reported as "between 23,500 and 22,000 years before present"
- LAsCO 2018–2021 reindeer bone: **21,500–21,000 BP**, with **no individual error terms stated on the page**
The LAsCO range is **1,000 to 1,500 years later** than the late-1990s results ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` **and** `radiocarbon-calibrated`, ambiguously mixed. The official page reports every determination as "BP" and **does not state whether the BP figures are calibrated or uncalibrated, and provides no calibrated ranges** ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)). The measurement-style figures with ± errors (15,516 ± 900; 17,190 ± 140; 16,000 ± 500; 18,600 ± 190; 18,930 ± 230) are conventional **uncalibrated** ¹⁴C ages; the ranges the page pairs with them ("23,500 and 22,000 years before present", "21,500 and 21,000 years before present") are calibrated calendar ranges. **This entity is a primary uncalibrated-radiocarbon flag** (see §Uncalibrated flags).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Grotte de Lascaux, Montignac, Dordogne, Vézère valley, France. Comparanda used in the attribution debate: Fourneau du Diable (Bourdeilles, Dordogne) and Roc-de-Sers (Charente); Labattut and Blanchard shelters ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The cave has produced "two sets of contradictory dates produced by testing different materials", plus artefacts with Magdalenian characteristics but a "Solutrean" pictorial style. Positions and who held them: **Henri Breuil** and **Denis Peyrony** linked Lascaux to the Gravettian; **Séverin Blanc** to the Solutreo-Magdalenian; **André Leroi-Gourhan**, on stylistic grounds, to the Solutrean, and **Norbert Aujoulat's** formal analysis also indicated a Solutrean tradition; **Annette Laming** held that the iconography could fit either cycle; **Arlette Leroi-Gourhan and Jacques Allain**, from the lithic/bone material and Glory's sections, assigned the complex to **Magdalenian II**, which André Leroi-Gourhan then accepted. The **LAsCO project (Langlais and Ducasse)** reassessment does not definitively assign the wall paintings to either culture ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper Solutrean attribution from the late-1990s AMS antler dates | c. 24,000–23,000 BP | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` (AMS on reindeer antler) + `typological` | superseded by LAsCO | [Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux) |
| Magdalenian II attribution (A. Leroi-Gourhan & Allain) | Magdalenian II | `typological` (lithic/bone assemblage, stratigraphy) | minority/legacy | [Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux) |
| Early Magdalenian from Glory's charcoal | 21,000–18,000 BP | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` (charcoal) | superseded | [Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Lascaux was decorated around 21,000 years ago, but the cave has been notoriously hard to date because charcoal, antler and bone samples gave answers thousands of years apart and the painting style looks older than the tools found inside.

## 4. Altamira (Spain) — parietal art

**A. Start date.** Minimum bound. Parietal art was produced over **at least 20,000 years, between 35,000 and 15,200 years ago**; the older end rests on the uranium-series minimum age of **35,550 years** for sample **BIG-UTh-O-50** (corrected age **36.16 ± 0.61 ka**), a red linear sign on the Polychrome Ceiling ([García-Diez et al. 2013, *JAS*, CENIEH copy](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/420/1/Uranium%20series%20dating%20reveals%20a%20long%20sequence%20of%20rock%20art%20at%20Altamira%20Cave%20(Santillana%20del%20Mar,%20Cantabria)_Garc%C3%ADa-Diez_et_al_2013.pdf)). Because U-series on overlying calcite gives only a *terminus ante quem*, the art is **at least** this old.

**B. End date.** Youngest AMS radiocarbon result on the black paintings: **15,204 cal BP**; the AMS series for the black paintings runs **19,258 to 15,204 cal BP**. The paper also reports a reliable young U-series minimum of **5,790 years** (sample BIG-UTh-O-46, **5.86 ± 0.07 ka**) over a red ladder-form sign, and an unreliable **2.85 ± 0.35 ka** result ([García-Diez et al. 2013](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/420/1/Uranium%20series%20dating%20reveals%20a%20long%20sequence%20of%20rock%20art%20at%20Altamira%20Cave%20(Santillana%20del%20Mar,%20Cantabria)_Garc%C3%ADa-Diez_et_al_2013.pdf)). Graphic production spans Early Upper Palaeolithic/Aurignacian to Middle Magdalenian.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** All U-series errors at **95% confidence**; minimum ages defined as mean minus 2σ.

| Sample | Corrected U-series age | Minimum age |
|---|---|---|
| BIG-UTh-O-50 (Polychrome Ceiling red sign) | 36.16 ± 0.61 ka | 35.55 ka |
| BIG-UTh-O-53 (large red dotted-outline horse, 182 cm) | 22.11 ± 0.13 ka | 21.98 ka |
| BIG-UTh-O-46 (red ladder-band sign, Red Side-Passage) | 5.86 ± 0.07 ka | 5.79 ka |
| BIG-UTh-O-71 (black ibex outline, La Hoya) | 2.85 ± 0.35 ka | 2.5 ka — **not considered reliable** (low ²³⁰Th/²³²Th, heavy detrital contamination) |

Only 3 of 8 Altamira calcite samples had acceptable contamination levels; four were not dated or were archaeologically meaningless ([García-Diez et al. 2013](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/420/1/Uranium%20series%20dating%20reveals%20a%20long%20sequence%20of%20rock%20art%20at%20Altamira%20Cave%20(Santillana%20del%20Mar,%20Cantabria)_Garc%C3%ADa-Diez_et_al_2013.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `uranium-series` (U-Th disequilibrium on calcite over/under pigment) for the old bound, plus `radiocarbon-calibrated` (AMS on charcoal-based black paintings, reported as **cal BP**) for the young bound ([García-Diez et al. 2013](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/420/1/Uranium%20series%20dating%20reveals%20a%20long%20sequence%20of%20rock%20art%20at%20Altamira%20Cave%20(Santillana%20del%20Mar,%20Cantabria)_Garc%C3%ADa-Diez_et_al_2013.pdf)). **Trap flagged explicitly by the paper itself:** the U-series ages are reported "in years or thousands of years **before the date of chemical separation of uranium and thorium**" and are **"not directly comparable with uncalibrated radiocarbon dates"** — they are *not* radiocarbon BP and need no calibration. Only the 19,258–15,204 figures are calibrated radiocarbon BP.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Cueva de Altamira, Santillana del Mar, Cantabria, northern Spain. The wider U-series programme covered ~11 decorated caves in the Cantabrian region, including El Castillo, La Pasiega and Tito Bustillo ([Pons-Branchu et al., HAL](https://hal.science/hal-02428073/document)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`.** The U-series-on-calcite chronology that gave Cantabrian art its very old ages was directly attacked by **Edwige Pons-Branchu, Raphaëlle Bourrillon, Margaret Conkey, Michel Fontugne, Carole Fritz, Diego Gárate, Anita Quiles, Olivia Rivero, Georges Sauvet, Gilles Tosello, Hélène Valladas and Randall White**. Their objections: uranium concentrations and ²³⁴U/²³⁸U variability were not published or discussed, so open-system uranium leaching — which makes calcite look *older* than it is — could not be excluded; detrital-thorium correction assumptions were not discussed; and no independent cross-dating was done. They showed that 10% or 15% uranium loss would reduce the flagship El Castillo age of **41,400 ± 570 years BP** to about **37,000** or **35,000 years**, and that using a detrital ²³⁰Th/²³²Th ratio of 2.5 ± 0.5 instead of 1.25 ± 0.625 reduces it to **less than 39,000 years**. They specifically objected to using a single date to argue Neanderthal authorship ([Pons-Branchu et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02428073/document)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pons-Branchu et al. downward revision of the oldest Cantabrian U-series ages | El Castillo O-83 real age ~35,000–39,000 yr rather than 41,400 ± 570 | `uranium-series` re-modelled for U loss and detrital Th | minority (methodological challenge, not a published replacement chronology) | [Pons-Branchu et al.](https://hal.science/hal-02428073/document) |
| Altamira polychrome-ceiling horse as Solutrean at latest | minimum 21,980 yr; Gravettian or Aurignacian not excluded | `uranium-series` | majority | [García-Diez et al. 2013](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/420/1/Uranium%20series%20dating%20reveals%20a%20long%20sequence%20of%20rock%20art%20at%20Altamira%20Cave%20(Santillana%20del%20Mar,%20Cantabria)_Garc%C3%ADa-Diez_et_al_2013.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** People decorated Altamira again and again over more than 20,000 years, from roughly 35,000 to 15,000 years ago, though the oldest of those ages come from dating the crusts on top of the paintings and some scientists think that method makes them look older than they really are.

## 5. Linear Pottery culture / Linearbandkeramik (LBK)

**A. Start date.** Formative LBK begins **5625–5480 cal BC (95% probability)**, probably **5565–5490 cal BC (68%)** in Model 2; Model 3 gives **5610–5475 cal BC (95%)**, probably **5545–5485 cal BC (68%)**. The first longhouses with flanking pits appear in the Formative phase, "probably in the decades around **5500 cal BC**" ([Jakucs et al. 2016, *Journal of World Prehistory*, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)).

The **earliest (*älteste*) LBK** proper begins with the Formative→earliest transition at **5395–5320 cal BC (95%)**, probably **5375–5330 cal BC (68%)**, with regional starts: southeastern group **5395–5240 cal BC (95%)**, eastern group **5410–5275 cal BC (95%)**, western group **5415–5285 cal BC (95%)** ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)).

**B. End date.** **`n.a.` from the modelled literature fetched.** The Bayesian models explicitly **do not** provide robust estimates for the end of the earliest/*älteste* LBK, and a modelled end date for the LBK as a whole is not stated; the authors say a much better dataset would be needed. The late LBK is said to begin around **5300/5250 cal BC** in Transdanubian terms ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)). Conventional non-modelled end estimates cluster at c. 4900 cal BC (see G). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** As listed in A, all as OxCal v4.2 / IntCal13 Highest Posterior Density intervals at **95%** and **68% probability**. Single-phase model for first appearance of longhouses with flanking pits: **5525–5475 cal BC (25%)**, **5455–5375 cal BC (70%)**; narrower, **5505–5485 cal BC (13%)**, **5425–5385 cal BC (55%)**. Underlying conventional radiocarbon ages are reported as e.g. **6420 ± 35 BP**, **6475 ± 40 BP**, **6292 ± 33 BP**, **6330 ± 33 BP**, **6285 ± 100 BP**, **6894 ± 66 BP** ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — explicitly **CALIBRATED** using Stuiver & Reimer's probability method with the **IntCal13** curve in **OxCal v4.2**, Bayesian, reported as cal BC; replicate measurements combined as weighted means before calibration. Underlying lab values are conventional **uncalibrated** ages in BP ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)). Legacy LBK phase divisions (Oldest/Older/Younger/Youngest) are `typological` ([Encyclopedia.com, "First Farmers of Central Europe"](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/first-farmers-central-europe)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** A belt through central Europe westward to the Dutch coast, covering Transdanubia, eastern Austria, north of the Danube, east of Linz, the upper Danube and the Rhine valley; the longhouse "diaspora" spread across more than **1,000 km** of central Europe within a generation or two ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)). **Type site: `n.a.`** — the fetched modelling paper names Vinča-Belo Brdo as the Vinča type site but "does not name an equivalent type site for the LBK" ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)). Key dated site in the model: Szederkény, Hungary.

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** The LBK's internal chronology is largely settled at the start and unresolved at the end, and the real disagreement is taxonomic: what counts as "Formative", "earliest/*älteste*", and late LBK, and whether the southeast–northwest cultural boundary with the Vinča world is real. Jakucs and colleagues argue against "clearly-defined cultural boundaries" in the initial Neolithic and show that formal models cannot yet estimate the end of the earliest LBK at all, which is the explicit limit on the culture's definition-by-chronology ([Jakucs et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040754/)). The counter-position — that LBK phases can be handled typologically because "difficulties with calibration and resolution make it virtually impossible to construct a chronology relying on carbon-14 dating alone" — is the older reference-work view ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/first-farmers-central-europe)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional "5500–4900 BC" LBK span | 5500–4900 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` summary + `typological` | majority as a textbook range | [*Antiquity* 2024, South Bohemia LBK](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/at-the-edge-of-neolithic-transition-strategies-of-the-linearbandkeramik-farmers-in-south-bohemia-czechia/0436B43F9B24063EA4F013938B5335AF) |
| Older typological scheme in lowercase "b.c." | origin c. 5700 b.c.; Older phase 5500–5300 b.c.; farthest west c. 4900 b.c. | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` ("b.c.") + `typological` | superseded | [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/first-farmers-central-europe) |
| Regional late-LBK terminus, Vráble (Slovakia) | LBK settlement c. 5250–5000 cal BC, lasting ~200–300 years | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (109 ¹⁴C ages) | majority regionally | [Meadows et al. 2019, *The Holocene*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0959683619857239) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** The first farmers of central Europe, known from their band-decorated pots and long timber houses, appeared shortly before 5500 BC and spread more than a thousand kilometres in a couple of generations; when exactly their culture ended is still not pinned down.

## 6. Megalithic Europe / Atlantic megalithic tradition

**A. Start date.** Emergence of the first megaliths **4794–3986 cal BC (95.4% probability)**, **4770–4005 cal BC (68.2%)**, which the author argues is probably reducible to a window of **200–300 years in the second half of the 5th millennium cal BC** ([Schulz Paulsson 2019, *PNAS* 116(9)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116)). Precursor and earliest structures: Passy necropolis first burial **5061–4858 cal BC (95.4%)**; Tumulus St-Michel, Carnac, first phase **4782–4594 cal BC (95.4%)**; earliest accessible megalithic grave, Prissé-la-Charrière, **4371–4263 cal BC (95.4%)**.

**B. End date.** The Atlantic tradition's main sequence closes in the later 4th millennium, but the tradition does not simply stop: there is a **Mediterranean revival in the 2nd millennium cal BC** (Balearics, Apulia, Sicily) ([Schulz Paulsson 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116)). So: continues, in altered form, well past the Neolithic; it does not continue into the historical period as a living tradition.

| Tavertet, Catalonia | not before 4722–4068 cal BC |

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — 2,410 radiocarbon dates, Bayesian-modelled in OxCal 4.1, explicitly **CALIBRATED**, reported as cal BC ([Schulz Paulsson 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Atlantic and Mediterranean Europe: northwest France (Brittany, the proposed origin region), Atlantic Iberia (Portugal, Catalonia), Ireland, England, Scotland, Scandinavia (Funnel Beaker), Corsica, Sardinia, and later the Balearics, Apulia and Sicily. Reference localities for the origin phase: **Passy necropolis** and **Tumulus St-Michel, Carnac** (northwest France) ([Schulz Paulsson 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Two long-standing hypotheses compete: **maritime diffusion** from a single northwest French origin versus **independent regional invention** in each Atlantic region. Diffusion was the 19th/early-20th-century orthodoxy; after radiocarbon dating arrived in the mid-20th century the field swung to independent invention. Schulz Paulsson's three-phase model — emergence in NW France in the second half of the 5th millennium, expansion in the first half of the 4th (Atlantic Iberia, Ireland, England, Scotland, France), then Scandinavia in the second half — supports maritime diffusion and is the current majority reading ([Schulz Paulsson 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent regional invention of megalithic building | multiple independent origins across the 5th–4th millennia cal BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (mid-20th-century readings) | minority / superseded by the 2019 Bayesian synthesis | [Schulz Paulsson 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813268116) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Big stone tombs and monuments seem to have started in one place — northwest France, just before 4000 BC — and then spread by sea along Europe's Atlantic coasts, rather than each region inventing them separately.

## 7. Stonehenge (England) — construction phases

**A. Start date.** Stage 1 (circular earthwork enclosure, bank and external ditch, 56 Aubrey Holes, cremations): the enclosure was built **3000–2920 cal BC**; the modelled stage boundary is **3100–2920 cal BC** ([Darvill, Parker Pearson, Jordan & Wainwright 2012, "Remodelling Stonehenge", *Antiquity* 86:1021–40, Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/20587/1/Antiquity%202012%20(Stonehenge%20Remodelled).pdf)).

**B. End date.** Stage 5 (rock art, Y and Z holes) runs **2020–1520 cal BC**, modelled boundaries **2010–1745 to 1620–1450 cal BC**; Y and Z holes **1630–1520 cal BC** ([Darvill et al. 2012](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/20587/1/Antiquity%202012%20(Stonehenge%20Remodelled).pdf)). ## 8. Corded Ware culture

**A. Start date.** Corded Ware was **widespread across Bohemia by 2900 BCE**, with the earliest directly dated individuals at **3018–2901 cal BCE** (VLI076) and **3010–2889 cal BCE** (STD003) ([Papac et al. 2021, *Science Advances*](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)). Other early individuals: OBR003 **2911–2875 cal BCE**, PNL001 **2914–2879 cal BCE**.

**B. End date.** **`n.a.` from the genomics paper** — "a specific end date is not stated" for Bohemia ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)). Regional ends from other fetched work: Switzerland, where the dendrochronological scheme is tightest, ends **2400 BC** (youngest felled tree at Wädenswil Vorder Au **2426 BC**; Ludwigshafen Seehalde **2418 BC**); across the whole distribution the end varies **2400–2000 BC** by territory ([Włodarczak / Furholt discussion, RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf)). Eastern Baltic: **2900–2300 cal BCE** ([*Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X18302566)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Individual calibrated ranges as above. Swiss dendrochronology restricts Corded Ware there to **2750–2400 BC**, against a usual start estimate of **2900–2750 BC** and some reconstructions as early as **3050–3000 BC** ([RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf)). Estonian AMS series **2900–2100 cal BC**, e.g. Riigiküla XIV Ta-2680 **3970 ± 100 BP → 2600–2300 cal BC** ([*Archaeofauna*](https://revistas.uam.es/archaeofauna/article/download/6619/7017)). Polish series **c. 2900–2300 cal BC**, younger phase 2500–2300 BC ([PLOS ONE, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6300194/pdf/pone.0207748.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` **plus** `aDNA-admixture-modelling` **plus** `dendrochronology` — this is a case where the chronology rests on radiocarbon and ancient DNA together, not typology. Papac et al. report **271 genomes** from Bohemia spanning c. 4900–1600 BCE (206 newly reported, 65 previously published) and **140 new radiocarbon dates** whose stated purpose was finer temporal resolution for Corded Ware, Bell Beaker and Únětice. Critically, the earliest radiocarbon dates for Corded Ware individuals were **cross-validated genetically**: those individuals occupy the most extreme positions on PCA axis PC2, consistent with first-generation eastern migrants, with later generations intermediate ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)). Independently, the Swiss lake-settlement scheme is `dendrochronology`, described as the most reliable available ([RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf)). The Estonian and Polish figures are explicitly **CALIBRATED** (cal BC), with underlying conventional ages in BP (e.g. 3970 ± 100 BP) ([*Archaeofauna*](https://revistas.uam.es/archaeofauna/article/download/6619/7017)).

Internal genetic chronology, dated by radiocarbon: a sharp Y-chromosomal turnover at approximately **2600 BCE**, with R1a-M417(xZ645) rising from 3/11 (0.27) in early Corded Ware to 10/11 (0.91) in late Corded Ware over a modelled **500 years**, implying a **15.79%** per-generation reproductive advantage (range 4.12–44.42%) ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Bohemia (Czechia) is the densely sampled core; the culture extends from Switzerland and the Rhine through Poland to the eastern Baltic and Estonia ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf); [RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf); [*Archaeofauna*](https://revistas.uam.es/archaeofauna/article/download/6619/7017)). **Type site: `n.a.`** — none of the fetched sources designates one; Corded Ware is defined by its cord-impressed pottery and single-grave burial rite rather than a named type site.

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** Radiocarbon-based schemes put the start at 2900–2750 BC and sometimes 3050–3000 BC, while **Włodarczak and Furholt** argue the Swiss lake-settlement dendrochronology — the most precise evidence available — restricts Corded Ware to **2750–2400 BC**, i.e. considerably shorter and later than the radiocarbon consensus; the end date also varies by 400 years between territories ([RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf)). Włodarczak set out the radiocarbon-versus-dendrochronology problem directly in [*Radiocarbon* 51(2):737–749](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-and-dendrochronological-dates-of-the-corded-ware-culture/9DF01BD08A4A23DB653AE66F101A2917). A separate school argues the dendro-anchored dates should be read against volcanic climate events, dating Corded Ware formation to **2910/2850–2844 BC** ([Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/9720392.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swiss dendrochronological "short" chronology (Włodarczak, Furholt) | 2750–2400 BC | `dendrochronology` | minority overall, majority for Switzerland | [RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf) |
| Volcanic/dendro event chronology for CW formation | 2910–2850–2844 BC | `dendrochronology` + climate-event correlation | minority | [Dialnet](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/9720392.pdf) |
| Early start reconstructions | 3050–3000 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/28887/WA308_44239_P243_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_I.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Corded Ware people, buried singly under mounds with cord-decorated pots, were spread across central Europe by about 2900 BC — dated by both radiocarbon and their DNA, which shows the earliest ones were newcomers from the east — and their culture faded out somewhere between 2400 and 2000 BC depending on the region.

## 9. Bell Beaker culture

**A. Start date.** "Bell Beaker pottery spread across western and central Europe beginning around **2750 BCE**" ([Olalde et al. 2018, *Nature* 555:190–196, Edinburgh accepted version](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). In Bohemia specifically, Bell Beaker appears from approximately **2500 BCE** ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)).

**B. End date.** Disappears **between 2200 and 1800 BCE** ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). The late Bohemian phase is **2400–2200 BCE**, after which R1b-P312 falls from 100% in late Bell Beaker to 20% in preclassical Únětice, implying a minimum **80% influx of new Y-lineages** at the Early Bronze Age transition ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Individual directly dated Beaker individuals, calibrated with conventional ages in parentheses:
- I4145, Kněževes: **2279–2033 cal BCE** (3740 ± 35 BP, Poz-84460)
- I1392: **2832–2476 cal BCE** (4047 ± 29 BP, MAMS-25935)
- I2416, Amesbury Down: **2470–2285 cal BC** (3830 ± 30 BP, Beta-432804)

([Olalde et al. 2018, Harvard copy](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/nature25738_Olalde_0_1.pdf)). British population turnover: replacement of **~90% of Britain's gene pool within a few hundred years**, with a detailed estimate of a **minimum 90 ± 2% local population turnover by the Middle Bronze Age**; R1b was completely absent in the Neolithic sample (n=33) but >90% of Y-chromosomes in Copper/Bronze Age Britain ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` **plus** `aDNA-admixture-modelling` — again a joint chronology. Olalde et al. generated genome-wide data from **400** Neolithic/Copper Age/Bronze Age Europeans including **234 Beaker-associated individuals**, and **106 new direct radiocarbon dates**; results are reported as **calBCE**/**calBC** with the underlying conventional ages in BP ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf); [Harvard copy](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/nature25738_Olalde_0_1.pdf)). Papac et al. add **140 new radiocarbon dates** for Bohemia and use a mean-date threshold of **2400 BCE** to split early from late Bell Beaker genetically ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)).

**Notation note.** The Edinburgh accepted manuscript uses uppercase **"BCE"**, not lowercase "bc" ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). *Nature* journal house style renders calibrated calendar dates in lowercase "bc"/"bce", which collides typographically with the old British convention where lowercase "bc" meant *uncalibrated*. See §Uncalibrated flags.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Western and central Europe, with Iberia genetically separate from the rest; Britain (Amesbury Down), Bohemia (Kněževes), central Europe. **Type site: `n.a.`** — the Beaker phenomenon is named for the vessel form, and none of the fetched sources designates a type site.

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The old debate over whether Beakers spread by migration of people or diffusion of ideas is now resolved differently in different regions by the same dataset: Olalde et al. found "limited genetic affinity between Iberian and central European Beaker-associated individuals" and therefore **exclude migration as a significant mechanism of spread between those two regions** (Iberian Beaker individuals best fit Iberian Middle Neolithic/Copper Age sources, with central and northern European sources excluded at *P* < 0.0028; non-Iberian Beaker individuals best fit central/northern European Neolithic sources, with Iberian sources excluded at *P* < 0.0104) — while in Britain the same phenomenon involved ~90% population replacement ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). The counter-argument to a single "Beaker migration" is therefore made by Olalde and colleagues themselves, against the diffusionist-versus-migrationist framing that preceded them.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regional Bohemian start later than the pan-European start | c. 2500 BCE, late phase 2400–2200 BCE | `radiocarbon-calibrated` + `aDNA-admixture-modelling` | majority regionally | [Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf) |
| Iberian Beakers as an independent, non-migratory phenomenon | same 2750–2200 BCE window, different mechanism | `aDNA-admixture-modelling` | majority | [Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** A distinctive bell-shaped drinking cup spread across western Europe from about 2750 BC to somewhere between 2200 and 1800 BC — carried by migrating people in Britain, where they replaced about 90% of the population's ancestry, but apparently only as a style in Iberia.

## 10. Ötzi the Iceman — the individual and date of death

| 2σ (95.4%) | 3230–3100 BC | 61.1% |

([Kutschera](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/courses/cem485/lectures/icemanage.pdf)). Independent check on his equipment: all dates but two are consistent with **3360–3100 BC**, the two outliers being 4790–4550 BC and 790–480 BC ([Kutschera & Rom, *Radiocarbon*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/ams-14c-dating-of-equipment-from-the-iceman-and-of-spruce-logs-from-the-prehistoric-salt-mines-of-hallstatt/C202D5BD00F1F56399367B0FD7979CA9); [VERA equipment paper](https://lehre4.rad.univie.ac.at/share/WWW_Exchange/SEKRETARIAT/Publikationen/Publikationen%20-%20Nur%20VERA/Electronic%20version%20of%20Papers/0023_Radiocarbon_Dating_Of_Equipment_From_The_Iceman.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — AMS ¹⁴C on tissue, combined across the Zürich and Oxford laboratories, then explicitly **CALIBRATED** with OxCal against INTCAL98. The **4550 ± 19 BP** figure is explicitly **UNCALIBRATED**; the 3370–3100 BC ranges are the calibrated result ([Kutschera](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/courses/cem485/lectures/icemanage.pdf)). This is the cleanest calibrated/uncalibrated distinction in the whole set.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Tisenjoch (Hauslabjoch), Ötztal Alps, on the Italian–Austrian border, South Tyrol; discovered 1991 ([South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology](https://www.iceman.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/30-years-%C3%96tzi-research-milestones.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `largely-settled`.** Two independent laboratories agree, the equipment dates converge on the same window, and the museum's own chronology matches ([Kutschera](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/courses/cem485/lectures/icemanage.pdf); [iceman.it](https://www.iceman.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/30-years-%C3%96tzi-research-milestones.pdf)). The only counter-signals are the two equipment outliers — one much older (4790–4550 BC) and one Iron Age (790–480 BC) — which are treated as intrusive or reused material rather than as rival dates for the body ([Kutschera & Rom](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/ams-14c-dating-of-equipment-from-the-iceman-and-of-spruce-logs-from-the-prehistoric-salt-mines-of-hallstatt/C202D5BD00F1F56399367B0FD7979CA9)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.** **none.** The only variation is the narrower museum range 3350–3120 BC versus Kutschera's 3370–3100 BC at 2σ, which is a presentational difference within the same measurement, not a rival claim ([iceman.it](https://www.iceman.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/30-years-%C3%96tzi-research-milestones.pdf); [Kutschera](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/courses/cem485/lectures/icemanage.pdf)).

**H. Plain-language summary.** The frozen man found in the Alps in 1991 died between about 3370 and 3100 BC, and his clothes and tools give the same answer, so his date is one of the most securely fixed in all of European prehistory.

# CENTRAL ASIA

## 11. Denisova Cave (Russia) — the site as an occupation sequence

**A. Start date.** The cave's whole-of-cave history is reconstructed over the past **300,000 years**; three South Chamber samples dated by multi-grain pIRIR are **older than 300 ka**, and the oldest dated South Chamber unit, layer 19, accumulated **before 247 ± 39 ka** ([Vasiliev et al. 2025, *Nature Communications*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)). The whole-of-cave environmental and occupation context spans **~300,000 to 20,000 years ago** ([Jacobs et al. 2019, *Nature* 565:594–599](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0843-2)).

**B. End date.** Pleistocene deposition continues to approximately **25–20 ka** (layer 11), layer 8 at **19 ± 4 to 15 ± 4 ka**, layers 7 and 5 from **14 ± 6 ka**, and a Holocene sample from layer 5 at **5.3 ± 0.5 ka** with Afanasievo-culture pottery dated **5.3–4.8 cal ka BP (95%)** ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)). So the site sequence **does continue into the Holocene and up to the Bronze Age**, i.e. into the protohistoric range.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** South Chamber, total age uncertainties at **95% confidence** unless noted:

| Layer / deposit | Modelled age |
|---|---|
| Layer 19 | before 247 ± 39 ka |
| Layer 17 | 202 ± 35 → 167 ± 29 ka |
| Layers 16–15 | 148 ± 22 → 120 ± 15 ka |
| Layer 14 | 106 ± 17 → 86 ± 12 ka |
| Layer 13 | 79 ± 11 → 67 ± 7 ka |
| Layer 12 | 65 ± 7 → 56 ± 7 ka |
| pdd-9/12 | 52 ± 7 → 28 ± 5 ka |
| Layer 11 | start 25 ± 5 ka, end 18 ± 6 ka (interpreted 25–20 ka) |

| Layers 7 and 5 | from 14 ± 6 ka |

| Layer 11 radiocarbon | 39.2–37.6, 34.5–32.3 and 24.2–23.8 **cal ka BP** (95%); three older samples **>49 ka BP** |

Deformed Middle Palaeolithic (dMP) deposits contain mixed components spanning ~180 to 20 ka, grouped at ~140–120, ~90–60 and ~50–20 ka ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)).

Hominin ages from the earlier Bayesian model, at 95.4%: oldest Denisovan present as early as **195,000 years ago**; Denisova 2 modelled **122,700–194,400 years**; Denisova 8 **105,600–136,400**; Denisova 3 (youngest Denisovan) **51,600–76,200 years**; all Neanderthal fossils plus Denisova 11 (the Neanderthal × Denisovan daughter) **80,000–140,000 years ago**; Upper Palaeolithic tooth pendants and bone points **43,000–49,000 cal BP**, the earliest in northern Eurasia; Initial Upper Palaeolithic 45,000–48,000 years ago ([Douka et al. 2019, *Nature* 565:640–644](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0870-z)). The 2025 paper refines Denisova 11 to **118–79 ka** and Denisova 4 to **84–55 ka** ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)).

**D. Dating method.** `luminescence` as the backbone — single-grain OSL on quartz and single-grain post-IR IRSL (pIRIR) on K-feldspar, because the quartz OSL signal saturates above 100–150 ka. **150 optical ages** across all three chambers (Main 53, East 37, South 60); 57 of 60 South Chamber samples measured by single-grain methods; 28 samples with paired OSL and pIRIR ages combined into weighted means, mean paired ratio **1.016 ± 0.015**, statistically consistent with unity; two Bayesian models (A with 31 ages, B with 50) gave statistically indistinguishable results ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)). Jacobs et al. used 103 sediment samples and >280,000 grains, with OxCal 4.2.4 Bayesian models (Main Chamber 43 ages, East 28, South 11) ([Jacobs et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0843-2)).

`radiocarbon-calibrated` for material younger than ~50 ka: 60 radiocarbon ages in the Main and East Chamber chronology, reported as **cal ka BP** ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)). Douka et al. combined **50 radiocarbon determinations** with `uranium-series`, optical, stratigraphic and genetic data in one Bayesian model ([Douka et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0870-z)).

**Trap flagged:** the great majority of Denisova Cave ages ("247 ± 39 ka", "65 ± 7 ka") are **luminescence** ages — elapsed time since grains last saw sunlight — and are *not* radiocarbon and need no calibration. Only the "cal ka BP" figures are calibrated radiocarbon. The 2025 paper reports **no uranium-series dates**, unlike Douka et al. 2019.

**Uncalibrated flag:** the layer-11 chronology in Reich et al. 2010 was reported in lowercase uncalibrated form — four of seven dates are infinite dates older than 50,000 years **bp**, three finite between 16,000 and 30,000 **bp** ([Reich et al. 2010, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09710)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Denisova Cave, Bashelaksky Range, foothills of the Altai Mountains, Siberia, Russia — three excavated chambers (Main, East, South) ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6); [Oxford School of Archaeology](https://www.arch.ox.ac.uk/denisova-cave)).

**F. Dispute status: `method-conflict`, now largely resolved.** The pre-2019 chronology rested on radiocarbon out to ~50,000 years plus "TL and palaeomagnetic ages of equivocal reliability", which Jacobs and colleagues replaced wholesale with single-grain luminescence ([Jacobs et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0843-2)). The remaining live problem is not rival chronologies but **stratigraphic integrity**: South Chamber was harder to date because of extensive phosphatization, burrowing, subsidence and possibly earthquakes; layer 11 contains two age components (~80–40 ka and ~30–20 ka); the dMP deposits are so mixed that temporal patterns of Denisovan and Neanderthal presence "cannot be reliably inferred"; and the Neanderthal mtDNA in layer 12 may come from reworked material. Layers 14 and 17 are described by the authors themselves as poorly constrained, and optical ages are "too imprecise to resolve time gaps or climatic fluctuations of several millennia" ([Vasiliev et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60140-6)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-2019 layer-11 chronology (Reich et al. 2010) | 50,000–30,000 yr; 4 of 7 dates infinite >50,000 **bp** (uncalibrated), 3 finite 16,000–30,000 **bp** | `radiocarbon-uncalibrated` | superseded | [Reich et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09710) |
| Occupation begins as early as 300,000 yr on the deepest tools | ~300 ka | `luminescence` + `typological` | majority as an upper bound | [Oxford School of Archaeology](https://www.arch.ox.ac.uk/denisova-cave) |
| Layer-15 East Chamber ~200 ka with mtDNA divergence ~229 ka | 205–192 ka (68.2%), 217–187 ka (95.4%); mtDNA divergence 229 ka (95% HPD 206–252 ka) | `luminescence` + molecular clock | majority | [Brown, Douka et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612221/) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Denisova Cave in Siberia holds a layered record of roughly the last 300,000 years, dated mostly by measuring how long sand grains have been buried in the dark, and it shows Denisovans, Neanderthals and later modern humans all using the same cave at different times.

## 12. Botai culture (Kazakhstan) — earliest horse management

**A. Start date.** Botai is dated to the **4th millennium BCE**, approximately **3500 BCE** ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021, *Scientific Reports*, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)); Outram and colleagues placed the evidence at **~3500 BCE**, "around 5,500 years ago… about 1,000 years earlier than thought" ([University of Bristol release](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2009/6202.html); [Outram et al. 2009, *Science* 323:1332–5, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19265018/)). Site-level occupation reported as **~3700 BC** onward ([Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2009-mar-06-sci-horses6-story.html)) and as six centuries from ~3600 BC ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/03/06/science/06horses.html)).

**B. End date.** Approximately **3100 BC** ([Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2009-mar-06-sci-horses6-story.html)). Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz note that the paper "does not provide a more precise beginning-and-ending occupation range"; the occupation is Eneolithic/Copper Age and mid-Holocene ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** **`n.a.`** — none of the fetched sources publishes calibrated ranges with error terms for the Botai occupation itself. The figures available are round-number summaries (3700–3100 BC; ~3500 BCE; "around 5,500 years ago") ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/); [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2009-mar-06-sci-horses6-story.html); [Bristol](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2009/6202.html)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` for the occupation (reported only as BC/BCE calendar ranges in the fetched sources, with no BP values or error terms given — so calibration status is stated as BC but **unverifiable from these pages**), combined with `aDNA` for the species and ancestry questions, and `typological` for the Eneolithic attribution ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/); [Outram et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19265018/)). The three original lines of evidence for management were osteometric (metacarpal metrics), pathological (bit wear), and organic-residue (δ¹³C and δD lipid analysis showing mare's-milk processing) ([Outram et al. 2009, Reading repository record](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/24842/)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Type site: the **Botai settlement**, northern Kazakhstan, on a tributary of the Ishim River near Kazakhstan's northwestern border with Russia; the Eneolithic Botai culture of the north Kazakh steppe ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/); [Outram et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19265018/)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence` — and the key claim was overturned.**

**Direct answer to the brief: Botai horses are NOT ancestral to modern domestic horses.** The 2018 ancient-genome study by **Gaunitz and colleagues** found that all analysed Botai specimens were ***Equus przewalskii***, not *Equus caballus*: Przewalski's horses are the feral descendants of the Botai-managed animals, and the Botai horses are a **sister taxon** to the domestic horse lineage, not its progenitors ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021, summarising Gaunitz et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)). This overturned the consensus that had formed after Outram et al. 2009.

The dispute did not end there. **Taylor and Barrón-Ortiz** go further and argue Botai provides no reliable evidence for domestication or transport **at all**, and is best explained as a site for the **regularized mass harvesting of wild Przewalski's horses**. Their specific rebuttals: the "bit wear" on the single lower second premolar is two adjoining oval areas matching bands of reduced cementum deposition, plausibly pit-form enamel hypoplasia rather than bit damage; the 18.0 mm enamel exposure exceeds the conventional 5 mm bit-damage threshold but is not a single parallel-sided lesion; the Botai frequency of such damage (**1/9, 11.1%**) is comparable to or lower than the **15.3%** seen in wild North American Pleistocene lower P2s (11/72), and multiple well-defined cementum bands occur in **25.6%** of wild upper P2s and **15.4%** of wild lower P2s; the pithouse "dung" and posthole "corrals" were assumed rather than demonstrated; and a human dental-calculus study found **no dietary milk proteins of any kind** at Botai. They place the earliest unambiguously managed *E. caballus* with the **Sintashta culture, early 2nd millennium BCE** ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)).

**Who argues the other side:** **Outram et al.** (2009, the original case), **Bendrey** (the osteological transport criteria), **Brown and Anthony** (original identification of bit wear), **Levine** and **Olsen** (partial support), and **Gaunitz et al.** themselves, who reconciled the genetics by proposing an earlier domestication of *E. przewalskii* that was later abandoned, with subsequent feralization ([Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)). **Outram** replied in 2023 that "nothing in Gaunitz et al. (2018) negated the evidence for husbandry at Botai", framing domestication as a multi-centred multi-stage process with DOM2 emerging in the Pontic-Caspian steppe in the mid-3rd millennium BCE ([Outram 2023, *Frontiers in Environmental Archaeology*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-archaeology/articles/10.3389/fearc.2023.1134068/full)). **Librado, Orlando and colleagues** located the DOM2 homeland in the Western Eurasian steppes, especially the lower Volga-Don, in the late 4th/early 3rd millennium, with DOM2 expanding from ~2200 bc and replacing local populations by ~1500–1000 bc ([Librado et al. 2021, *Nature* 598](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Botai as earliest horse domestication, ancestral to modern horses | ~3500 BCE | `radiocarbon-calibrated` + osteometry + bit-wear pathology + lipid residue | **superseded** (2018 aDNA) | [Outram et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19265018/); [Bristol](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2009/6202.html) |
| Botai as an abandoned *E. przewalskii* domestication, later feralized | ~3500 BCE | `aDNA` (42 ancient horse genomes, 20 from Botai) | majority | [Gaunitz et al. 2018, via Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/); [CNRS release](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/unsaddling-old-theory-origin-horses) |
| Botai as mass harvesting of wild horses, no domestication | ~3500 BCE; earliest managed *E. caballus* instead with Sintashta, early 2nd millennium BCE | comparative dental pathology + `aDNA` | minority but influential | [Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/) |
| Husbandry at Botai stands regardless of the genetics | ~3500 BCE | archaeological argument | minority | [Outram 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-archaeology/articles/10.3389/fearc.2023.1134068/full) |
| DOM2 (modern horse) homeland in the lower Volga-Don | late 4th/early 3rd millennium bc; expansion from ~2200 bc | `aDNA` + `radiocarbon-calibrated` | majority for modern horse origins | [Librado et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Around 3500 BC the people of Botai in northern Kazakhstan were living almost entirely off horses, but DNA published in 2018 showed those animals were Przewalski's horses, a different lineage — so whatever Botai people were doing with horses, today's riding horses did not come from them.

## 13. Afanasievo culture

**A. Start date.** Revised AMS-based "short" chronology: **31st century BC** for the Altai (Altai burials 31st–29th centuries BC), with the Middle Yenisei starting in the **29th century BC** ([Poliakov, Svyatko & Stepanova, "A Review of the Radiocarbon Dates for the Afanasyevo Culture", QUB](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)). Revised "core" Altai chronology given elsewhere as **ca. 3100–2900 cal BC** ([Hermes et al. 2020, *Archaeological Research in Asia*](https://kaei.asu.ru/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Hermes_Tishkin_dr.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **25th century BC** — Middle Yenisei burials run to the 25th century BC; the Afanasyevo–Okunevo boundary falls in the **26th–25th centuries BC**, with the two cultures coexisting no more than 100 years, and the Afanasyevo–Karakol boundary at the **28th–27th century transition** ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)). Does not continue into the historical period.

Sample-level uncertainty from the same programme: UBA-22983 **4322 ± 37 BP → 3077–2886 cal BC (2σ)**; UBA-22984 **4392 ± 40 BP → 3310–2905 cal BC**; UBA-22985 **4394 ± 37 BP → 3307–2907 cal BC**; UBA-22986 **4346 ± 35 BP → 3340–3026 cal BC** ([Svyatko et al. 2017, TSU-hosted PDF](https://news.tsu.ru/upload/medialibrary/1bf/2017_05_svyatko_et_al..pdf)). Bayesian start/end at 2σ for the Minusinsk Basin: start **2916–2583 BC**, end **2575–2402 BC** (n=5) ([Svyatko et al., *Radiocarbon*, Minusinsk review](https://scispace.com/pdf/new-radiocarbon-dates-and-a-review-of-the-chronology-of-1v8b7fy36z.pdf)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` — 102 dates total (39 Middle Yenisei, 63 Altai including Mongolia; 76 by beta counting/LSC-GPC, 25 by AMS), explicitly **CALIBRATED** with **OxCal 4.3.2** and **IntCal13**, presented as **cal BC (2σ)**. The review explicitly distinguishes uncalibrated ¹⁴C BP from calibrated cal BC and notes that "early researchers often used only non-calibrated dates, which seriously affected the conclusions" ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)). Legacy chronology was `typological` (see F/G).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Altai-Sayan mountain region: the **Altai Mountains** (77 cemeteries, ~40 settlements, one ritual site, one mine, traces in four caves; mainly the Katun valley and tributaries Koksa, Argut, Chuya, Kadrin, Ursul, Sema; 450–4000 m a.s.l.) and the **Middle Yenisei / Minusinsk Basin** (35 cemeteries, 12 settlements; ~350 × 100 km at 300–400 m a.s.l.). The two groups are 300–500 km apart. Outliers in northwest Mongolia (Kurgak-Govi 1), northwest China and the Zeravshan valley ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)). **Type site: `n.a.`** — the review names **Afanasieva Gora** (Middle Yenisei) and refers to "the Afanasyevo cemetery of Afanasieva Gora", but does not explicitly designate it the type site ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies` — "long" versus "short".** The long chronology (~1400–1500 years of duration, 38th–25th centuries BC in the Altai) came from relative cultural analogies, typological comparison, early wood and charcoal dates, and broad, often uncalibrated LSC results. The counter-argument, made by **A. V. Poliakov, S. V. Svyatko and N. F. Stepanova**, is that AMS dates on human bone cluster far more tightly, and that in the Altai LSC dates on wood run **250–600 radiocarbon years older** than human-bone dates, with inter-laboratory discrepancies of up to **550 radiocarbon years** (Tytkesken VI kurgan 95) and **500** (Bersyukta I kurgan 1); human-bone LSC errors of 70–200 years expand past 1000 years after calibration. They also argue the long chronology conflicted with the small number of sites, small cemeteries, absence of internal periodization, and the accepted Yamnaya–Afanasyevo relationship — which would otherwise force cultural innovations to travel east to west ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)). The review does not name a single proponent of the long chronology; it attributes it to multiple earlier researchers and to earlier radiocarbon interpretations.

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Long" chronology | Altai 38th–25th c. BC; Middle Yenisei 33rd–25th c. BC; ~1400–1500 yr duration | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (LSC, wood/charcoal) + `typological` | superseded | [QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** Afanasievo herders in the Altai and upper Yenisei used to be thought to have lasted well over a thousand years, but better dating on human bones has squeezed them into roughly 3100–2500 BC, with the Altai group earlier than the Yenisei one.

## 14. Andronovo culture

**A. Start date.** Radiocarbon places the Andronovo period at the **19th–18th centuries BC**: the Fedorovo stage in the Baraba forest-steppe dates to the **18th–15th centuries BC**, values "some 300–500 yr older than previously thought" ([Molodin et al., *Radiocarbon*, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/14c-chronology-of-burial-grounds-of-the-andronovo-period-middle-bronze-age-in-baraba-forest-steppe-western-siberia/FA45D48A76CEEB27D06ECBDF475752F6)). By region, Fyodorovka dates run **1980–1510 BC** in the Southern Transurals, **1800–1500 BC** in the Baraba forest-steppe, **1900–1500 BC** in the Minusinsk Basin, and **2000–1700 BC** in the Upper Ob and Altai ([Grigoriev 2021, "Andronovo Problem", *Open Archaeology*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)). Alakul, the other constituent culture, has a general range of **2300–1450 BC** with the early portion regarded as unreliable ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)).

**B. End date.** The **15th century BC is the upper chronological limit of the Andronovo period** ([Molodin et al., *Radiocarbon*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/../radiocarbon/article/14c-chronology-of-burial-grounds-of-the-andronovo-period-middle-bronze-age-in-baraba-forest-steppe-western-siberia/FA45D48A76CEEB27D06ECBDF475752F6)). Grigoriev places the end of the Andronovo *system* around the beginning of the **14th century BC**, when the steppe passes to Cordoned Ware cultures and southern Siberia to Karasuk ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)). Does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** Nine new dates from four Andronovo sites (Pervomayskoye I, Potroshilovo II, Ust-Bir I, Yarki II) all fall in the **18th–15th centuries BC, 1744–1500 cal BC**; Bayesian start **1742–1549 BC**, end **1608–1440 BC** (n=9); traditional archaeological chronology was 17th/16th–13th centuries BC ([Svyatko et al., Minusinsk review, *Radiocarbon*](https://scispace.com/pdf/new-radiocarbon-dates-and-a-review-of-the-chronology-of-1v8b7fy36z.pdf)). Sample level, Potroshilovo: Bln-5163 **3397 ± 30 BP → 1740–1680 / 1670–1630 cal BC**; Bln-5194 **3295 ± 32 BP → 1610–1520 cal BC**; Bln-5198 **3269 ± 28 BP → 1610–1510 cal BC**; Bln-5197 **3189 ± 28 BP → 1500–1420 cal BC**; Bln-5193 **3164 ± 28 BP → 1500–1470 / 1460–1400 cal BC**; Bln-5031 (wood) **3408 ± 37 BP → 1750–1630 cal BC** ([*Radiocarbon*, Arizona archive](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/viewFile/3942/3367)).

In the Southern Urals, **Fedorovka is significantly later** than Sintashta: **1742–1451 cal BC**, against Sintashta's earliest 2200–2000 cal BC, an early "Sintashta" phase at 2004–1852 cal BC (boundary medians) with boundaries **2170–1900 cal BC** and **1940–1660 cal BC** (both 95.4%); Cherkaskul single date **1731–1541 cal BC (95.4%)**; Final Bronze Age **1410–1170 cal BC** ([*Radiocarbon*, Southern Urals, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/migrations-and-cultural-evolution-in-the-light-of-radiocarbon-dating-of-bronze-age-sites-in-the-southern-urals/672AA3ADD1BB703CA632E3207A8AC0ED)). Tree-ring-anchored wiggle-matching at the Lisakovsky cluster in Kazakhstan places the Alakul–Fedorovo cultural overlap in a 120-year window, **1780–1660 cal BC**, against a ceramic typological date of 1500–1400 BC ([Panyushkina et al., *Radiocarbon* 2008, Arizona](https://www.ltrr.arizona.edu/~tswetnam/CMATE/Lisak-Radiocarbon2008.pdf)).

Grigoriev's explicit caution on precision: at **68.2% probability** confidence intervals are **200–300 years**, and at **95.4%** they become too broad for detailed study of a dynamic system ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` (explicitly **CALIBRATED**, reported as cal BC with underlying conventional BP values, e.g. 3397 ± 30 BP → 1740–1630 cal BC) ([Arizona *Radiocarbon* tables](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/viewFile/3942/3367)), plus `dendrochronology` used as ¹⁴C wiggle-matching against a composite floating tree-ring chronology at Lisakovsky ([Lisakovsky *Radiocarbon* 2008](https://www.ltrr.arizona.edu/~tswetnam/CMATE/Lisak-Radiocarbon2008.pdf)), against a legacy `typological` chronology.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** From the **Southern Urals** in the west to the **Yenisei River in southern Siberia and western Xinjiang** in the east; north to the southern taiga; south into southern Central Asia including Tajikistan and Uzbekistan; concentrated in the centre of Eurasia through the 2nd millennium BC ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)). **Type site:** a cemetery near the village of **Andronovo, on the Yenisei River in southern Siberia**, on which **S. A. Teploukhov** based the culture in **1927** (earliest related publication 1863 by Zyryanov). The fetched source does **not** state that the type site is near Achinsk ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)).

**F. Dispute status: `definitional`.** This is the entity where the naming problem outranks the dating problem. **Stanislav Grigoriev** argues the term "Andronovo culture" is "incorrect, scientifically inappropriate and taxonomically meaningless" because it lumps together cultures that were not related, and that "Andronovo cultural-historical commonality" — introduced by **A. A. Formozov in 1951** — has no functioning scientific concept and survived by inertia. In his narrow definition, "Andronovo" means only **Alakul (high and late stages) and Fyodorovka**; Sintashta, Petrovka and early Alakul are pre-Andronovo, and Sargari and Begazi-Dandibay are outside the system. He recommends avoiding both terms ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)).

The chronological dispute that follows from this is whether Alakul and Fyodorovka were **successive** or **synchronous**. **K. V. Salnikov** proposed Fyodorovka → Alakul → Zamaraevo; **G. B. Zdanovich's** dominant later model ran Sintashta → Petrovka → Alakul → Fyodorovka → Sargari; **V. S. Stokolos** showed the two ceramic types co-occur in settlements and split them into two contemporary cultures; **N. B. Vinogradov** proposed treating Petrovka as early Alakul. Synchronism is supported by **Kosarev, Potemkina, Kuz'mina, Bobrov, Grigoriev, Korochkova and Stefanov**; an Alakul-from-Sintashta origin by **A. V. Matveev** and Grigoriev. Radiocarbon overlap of the Alakul and Fyodorovka intervals does not support simple genetic succession, and Fyodorovka dates from **Adunqiaolu in Xinjiang in the 19th century BC** undercut a simple west-to-east spread ([Grigoriev 2021](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opar-2020-0123/html?lang=en)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional archaeological chronology | 17th/16th–13th centuries BC | `typological` | superseded | [Minusinsk review](https://scispace.com/pdf/new-radiocarbon-dates-and-a-review-of-the-chronology-of-1v8b7fy36z.pdf) |
| Conventional southern Urals Late Bronze Age dating of Andronovo (incl. Alakul and Fedorovo) | 1500–1100 BC | `typological` (ceramics) | superseded | [Lisakovsky *Radiocarbon* 2008](https://www.ltrr.arizona.edu/~tswetnam/CMATE/Lisak-Radiocarbon2008.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** "Andronovo" is a catch-all label for Bronze Age steppe cultures — mainly Alakul and Fyodorovka — that flourished roughly 2000–1500 BC from the Urals to the Yenisei; radiocarbon shows they overlapped in time rather than following one another, and specialists now think the label itself is misleading.

## 15. Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC / Oxus civilisation)

**A. Start date.** **2200 BC** — "a series of radiocarbon dates from a number of BMAC settlements places the civilization" at 2200–1700 BC, and Sarianidi himself eventually accepted 2200–1700 BC after examining C-14 dates of 2100–1800 BC ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM repository PDF](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)). Gonur Depe itself was inhabited **2300–1500 cal BC** on 60 C14 dates ([Zaytseva et al. 2008 chronology, reported in *Anthropological Review* Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf)), and construction of North Gonur began 2300–2250 BC with all known radiocarbon dates in the range **2300–1600 BC** ([Sarianidi, margiana.su](https://margiana.su/publication/articles/Art-Urm-2013-Gonur-Depe%20eng%20allFig=.pdf)).

**B. End date.** **1700 BC** — "by 1700 B.C., virtually all fortified BMAC settlements had been abandoned" ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)). Gonur's main necropolis runs mainly **2300–1800 cal BC**, with palace/temple "ruins" burials in the mid-2nd millennium BC ([Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf)). The successor **Takhirbay culture** of the mid-2nd millennium BC has an essentially different settlement regime and material culture ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)). Does not continue into the historical period as the same entity, though the region does (Bactria and Margiana are later Greek/Persian geographical terms; Margiana/Margush was a Persian satrapy).

**C. Uncertainty bounds.** **Weak, and the sources say so.** No calibrated ranges with error terms are published in the fetched literature. Competing intervals: **2200–1700 BC** (accepted general range), **2500–1700 BC** (H. Junger's dates), **2100–1700 BC** (the Gonur C-14 bracket, "often derived from unspecified contexts"), **2100–1800 BC** (the dates Sarianidi examined), **2300–1500 cal BC** (Gonur on 60 C14 dates), **2300–1600 BC** (Sarianidi's stated full range), and **2800/2700–1800/1700 BC** for Rossi Osmida's continuous Adji Kui 1 sequence ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1); [Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf); [margiana.su](https://margiana.su/publication/articles/Art-Urm-2013-Gonur-Depe%20eng%20allFig=.pdf)). A 2025 report states Gonur's chronology now rests on **over 170 radiocarbon dates, one third AMS**, with a new study analysing 57 dates across five key sites ([News Central Asia](https://www.newscentralasia.net/2025/05/21/new-research-proves-the-special-role-of-the-gonur-depe-proto-urban-center-turkmenistan-in-the-development-of-a-mysterious-vanished-civilization/)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` in principle — the Gonur figures are given as "cal BC" ([Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf)) — but the fetched literature repeatedly reports BMAC dates as bare "B.C." without stating calibration, error terms, or laboratory codes, and Lamberg-Karlovsky stresses that "the limited number of radiocarbon dates" from Gonur, Togolok and Sapeli are "often derived from unspecified contexts" and "are not associated with specific stratigraphic levels or material inventories" ([UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)). Where the chronology is strongest — Adji Kui 1 — it is `radiocarbon-calibrated` anchored to a real stratigraphic sequence of four superimposed architectural levels over an underlying Eneolithic settlement dated to ~2700 BC. Everything else is effectively `typological`.

**E. Geographic range and type site.** Centred on the **Murghab Oasis, Turkmenistan**, along the Murghab River, which rises in the Paropamisus Mountains of Afghanistan and debouches into the desert; settlement concentrated in the Murghab deltaic fan and its oases, in a linear pattern along canals. BMAC artefacts occur from **Susa to Mohenjodaro** and in the Persian Gulf, at Tepe Yahya, Shahdad, Khinaman, Hissar, Jiroft, Harappa and Tell Abraq; the interaction sphere reaches from the Aegean to China. **Type site: Gonur Depe**, Turkmenistan, uncovered in the late 1970s and excavated by **Victor Sarianidi** — described in the same article as both a "40+ hectare" and a "20+ hectare" site, with monumental fortifications, temples, palaces, craft and ritual areas, and a cemetery of more than 2,500 burials (given elsewhere in the same text as 2,853 excavated tombs) ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)). The 2,843/2,853 tomb count is also reported for the main necropolis ([Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf)).

**F. Dispute status: `contested-evidence`.** The chronology is not disputed between rival numerical schemes so much as regarded as **not properly established**. **Lamberg-Karlovsky** holds that BMAC chronology and stratigraphy "remain deeply problematic": Gonur was published as a single-period site despite more than 30 years of excavation and up to four superimposed temples, materials from distinct strata were never published, and the radiocarbon dates are not tied to levels or inventories. **Salvatori** likewise argues Gonur's chronology is unusable as published and that stratified **Adji Kui** is the better chronological evidence. **Sarianidi**, the excavator, first advocated a chronology running into the second half of the 2nd millennium BC, then accepted 2200–1700 BC, and interpreted the site through a migration-from-Anatolia model with Indo-Iranian "Aryan" and proto-Zoroastrian identifications, which Lamberg-Karlovsky rejects as resting "more on allegation than demonstration", arguing instead for indigenous Central Asian origins on the evidence of the mid-7th-millennium Neolithic, 5th-millennium Ilgynly Tepe, and Namazga and Altyn Depe urbanism through the 4th and 3rd millennia ([Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Junger's longer range | 2500–1700 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) |
| Rossi Osmida's continuous Adji Kui 1 sequence | 2800/2700 – 1800/1700 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` + stratigraphy | minority, favoured by Lamberg-Karlovsky as best-stratified | [Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) |
| Sarianidi's original long chronology | into the second half of the 2nd millennium BC | `typological` | superseded, abandoned by its own author | [Lamberg-Karlovsky, UAM](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/660123/3902.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) |
| Gonur on 60 C14 dates | 2300–1500 cal BC (necropolis mainly 2300–1800 cal BC) | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | majority for Gonur | [Warsaw PDF](https://www.anthropology.uw.edu.pl/07/bne-07-03.pdf) |
| Sarianidi's stated full radiocarbon envelope | 2300–1600 BC; North Gonur construction from 2300–2250 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` | minority | [margiana.su](https://margiana.su/publication/articles/Art-Urm-2013-Gonur-Depe%20eng%20allFig=.pdf) |

**H. Plain-language summary.** A walled, canal-irrigated civilisation flourished in the oases of southern Turkmenistan roughly 2200–1700 BC, but because its main site was dug for decades and published as if it were all one period, its dates are much shakier than the scale of the ruins suggests.

## 16. Yamnaya culture

**A. Start date.** The Yamnaya archaeological complex **appeared around 3300 BCE** across the steppes north of the Black and Caspian Seas ([Lazaridis et al. 2025, "The genetic origin of the Indo-Europeans", *Nature*, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). Anthony's review of the earliest phase reaches the same figure independently: seven radiocarbon dates from early Yamnaya components at Repin, Mikhailovka II and Generalka 2, plus 43 dates from early Yamnaya individuals in 27 kurgan cemeteries, are older than 4350 BP with calibrated averaged midpoints between **3203–3107 BC**, indicating early Yamnaya sites began to appear about **3300–3200 BC** ([Anthony 2021, "Early Yamnaya Chronology"](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An)).

**B. End date.** **c. 2600–2350 BC**, varying by region and by author. Calibrated ¹⁴C for Yamnaya gives **3200–2350 cal BC** on 33 dates for human bone, wood and textiles ([Shishlina, *Radiocarbon*, Arizona archive](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/viewFile/2967/2726)); the Volga-Ural three-stage periodization ends with the late Poltavkinsky stage at **2600–2300 BC**, with the Yamna culture as a whole existing 3300–2350 BC ([Boldyrevo kurgan paleoecology paper](https://www.academia.edu/110787248/Paleoecology_During_the_Creation_of_a_Large_Boldyrevo_Kurgan_of_the_Yamnaya_Culture_in_the_Southern_Cis_Urals_Russia)). Yamnaya descendants continue: between **3300–1500 BCE** Yamnaya people and their descendants spread Indo-European languages and transformed Europe, Central and South Asia, Siberia and the Caucasus ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). The culture itself does not continue into the historical period.

**C. Uncertainty bounds.**
- Ancestral formation: average date of mixture in Core Yamnaya **4038 ± 48 BCE**, estimated with DATES; the Serednii Stih population represented by 13 individuals at **4996–3372 BCE**, explicitly **uncorrected for freshwater reservoir effects**; Ekaterinovka **5050–4450 BCE** on three herbivore-bone dates chosen to avoid reservoir effects; Khvalynsk **~4500–4350 BCE** on two herbivore bones; Berezhnovka-2 **4450–3960 BCE** ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)).
- Population growth: HapNe-LD 95% confidence intervals for time before growth, **3829–3374 BCE** (first 300 years of sampling) and **3642–3145 BCE** (later 300 years), overlapping at **3642–3374 BCE** ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)).
- Individual burials: Boldyrevo IV mound construction **3200–2600 BC** calibrated, over roughly 200 years ([DOAJ record, *Povolzhskaya Arkheologiya*](https://doaj.org/article/217444a763954a91902d788f4d727cdc)); Krivyanskyi IX kurgan 4 grave 21A **3345–3096 cal BC**, kurgan 2 grave 2 **2881–2633 BC** ([Anthony 2021](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An)); Mandjikiny-2 bone pin **4320 ± 60 BP → 3325–3233 cal BC (p=0.35)** ([*Geochronometria*/Sciendo](https://sciendo.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/s13386-011-0015-8.pdf)); Peschany-4, 27 dates on human, sheep, horse bone, willow wood, plant litter and charcoal, **2900–2600 BCE** ([Lazaridis et al. supplementary, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2024/04/18/2024.04.17.589597/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true)).
- Whole-culture Bayesian envelope: **3400–2300 cal BC**, with the absolute dating described as debated ([DFG GEPRIS project record](https://gepris.dfg.de/gepris/projekt/547670969?language=en)).
- Reservoir-effect corrections matter: Shishlina found that correcting for them moved the Early Catacomb culture from 3300/2900–2450 cal BC to **2600–2350 cal BC** ([Shishlina, *Radiocarbon*](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/viewFile/2967/2726)).

**D. Dating method.** `radiocarbon-calibrated` **plus** `aDNA-admixture-modelling`, tightly integrated — this is the clearest case in the set of dating resting on ancient DNA and radiocarbon together rather than typology. Lazaridis et al. assembled ancient DNA from **428 individuals** (367 newly reported, spanning 6400–2000 BCE; 68 with improved data quality; 803 libraries of which 195 failed screening), added **211 Yamnaya and related Afanasievo individuals** to 75 previously published, and reported **198 new radiocarbon dates**. The paper states explicitly that ancient DNA was used to identify clines, ancestry, admixture proportions and relatedness, while radiocarbon dates placed individuals and groups in chronological order and were used to estimate the timing of admixture and population growth; individuals were labelled using geographical, temporal, archaeological and genetic clustering information jointly ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). Anthony's chronology is `radiocarbon-calibrated` on 50 dates in the 4350–4650 BP range, screened against duplicate dates on the same sample ([Anthony 2021](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An)).

**Calibration status.** The PMC version of Lazaridis et al. uses **"BCE"**, **"cal BCE"** and **"BP"**, and **does not use lowercase "bc"** ([Lazaridis et al. 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). The *Nature*-published version of the same paper renders the same dates in *Nature* house-style lowercase — "appeared around 3300 bc", "Yamnaya ancestors formed around 4000 bc", "grew rapidly after 3750–3350 bc" ([Lazaridis et al. 2025, nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08531-5)). These are **calibrated** dates in lowercase notation, not uncalibrated ones — see §Uncalibrated flags. Librado et al. likewise write "late 4th/early 3rd millennium bc" and "from ~2200 bc" ([Librado et al. 2021, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9)).

**E. Geographic range and type site.** At maximal extent by 3000 BCE, **Hungary in the west to Kazakhstan in the east**; Core Yamnaya sampling covers ~5,000 km from Hungary to southern Siberia, and the later expansion extends "from China to Hungary" ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). Key dated settlements: **Repin** (lower Don), **Mikhailovka II** (lower Dnipro), **Generalka 2** ([Anthony 2021](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An)); Lazaridis et al. name Mykhailivka on the lower Dnipro as the best guess for where the Yamnaya horizon emerged ([Lazaridis, X thread](https://x.com/iosif_lazaridis/status/1887184409759261047)). **Type site: `n.a.`** — Yamnaya ("pit-grave") is named for its burial form; no fetched source designates a type site.

**F. Dispute status: `rival-chronologies`.** The 3300 BCE appearance date is now well supported by radiocarbon and aDNA jointly, but the **absolute dating of the culture as a whole remains debated**, with Bayesian modelling giving a range as wide as **3400–2300 cal BC** ([DFG GEPRIS](https://gepris.dfg.de/gepris/projekt/547670969?language=en)). Two specific live disagreements. First, **Anthony** stresses that only **10–20% of Yamnaya radiocarbon dates fall in the early 3300–3000 BC range**, so most Yamnaya sites are late Yamnaya, correlated with Mikhailovka level III at 3000 BC and later — against the earlier compilation by **Telegin**, who used 210 radiocarbon dates to argue for a 3300–3000 BC start with considerable overlap between EBA Yamnaya and MBA Catacomb graves at 2800–2400 BC ([Anthony 2021](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An)). Second, **Shishlina** argues that freshwater and marine reservoir effects systematically inflate steppe bone dates, and that correcting them shortens and shifts the sequence substantially ([Shishlina, *Radiocarbon*](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/radiocarbon/article/viewFile/2967/2726)) — a position Lazaridis et al. partly accommodate by flagging their Serednii Stih dates as "uncorrected for freshwater reservoir effects" and by preferring herbivore bone for the Ekaterinovka dates ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). A third school argues radiocarbon confidence intervals are simply too wide and prefers dendrochronological/volcanic-event anchoring, dating the Yamnaya migration to the Carpathian Basin and Corded Ware formation to **c. 2910/2850 BC** ([Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/9720392.pdf)).

**G. Strongest alternative claim.**

| Label | Proposed date | Dating method | Standing | Source |

| Telegin's compilation | start ~3300–3000 BC; Yamnaya/Catacomb overlap 2800–2400 BC | `radiocarbon-calibrated` (210 dates) | superseded in detail, consistent in outline | [Anthony 2021](https://www.scribd.com/document/685740317/Anthony-2021-Early-Yamnaya-Chronology-An) |

# SUMMARY REPORTS (required by spec)

## 1. Entities whose dates rest on UNCALIBRATED radiocarbon

**Genuinely uncalibrated, or presented ambiguously as raw BP:**

1. **Lascaux (#3).** The strongest case in the set. The official French Ministry of Culture dating page reports every determination as "BP" — **15,516 ± 900 BP**, **17,190 ± 140 BP**, **16,000 ± 500 BP**, **18,600 ± 190 BP**, **18,930 ± 230 BP** — and the page "does **not explicitly state whether the BP dates are calibrated or uncalibrated**" and "does **not provide calibrated date ranges** for any of the radiocarbon results" ([Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/lascaux/en/dating-figures-lascaux)). The measurement-form figures are conventional uncalibrated ages; the paired ranges ("23,500–22,000 BP", "21,500–21,000 BP") are calendar ranges. A reader taking the page at face value will conflate the two.

2. **Denisova Cave (#11), pre-2019 chronology.** Reich et al. 2010 dated layer 11 explicitly in lowercase uncalibrated form: four of seven dates are infinite dates older than 50,000 years **bp**, three finite between 16,000 and 30,000 **bp** ([Reich et al. 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09710)). Superseded by luminescence, but this is the notation in the paper that introduced Denisovans.

3. **Chauvet (#2), one anchor value.** The Sixth International Radiocarbon Intercomparison mean used to validate the Chauvet laboratory series, **31,765 ± 136 BP** (75 determinations, 47 laboratories), is an explicitly **uncalibrated** conventional age sitting inside an otherwise fully calibrated paper ([Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113)).

4. **Ötzi (#10), the input value.** The combined **4550 ± 19 BP** is explicitly uncalibrated, with BP defined as before 1950 AD, before OxCal/INTCAL98 calibration to 3370–3100 BC ([Kutschera](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/courses/cem485/lectures/icemanage.pdf)). Cleanly labelled — included for completeness, not as a problem.

**Lowercase "b.c." / "bc" in the sense of uncalibrated, in older literature:**

5. **LBK (#5).** An older reference work presents the whole LBK scheme in lowercase "b.c." — origin c. **5700 b.c.**, Older phase **5500–5300 b.c.**, farthest west c. **4900 b.c.** — and states that "difficulties with calibration and resolution make it virtually impossible to construct a chronology relying on carbon-14 dating alone" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/first-farmers-central-europe)). Related central European Neolithic dates are quoted as "**3110 ± 110 bc**" and "**2130 + 65 bc**" ([Academia.edu, radiocarbon dates of the earliest Neolithic in central Europe](https://www.academia.edu/120530120/Radiocarbon_Dates_of_the_Earliest_Neolithic_in_Central_Europe)).

6. **Afanasievo (#13).** Not uncalibrated itself, but the review states directly that "early researchers often used only non-calibrated dates, which seriously affected the conclusions" — the long chronology this entity is famous for was partly an artefact of uncalibrated LSC dates ([QUB review](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/203637844/ReviewOf.pdf)).

**Lowercase "bc" that is NOT uncalibrated — a notation trap to flag, not a dating error:**

7. **Yamnaya (#16), Bell Beaker (#9), Corded Ware (#8), Stonehenge (#7), Botai (#12).** *Nature* and some Antiquity-adjacent house styles render **calibrated** calendar dates in lowercase "bc"/"bce", which collides directly with the old British archaeological convention in which lowercase "bc" meant *uncalibrated*. Confirmed instances: the *Nature* version of the Indo-European paper writes that the Yamnaya complex "appeared around 3300 bc" ([Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08531-5)), while the PMC version of the identical paper writes "BCE" and "cal BCE" throughout with no lowercase "bc" ([PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)). The *Nature* horse-domestication paper likewise dates the DOM2 expansion "from ~2200 bc" ([Librado et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9)). A Stonehenge monograph preview uses lowercase "bc" in the same way ([Bloomsbury preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781350192249_A46419761/preview-9781350192249_A46419761.pdf)). The accepted manuscript of the Beaker paper uses uppercase "BCE" for the same values that appear as lowercase "bc" in the journal version ([Olalde et al. 2018, accepted version](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). **Rule for the app: for post-2015 genetics papers in *Nature*, lowercase "bc" is calibrated calendar BC, not uncalibrated. For pre-1990 British archaeological literature, lowercase "bc" is uncalibrated. Do not treat the two as the same notation.**

**Entities in this set with no uncalibrated dependency at all:** Neanderthal Europe (#1, U-series + luminescence + ESR), Altamira (#4, uranium-series plus calibrated AMS), Megalithic Europe (#6, 2,410 calibrated dates in OxCal), Andronovo (#14, IntCal-calibrated), BMAC (#15, published as cal BC), Afanasievo (#13, recalibrated in OxCal 4.3.2/IntCal13 even where the underlying measurements are old beta-counting).

## Notes requested specifically for these two regions

**Stonehenge phase structure** — given in full under entity #7, field A. All five stages are **Bayesian-modelled posterior density estimates at 95% probability** from OxCal 4.1 with the Reimer 2009 curve, using 52 of 62 available radiocarbon dates with 10 rejected, at an agreement index of Amodel = 115% ([Darvill et al. 2012](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/20587/1/Antiquity%202012%20(Stonehenge%20Remodelled).pdf)). Stage 1 (ditch, bank, Aubrey Holes) **3000–2920 cal BC**; Stage 2 (first stone settings, sarsen circle and trilithons, with the bluestones brought into the monument) **2620–2480 cal BC**; Stage 3 **2480–2280 cal BC**; Stage 4 (bluestone oval and outer bluestone circle) **2280–2020 cal BC**; Stage 5 (Y and Z holes) **2020–1520 cal BC**. The key reordering relative to older schemes is that the **sarsens precede** the final bluestone arrangements.

**Chauvet dispute** — given in full under entity #2, field F. The radiocarbon side is [Quiles et al. 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523158113) with 259 determinations, ³⁶Cl rockfall dating and thermoluminescence. The stylistic objection was made by **C. Züchner** and, in its fullest form, by **Paul Pettitt and Paul Bahn** in *Antiquity* 89(345), proposing a Solutrean-or-later chronology of roughly 22,000–18,000 BP ([Pettitt & Bahn 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/an-alternative-chronology-for-the-art-of-chauvet-cave/723AB31F4E88629A2B7323BDD4845450)).

**Botai** — stated unambiguously under entity #12: **Botai horses are not ancestral to modern domestic horses.** They are *Equus przewalskii*, and modern Przewalski's horses are their feral descendants ([Gaunitz et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao3297); [Taylor & Barrón-Ortiz 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8018961/)). Modern domestic horses (DOM2) instead originate in the lower Volga-Don steppe and expand from ~2200 BC ([Librado et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04018-9)).

**Yamnaya, Corded Ware and Bell Beaker: where dating rests on aDNA + radiocarbon together** — all three now rest on genome-wide ancient DNA paired with **direct radiocarbon dates on the same skeletons**, not on typology: 198 new radiocarbon dates alongside 428 individuals for Yamnaya ([Lazaridis et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922553/)), 140 new radiocarbon dates alongside 271 genomes for Bohemian Corded Ware ([Papac et al. 2021](https://www.stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Papac2021-up.pdf)), and 106 new direct radiocarbon dates alongside 400 genomes for Bell Beaker ([Olalde et al. 2018](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/56097393/OlaldeEtalN2018TheBeakerPhenomenon.pdf)). In each case the genetic signal and the radiocarbon dates constrain each other: the earliest Corded Ware individuals' dates are corroborated by their position on PC2 in the genetic PCA, and the Yamnaya population-formation date of 4038 ± 48 BCE comes from a DATES admixture estimate calibrated against radiocarbon-dated individuals.

**Uncalibrated-radiocarbon flags** — see summary section 1. The genuine cases are **Lascaux** (the official ministry page reports raw BP throughout without stating calibration and gives no calibrated ranges) and the **pre-2019 Denisova Cave** chronology (lowercase "bp" in Reich et al. 2010). The critical warning for the app is the **notation collision**: post-2015 *Nature* papers use lowercase "bc" for *calibrated* calendar dates, while pre-1990 British archaeological literature used lowercase "bc" for *uncalibrated* dates. The two must not be conflated.
